# Reference cards

`reference-cards.html` holds the printable table aids: one **general card** for every player and one **province card** per role. Each card is two-sided and half a letter sheet. The page is generated by `tools/build_reference.py`, so never edit the HTML by hand; change the script and rebuild:

```sh
python3 tools/build_reference.py   # writes reference-cards.html, then runs add_glyphs on it
```

The page stands alone, like `i-ching.html`. Manish plans to link to it from the rulebook and elsewhere later.

## What Manish asked for

- **Two sets of cards**: one general card that everyone gets, and one per-player card for each province.
- **Two-sided cards**, at half letter size (5.5 × 8.5 in): two per letter sheet, printed double-sided and cut in half.
- **A separate HTML page**, like the I Ching page.
- **On the general card**: the turn order (always), plus the extras he picked from a list: realia ownership, money at a glance, the Harvest Pile in and out, and the public works table.
  - He was also offered, and **did not pick**: a population track or I Ching deck tracker, climbing seats (who may replace whom), revolt and Chaos, and game end and scoring. Leave those off the general card unless he asks.
- **On each province card**: the income (harvest) table, the realia to collect, what to do each round, and the public works owed to that province.

Later corrections from Manish, all applied:

- The new harvest squares section opens with **"Only available once your population has doubled (×2)"**, in bold.
- The line under each income table says **"+ 20 per new harvest square you own"**. The Warlord's card leaves that out, since he can't buy squares.
- The Warlord's raid rules are **two separate lines**: raiding realia (5, the piece, or its cost), and raiding the Emperor's buildings (the Emperor pays the full cost, 20). A third line says any other square, canal and reservoir squares included, is an ordinary raid that leaves the work undamaged.
- The Warlord's toll line says the Great Wall counts as a public work for the toll, and that it can't be raided.

## Layout

**General card.**
- **Front, "The round":** the nine steps of a round, numbered, each with who acts. A note says Round One starts at step 4. An "at any time" box covers deals and revolt. Below that, the realia table: which province sells each piece.
- **Back, "Money and building":** money at a glance (every fixed number, as a definition list), the Harvest Pile in and out, and the public works table. This is the densest card. It uses the `.dense` styles to fit, so check it first after any edit.

**Province card.**
- **Front:** a one-line character sketch in italics, what to do each round, the objective by player count (4–5, 6, 7+), the province's starting realia (including the pair it never needs at 4–5 players), and who the player may replace. Then a card-specific extra, if any: "Beware the Warlord" for the Emperor, "Your army" for the Warlord. Last, the three personal disasters as a short title plus the cost.
- **Back:** the income table (larger type: it's the most-read thing on the card), the public works owed, with cost, who pays and the yearly bonus, and when and where new harvest squares can be bought.

**Header colours by class.** General: ink. Emperor: vermilion. Official, Gentry and Scholar: dark. Peasants: walnut. Warlord: slate. Each header carries a FRONT or BACK tag so the halves can be told apart once cut.

**Glyphs.** Every piece name gets its glyph from `images/` via `add_glyphs.py`, the same as the rulebook and the playthrough. Glyphs in the coloured headers are drawn white.

## Printing

- **Screen:** each card shows its front and back side by side.
- **Print:** landscape letter, no margins, two cards per sheet, five sheets. Two general cards come first, then Provinces 1–2, 3–4, 5–6 and 7–8. On each back sheet the two cards are swapped, so the backs line up when the page is printed **double-sided, flipped on the short edge**. The page tells people to switch to the long edge if the backs come out upside down. **This hasn't been tested on a real printer.**
- **Fit:** every card must fit its 5.5 × 8.5 in box with no overflow. To check, render the page in a headless browser and compare each `.screen .card`'s `scrollHeight` with its `clientHeight`. Also measure the gap between the last section and the footer. Then print to PDF with `preferCSSPageSize` and look at the sheets. The general back has only a few pixels to spare.

## Where the content comes from

Every number mirrors the rulebook and `RULINGS.md`, but **the script hardcodes them**. When a ruling or a rulebook number changes, update the matching table in `build_reference.py` and rebuild:

| Table in the script | What it holds | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `TURN` | The nine steps of a round | Section 5, Note 5 |
| `MONEY`, `PILE_OUT`, `PILE_IN` | Fixed sums; the Harvest Pile | Sections 7–14 |
| `WORKS`, `OWED`, `OWED_NOTE` | Public works, costs, who pays, bonuses | Section 12, each province's booklet section |
| `STATS`, `income_table()` | Population, consumption and harvest; the income rows ×1 to ×6 | Section 7, Note 6 |
| `NEED45`, `STUCK` | Objectives at 4–5 players; the pair each province never needs | Sections 10–11, Note 8 |
| `DUTIES`, `REPLACE`, `NEWLAND`, `EXTRA` | Each round's duties; seat changes; new land; the extras | Part Two, Sections 11, 13, 14, Note 10 |
| `CHARACTER`, `DISASTERS` | Character sketches; personal disasters | Part Two |

**Copyright.** The character sketches and disaster titles are short paraphrases in our own words, not booklet text. Keep them that way: no quoting the booklets on the cards.
