Reference Cards

Dynasty (1969) · a general card, a card for running the round, and one card per province, two-sided

Every player gets the general card (the round, the realia, the money and the public works) and the card for their own province (income table, duties, objective, what they are owed, personal disasters). Whoever runs the round, usually the Emperor or whoever is teaching the game, also gets Running the round: a script for each step, with what to check before moving on. Each card is half a letter sheet, front and back.

To print: print this page on letter paper, landscape, double-sided, flipped on the short edge, with background graphics on. You get six sheets: two general cards, two Running the round cards, then provinces 1–2, 3–4, 5–6 and 7–8. Cut each sheet down the middle. If the backs come out upside down, switch to flipping on the long edge. Print the first sheet again for more general cards.

Figures are in millions of bags of grain and follow the rulebook.

Running the round

Running the round

Read each line aloud, then check before moving on

  1. 1
    I ChingScholar

    “Scholar, turn the top card and read it to us.”

    The population counter moved, if it changed. Everyone knows their harvest. Any Personal Disasters and levies are paid to the Pile.

  2. 2
    HarvestGentry

    “Gentry, pay out the harvest.”

    Everyone has their grain: harvest, + Public Works bonuses, + 20 per new harvest square, − what their people eat. Anyone short sells realia back at 2, then goes into debt.

  3. 3
    RentPeasants

    “Gentry, are you asking for rent?”

    Each Peasant asked has paid at least 5. Not asked, not owed.

  4. 4
    Tax declaredEmperor

    “Emperor, what is the tax this year?”

    One rate for everyone, 10% to 90%.

  5. 5
    Tax paidOfficial

    “Pay the Official your share of this year’s harvest income.”

    Harvest income only: no rent, salaries, commissions or tolls. Rounded down to the half million. A loss pays nothing; the Emperor pays none; the Warlord may refuse. The Official then hands all of it to the Emperor.

  6. 6
    Public WorksEmperor

    “Emperor, what are you building?”

    One project: any number of sections of one work. Palaces before what needs them; nothing else built means the Marble Pagoda. Cost shares paid; new harvest squares bought from the Pile.

  7. 7
    AuctionProvinces 2 to 7, in order

    “Province 2, what are you selling?”

    One piece each; minimum bid 2. The Emperor never bids. Seller keeps ¼, rounded down to the half million; the rest to the Pile. The buyer places it at once. Then the Pile auctions anything sold back to it. The Warlord raids instead.

  8. 8
    SoldiersProvinces

    “Anyone moving soldiers?”

    Only after the Warlord’s have moved; one square each. A bought soldier stays put until its province has been raided.

  9. 9
    SalariesEmperor

    “Emperor, pay the Official and the Scholar.”

    At least 5 each.

Running the round

Round One, Chaos and the end of a round

Round One

  • No I Ching card and no harvest: start at step 3, rent.
  • The 60 everyone starts with counts as the year’s harvest income, so it is what tax is charged on.
  • Everything from rent onward runs as normal, the auction and the Warlord’s first raids included.

During Chaos

  • Stopped: no tax, no salaries, no Public Works, and so no new harvest squares (no Emperor to share the cost).
  • Still on: harvests are paid, and the Warlord still rides. The fallen Emperor keeps Province 1 for now.
  • It ends when a new Emperor is acclaimed, even mid-round: the new Emperor then declares tax, builds and pays salaries that round.
  • The count: each claimant's grain, soldiers and friends (backers, one claim each). Leading one is enough if no rival leads another; otherwise whoever leads more wins. A tie leads nobody. The fallen Emperor can't claim.

Before the next round

  • The population counter shows the current figure.
  • Works built this round add to harvests from next round.
  • Who is in debt? Until it’s paid they can’t bid, trade realia, give grain, share costs, buy soldiers or change seats, and everything they receive pays it first.
  • Realia sold back to the Pile goes up at the next auction. Realia returned on a seat change goes home, to be sold again.
  • Is every public work owed now built? Then the game is over: the reign was glorious.
Any time, any step
  • Trade, bribe, threaten and make deals. No deal is binding.
  • Buy soldiers from the Warlord: 20 each.
  • Sell realia back to the Pile for 2.
  • Keep your grain secret, or be cagey about it.
  • Revolt, if two other players agree the Emperor has failed.

Province 1 · Emperor

1 · Emperor

Province 1 · population 14M · eats 60 · harvest 100/75/50

The Son of Heaven: forceful, thoughtful, just. You care for the masses, not for petty misfortunes, and your word is law.

Each round

  • Declare the tax: one rate, 10–90%, for everyone.
  • Build one Public Works project. In a round you build nothing else, build the Marble Pagoda.
  • At the end of the round, pay the Official and the Scholar at least 5 each.
  • Settle disputes. Negotiate with the upper class first.
  • Never bid at an auction.

Your objective

Stay in power. Build every public work you owe and your reign ends in glory.

Who you may replace

You cannot climb; you can only fall. The Official, Gentry, Scholar or Warlord takes the throne by completing their objective. You then take their seat and draw 60 from the Pile.

Beware the Warlord

Every raid on your province costs you 10, not 5. A raid on the Summer Palace, Winter Palace or Marble Pagoda costs its full price, and the building stays.

Personal disasters

  1. Thrown by a gift horse4
  2. The Prince breaks an ivory treasure10
  3. The burning of the books30

Paid to the Harvest Pile. When I Ching calls for one, play the next in order; after the third, start again.

Province 8 · Warlord

8 · Warlord

Province 8 · population 8M · eats 20 · harvest 40/30/20

Cruel and vicious. You lie, cheat and above all steal, and you mean to buy enough soldiers to take the throne.

Each round

  • Move every soldier one square, any direction.
  • Raid one unraided square per soldier: 5, or 10 in Province 1.
  • Raiding realia: take 5 or the piece itself.
  • Raiding the Summer Palace, Winter Palace or Marble Pagoda: the Emperor pays you its full cost (20). The building stays.
  • Any other square, canal and Reservoir squares included, is an ordinary raid, and the work is undamaged.
  • Sell soldiers to anyone who asks: 20 each, you keep 25%.
  • Pay 5 per soldier that crosses a river or a public work, the Great Wall included. The Wall stands outside the provinces, so it can't be raided.
  • Pay your tax, or refuse: only soldiers or politics can make you. You hold no realia and never auction.

Your objective

Raid a different piece of realia from each player. Then take any seat, the throne included.

Who you may replace

Any player, including the Emperor.

Your army

2 soldiers at 5–6 players, 4 at 8 or more, starting anywhere on undeveloped land. A raided square can never be raided again, but you may stand on it. Soldiers capture by moving onto a soldier orthogonally, never diagonally. Bought soldiers stay frozen in their province until you raid it.

Running the round

Round One, Chaos and the end of a round

Round One

  • No I Ching card and no harvest: start at step 3, rent.
  • The 60 everyone starts with counts as the year’s harvest income, so it is what tax is charged on.
  • Everything from rent onward runs as normal, the auction and the Warlord’s first raids included.

During Chaos

  • Stopped: no tax, no salaries, no Public Works, and so no new harvest squares (no Emperor to share the cost).
  • Still on: harvests are paid, and the Warlord still rides. The fallen Emperor keeps Province 1 for now.
  • It ends when a new Emperor is acclaimed, even mid-round: the new Emperor then declares tax, builds and pays salaries that round.
  • The count: each claimant's grain, soldiers and friends (backers, one claim each). Leading one is enough if no rival leads another; otherwise whoever leads more wins. A tie leads nobody. The fallen Emperor can't claim.

Before the next round

  • The population counter shows the current figure.
  • Works built this round add to harvests from next round.
  • Who is in debt? Until it’s paid they can’t bid, trade realia, give grain, share costs, buy soldiers or change seats, and everything they receive pays it first.
  • Realia sold back to the Pile goes up at the next auction. Realia returned on a seat change goes home, to be sold again.
  • Is every public work owed now built? Then the game is over: the reign was glorious.
Any time, any step
  • Trade, bribe, threaten and make deals. No deal is binding.
  • Buy soldiers from the Warlord: 20 each.
  • Sell realia back to the Pile for 2.
  • Keep your grain secret, or be cagey about it.
  • Revolt, if two other players agree the Emperor has failed.
