## Years 1–2

Two years. I paid thirty to the crown and five to Rich, my field went under the floods, and the crown still wanted half of it. So I called it, the whole table stood with me, and now there's nobody in that chair. This year's tax is void, which leaves me with about twenty-nine grain, the first time I've gone up instead of down. I bid small, and I lost every piece I bid on. Paddy has a full set now, and I have a promise from Clark, said out loud: half the rate on flooded fields. Rich wants the throne with a granary we filled for him, and he won't get it while my name's on someone else's claim. My backing is the only thing I own that's worth much right now, and I'll make them bid for it.

## Years 3–4

Two years with nobody in the chair, and I've gone from twenty-nine grain to a hundred and twelve. No tax, no salaries, no palace. Rich takes his five and wants a thank-you for it. He can keep waiting. Rob walked Clark's Deed into the desert and then Paddy's, and he ate ten out of Rich's own fields, which I'll admit I enjoyed watching. Clark finally put it in writing: the first canal to Paddy, the second to me, and half rate for any province that floods. That's the first time my coast has been on paper, so my name stays on his claim. That's for now, though, not for good. Rich is near two hundred and he'll start buying friends soon, so Clark has to keep outbidding him, every year, until I'm the one who decides it's time.

## Years 5–6

Year five was another average harvest with nobody in the chair, and nothing changed: thirty-five in, five to Rich, and three claimants who each led one count. Then the people doubled, and suddenly every mouth on my coast ate twice. There was land at twenty a square and nobody could sell it to me, so Chaos stopped costing nothing and started costing me something every year. That's what brought everyone round. Clark wrote it all down, with years beside it, and in the end the whole table backed him, Rich included, right after he'd sworn he never would. I've got six squares of sea on the north of the coast now. They cost me forty and the crown eighty, and they're well away from Rob's swords. My canal is written in for Clark's second year, and I'll read it back to him. Rich bought my own Jar for thirty-six, I got nine of it, and he had a full set for about an hour before Rob walked off with his Bowl. Reina beat me to Paddy's Bowl by one grain. So I'm on a hundred and twenty with no pieces and a crown I helped put up, and next year I find out whether my name bought me a canal or only another promise.

## Years 7–8

Clark gave me the I Ching to read, three grain for the ink, so for two years I've been the one telling the table what Heaven says. The first year was a bad harvest. I got ninety from the pile and paid twenty-seven in tax and five to Rich, and I still bought six squares of sea for forty, because land that pays for itself in a year is cheapest when everyone else is too frightened to buy it. The second year was a good one: two hundred and sixty in, seventy-eight to the crown, five to Rich, and my canal went in, both sections, in the order Clark wrote down in Year 4. I read that page back to him and he kept it. I bought four more squares and asked the crown for only ten. I didn't do it for Clark. A crown with half a grain in its purse ends up paying Rob for scenery, and I've already lived four years with nobody in that chair. I'm on about two hundred and fifty now, with sixteen squares of coast and a canal running through it, and that's more than I ever had under Chaos. The Wall over the north is written in for Year 9, Reina has sworn she won't move the date again, and I'll be counting to make sure it goes up.

## Years 9–10

In Year 9 I read the Emperor's Share and paid Clark ten for my own ditch. Then he asked fifty, my part of it was a hundred and sixty-seven and a half, and ten links of Wall went up that same year. I said I'd count it into the mortar and I did. In Year 10 the canals gave way everywhere except where they'd been finished, so mine cost me two for holding. The tax went back to thirty, a hundred and thirty-six and a half from me, and the last two links went up over Reina's roof, Year 8 to Year 10, exactly as written. In those two years I bought fourteen squares and asked the crown for almost nothing, because a crown sitting on two grain or on zero pays Rob for scenery and never pays for stone. I'm on four hundred and eighty-four and a half with thirty squares of coast. That's more grain than I ever saw in the years I was paying thirty to a palace I'd never sit in. There's still two hundred and ten owed: Clark's canal, Rich's reservoir, Reina's irrigation and the Pagoda. The drawer has seventeen and a half in it, so Clark will need the coast again. He'll get it, at my price, and I'll be counting every grain.


## Years 11–12

Year 11 was Fortune and Misfortune. Heaven passed over the coast, the pile paid me six hundred and forty, and I paid a hundred and ninety-two of it to the crown for a Reservoir in Rich's fields. I took the crown's full twenty on eight squares and then said out loud that the drawer would keep three hundred and ten, when it was really a hundred and fifty-five, and Paddy corrected me in front of the table. I don't enjoy being the one who's corrected, so in Year 12 I paid a hundred and eight of my own for six more squares and asked the crown for two a square. Clark dug his own canal from his own drawer, on the year he wrote down, and dropped the rate to twenty, so I paid a hundred and fifty-five instead of a hundred and ninety-two. I bid three for the Bowl, Reina paid twelve for it, and a year later it's sitting in Rob's desert. Rob took five off me at P21 and called it my first, but it's my second, because I remember P25 in Year 1. I'm on about fourteen hundred and thirty-five now, with forty-four squares of coast, and only fifty of stone left to build: Reina's Irrigation next year and then the Pagoda. Two more years, and when they're done, the grain in my drawer is my score.


## Years 13–14

In Year 13 I read a bad harvest, and the coast still came out ahead. Rich asked his five, the same five as Year 1, and for once I thanked him for it out loud. Clark held the rate at twenty. This time I took his full twenty a square on ten more squares, because the drawer had nearly a thousand in it and only twenty of stone left to buy. Rob took another five off me at P20, which makes it his third, if anyone's keeping count, and I am. In Year 14 I read the Utility Tax and paid four grain to keep up a canal I'd already paid for twice. Clark put it into the Pagoda as I asked, and when that last stone went up on G22 the book closed, every line built in the year it was written. I end on two thousand nine hundred and ninety-one and a half, more than anyone at the table, crown included. In Year 2 I had twenty-nine and a revolt. I never sat in a palace and never wanted to. I counted every grain that went out of my barn, and that's why the coast has the most left now.
