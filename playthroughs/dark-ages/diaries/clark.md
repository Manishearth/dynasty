## Years 1–2

Years 1 and 2. I had a complete set by the end of the first year's auctions, Deed, Jar and Bowl, and I've kept them in the drawer ever since. A throne you buy has to be guarded every night, so I'd rather be given one. This year Reina put fifty on a drowned field for the second year running and wouldn't cut a grain of it when we asked her to. Five names stood by the revolt and mine was one of them. The chair has been empty since. The first count went three ways, Rich on his granary, Rob on his two swords and me on Silke's one hand, so nobody was acclaimed. That cost me a salary, and Rob has taken another 5 out of Province 2 and calls it the Warlord's tax, which leaves me at 21.5. New in the ledger: Paddy now holds a full set as well, Rich is already calling himself the friend of "a man with a full set and no chair", and the Gentry's man is precisely the man I won't have on that throne. I've given Paddy my word on the first canal, Silke has my word on the flood rate, and I can wait. Chaos costs the landlord more than it costs me, and I'll be the one keeping count when the table gets tired of waiting.

## Years 3–4

Years 3 and 4. Rob took my Deed from J14 to M15 in a year Heaven called careless, then took Paddy's to T24 in a year it called average. That's two pages under his name now, and I've written both down with the squares. The set is broken, but under Chaos a set can't reach the chair anyway, and with no tax and no salary for two years I've gone from 22.5 to 115.5, which no Emperor would have let me do. I finally put the canals in writing in front of the whole table: Province 6 in the first year I sit, Province 5 the year after, and half the rate on any flooded field. Paddy heard it, and the next day he turned Rich down over the Jar. Reina wants walls over the north before she'll lend her name, Silke wants her terms on paper before the harvests turn, and Rich is still counting grain as if grain had ever made anyone a friend. Rob's lads ate ten out of the Gentry's own fields this year, and I enjoyed writing that down more than I should have. I'll keep Silke and keep my word to Paddy, and I'll wait until the table is tired of feeding swords.

## Years 5–6

Years 5 and 6. For one more year of drought the count went three ways, and I kept my claim and kept my mouth to my promises. Then the people doubled, the land opened with no chair to sell it, and the table lost its patience all at once. Paddy came first, then Reina for the Wall and the Pagoda, and in the end even Rich and Rob put their names on mine. Every one of them is in the ledger now, and every one of them came with a price. I was given the throne, I never bought it, and that's the kind that lasts. I owned up straight away that the canal has to wait one year behind the Winter Palace. The palace stands on H20, and I paid dearly in land to make the delay go down easier: 150 for Silke's and Paddy's squares, 60 for my own, and the 20 for Paddy's soldier. Rob took that soldier back the same afternoon, so the coast-guard line is kept in the letter and broken in fact. The reserve that started at 401.5 stands at 145.5, and 850 in works is still owed. Rich has his rent, his pile and a set he held for less than an hour before Rob carried his Bowl off to N12. That's a fourth page under the Warlord's name, and Rob is still waiting for me to put a number on peace. Next year Paddy's canal goes in, as written, and I'll tax enough to keep building, because Silke and Paddy are counting, and so am I.

## Years 7–8

Years 7 and 8. Two canals in two years, Province 6 in the lean year and Province 5 in the fat one, both at thirty percent, and both written "built" in hands other than mine. Paddy's fifty came back in the year I named, and then something I hadn't budgeted for happened: Silke and Paddy took two and a half a square instead of my ten and told me to keep the rest for the Wall. I've written that down too, as kindness and as leverage both, because a province that refuses its due has lent the crown something, and I mean to repay it in stone. It cost the reserve down to half a grain at one point, and it stands at 21 now with 690 still owed. Rich has put a quarter of every Province 3 link in Reina's ledger, which is the first page I've ever opened for a landlord on the credit side. Reina moved the Wall to Year 9 in ink and swears she won't move it again, and she's right not to. I moved it once in the open and I won't do it twice. Rob has taken Paddy's Customs House, made a fifth page under his name, and promised me a bill next year. A crown with 21 in the drawer can't pay scenery money, so the real answer is the Wall, which is the one work that keeps swords out of the north.

## Years 9–10

Years 9 and 10. The Wall went up over the north, ten links in the first year and the last two in the second, from Province 1 all the way to the desert, and it was never moved a third time. I asked fifty percent for it once and went back to thirty, and nobody said the word revolt, because everyone could see the stone. Rich paid his forty before Reina had finished reading the line, Reina paid twenty twice for stone over her own roof, and Silke and Paddy each turned down half my land money and told me to put it toward the works. I've written every one of those on the credit side, and that's more than I ever expected to write for a landlord. It cost the drawer down to nothing once. I had to pay Reina five and owe her the other five, and I cleared that before a single link was ordered in Year 10, so "settled" is now in her hand and not just mine. Rob carried off Rich's Customs House, then put Reina in debt over a Bowl, and still wants paying not to do it. That's page six, and the answer hasn't changed. Four squares under the Wall are mine now, eighty a harvest from next year. Still owed: 210, the Province 1 canal, the reservoir, the irrigation and the Pagoda. Reina's already reading the Pagoda to me, and I mean to give her something to strike off.


## Years 11–12

Years 11 and 12. Two works in two years. The Reservoir went in on Rich's quarter, the same twenty he's always paid, and the Province 1 canal went in out of my own drawer in the very year I named, so Silke has now written "the ink holds" in her own hand and doesn't have to take my word for it. I took the rate down to twenty in a steady year, and the coast did something even I hadn't foreseen. They paid nearly all of their own land and left the crown two a square, which puts a second kindness on their page, and Paddy said "two figures kept" out loud in front of everyone. I made one mistake, naming F17 to F20 as new land when they were mine already. I wrote it against myself before anyone else could, because a man who keeps a ledger has to keep his own page honest as well. Rob carried Reina's Bowl off to M16 and collected his fives from Rich, Reina and finally Silke. That's page seven, and Rich now has to pay Rob's own old soldier to guard his Jar, which is the funniest line in the whole book. The drawer holds 426.5 and only fifty is owed. Next year is Reina's Irrigation, which she's already written into Year 13 herself, and then the Pagoda in a year with nothing else beside it. I intend to be in this chair when she strikes off the last line.


## Years 13–14

Years 13 and 14. I did the last two works in the last two years, the Irrigation in the lean one and the Pagoda on G22 in a year with nothing else beside it, both out of my own drawer and both at twenty percent. Reina wrote "settled" by the last line in her own hand, and that's the only seal I ever wanted. Silke finally took my twenty a square and said thank you to the landlord in the same breath, Paddy paid his own way to the end so he could have it written down, and Rich never raised the rent by a grain in fourteen years. I've written all of it on the credit side, even his. Rob walked W3 to I17 and took ten from my own province for the view, which makes page eight. The Wall he spent a decade laughing at is the reason that's where the book closes. The drawer ends at 1365.5. The coast ends richer than the crown, and I'm glad, because a coast that grew fat under my rate is the best proof that the rate was fair. I was given the chair, I never bought it, and the same table that gave it to me watched me pay off every promise I made it. I'm still sitting in it, and the ledger is closed with nothing owed on either side.
