## Years 1–2

Two years in and the throne is empty, and I never even had to lay a finger on it. Reina taxed them half twice over, flooded fields and all, and when I called the revolt every one of them stood up beside me, the Gentry and the Official too, the very men who'd been eating at her table. It's cost me nothing yet. I've passed every lot, my grain sits at seventy-five, and with no Emperor there's nobody to pay me off or send men after me, so my soldiers just carry on. The count for the chair is three ways deadlocked: Rich has the granary, Clark has Silke, and I have the only swords on the board. I'm happy for that to go on for a good long while. Next year W1 takes Clark's Deed and W2 goes down the coast to see Paddy's shiny new set "from a distance". If they want an Emperor badly enough, they'll know whose price has to be met.

## Years 3–4

Two more years without a crown and I've never had it so good. Heaven sent a fat harvest and then the sealed treasury paid me two grain for my trouble, and I spent twenty of it on a third lad and got the rest back off Rich's fields in a single night. Clark's Deed is sitting at M15 and Paddy's is sitting at T24, so that's two sets broken, and there's nobody in the chair for them to complain to, only Clark's ledger, and he can write my name in it as often as he likes. Paddy's been watching W2 in his field with a face like thunder, and Rich has started whispering to him about keeping my lads out, which tells me the swords are finally being taken seriously. The count's still stuck three ways: Rich has the grain, Clark has Silke and now a canal on paper, and I've got the only soldiers on the board. Rich and Silke are the two pieces I'm missing, so W1 and W3 are already standing in Province 3, and Silke's coast opens up again next year. The longer they argue over who sits, the longer I get to keep collecting.

## Years 5–6

Well, they finally found someone to sit in the chair, and it's Clark, the little man with the ledger, which makes it the only one of my sins he's ever going to get to do anything about. I held out through the drought, and when the people doubled, the coast went over to him one name after another. In the end I put my own name on his claim too. An empty throne never paid me a grain to stay at home, and an Emperor might. It cost me the count and a soldier Paddy made me sell him, and I took that soldier straight back the same afternoon along with Paddy's Jar, and Rich's Bowl about an hour after he finished his set. That makes four pieces in my desert, and only one of them was ever paid for, by the man who lost it. Clark promised me "my figure" once my lads are back on the sand. I'll believe that when I'm holding the grain. Until then Reina's new Bowl is sitting at G14, Silke's coast is still untouched, and there's a brand-new Winter Palace on H20 that costs the crown twenty if anyone so much as breathes on it. The dynasty has an Emperor again, and I'm still the most dangerous man at the table. I've never been happier.

## Years 7–8

Two years under Clark, and the funny thing is the place works. Two canals are dug, Paddy's fifty is paid back, and Reina reads the dates out like a schoolmistress. Every province is spending its grain on fields and stone, and none of it is coming to me. I did get Paddy's Customs House, though. I paid his toll like a gentleman, walked it home to U21, and that's five pieces in my desert now. Then Heaven tied my lads' hands for a whole year, and it cost me five for the privilege. My grain sat at a hundred and seven and didn't move, which is the dullest thing that's happened to me since Year 1. Clark told me to my face the crown doesn't pay for scenery. Fair enough, but he still keeps checking how far my lads are from his palaces, and a man who counts the rounds is a man who'll pay before the count reaches nothing. What he doesn't see is W3 standing in Reina's field, three rounds off her Bowl. Reina and Silke are the last two names on my list, and the Wall goes up over the north next year with everyone's stone on it. I'll let them build it. A finished dynasty with fat barns is a much better thing to raid than a broken one, and I'd rather be paid to keep my distance than sit on a chair someone will only try to take from me.

## Years 9–10

Well, they built the thing. Twelve links of Wall from Province 1 to the sand, and every province put its own grain in the mortar, even Reina, who hadn't two coins to rub together by the end of it. I took Rich's Customs House to L8 for my trouble, which makes six pieces on my desert, and then Heaven took half my granary in a single night. I'm sitting on sixty-three and a half, which is the poorest I've been since Year 1, and I can't say I enjoyed that part. The real joke was on me, though. I walked W3 all the way up to Reina's Bowl, and my last five sent her into debt and the Bowl went off to the pile before my lad could lay a hand on it. So Reina's still on my list with nothing left on the board to take, and Silke's coast is fatter than the treasury ever was. Clark told me my figure was nothing, then put seventeen and a half in a drawer and told me that wasn't mine either. Fine. There's a Pagoda and a reservoir still to go up, and the palaces haven't moved. When Reina buys that Bowl back, and she will, I'll be standing beside it.


## Years 11–12

Two years, and I finally got Reina. She bought that Bowl back for twelve, and she put it in the far corner of Province 2 like that would put me off. My lad walked over and carried it home to M16, so that's seven pieces on my sand. Reina's was the one I wanted most, and it was honestly raided. It cost me a soldier, mind. Rich bought him off me for twenty, I kept five, and now my own old lad is standing on Rich's Jar guarding it against me, and Rich is very pleased with himself about it. Heaven kept my hands off the coast for a year as well, but when it let go, W2 went straight into Silke's fields for five. I got that wrong at the table, actually: it was her second five, not her first, since I had one off her in Year 1. And five in cash doesn't tick her box. I need one of her pieces, and she's too clever to put one down. The works are nearly done. The Reservoir and Clark's canal are in, and only the Irrigation and the Pagoda are left, fifty between them, with four hundred and twenty-six in the drawer. That's more money than that drawer has ever held, and Clark is keeping his figures, which is very dull of him. I'm on sixty-eight and a half, so I'm still the poorest man at the table who isn't Reina. Next year W2 waits in Silke's barns for the first thing she buys, and the other two lads keep the north honest. If she won't give me a piece, I'll at least make sure I finish with a fatter purse than I started the decade with.


## Years 13–14

Well, they finished it. Clark put his Pagoda up on G22 between his two palaces, and my W3 was standing on I17 watching, close enough to count the roof tiles and nowhere near close enough to touch one. That's the dynasty saved, and I'm the man who spent fourteen years trying to eat it. I end on sixty-one and a half, which is the thinnest purse at the table. A bad harvest took twenty, Heaven took half of it back in Year 10, and Clark's figure stayed "nothing" right to the last line, the stubborn little man. I never got Silke. She was too clever to put a single piece down where my lads could reach it, and I'll admit I tip my hat to her for that. What I did get is seven pieces on my sand, three of them honestly raided off three different seats, two Land Deeds, and Reina's Bowl, the one I wanted most. I also got a whole table that spent a decade counting the rounds between my soldiers and their barns. They built a Wall, three canals, a reservoir and a Pagoda, and I'd like to think half of that was them hurrying to finish before I arrived. I didn't sit on the throne, I didn't break it, and I didn't leave rich. But nobody at that table will forget whose name was on every raid, and I had the time of my life doing it.
