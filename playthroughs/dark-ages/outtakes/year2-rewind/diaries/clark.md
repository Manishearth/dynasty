# Clark Grafton's diary

## Setup to Year 2

Two years in, and the ledger is fuller than my granary. In the first year I bought a full set for twenty grain, scroll, jar and bowl, and the crown took half of everything at fifty percent to pay for a palace nobody asked for. The Floods drowned my fields in the second year. Rob has raided the Official three times now, and when the tent called its revolt I held the line, because Chaos would have frozen my set. Reina paid me back with a soldier and five grain, which is gratitude at the going rate. My mistake this year was letting Rich have the last Bowl by a single grain, and now the landlord holds a set as well. He agreed to the revolt, then withdrew it for a Reservoir, and he'll sell his loyalty again the moment the price suits him. I'm down to twenty-three, just enough to cash in my set. Rob's man sits on I13, one step from the Bowl, and my soldier on J14 is close enough to catch him next year. So: guard the pieces, catch the Warlord's man, collect the taxes cleanly, and keep every grievance on file. If anyone takes that chair before me, it won't be Rich.

## Years 3–4

Two more years at fifty percent, four in all. Silke has asked twice whether the cause exists, and twice I said no, because Chaos would have frozen my set, and a crown handed out by acclamation goes to whoever has the soldiers and the friends. The best line in the ledger this year is that I took Rob's man off I14 with my own soldier, standing next to my Bowl, and Silke took his other one the same round, so for a while the tent had no army at all. Reina has started paying me for my loyalty: five, then fifteen, a price I can see going up. The landlord took his forty back with a smile, and for the second spring running he was bought, and he says "stayed bought" as if it were a virtue. I'll remember both of those. Rich now has a hundred and twenty-eight and a set of his own, and Rob's new man on M15 is watching Rich's Bowl, which is the first time the tent and I have wanted the same thing. Paddy has promised the cause out loud for next year, so the revolt now runs on a clock, and when it goes off, my set had better already be sitting in that chair. Eighty-three and a half in grain, the set intact, every grievance dated. It's time to stop sitting still.
