## Rich's diary so far, in his own words

## Setup to Year 2

Two years in and the ledger is kinder to me than to anyone else at that table, except perhaps the man in the tent. I paid sixty in tax, twenty-nine for three trinkets and nothing at all for the Reservoir, which Her Majesty has promised to half-fund, and I mean to hold her to every grain of that promise next spring. One word in favour of Rob's revolt and one word against it bought that, and it bought me a reputation I'm quite happy to have: Rich can be bought, and he stays bought. The set is the real prize. Clark has one too and he's poorer than me, so he'll be watching mine the way I'm watching his, and whichever of us moves first will need friends. Silke thinks I owe her for the rent, Paddy thinks I overpaid him, and both are good feelings to leave in a peasant. Next year I'll collect the Reservoir, smile at the throne, keep well over twenty grain in hand, and keep an eye on Rob's soldier four rounds from my pieces, and on whoever starts courting him.

## Years 3–4

Two more years, and the Reservoir sits between my pieces and the desert, pays me twenty a year, and has cost me exactly nothing, because Her Majesty handed back my forty to keep me out of Rob's revolt. That makes twice I've been paid to stand still, and both times I kept the bargain to the letter, which is the whole trick: a vote that stays bought fetches a better price the next time it's sold. I've a hundred and twenty-eight in grain, a complete set, a fivepence rent that keeps both peasants fond of me, and Clark now on fifteen a year and writing my name in his book more often than I'd like. The weather is changing, though. Rob has a new soldier on M15 within sight of my Bowl, Paddy has promised the table his vote next year unless something gets built on the coast, and Silke would back a revolt over the colour of the sky. Next year I keep well above twenty grain, think about a soldier or a friendly word with Silke's to cover the Bowl, and quietly let the throne know that if Paddy turns, the Gentry's price goes up, not away.
