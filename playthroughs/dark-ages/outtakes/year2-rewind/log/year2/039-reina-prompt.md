## At the table since your last turn

Reina answers: the tax stays at 50%; the Winter Palace is put off a year; the crown pays Silke and Clark 20 each now, to buy a soldier each from Rob; and she promises the Province 3 Reservoir in Year 3, the crown paying half its 80. Rob, Silke and Rich are each asked, at once, to stand by the revolt or take it back.

(Storyteller's note: A soldier bought from the Warlord costs 20, of which he keeps 5 and 15 goes to the Harvest Pile, so two sales bring Rob 10, not 40. The crown's 40 went to Silke and Clark.)

Rob: "Your Majesty, that's the most generous thing anyone's ever done for me. You've answered 'you can't control the Warlord' by paying the Warlord forty grain. I'll sell those two lads, I have to, and I'll still be standing by my call. Silke, Rich, have a think about who just got paid for this."

Silke: "Twenty grain and a soldier on my own beach. That's the first time this crown has spent anything on my coast, Majesty, and I'll take it back for that. But count with me: a revolt today hands the throne to whoever has the most grain and soldiers, and that's the man who's been raiding me. I'm not crowning my own thief. Rob, your boat leaves my sand or my soldier meets it."

Rich: "Majesty, that is a plan, and a generous one. Rob's own soldiers sent against him, and the Reservoir for Province 3 next year, half paid by the crown. I said I'd be beside the throne if you answered, and you've answered, so here I am. I withdraw, and I'll pay my thirty now. Rob, no hard feelings. You did make an excellent point, and the crown has just bought two of your men with it."

Rob stands by the revolt; Silke and Rich take it back. The revolt is dropped. Clark collects the 50%: 5 from himself, 30 from Rich, 5 from Silke, 30 from Paddy, 70 to the crown. Nothing is built this year.

(Storyteller's note: Silke's count: the acclamation compares claimants on grain in hand, soldiers and friends, and a claimant leading in only one count isn't acclaimed if a rival leads in another. Rob holds 65 grain; Rich holds more than him, and so does Paddy.)

Clark: "My last Customs House goes up at four, and it's a bargain at that. I'm sitting the rest out, friends. The Official already has everything he needs, including, I'm pleased to say, a soldier on order courtesy of the crown. And Rich, a word in public: you agreed to that revolt, then withdrew it once a Reservoir was promised. That's in the book too, under 'price of loyalty: eighty grain.'"

Rich: "Friends, let's not feed the Pile out of spite. Every piece left is the last of its kind. Silke, Paddy, you each need the Customs House and something else, and there's only one Customs House, so one of you will be short whatever happens. Let me have the Jar and the Bowl quietly and I won't lift a finger on the Customs House. Bid against me and we all end up poorer, and Rob ends up laughing."

Silke: "Forty-one and a half, friends, and twenty of it is the crown's soldier, so I'm bidding with twenty-one and a half and not a grain more. Paddy, we want the same two pieces, so don't let the Pile eat us both. And Rich, you've seventy-four and a half and you're one bowl short of a set. I can count that too."

Paddy: "Last of each on the table, so I'll say it plain: I mean to have all three this year, and I'll pay fair for them, not foolish. Silke, you and I want the same two pieces, and there's only one of each. If we run each other up, the Pile eats it and neither of us gets fed. My Bowl goes up at two again. Somebody give it a good home."

Rob: "No hard feelings, Rich? None at all. You stood beside the throne, and you'll find I'm very fond of people who stand still. Bid away, everyone. Silke, mind your soldier, he used to be mine and he still knows the way home."

The auctions: Paddy buys Clark's Customs House for 7 and Rich's Land Deed Scroll for 7, each over Silke's 6. Rich buys Silke's Dragon Jar for 11 (over Paddy's 10 and Rob's 9) and Paddy's Wooden Bowl for 11 (over Clark's 10 and Silke's 9.5). Rich now holds a complete set: Customs House, Dragon Jar and Wooden Bowl. Paddy holds a Customs House and a Land Deed, and every Dragon Jar is sold.

Paddy: "Thank you, Clark, Rich. Seven each and paid on the nail. They're going right at the back of the rice, as far from the sea as Province 6 goes. Rich, congratulations on the set, and on paying eleven for my Bowl. I'll not complain about that one."

Rich: "A Jar and a Bowl, and both at a gentleman's price. Thank you, Silke, thank you, Paddy, a quarter of it is in your pockets. I'll keep them in the deep south of my province, beside the Customs House, well away from anyone's old soldiers. And Clark, write it down properly: the price of loyalty was eighty grain, and the crown paid it to the province that feeds you all."

Silke: "The crown paid twenty for a soldier on my coast, and the crown gets one. Twenty to Rob, five for his pocket and fifteen for the Pile, and he stands right next to Rob's boat. And Rich, count with me: one set at the table, and it's yours. I won't forget whose rent paid for it."

Clark: "The crown's twenty, spent as the crown intended. One soldier, standing guard over the Official's Land Deed. Rob, your lad on H12 can admire it from a distance.
