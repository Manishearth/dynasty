diary: Two more years, and the Reservoir sits between my pieces and the desert, pays me twenty a year, and has cost me exactly nothing, because Her Majesty handed back my forty to keep me out of Rob's revolt. That makes twice I've been paid to stand still, and both times I kept the bargain to the letter, which is the whole trick: a vote that stays bought fetches a better price the next time it's sold. I've a hundred and twenty-eight in grain, a complete set, a fivepence rent that keeps both peasants fond of me, and Clark now on fifteen a year and writing my name in his book more often than I'd like. The weather is changing, though. Rob has a new soldier on M15 within sight of my Bowl, Paddy has promised the table his vote next year unless something gets built on the coast, and Silke would back a revolt over the colour of the sky. Next year I keep well above twenty grain, think about a soldier or a friendly word with Silke's to cover the Bowl, and quietly let the throne know that if Paddy turns, the Gentry's price goes up, not away.
