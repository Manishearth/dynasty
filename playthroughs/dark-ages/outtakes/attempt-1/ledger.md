# Character ledger

Kept current at the end of each round. What each character has promised, offered, refused and resented, their running bits, and their best lines so far. Then the Storyteller's rulings, as precedents. Line numbers like (009) are log entries in `log/yearN/`. A launch prompt carries each character's entry (`briefs.py` finds it by the full name at the start of the heading), so keep entries short and current.

## Storyteller's rulings

- (083) Year 5, first thing: public rules note that Chaos stops only seat changes onto the throne; a set may still replace the Official, the Gentry or another peasant (Chapter 2's "crockery" misreading).
- (091) Year 6, first thing (the Director, under "Rules misreadings", after Rich's "no soldiers, Paddy is richer"): public note that the acclamation counts claimants only; a non-claimant counts only as one friend; soldiers count for the claimant who controls them; anyone may buy a soldier from the Warlord for 20, allowed under Chaos, though not from the sealed treasury.
- (089) With the crown paying the whole Reservoir, the crown chose the site (L12:M14), clear of Reina's pieces.

- (008, 009) The Harvest Pile is the common bank; the Gentry keeps it but its grain isn't his. Noted to the table.
- (011) Auction ties go by seat order (Clark over Silke on the Land Deed at 6).
- (018) In Year 2 the four pay-or-revolt replies stood in for the Official's collect prompt; all paid, and Clark collected.
- (027, 028) A close lot gets one live raise from the top two, covering every close lot at once; the final limits are settled as ceilings.

- (043, 044) A set may take the throne after the tax is declared and before it's collected. The payer who moved has paid; the crown's year passes to the new Emperor, who may keep or change the rate for those who haven't paid and chooses the work and the salaries. Every seat still owing gets the pay-or-revolt prompt again.
- (053, 054) A live raise may be sent with the board card replaced by a one-line note when nothing on it has changed.
- (062) The same holds when the second set moves in the next year: the new Emperor keeps or changes the declared tax; the old Emperor, now in the mover's seat, owes that seat's tax and takes its job (Rich collected as the Official).
- (059) With no Scholar, the reader of the I Ching is the Emperor's appointee; a new Emperor may read it himself or appoint someone, paid what he chooses.

## Storyteller's tips

- (017, Year 2, after the tax declaration; the Director's steering) Public, to Silke: a revolt can be called at any step (§16); the caller and two others agreeing on a cause from the board card; the Emperor may answer; one called before the tax is paid voids it; what Chaos stops and what it doesn't; the sealed reserve; the acclamation counted on grain, soldiers and friends.
- (041, Year 3, after the tax declaration; the Director's steering) Public, to Clark: "Rich's set can take the throne at any step while there's an Emperor on it. Once a revolt stands, no set can: under Chaos the throne passes only by acclamation, so Rich would have to win the count on grain in hand, soldiers on the board and friends, like any other claimant. A revolt is the one thing that turns a complete set back into three pieces on a shelf."
- (086, Year 5, after the tax declaration; the Director's steering) Public, to Rich: "the reserve stands at 464. Under Chaos it's sealed, nobody may spend it, and whoever is acclaimed Emperor inherits all of it. The acclamation counts grain in hand, soldiers on the board and friends, and anyone but the Emperor who was deposed may claim." (The Director's text said 429, the end-of-Year-4 figure; filed with the figure after the harvest.)
- (093, Year 6, after the tax declaration; the Director's mid-chapter steering) Public, to Rob: "anyone may call a revolt, the Warlord included, and anyone but the deposed Emperor may claim the throne in the acclamation, the Warlord included. While Warlord soldiers raid pieces off the board, 'the Emperor can't control the Warlord' is a cause anyone can name. Once a revolt is called with two agreeing, play stops until it stands or is dropped. The acclamation then compares only the claimants, on grain in hand, soldiers on the board and friends."
