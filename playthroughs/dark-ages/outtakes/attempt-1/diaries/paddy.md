# Paddy Doyle's diary

## Years 1–2

Two years in and I've paid every grain I owed: five to Rich each year, twenty-four to the crown twice, and twenty-six at auction for a Customs House and a Dragon Jar that now sit at the bottom of the coast, as far from Rob as I could put them. The flood year was kind to Province 6 and I spent most of what it gave me, which sits badly with me, but the box was emptying and it was buy then or never. I lost the Deed to Silke by a single grain because she'd sooner pay double than watch me climb, and I can't blame her much, since the water hit her and not me. So I'm one scroll short, she holds it, and next year I'll have to go to her and ask her price without owing her more than I can stand. Rich has a full set and smiles too much, Rob is creeping along the Wall, and Clark writes everything down. I'm keeping my head down, my rice dry and my word good.

## Years 3–4

Two Emperors in two years, and neither of them took the throne with a sword. Rich took it with a set halfway through the tax, and Clark took it off him the same way a year later, and both times the rest of us paid our twelve or our seven and said thank you. I paid every grain I owed again: rent to Rich and Reina, tax to the crown. The canal is finally being dug through Province 6, eighty grain of it, and Clark kept that promise when it would have been easy to let it die with the old chair, so I count him a man who feeds the coast until he shows me otherwise. I stood aside on the Bowl twice and kept it at the floor so Silke could finish her set, and when the Deed came up both she and Reina kept their word and let me have it for eleven. So now I'm sitting on the bottom of the coast with a Deed, a Customs House and a Jar, a hundred and one grain, and nobody holding a debt over me. I can't believe I'm writing that down. I could take Rich's chair, or Reina's, or even Silke's, but Silke has been fair with me and I won't be the one to turn her out. I'll hold the set quietly, watch where Rob walks, and only move up when the move puts more rice on the table in Province 6 than staying put would.

## Years 5–6

Two more years and I've paid every grain I owed: five rent to Reina each year, eleven and then eight to the crown, and thirty for six squares along the bottom of the coast that the crown paid three parts of. The canal came in at last, both Clark's promise and the twenty it adds to my harvest, and in the dry year that was the only reason Province 6 ate better than anyone else. Clark kept every word he gave: Reina's Reservoir, Silke's canal, land for the coast. But keeping them has emptied the reserve from over four hundred down to sixty, and Rob noticed before I did. Somebody whispered to Rich that a revolt hands the whole treasury to whoever's acclaimed, and now there's next to nothing left in it, so the only prize in a revolt is the chair itself. I don't like how Rob talks about pages and Warlords in two provinces at once. I have a hundred and forty-odd grain, a full set, six new squares coming into harvest next year, and still no debt to my name, which is more than a rice farmer has any right to. I'll keep paying, keep watching Rob's men on the walk south, and if the table turns on Clark I'll stand with the man who actually fed the coast, not the one who only talks about it.
