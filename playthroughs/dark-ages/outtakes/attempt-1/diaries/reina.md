# Reina Osorio's diary

## Years 1–2

Two years in, and the dynasty already has something to show for itself: the Summer Palace at G20, out of every soldier's reach, and both sections of the Grand Canal dug through Province 1, which will pay me twenty a year for as long as I hold the seat. I taxed forty percent both years. It was fair, a share of what the heavens gave, so the flooded fields paid four. But Clark has started saying "consistently" out loud, Rob says the desert votes, and Silke now knows from the Storyteller exactly how a revolt is called. Three voices is all it takes. The man I really watch is Rich, smiling and generous with a full set on his fields that lets him take my chair whenever he likes; Clark and Paddy are each one piece short of the same. Next year I'll ease the rate a little without calling it a concession, start on the Winter Palace so the coast's canals open, and let Paddy and Silke see that the works are coming to them. The reserve is healthy, around 142, and I mean to keep it that way. A crown that bends breaks, but a crown that builds where people can see it gives them fewer reasons to bend it.

## Years 3–4

Rich took my throne in the middle of my own tax year, smiling, with my reserve of two hundred and sixteen in his arms, and then cut the rate I'd just eased so that he'd be the one people loved. A year later Clark did the same to him. Three Emperors in four years. The lesson I take is that the chair belongs to whoever holds a set, so I'm building one: a Bowl at twenty-two and a Jar at fifteen, side by side at L16 and K17, with only the Customs House still to find. It has cost me most of what I came down with, and Rob has a soldier one step from the Bowl, so I'll have to watch my own fields as closely as I used to watch everyone else's. I asked the peasants for the floor rent and nothing more, and I told Clark I'd stand with him against any revolt for the Reservoir in Province 3. A revolt would turn my two pieces back into crockery, so I want this table quiet. Silke and Paddy both hold full sets now, and neither can touch the throne, but either could take my chair. Next year: get the Reservoir built and hold Clark to his word, buy the first Customs House that comes up, keep twenty grain in hand, and sit back on the throne with a set that nobody can argue with.

## Years 5–6

Clark kept his word, and I give him that without grudging it: the Reservoir stands in Province 3, built with the crown's eighty, and it will pay me twenty a year. So I kept mine, paid my twenty percent twice, held the rent at five and stood beside him while Silke and Paddy did their sums. What it cost me was the Bowl. Rob walked it into the desert after four years of telling me he would, and I was too busy watching the throne to watch my own field. That's the lesson, and it's an expensive one. Now I hold a Jar and nothing else, and every soldier step through Province 3 takes another five from me. The coast has done well out of the crown, with six squares each and full sets, while I can't buy an inch until four Wall links go up above me, and the reserve that stood at four hundred has gone down to sixty in a single afternoon. A treasury that empty gives the whole table a cause, and Rob has started saying so out loud. Next year I want the Wall links on Clark's page in return for my support, and I want to know what Rob would take for my Bowl. I'll keep my grain dry, because if this table falls into an acclamation, grain in hand and friends are what will count, and I mean to have more of both than anyone expects.
