# Robert Land's diary

## Years 1–2

Two years in, and I've paid the crown nothing while everyone else paid forty percent twice. Reina's sitting on a mountain of grain, building palaces and canals, and Clark has started counting to three out loud, bless him. I've spent twenty on a third soldier and taken ten off Rich at the doorstep, and the box is empty now, so every piece I need is out on the board where I can walk to it. Next is Rich's Customs House, two steps away, then Clark's scroll under the Wall, then the long stroll down to the coast for Paddy and Silke, who think distance is a kind of armour. And if Clark ever finds two friends for his revolt, the desert will happily make three. A table with no Emperor suits me fine, and an empty throne suits me even better.

## Years 3–4

Three Emperors in four years, and every one of them bought the chair with a cupboard of crockery. Rich took it in Year 3 and Clark took it off him a year later, before the tax was even in. I spent most of Year 3 walking towards Clark's set under the Wall, and the moment I got close he climbed onto the throne and his pieces went back in the box. That's a whole march wasted, and I'll admit I was a little impressed. Still, I passed every auction, let the rest of them pour their grain into the Pile, took five off Reina and five off Rich, refused my tax for the fourth year running, and I'm sitting on eighty-odd grain with three soldiers on the board, which is more than any of these chair-swappers can say. Next year W2 steps onto Reina's Bowl, or her Jar if she moves the Bowl, and I take it into the desert. W1 comes in behind to finish the job, and W3 strolls on to the palaces to send Clark a bill for twenty. Silke and Paddy are both holding full sets and smiling politely. That's two more people who might like the chair, and two more who might back a revolt if I can show the table that this Emperor can't control his Warlord. I'd rather they all stopped trading the throne and started counting grain and soldiers, because on that count the desert does very well.

## Years 5–6

Five years of walking and I finally have something to show for it. Reina's Wooden Bowl is sitting on M16 in my desert, and she's promised to remember how long the walk took, which I'll take as a compliment. I left her the Jar on purpose. One piece settles my business with Province 3, and a Gentry who's been robbed twice stops being a neighbour and starts being a crusade. Meanwhile Clark has been the best Warlord's assistant I could ask for. In two years he poured the crown from four hundred and twenty-nine down to sixty: reservoirs, canals, the coast's land paid three-quarters by him, and a little seaside estate for himself. He kept every promise, and he spent the treasury doing it, so the reserve is too low and he can't control the Warlord, and either one is a cause anyone at this table can name. I didn't call a revolt, because nobody calls one alone, and Silke and Paddy are too busy admiring their new beaches to back it just yet. The cost so far is a few tolls and four more years of everybody muttering my name, against a hundred-odd grain in hand and three soldiers on the board. Next comes the long walk to the coast, W2 to Paddy's scroll first and Silke's pieces after, with W3 working down towards the palaces to send Clark a bill for twenty he can no longer afford. The first time the crown misses a salary, I'll be standing there ready to make the third voice.
