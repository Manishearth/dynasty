# Richard Baron's diary

## Years 1–2

Two years in, and I'm the only man at this table holding a complete set. Twenty-one grain at the first auction bought me a key to any chair in the room, and I'd call that the best bargain since the Wall went up. Reina has taken forty percent twice now and sunk her grain into a Palace and a Canal on her own soil. Let her. Every work she finishes is furniture I'll inherit, and every forty percent puts Clark and Silke one step nearer saying "consistently" out loud. The second auction cost me nothing, since I let my Bowl bid lapse at nine and Clark got his porcelain. He's still a Jar short, though, the box is empty, and Paddy is a Deed short with Silke sitting on the last one. So I'm still the only one who can walk into the throne. Rob's man is standing on my doorstep at J10, raiding five a time. That's a nuisance today and an argument for me tomorrow: "the Emperor can't control the Warlord." Next year I keep the rent at five, keep smiling at Her Majesty, keep my grain above twenty, and watch for the moment the crown wobbles. When it does, I won't need a revolt or anybody's permission, only the right afternoon.

## Years 3–4

Years three and four. I picked my afternoon and it was the right one: I paid my eighteen like an honest man, showed my set, and sat down on a reserve of three hundred and thirty-five. I cut the tax, built the Winter Palace, raised Clark's pay, hired Silke to read the cards, and dug Paddy his canal, every grain spent on friends. My mistake was forgetting that Clark had a key as well as a ledger. One year later he used it on me as neatly as I'd used mine on Reina, and I watched my reserve go across the table with a smile on my face. The auction shut me out of all three pieces by a single grain each, which was polite of the table and no comfort at all. Now I'm the Official with about sixty grain, nothing on the board, and Rob's man raiding my doorstep. Clark is keeping my fifteen-grain salary, and I'll give him credit for that in public as often as he likes. Two peasants on the coast now hold full sets and can unseat me whenever they please, so I'll be very nice to Silke and Paddy. Next: collect every grain, keep my own above twenty, wait for the pieces that come back on the block when somebody uses a set, and remember that the richest man at the table is sitting in the chair again. This time he isn't me, but I've done it before.

## Years 5–6

Years five and six. I paid my seven and my four, collected every grain for the crown, and took my fifteen twice with a smile. Nobody called a revolt, and I'm glad I didn't, because Paddy outweighed me in grain and I had no soldiers and no cause anyone would have believed. Then the population doubled and I was five a year in the red, with my land shut behind four Wall links nobody has built. Meanwhile Clark spent three hundred and forty of the crown's grain in one afternoon on the coast: canals for Silke and land for both peasants. They each now hold a complete set and six squares of new land, which makes them the two most dangerous friends I have. The reserve is down to sixty, and that's interesting. A poor crown can't buy friends, and "the reserve is too low" is a cause that names itself. I'm sitting on about a hundred and fifty grain with nothing on the board, and Rob's man raids me every year at the Wall. Next year I press the Wall links (Reina is on my side for that), stay sweet with Silke and Paddy, and count. If the crown wobbles again, I'll want to be the richest friend in the room when it does.
