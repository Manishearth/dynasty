# Clark Grafton's diary

## Years 1–2

Years One and Two, entered in full. Forty percent twice running is one year short of "consistently excessive", and I've marked the page. Rob pays nothing, keeps three soldiers on the board and walks through Rich's fields for sport, which is "the Emperor can't control the Warlord" in all but name; that page is marked too. The Deed cost me 6 and the Bowl 10. I lost the last Jar to Paddy by a single grain, and I won't forget it. Rich is the danger: a full set, a fat granary and a smile for everyone, and he can step into any chair he likes, the throne included. So I don't call a revolt that only hands him the crown. I get a Jar first, from Paddy, who now owes the whole table, or from Rich, if his shelves can be made to feel crowded. I keep twenty in hand, keep paying on time, and keep the ledger. When I do move, I want three friends behind me who have already signed it.

## Years 3–4

Years Three and Four, entered in full, and the best two pages I've written. Rich took the throne halfway through Reina's tax year, just as I said he would, and in doing it he put his own Dragon Jar back on the block. I bought it from Silke for six grain. Three years of chasing, and in the end it cost me less than the Deed did. Then I waited one round, and no longer, because Rob was three steps from my Deed and Rich's chest had grown fat on his own generosity. I presented the set and took a crown with 444 in the reserve. It cost me one auction, one promise to Reina (no bid on the next Customs House or Bowl) and a second promise in private (the Province 3 Reservoir next year), and I mean to keep both, because a crown the table gives is held by friends, and Reina's name is the first one on my page. I've kept the tax at twenty, built Paddy's canal and paid Rich his fifteen, so there isn't a single cause for revolt anyone can honestly name against me, and I know every one of them by heart. What comes next: both peasants hold full sets and can't touch the throne. Rich has fifty grain and a grudge, and Rob has three soldiers and nobody who can control him, which is the one cause still open in my own book. So I build the Reservoir, I keep my palaces out of Rob's reach, and I give Rich no grievance he can write down. Three Emperors in four years. I intend to be the last.

## Years 5–6

Years Five and Six, entered in full, and I'll enter my own mistake with them, because a ledger that leaves out the keeper's errors is a lie. Every promise is kept: twenty percent four years running, the Reservoir paid to the grain, the coast's canal dug, Rich paid on the day and Silke's fee never late. But I wrote "fifteen of every twenty" without writing "up to two squares", and Silke and Paddy each read it as six. I kept my word to the letter, and the letter cost ninety grain a peasant. Add my own four squares and the reserve went from 429 to 60 in one afternoon. "The reserve is too low" was a cause I had crossed off, and now it's open again, and Rob said it out loud before anyone else thought to. Here's what I bought with it: two peasants with full bellies, full sets and every reason to keep me in the chair, a Gentry who has said twice in public that she stands with me, and an Official whose only grievance is a petition. Against that I have Rob in two provinces with Reina's Bowl, and a treasury so small that if there were a revolt, an acclamation on grain in hand wouldn't favour me. So next comes thrift, and I mean it this time: the cheapest work that honestly counts, salaries at the floor I've already set, no more open-ended promises, and the north's Wall links promised only as fast as the reserve grows back. I'll write every grain of every offer into the book before I say it out loud.
