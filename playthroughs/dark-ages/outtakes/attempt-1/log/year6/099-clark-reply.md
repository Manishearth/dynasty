diary: Years Five and Six, entered in full, and I'll enter my own mistake with them, because a ledger that leaves out the keeper's errors is a lie. Every promise is kept: twenty percent four years running, the Reservoir paid to the grain, the coast's canal dug, Rich paid on the day and Silke's fee never late. But I wrote "fifteen of every twenty" without writing "up to two squares", and Silke and Paddy each read it as six. I kept my word to the letter, and the letter cost ninety grain a peasant. Add my own four squares and the reserve went from 429 to 60 in one afternoon. "The reserve is too low" was a cause I had crossed off, and now it's open again, and Rob said it out loud before anyone else thought to. Here's what I bought with it: two peasants with full bellies, full sets and every reason to keep me in the chair, a Gentry who has said twice in public that she stands with me, and an Official whose only grievance is a petition. Against that I have Rob in two provinces with Reina's Bowl, and a treasury so small that if there were a revolt, an acclamation on grain in hand wouldn't favour me. So next comes thrift, and I mean it this time: the cheapest work that honestly counts, salaries at the floor I've already set, no more open-ended promises, and the north's Wall links promised only as fast as the reserve grows back. I'll write every grain of every offer into the book before I say it out loud.
