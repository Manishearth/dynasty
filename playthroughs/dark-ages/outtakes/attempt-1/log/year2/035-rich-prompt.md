## At the table since your last turn

Clark: "These are the last of the Jars and Bowls, friends. I'll bid fair and firm on both, and I won't chase them into the Harvest Pile. Silke, Paddy, the Land Deed is all yours. I'm standing aside on it, and I'd take it kindly if the Jar and the Bowl went the same courteous way."

Silke: "Last of everything, so let's be plain. Paddy has sixty-three and a half, I have thirty-three and a half, and we both need the same Customs House and the same Deed. Paddy, you want my Dragon Jar and I want your Bowl. If you stand aside on one of the papers, I'll stand aside on the other, and three-quarters of a bidding war doesn't go into the pile. Say so now. Otherwise I bid, and my Jar opens at eight."

Paddy: "These are the last pieces in the box, so I'll say it straight: I'm after all three, and I'd rather not hand the Harvest Pile three-quarters of a bidding war to get them. Silke, Clark, if you stand aside I'll owe you, and I pay what I owe. My Bowl goes up at two, same as ever."

Rob: "Passing again. I'm a simple man of the sands. Silke, Paddy, buy yourselves something pretty this year, you've earned it. And Clark, I can count to three as well. Forty, forty, and we'll see. I'm only saying that when the time comes, the desert votes."

Silke: "Clark has the last Bowl, so my set is finished before it started. Fine. But Paddy, you won't get yours by outspending a flooded silk farmer either. Thirteen on the Customs House, and everything I have left on the Deed. If you want to climb past the coast, it'll cost you double, and three-quarters of it goes into the pile."

Paddy: "Silke, the last Bowl's gone to Clark, so neither paper finishes anything for you now. All a war buys either of us is a fuller Harvest Pile. I'll go to eighteen on each, and that's my last word. Let me have them and I'll owe you, and you know I pay what I owe."

After one live raise, the year's lots are settled: Paddy buys Clark's Customs House for 14 (Silke stopped at 13), Silke buys Rich's Land Deed Scroll for 19 (Paddy stopped at 18), Paddy buys Silke's Dragon Jar for 12 (Clark stopped at 11), and Clark buys Paddy's Wooden Bowl for 10 (Rich stopped at 9, Silke at 8). Rob passed on all four. Every province's stock is now sold.

Paddy: "The Customs House and the Jar go down at the far end of the coast, by the water, as far from the sand as Province 6 goes. Silke, you've got the Deed, fair and square. When you're ready to talk about it, I'll be here, and I'll be fair."

Silke: "Nineteen for a scroll that finishes nothing for me. I know, Paddy, I can count. It goes on P26, as far from the dunes as the coast allows. Paddy, you're one Deed short and the box is empty, so whenever you want to talk about it, the silk farm is open for business."

Clark: "The Bowl goes on H16, beside the Deed and well back from Rob's sightseers. Paddy, thank you for the Bowl. You beat me to the Jar fair and square, and I don't hold that against a man, though I do write it down. The box is empty now, friends, so anyone with a spare Jar and an interest in the Official's goodwill knows where I sit."

Paddy puts the Customs House on S25 and the Dragon Jar on S24; Silke the Land Deed Scroll on P26; Clark the Wooden Bowl on H16. Clark needs only a Dragon Jar; Paddy only a Land Deed Scroll. Rich's is still the only complete set.

Rob: "Congratulations, everyone, the box is empty and the shelves are full. That's the nicest thing you could have done for me. Rich, I've wiped my feet at J10, and that's five for the doormat. Paddy, Silke, I'm sure the coast is lovely this time of year."
