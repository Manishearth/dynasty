diary: Years three and four. I picked my afternoon and it was the right one: I paid my eighteen like an honest man, showed my set, and sat down on a reserve of three hundred and thirty-five. I cut the tax, built the Winter Palace, raised Clark's pay, hired Silke to read the cards, and dug Paddy his canal, every grain spent on friends. My mistake was forgetting that Clark had a key as well as a ledger. One year later he used it on me as neatly as I'd used mine on Reina, and I watched my reserve go across the table with a smile on my face. The auction shut me out of all three pieces by a single grain each, which was polite of the table and no comfort at all. Now I'm the Official with about sixty grain, nothing on the board, and Rob's man raiding my doorstep. Clark is keeping my fifteen-grain salary, and I'll give him credit for that in public as often as he likes. Two peasants on the coast now hold full sets and can unseat me whenever they please, so I'll be very nice to Silke and Paddy. Next: collect every grain, keep my own above twenty, wait for the pieces that come back on the block when somebody uses a set, and remember that the richest man at the table is sitting in the chair again. This time he isn't me, but I've done it before.
