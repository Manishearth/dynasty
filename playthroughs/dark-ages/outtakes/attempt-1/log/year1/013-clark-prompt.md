## At the table since your last turn

Rich: "A magnificent Palace, Your Majesty. I'll be able to see it from my window, which is where I'll be counting what's left after forty percent. Friends, my Land Deed opens at a modest three. I'm not one to gouge a neighbour in the first year. As for the rest of you, I'll bid like a gentleman: once, sensibly, and with no tantrums."

Silke: "Let's count, shall we? Five to Rich, twenty-four to the crown, and I'm down to thirty-one from sixty. Paddy's the same. Clark has thirty-six, Rich forty-six, Rob sixty for doing nothing at all, and Her Majesty a hundred and thirty-six. So I'll bid small and honest, and anyone who wants a war over a Customs House can pay three-quarters of it into Rich's pile. My Dragon Jar starts at four. It's good porcelain, and the Palace will want something to put on its shelves."

Paddy: "Five for Rich, twenty-four for the crown, and the rice still in the paddies. I'll put in an honest bid or two, but I'm not starting a war over a jar in the first year. If someone else wants a piece more than I do, say so now and I'll stand aside and remember the favour. The Bowl goes up at two, same as anyone's."

Rob: "Bid away, friends, bid away. I don't need a single jar or bowl. Buy them, set them out nicely on your fields, and polish them up. And Your Majesty, a respectful distance is eight rounds. I've counted."

(Storyteller's note: The Harvest Pile is the common bank: Rich keeps it and pays the harvests out of it, but its grain isn't his.)

The auctions are settled lot by lot from everyone's limits: Rich buys Clark's Customs House for 7 (Silke stopped at 6), Clark buys Rich's Land Deed Scroll for 6 (Silke also stopped at 6; the tie goes to Clark's seat), Rich buys Silke's Dragon Jar for 7 (Clark stopped at 6) and Paddy's Wooden Bowl for 7 (Clark stopped at 6). Rob passed on all four.
