# Chance and steering in the Dark Ages

The Director steers this game towards the arc in `scenario.md`, more openly than most (Manish asked for it). Every piece of steering is the Director's, made before the game or between chapters through a handoff, and is listed below when it happens. A Storyteller never steers on its own.

- **Seed:** `5130`, chosen by the Director with `deck_scout.py` and approved by Manish.
- **How the deck is shuffled:** the twenty card keys from `cards.py`, sorted alphabetically, then `random.Random(5130).shuffle(keys)` (Python 3).
- **Deck order, top first** (Year 1 draws no card, so the first of these is Year 2's):

  1. rainflood · 2. careless · 3. chest · 4. blight · 5. double3 · 6. dribble1 · 7. repairs · 8. share · 9. canal · 10. goodevil · 11. winds · 12. dribble3 · 13. utility · 14. half1 · 15. dribble2 · 16. double2 · 17. half2 · 18. heaven · 19. double1 · 20. breach

  The players are never shown this list, and the narrator never hints at it. Population cards past their caps are still drawn in order; the engine treats a capped one as a good harvest.

- **Booklets:** not drawn by chance. Each player sits in the province their name puns on: Reina 1, Clark 2, Rich 3, Silke 5, Paddy 6, Rob 8.

## Steering log

- **Before Chapter 1, the deck: seed 5130.** Searched seeds 1–60,000 for: land opening (a doubling) in Years 5–7; no negative bare income for any seat before it; a lean year at or right after the doubling; at most x2 until Year 13; at most one lean year in the recovery. Five passed. 5130 gives four x1 years with positive incomes (Year 2 rainflood, 3 careless, 4 chest, 5 blight), when no tax is worth revolting for. The population doubles in Year 6, then a bad Year 7 (−70 for Province 1, −30 for every province) with no land to buy under Chaos. Years 8–14 are x2, mostly average, with one bad year (13): seven years to recover. The first x3 is Year 15. Alternatives scouted: 36521 (the same shape, but x4 in Year 14 and x6 in Year 15, so a hard deadline at Year 13) and 16427 (x1 to Year 6 and a doubling in Year 7, but the share and utility cards fall inside Chaos). Rejected as harsher: 2136 and 1049 (x3 by Year 6–8) and 4292 (x3 at Year 13, fewer good recovery years).
- **Before Chapter 1, the actor's notes** (`ledger.md`, **Playing it**). Stances only, approved by Manish. Reina proud and in a hurry to build, never answering a grievance with a cut. Clark ambitious, with the revolt clauses by heart. Rich greedy, fawning while the crown is strong. Silke sharp, selling her backing dearly. Paddy hating to owe, siding with whoever feeds Province 6. Rob an opportunist who'd sit on the throne himself.
- **Before Chapter 1, the revolt line:** `REVOLT_LINE = True` in `game.py` puts the seven causes for revolt and what Chaos stops on every board card (a `briefs.py` flag, this game only). Why: in the first attempt no prompt ever offered a revolt.
- **Before Chapter 1, a procedure for Years 2–4** (in `handoff/chapter-1.md`, and later handoffs): after the crown declares the tax, each seat that owes it gets a short `free --short` prompt: pay, or call or back a revolt. Why: no seat but the Official is prompted between the declaration and the payment, and a revolt then voids the year's tax.
- **Before Chapter 1, one Storyteller's tip, to Silke, public,** right after Year 2's tax is declared: what Chaos stops and doesn't, how a revolt is called and called off, and how the acclamation is counted. Rules and the board only.
- **Before Chapter 1, the setup line** (`game.py`, R(0)): the appointee's pay is the Emperor's choice; the Warlord may hire one reinforcement.
- **Before Chapter 2, one Storyteller's tip, to Clark, public,** after Year 3's tax declaration: while there's an Emperor, Rich's set can take the throne at any step, but once a revolt stands it can't, and Rich would have to win the acclamation like anyone else. Why: in Year 2 all four taxpayers declined to revolt, saying a revolt would only hand Rich the throne, which is the reverse of this game's rule. Clark, because he's the one who keeps naming the causes. The pay-or-revolt prompt continues in Years 3–4, as planned. Notes unchanged.
- **Before Chapter 2, a rules clarification (not steering):** the revolt line on every card now adds that under Chaos no set can take the throne, which passes only by acclamation. Any further public claim that a revolt hands Rich the throne is corrected as a misstatement.
- **Year 3, how the Clark tip backfired:** Rich, prompted straight after it, read it as a warning that a revolt would disarm his set, and took the throne with the set at once. In Year 4 Clark took it from Rich with his own set. Lesson: a public tip that a held set is about to lose its value is acted on first by whoever holds the set.
- **Year 4, Manish's rulings and permissions (mid-chapter):**
  - A called revolt stops play until it stands or is dropped (`revolt_called()`).
  - Storytellers correct rules misreadings in players' stated reasoning.
  - The Director may steer mid-chapter when a situation calls for it.
  - Storyteller 2 was told the first two by message; no other steering in Year 4.
  - Manish chose to keep this game rather than restart it.
- **Before Chapter 3, three actor's notes rewritten** after two set swaps (Rich took the throne in Year 3, Clark in Year 4). Clark is now an Emperor who hoards the reserve against the lean years, spending only what the books oblige. Rich is a deposed Emperor who wants the chair back, for whom the table is the only road. Reina is deposed, sells her support for Province 3 works, and is freed by any broken promise. Silke, Paddy and Rob are unchanged. Why: the old notes named seats their characters no longer hold. Two deposed rulers without sets, plus a Warlord who likes Chaos, make a revolt coalition. A hoarding crown gives "won't subsidise new land" a chance when land opens in Year 6.
- **Before Chapter 3, one Storyteller's tip, to Rich, public,** after Year 5's tax declaration: the reserve (429) is sealed under Chaos and passes whole to whoever is acclaimed. Why: Clark taxes 20% and builds every year, so the only motive left to revolt is the prize. Rich, because he's the claimant with no other road. The pay-or-revolt prompt continues in Years 5–6, with the crown's land offer included in Year 6.
- **Before Chapter 3, a rules correction (not steering):** in Chapter 2 several players said a revolt would make their sets worthless. Under Chaos a set may still replace the Official, the Gentry or a peasant, so this is a public note at the start of Year 5.
- **Year 5 to 6, mid-chapter, a rules correction (not steering):** Rich declined to revolt because he had "no soldiers" and "Paddy is richer". Storyteller 3 files a public note at the start of Year 6: only claimants are counted in the acclamation, soldiers count for the claimant who controls them, and anyone may buy a soldier from the Warlord, who must sell.
- **Year 6, mid-chapter, one Storyteller's tip, to Rob, public,** filed before the pay-or-revolt prompts: anyone may call a revolt, the Warlord included; the Warlord may claim in the acclamation; raided pieces make "can't control the Warlord" a cause; a called revolt stops play; only claimants are counted. Why: Clark is closing every cause one by one (he keeps the tax at 20%, has dug the Province 5 canal, pays 15 of every 20 on peasant land, and has bought land for Province 1), and his aside says so. Rob's raids are the one live cause, and Rob is the player who gains most from Chaos. Rob doesn't pay tax, so the pay-or-revolt round never reaches him.
- **Before Chapter 4, Rich's and Reina's notes rewritten:** both are losing grain every year under a Wall nobody builds, and both want the chair back. Clark, Silke, Paddy and Rob are unchanged. Why: by Year 7 the squeeze has moved up the table. Land carries the crown and the peasants, while Provinces 2 and 3 can't buy land until their Wall links stand.
- **Before Chapter 4, the pay-or-revolt prompt extended** to Rich and Reina even when they owe no tax, and to Rob in any year he raided. It now carries the crown's works and land offer. Why: in a year when their incomes are negative, the two most aggrieved players owe no tax, so the round passed them by.
- **Before Chapter 4, one Storyteller's tip, to Silke, public,** after Year 7's harvest: the acclamation can seat a peasant, and a peasant's set survives Chaos. Why: the peasants are the richest players. Silke's diary already says the next chair goes to "whoever holds grain and friends when the reserve runs dry".
- **The game runs to completion** (Manish): no `LAST_ROUND` is set. If the recovery is failing, the Director may rewind a year and replay it, logged here.
