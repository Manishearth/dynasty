diary: So it's Clark. The man with the ledger wears the crown, and my name is written in it beside the others, because once the people doubled and my granary began losing five a year with no land to buy, an empty chair was costing me more than a clerk in it would. I made my offer a year too late, and Paddy told me so to my face. He was right, so I backed the winner rather than be counted out by him, and I've given my word that the Gentry won't pull down what he helped put up. I'll keep it, as I keep all of them. The rent is still the customary five, the pile is still mine to run, and I paid thirty-six for Silke's Jar and had a full set for one afternoon before Rob's lad walked off to the desert with my Bowl and took another five on the way. That's twenty-five grain Rob has eaten out of Province 3, and now a piece as well, so the next grain I spend goes on swords of my own before it goes on any more clay. Clark has promised the Wall over the north for the year after Paddy's canal, and my only land lies under four links of it. I'll read that line back to him every year, politely, until it's laid.
