diary: Two years and I've gone from thirty-seven grain down to single figures, and I didn't spend a grain of it on anything. The bad harvest took thirty, the utility tax took two, and Rob's soldier took five a year and called me "love" each time. The coast kept me out of debt. Silke paid me ten when I had nothing to offer her, and she said so out loud, which was fair, so the year after I took the floor from both of them and said nothing more about it. Rich has surprised me. He's emptied the crown to zero twice, paid every grain he promised, and given Paddy a Canal, and a crown that keeps its word to the peasants is a hard one to call a revolt against. Clark still has the full set and still writes everything down, and I think he's waiting for the crown to miss a single payment. Rob now holds a Customs House from me, one from Paddy and Clark's Jar, so I'll have to buy any set I want back off the desert. I've no grain, no pieces and no soldiers, so the only thing I'm building now is goodwill on the coast. When the throne changes hands, whether it's Clark taking it or Rich losing it, friends will count. Next I need grain in hand before I can be anything more than the woman who kept the Pile honest.
