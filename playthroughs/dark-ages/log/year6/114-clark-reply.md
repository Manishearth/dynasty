diary: Years 5 and 6. For one more year of drought the count went three ways, and I kept my claim and kept my mouth to my promises. Then the people doubled, the land opened with no chair to sell it, and the table lost its patience all at once. Paddy came first, then Reina for the Wall and the Pagoda, and in the end even Rich and Rob put their names on mine. Every one of them is in the ledger now, and every one of them came with a price. I was given the throne, I never bought it, and that's the kind that lasts. I owned up straight away that the canal has to wait one year behind the Winter Palace. The palace stands on H20, and I paid dearly in land to make the delay go down easier: 150 for Silke's and Paddy's squares, 60 for my own, and the 20 for Paddy's soldier. Rob took that soldier back the same afternoon, so the coast-guard line is kept in the letter and broken in fact. The reserve that started at 401.5 stands at 145.5, and 850 in works is still owed. Rich has his rent, his pile and a set he held for less than an hour before Rob carried his Bowl off to N12. That's a fourth page under the Warlord's name, and Rob is still waiting for me to put a number on peace. Next year Paddy's canal goes in, as written, and I'll tax enough to keep building, because Silke and Paddy are counting, and so am I.
