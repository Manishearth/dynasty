diary: Two years, and both of them ended with the treasury at exactly zero. Nobody can call that carelessness, because every grain went where I'd said it would, but I don't like how it feels. Clark got his thirty both years, to the letter, and wrote every grain of it in that book of his as if he were drawing up the charges for a trial. A bad harvest left me nine and a half. A good one, with the utility levy and thirty percent from Silke's fields, paid for the first section of Paddy's canal and some help on the coast's land, and I spent all of it again. Paddy is the one I'm glad of. He waited a year without complaining and thanked me when I kept my word, and if anyone calls a revolt, his is the hand I most need to stay down. Silke worries me more. She has ten sea squares now, pays most of the crown's tax and counts it all out loud, and she's told the table the next canal had better be hers. I didn't promise it, but I'll probably have to give it to her. Rob has taken a piece from every one of us except Silke and me, and he complimented my canal, which I don't trust at all. His soldiers are three steps from my land. What comes next is simple and hard: I have to stop ending the year on nothing. The rate stays at thirty, because I said so and they believe it. But the reserve needs a cushion before the Wall and the x4 card catch up with me. If that card comes while I'm sitting on zero, I'll need a lot more than smiles.
