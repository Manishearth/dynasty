diary: A drought year and a doubling year. I read both cards aloud myself, and I watched Chaos stop being cheap the moment every mouth ate twice and the land sat there unbought for want of a chair. I held my name until Clark wrote the Wall and the Pagoda in beside his canals, and then I gave it. The rest came after me like water through a breached dyke: Paddy, then Rich, of all people, and then Rob, who sells everything. So the chair is filled, and the man in it isn't me. The two hundred and fifty-one I guarded for four years went to him in one breath, and the first thing he did was push my Wall back two years behind a palace for himself. The law demands the palace, I know, but I've written the year down. Now I'm his Official: seventy-odd grain, one Wooden Bowl on G14 under a Wall that doesn't stand yet, and a salary of ten I'm meant to call generous. Rob broke Rich's set within the hour and bought back the soldier he'd sold to Paddy for nothing, which is what comes of a light tax and a crown that buys guards by proxy. I'll carry Clark's tax honestly and read his ledger back to him every year until the stones go up over the north. If I can't raise the dynasty's monuments with my own hands, I'll make sure the man who took my chair raises them with his.
