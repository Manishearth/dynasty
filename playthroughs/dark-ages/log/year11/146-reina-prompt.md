## Reina's diary so far, in her own words

## Years 1-2

I taxed at half, built the Summer Palace, and by the second harvest I was sitting in Province 3 holding the Pile. Silke warned me out loud, and I counted my reserve when I should have counted Rich's set. That's the lesson, and it cost me a throne and a hundred and ninety-five grain I'd meant to put into stone. Then I paid nine for a Customs House and went to eleven and a half for the rest, and Clark outbid me at sixteen. Now he's the one with the full set, and Rich is paying him thirty a year to keep it in his pocket. That arrangement won't last. Rob has put a man at I11 and another at M15 to watch my Customs House, so I'm down to thirty-eight and the soldiers have the measure of my province. Next year I keep the Pile scrupulously honest, I buy the Jar and the Bowl as cheaply as the coast will let me, and I hold twenty in reserve whatever Rob takes. The coast should be my friend, because Silke was right before anyone else was. And the palace on G24 is still mine in every way that matters.

## Years 3-4

Years 3 and 4 I kept the Pile honest to the grain and asked the coast for nothing past the floor, and in return I lost everything I'd built. I went to twenty-eight for the Jar and fourteen for the Bowl, and Clark took both by one grain apiece. He didn't want them, he only wanted me not to have them. Then Rob's soldier walked my Customs House out into the desert, and I'm thirty-seven grain and no pottery, starting from nothing. Silke offered me five grain to promise away the next Customs House and I turned her down. It was the right call, but Clark wrote it in his book as a broken word, and that will cost me with the coast. The thing to watch now is that Rich has spent his reserve down to nineteen and a half on palaces, pagodas and Clark's thirty a year. Clark has the full set and ninety-nine grain, so the crown is hollow and the man paid to guard it is the one best placed to take it. I can't take it myself this year. What I can do is get my Customs House back from Rob or buy a new one, make it plain to Silke and Paddy that I'm the landlord who never squeezed them, and be standing by with the coast when Clark makes his move, so that whatever's left afterwards comes to me.

## Years 5-6

Two years and I've gone from thirty-seven grain down to single figures, and I didn't spend a grain of it on anything. The bad harvest took thirty, the utility tax took two, and Rob's soldier took five a year and called me "love" each time. The coast kept me out of debt. Silke paid me ten when I had nothing to offer her, and she said so out loud, which was fair, so the year after I took the floor from both of them and said nothing more about it. Rich has surprised me. He's emptied the crown to zero twice, paid every grain he promised, and given Paddy a Canal, and a crown that keeps its word to the peasants is a hard one to call a revolt against. Clark still has the full set and still writes everything down, and I think he's waiting for the crown to miss a single payment. Rob now holds a Customs House from me, one from Paddy and Clark's Jar, so I'll have to buy any set I want back off the desert. I've no grain, no pieces and no soldiers, so the only thing I'm building now is goodwill on the coast. When the throne changes hands, whether it's Clark taking it or Rich losing it, friends will count. Next I need grain in hand before I can be anything more than the woman who kept the Pile honest.

## Years 7-8

Two good harvests running, and for the first time since I lost the chair my purse went up. I'm at sixty-six. I asked the coast for ten, said there were no conditions and meant it, and both of them paid it gladly in front of everyone, which matters more to me than the grain does. Rob still had five off me in Year 7, and the horsemen and my chef's send-off cost me a little more, but nothing I couldn't stand. Rich has finally started the Wall, two links, both over his own province, paid for out of Silke's tax, and she has noticed. Clark has noticed too, and he wrote the province number into his book. The man I ought to be watching now is Rob. He has three pieces and needs only Silke's Scroll, and W2 is one square away from it. If the desert takes the throne, every set on this board goes through his hands, and mine will be worth even less than it is. So next year I keep building up grain, I keep the coast thinking of me as the landlord who never squeezed, and I tell Silke plainly to guard that Scroll, because her safety and mine have become the same thing.

## Years 9-10

Two years of fat harvests, and I've counted out more grain to the coast than I ever spent as Empress. I'm past a hundred now, though I still hold no pieces and Province 3 can't grow by a single field. I asked for ten each year with no conditions and both of them paid it gladly, and I warned Silke about the Scroll, and she did better than guard it: she bought Rob's own soldier and used it to knock W2 off the board. Rich has finished his own Wall and started on Clark's, he's paid Clark's thirty for nine years running, and he's buying fields in the north-east sea. He's even rebuilt his reserve. A crown like that won't be overthrown by complaints, and Clark knows it, so he just keeps counting. Rob is the danger, and he's poor and down to three soldiers, with two of them four rounds from that Scroll. What comes next is the same patient work. I keep the grain, I keep the coast, and I watch for the first set to change hands, because my Wall above Province 3 still has nothing built on it, and nobody at this table is going to build it for me.
