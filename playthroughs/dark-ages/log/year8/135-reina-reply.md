diary: Two years of carrying another man's tax, and I'll admit I carried it well. Every grain was counted twice and delivered whole, even the six I paid from my own bowl. Both canals stand now, Paddy's first and then Silke's, just as the page said. Clark paid Paddy his fifty in the year he promised it, and I wrote "settled" beside it in my own hand. The coast eats better for it. Still, those spades were bought with the reserve I guarded through four years of Chaos while the rest of them called it a habit. I don't expect anyone to say so, so I've said it myself every time the ledger opened. The Wall has slipped twice, from Year 8 to Year 9. Rich, who never gave this crown anything but honey, suddenly has a quarter of every link to offer now that the stone would sit over his own province. The surprise is Silke and Paddy: they took less than they were owed on their land so the reserve could pay for my Wall. Rob's soldier is standing in my own field on J14, "sightseeing," and next year he's promised to send a bill. Twenty-one in the reserve, and the north still bare. In Year 9 I read that page aloud to the whole table, and this time the stones go up.
