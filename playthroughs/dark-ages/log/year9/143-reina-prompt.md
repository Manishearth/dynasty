## Reina's diary so far, in her own words

Two years, one palace, and a throne I lost on a flooded spring. I taxed half because the fields were full and an empire that waits for lean years never builds. They called that a habit, and when the vote came all five stood together, Rich loudest of all after years of honey in his mouth. I won't pretend I'd answer differently. A crown that cuts its tax because the table shouts has stopped being a crown. Now the reserve sits sealed beside me, a hundred and ninety-five that I can't touch. The ones who emptied my chair can't agree who fills it: Clark has his ledger, Rich has his granary, and Rob has his swords. While they argue, nothing gets built. Rich will never have my name. If Clark actually keeps his canals in writing, he may earn it. I still want the walls, the Pagoda and the canals raised over this land, and if I can't raise them myself, I'll make sure whoever sits there does.

## Years 3–4

Two more years with nobody in my chair. My sealed treasury has gone on paying for disasters, and for the charity Heaven sends, to the same five who put me out of it. The first year was fat and the second was average, and Chaos felt cheap to all of them. It won't feel cheap much longer: Rob has broken two sets and eaten out of Rich's granary, and Rich's honeyed "customary five" doesn't fool even Silke. Clark finally put his canals on paper, Province 6 first, then Province 5, with half rate on flooded fields, and I'll admit it was well done. But I didn't hear a word about the Wall over the north or the Pagoda, and I won't give my name away for half of what this land is owed. Rich will never have it. Rob can have it the day his swords build something. The day Clark writes the walls in next to the canals, he may find the chair a good deal closer than he thinks.

## Years 5–6

A drought year and a doubling year. I read both cards aloud myself, and I watched Chaos stop being cheap the moment every mouth ate twice and the land sat there unbought for want of a chair. I held my name until Clark wrote the Wall and the Pagoda in beside his canals, and then I gave it. The rest came after me like water through a breached dyke: Paddy, then Rich, of all people, and then Rob, who sells everything. So the chair is filled, and the man in it isn't me. The two hundred and fifty-one I guarded for four years went to him in one breath, and the first thing he did was push my Wall back two years behind a palace for himself. The law demands the palace, I know, but I've written the year down. Now I'm his Official: seventy-odd grain, one Wooden Bowl on G14 under a Wall that doesn't stand yet, and a salary of ten I'm meant to call generous. Rob broke Rich's set within the hour and bought back the soldier he'd sold to Paddy for nothing, which is what comes of a light tax and a crown that buys guards by proxy. I'll carry Clark's tax honestly and read his ledger back to him every year until the stones go up over the north. If I can't raise the dynasty's monuments with my own hands, I'll make sure the man who took my chair raises them with his.

## Years 7–8

Two years of carrying another man's tax, and I'll admit I carried it well. Every grain was counted twice and delivered whole, even the six I paid from my own bowl. Both canals stand now, Paddy's first and then Silke's, just as the page said. Clark paid Paddy his fifty in the year he promised it, and I wrote "settled" beside it in my own hand. The coast eats better for it. Still, those spades were bought with the reserve I guarded through four years of Chaos while the rest of them called it a habit. I don't expect anyone to say so, so I've said it myself every time the ledger opened. The Wall has slipped twice, from Year 8 to Year 9. Rich, who never gave this crown anything but honey, suddenly has a quarter of every link to offer now that the stone would sit over his own province. The surprise is Silke and Paddy: they took less than they were owed on their land so the reserve could pay for my Wall. Rob's soldier is standing in my own field on J14, "sightseeing," and next year he's promised to send a bill. Twenty-one in the reserve, and the north still bare. In Year 9 I read that page aloud to the whole table, and this time the stones go up.
