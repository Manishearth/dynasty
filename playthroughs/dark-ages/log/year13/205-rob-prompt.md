## Rob's diary so far, in his own words

Two years in and the throne is empty, and I never even had to lay a finger on it. Reina taxed them half twice over, flooded fields and all, and when I called the revolt every one of them stood up beside me, the Gentry and the Official too, the very men who'd been eating at her table. It's cost me nothing yet. I've passed every lot, my grain sits at seventy-five, and with no Emperor there's nobody to pay me off or send men after me, so my soldiers just carry on. The count for the chair is three ways deadlocked: Rich has the granary, Clark has Silke, and I have the only swords on the board. I'm happy for that to go on for a good long while. Next year W1 takes Clark's Deed and W2 goes down the coast to see Paddy's shiny new set "from a distance". If they want an Emperor badly enough, they'll know whose price has to be met.

## Years 3–4

Two more years without a crown and I've never had it so good. Heaven sent a fat harvest and then the sealed treasury paid me two grain for my trouble, and I spent twenty of it on a third lad and got the rest back off Rich's fields in a single night. Clark's Deed is sitting at M15 and Paddy's is sitting at T24, so that's two sets broken, and there's nobody in the chair for them to complain to, only Clark's ledger, and he can write my name in it as often as he likes. Paddy's been watching W2 in his field with a face like thunder, and Rich has started whispering to him about keeping my lads out, which tells me the swords are finally being taken seriously. The count's still stuck three ways: Rich has the grain, Clark has Silke and now a canal on paper, and I've got the only soldiers on the board. Rich and Silke are the two pieces I'm missing, so W1 and W3 are already standing in Province 3, and Silke's coast opens up again next year. The longer they argue over who sits, the longer I get to keep collecting.

## Years 5–6

Well, they finally found someone to sit in the chair, and it's Clark, the little man with the ledger, which makes it the only one of my sins he's ever going to get to do anything about. I held out through the drought, and when the people doubled, the coast went over to him one name after another. In the end I put my own name on his claim too. An empty throne never paid me a grain to stay at home, and an Emperor might. It cost me the count and a soldier Paddy made me sell him, and I took that soldier straight back the same afternoon along with Paddy's Jar, and Rich's Bowl about an hour after he finished his set. That makes four pieces in my desert, and only one of them was ever paid for, by the man who lost it. Clark promised me "my figure" once my lads are back on the sand. I'll believe that when I'm holding the grain. Until then Reina's new Bowl is sitting at G14, Silke's coast is still untouched, and there's a brand-new Winter Palace on H20 that costs the crown twenty if anyone so much as breathes on it. The dynasty has an Emperor again, and I'm still the most dangerous man at the table. I've never been happier.

## Years 7–8

Two years under Clark, and the funny thing is the place works. Two canals are dug, Paddy's fifty is paid back, and Reina reads the dates out like a schoolmistress. Every province is spending its grain on fields and stone, and none of it is coming to me. I did get Paddy's Customs House, though. I paid his toll like a gentleman, walked it home to U21, and that's five pieces in my desert now. Then Heaven tied my lads' hands for a whole year, and it cost me five for the privilege. My grain sat at a hundred and seven and didn't move, which is the dullest thing that's happened to me since Year 1. Clark told me to my face the crown doesn't pay for scenery. Reina and Silke are the last two names on my list, and the Wall goes up over the north next year with everyone's stone on it. I'll let them build it. A finished dynasty with fat barns is a much better thing to raid than a broken one, and I'd rather be paid to keep my distance than sit on a chair someone will only try to take from me.

Well, they built the thing. Twelve links of Wall from Province 1 to the sand, and every province put its own grain in the mortar, even Reina, who hadn't two coins to rub together by the end of it.
