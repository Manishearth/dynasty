## Clark's diary so far, in his own words

Years 1 and 2. I had a complete set by the end of the first year's auctions, Deed, Jar and Bowl, and I've kept them in the drawer ever since. A throne you buy has to be guarded every night, so I'd rather be given one. This year Reina put fifty on a drowned field for the second year running and wouldn't cut a grain of it when we asked her to. Five names stood by the revolt and mine was one of them. The chair has been empty since. The first count went three ways, Rich on his granary, Rob on his two swords and me on Silke's one hand, so nobody was acclaimed. That cost me a salary, and Rob has taken another 5 out of Province 2 and calls it the Warlord's tax, which leaves me at 21.5. New in the ledger: Paddy now holds a full set as well, Rich is already calling himself the friend of "a man with a full set and no chair", and the Gentry's man is precisely the man I won't have on that throne. I've given Paddy my word on the first canal, Silke has my word on the flood rate, and I can wait. Chaos costs the landlord more than it costs me, and I'll be the one keeping count when the table gets tired of waiting.

## Years 3–4

Years 3 and 4. Rob took my Deed from J14 to M15 in a year Heaven called careless, then took Paddy's to T24 in a year it called average. That's two pages under his name now, and I've written both down with the squares. The set is broken, but under Chaos a set can't reach the chair anyway, and with no tax and no salary for two years I've gone from 22.5 to 115.5, which no Emperor would have let me do. I finally put the canals in writing in front of the whole table: Province 6 in the first year I sit, Province 5 the year after, and half the rate on any flooded field. Paddy heard it, and the next day he turned Rich down over the Jar. Reina wants walls over the north before she'll lend her name, Silke wants her terms on paper before the harvests turn, and Rich is still counting grain as if grain had ever made anyone a friend. Rob's lads ate ten out of the Gentry's own fields this year, and I enjoyed writing that down more than I should have. I'll keep Silke and keep my word to Paddy, and I'll wait until the table is tired of feeding swords.

## Years 5–6

Years 5 and 6. For one more year of drought the count went three ways, and I kept my claim and kept my mouth to my promises. Then the people doubled, the land opened with no chair to sell it, and the table lost its patience all at once. Paddy came first, then Reina for the Wall and the Pagoda, and in the end even Rich and Rob put their names on mine. Every one of them is in the ledger now, and every one of them came with a price. I was given the throne, I never bought it, and that's the kind that lasts. I owned up straight away that the canal has to wait one year behind the Winter Palace. The palace stands on H20, and I paid dearly in land to make the delay go down easier: 150 for Silke's and Paddy's squares, 60 for my own, and the 20 for Paddy's soldier. Rob took that soldier back the same afternoon, so the coast-guard line is kept in the letter and broken in fact. The reserve that started at 401.5 stands at 145.5, and 850 in works is still owed. Rich has his rent, his pile and a set he held for less than an hour before Rob carried his Bowl off to N12. That's a fourth page under the Warlord's name, and Rob is still waiting for me to put a number on peace. Next year Paddy's canal goes in, as written, and I'll tax enough to keep building, because Silke and Paddy are counting, and so am I.
