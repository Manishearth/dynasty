diary: Two years with nobody in the chair, and I've gone from twenty-nine grain to a hundred and twelve. No tax, no salaries, no palace. Rich takes his five and wants a thank-you for it. He can keep waiting. Rob walked Clark's Deed into the desert and then Paddy's, and he ate ten out of Rich's own fields, which I'll admit I enjoyed watching. Clark finally put it in writing: the first canal to Paddy, the second to me, and half rate for any province that floods. That's the first time my coast has been on paper, so my name stays on his claim. That's for now, though, not for good. Rich is near two hundred and he'll start buying friends soon, so Clark has to keep outbidding him, every year, until I'm the one who decides it's time.
