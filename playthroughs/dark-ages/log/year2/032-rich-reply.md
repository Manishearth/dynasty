diary: Two years, and I went from keeping the Pile to sitting on the throne, for the price of nineteen grain at auction and a lot of smiling. Reina was gracious about it, which worries me more than shouting would have. She bid hard for her Customs House, and she'll be back. Clark is the real thorn now. He bought his set out from under her and sold me his loyalty for thirty a year, and I've promised it to him to the letter: thirty while he's Official and I'm Emperor, no more and no less. It's cheap next to the reserve, but every grain I give him keeps his set alive, so I'm counting on Rob's soldiers and the auction house to thin his purse for me. The canal starts paying next year. I'll keep the tax kind, the works rising and the peasants grateful, and I'll keep watching Clark's grain, Reina's purse and Silke's sharp tongue.
