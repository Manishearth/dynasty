diary: Two years, one palace, and a throne I lost on a flooded spring. I taxed half because the fields were full and an empire that waits for lean years never builds. They called that a habit, and when the vote came all five stood together, Rich loudest of all after years of honey in his mouth. I won't pretend I'd answer differently. A crown that cuts its tax because the table shouts has stopped being a crown. Now the reserve sits sealed beside me, a hundred and ninety-five that I can't touch. The ones who emptied my chair can't agree who fills it: Clark has his ledger, Rich has his granary, and Rob has his swords. While they argue, nothing gets built. Rich will never have my name. If Clark actually keeps his canals in writing, he may earn it. I still want the walls, the Pagoda and the canals raised over this land, and if I can't raise them myself, I'll make sure whoever sits there does.
