## At the table since your last turn

Clark: "Since nobody else has the nerve, Your Majesty, I'll be the first. I've collected this crown's tax and I've kept its books, so I know exactly what that chair costs and who it's cost. My terms are a fair rate, a lighter one on flooded fields, and works that actually get built. Rich, I'm sure your door's very open, but I've never yet met a landlord who opened his door without charging for it."

Rich: "Clark, you're quite right that I've never opened my door for free, and you've never opened your ledger without meaning to use it. I won't watch this empire handed over on twenty-six grain. So I'm putting my granary into the count, a hundred and four and a half of it. My terms are the customary five in rent, a light tax, and a full Harvest Pile for every province. And anyone who'd like my granary behind them instead of in front of them knows where I live."

Silke: "A hundred and four and a half, Rich, and I can count every grain of it, because about forty of them came out of my field and Paddy's as rent and pile shares. You don't get to buy the chair with that. Clark, you said a lighter rate on flooded fields. Province 5 floods. Say it in front of everyone and my name's beside yours."

Paddy: "Clark, you've a lighter rate for flooded fields, and Rich, you've a full pile, but I haven't heard either of you say a single word about Province 6, or tell me which work gets built first and when. Rob: "A ledger, a granary, and not one sword between them. Reina, you said a toll-gate bandit would have to do better, so here's better: I claim the throne, with two soldiers on the board who are the only reason anyone's harvest is still standing. Rich: "Silke, every grain of rent was the customary five, and the Storyteller's just confirmed it, so I'll thank you not to make me a robber in front of company. Paddy: "I lost the Deed last year on a tie at six, so this year I'll go a little further, but only a little. I'm not burning my winter grain in a bidding war while there's nobody on the throne to build anything with it. My bowl goes up at two, same as always."
