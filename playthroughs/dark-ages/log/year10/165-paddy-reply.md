diary: Two years, and the Wall stands from Province 1 to the desert, built in Year 9 and Year 10 the way Clark wrote it down in Year 8. It doesn't cover my roof, and I paid a hundred and forty-seven in tax under it at fifty and was glad to, because a crown that keeps its figures is worth paying. The Emperor's Share took ten off my canal and the river took two more for a ditch that never broke. It's a strange tax that fines you for being lucky, but I'll not grumble about it with water still running in my fields. I bought ten more squares up the north end of the coast, which makes twenty-four now. I let the crown off half its share both years, because I'd rather see the last works dug than see my granary fat and the purse empty. Rob's lad still takes his five off me every year like a man collecting the milk, and I've four hundred and sixty grain and not one piece on the board for him to carry off. What's left is two hundred and ten: Clark's own canal, Rich's reservoir, Reina's irrigation and the Pagoda. I mean to keep paying until every line of it is struck off. Reina's got her pen ready, and for once I'm glad of the way she counts.
