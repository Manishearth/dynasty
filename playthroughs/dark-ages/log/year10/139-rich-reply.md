diary: Two years, and I finally have something in the jar worth counting: a hundred and eighty-eight at the end of Year 10, after nine years of Clark's thirty, paid every time to the letter. The Wall over my own province is finished, and I've put seven more fields into the sea in the north-east corner, far from the desert, so next year's harvest should have some weight to it. I learned the price of generosity, too. Ten a square on the coast cost me a hundred and sixty in a single afternoon, because Silke buys mud the way other people buy tea, so the subsidy went back to five, and she made sure the whole table heard the arithmetic. Two links of the Wall now stand over Clark's roof, and for once he wrote it down as something given and not something owed. That's eighty grain I'd call well spent. My one real blunder was Rob's soldier. I paid him twenty and set the man down right in front of W1, and W1 walked over and ate him, and took ten from my garden on the way. Silke was right, curse her: buying the soldier wasn't the lesson, parking him in front of the Warlord was. Next time I'll stand my guard where Rob can't reach him first, or I'll simply pay Rob to march somewhere else. Next year: thirty percent again, the last two links over Clark, five a square for the coast, and keep the jar growing, because Silke's "I'll remember" is getting louder as her fields multiply.
