# Narration

The narrator's lines, chapter by chapter: the chapter title, each year's opening line, and the turning points, each tagged with the log number it follows. The narrator isn't part of the game, so none of this goes into `game.py` or the playthrough page; the page must make sense without it.

## Chapter 1: Heaven's Own Share

- (002) Year 1: The first year of Reina's reign. Sixty each, no card, two soldiers at the edges of the map, and an Emperor who keeps the stars herself, unpaid.
- (004) Half the harvest, in the first year. A hundred and twenty grain goes north, and a palace goes up on G20.
- (011) Six, seven, seven: three lots to the same bidder, and the Official holds a complete set in his first year. He has sixteen grain and a half; the set wants twenty.
- (013) Year 1 ends with the reserve at 155, one complete set, one soldier two steps from it, and the coast five grain lighter.
- (014) Year 2: The second year of Reina's reign, and Heaven floods the Official's fields and the Silk Peasant's, and spares the crown's own. Fifty in the hundred, again.
- (021) Five hands go up before the Official has counted a grain: the Silk Peasant and the Warlord call it, and the Official, the Gentry and the Rice Peasant agree.
- (027) The Empress answers with a palace and a promise, and nobody takes their name back. The dynasty breaks in its second spring; the treasury, 195, is sealed in the north-east.
- (033) Three claimants and three counts: the granary, the sword and one friend. Nobody leads twice, so nobody sits.
- (040) The Rice Peasant goes three grain past the Gentry's word, and walks away with a complete set and no throne to spend it on.

## Chapter 2: Swords Eat

- (050) Year 3: The first year with nobody on the throne. Every field comes in full, every booklet turns a page of misfortune, and not a grain of it is taxed.
- (052) The Official's Deed goes west into the sand, and his set goes with it. The Warlord hires a third pair of boots on the proceeds of a fat year.
- (058) The claims are put again, and nobody moves. A granary, three swords and one friend: the same three-way tie, a year older.
- (059) Year 4: The second year without a crown. Heaven's charity arrives from a treasury nobody may open, two grain apiece, paid to the five who sealed it.
- (061) A second Deed goes into the sand, and the Gentry's own fields are raided for the first time: ten grain, out of nearly two hundred.
- (063) The Official reads his canals into the record. It is the first work anyone has promised on paper since the revolt.
- (068) The Gentry offers twenty-five for a Jar, and the Rice Peasant keeps it. Year 4 ends as Year 3 did: no Emperor, and every granary fuller.

## Chapter 3: A Figure, Not a Speech

- (075) Year 5: The third year without a crown. Not a cloud, an average harvest, and the deposed Empress reminding the table that land can't be bought from an empty chair.
- (077) A Jar goes west to join the Deed it used to sit beside. The Rice Peasant is down to one piece; the Warlord's desert is up to three.
- (083) The claims are put a third time, and nobody moves. But the Rice Peasant names his price out loud: a figure, not a speech, by next year.
- (084) Year 6: Heaven doubles the people, and every province eats twice. There is land for sale at twenty a square, and no chair to sell it from.
- (096) Five names, one claimant. The Official is acclaimed in the fourth year of Chaos, and the treasury sealed in Year 2 opens at 251.
- (098) The new Emperor's first work is the Winter Palace, because the law says the canals wait for it. The Empress-that-was notes the Wall's new date.
- (102) The crown pays for fourteen squares in a single afternoon: three for itself, six for the Silk coast, five for the Rice coast.
- (112) The Gentry holds a complete set for one round. The Warlord collects the Bowl, and the soldier he sold that afternoon, before dark.

## Chapter 4: Fat Year or Thin

- (120) Year 7: The first full year of Clark's reign. Heaven sends a bad harvest to every province, and the new Emperor promises the old rate and a canal anyway.
- (122) Both sections of the Rice coast's canal, in the year they were promised. The chronicle notes the date, because it is the first promise of this reign to arrive on time.
- (125) The Rice Peasant buys four squares and asks the crown for ten of the sixty it offered. The other fifty go into the record, which is getting long.
- (126) The Warlord pays the toll on the new canal, then carries off the Customs House beside it. The crown ends the year with half a grain in its reserve.
- (127) Year 8: Storms and Raiders. A fat harvest for everyone, a disaster for the north, and the Warlord's own booklet keeps his soldiers' hands in their pockets.
- (129) The second canal in two years, and the crown's one debt paid on the day it said. The Wall over the north moves to next year, and the Official writes the new date in ink.
- (132) Two peasants each refuse thirty grain of the crown's money, so that the crown can afford a wall. The chronicle has no earlier entry like it.
- (133) A soldier walks into the Official's province and takes nothing. Three squares from her Bowl, with a year to look at it.

## Chapter 5: Counted Into the Mortar

- (140) Year 9: The Emperor's Share. An average harvest, half of every canal's yield to the crown, and the Wall over the north on the page for the third time.
- (144) Ten links of the Great Wall in a single year, four hundred grain of stone. The Gentry pays his quarter to the grain, and the strips below the Wall open in the north-east and the north-west.
- (147) Three provinces buy land, and two of them all but refuse the crown's money. The crown ends the works step with seven and a half grain, and the coast's granaries near three hundred each.
- (148) The Warlord walks off with the Gentry's Customs House, the sixth piece on his sand. A second soldier stands two steps from the Official's Bowl.
- (150) Year 10: The Canal Gives Way, and no canal does. Heaven takes half the Gentry's granary and half the Warlord's, and the coast holds over fourteen hundred grain between two farmers.
- (153) The twelfth link goes up over the Official's province. The Great Wall, promised for Year 8 and moved once, stands whole from the north-east to the desert in Year 10.
- (158) Thirty squares bought in one afternoon, by every province at the table. Two coast farmers halve the crown's offer, and the Official buys one square with her last ten grain.
- (159) The Official, who paid her last full twenty into the Wall, sells her Bowl back to the pile to pay the Warlord's five, and still falls one grain short. The first debt of the reign.

## Chapter 6: The Ink Holds

- (167) Year 11: Fortune and Misfortune. A fat harvest everywhere, a disaster for three seats, and the Harvest Pile pays out so much that it briefly owes itself forty.
- (174) The Reservoir goes in beside the Gentry's Jar, his quarter paid to the grain. The crown buys twenty squares of other people's land in one afternoon; the Rice Peasant pays for his own five.
- (182) The Official buys her Bowl back from the pile for twelve and carries it to the farthest corner of her province.
- (183) A soldier follows it there and stops one square short. Three works, a hundred and thirty grain, are left.
- (184) Year 12: Steady Weather, a year nobody writes poems about. The crown has the purse for both its remaining works and then some.
- (186) The Emperor lowers the tax in a year when he could have raised it, and digs his own canal with his own grain. Two works left, fifty grain between them.
- (191) The Gentry buys a sword from the Warlord and stands it on his Jar. The Warlord pockets five and calls it the best trade of the evening.
- (192) The Official's Bowl, bought back for twelve the year before, goes west to M16. The Warlord's list is down to one name, and that name owns nothing but sea.

## Chapter 7: The Last Line of the Book

- (199) Year 13: a Bad Harvest, the seed rotted and the ox lame. The coast's fifty-odd squares of sea do not notice. Fifty grain of stone is left in the whole dynasty.
- (202) The Official's Irrigation goes in on G13 and G14, under the Wall she asked for first, at no cost to her. One line remains in the book: twenty grain of Pagoda.
- (204) The crown pays for fifteen squares and is told, politely, to keep its money for the other five.
- (205) The Warlord pays the Emperor five to cross his river and takes ten back on the far bank. A soldier stands in Province 1, three rounds from the palaces.

- (206) Year 14: the Utility Tax, a levy on the upkeep of things already paid for. Eighteen grain to the crown, which has never needed it less.
- (209) The Marble Pagoda goes up on G22, twenty grain, with nothing beside it. The last line of the book is struck off, and the dynasty that broke in its second year survives its fourteenth. A Warlord soldier stands at I17 and watches.
