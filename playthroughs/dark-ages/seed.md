# Chance and steering in the Dark Ages

The Director steers this game towards the arc in `scenario.md`, more openly than most (Manish asked for it). Every piece of steering is the Director's, made before the game or between chapters through a handoff, and is listed below when it happens. A Storyteller never steers on its own.

- **Seed:** `5130`, chosen by the Director with `deck_scout.py` and approved by Manish.
- **How the deck is shuffled:** the twenty card keys from `cards.py`, sorted alphabetically, then `random.Random(5130).shuffle(keys)` (Python 3).
- **Deck order, top first** (Year 1 draws no card, so the first of these is Year 2's):

  1. rainflood · 2. careless · 3. chest · 4. blight · 5. double3 · 6. dribble1 · 7. repairs · 8. share · 9. canal · 10. goodevil · 11. winds · 12. dribble3 · 13. utility · 14. half1 · 15. dribble2 · 16. double2 · 17. half2 · 18. heaven · 19. double1 · 20. breach

  The players are never shown this list, and the narrator never hints at it. Population cards past their caps are still drawn in order; the engine treats a capped one as a good harvest.

- **Booklets:** not drawn by chance. Each player sits in the province their name puns on: Reina 1, Clark 2, Rich 3, Silke 5, Paddy 6, Rob 8.

## Steering log

**The restart.** After six years with no revolt, Manish had the game restarted from the setup (2026-10-28). The first attempt's Years 1–6 are in `outtakes/attempt-1/`, with their own log and handoffs; its steering is listed under "Attempt 1" below. Same seed, same cast, fresh agents.

- **The restart, the deck: seed 5130 kept.** The deck wasn't what failed. Its x1 Years 2–5, doubling in Year 6 and bad Year 7 are the windows the new plan needs: Chaos as a tax holiday, then famine with no land allowed. A new seed would change only the auctions, which the agents decide anyway.
- **The restart, the plan: a revolt at Year 1's tax, before the first auction.** In attempt 1 cheap sets moved the throne twice (Years 3 and 4), and every set-holder had a reason to act before a revolt and none to back one. Year 1's tax is declared in step 4 and the first auction is step 7, so a revolt then comes before anyone holds a set.
- **The restart, the actor's notes:**
  - **Reina** taxes very hard from the first harvest, to build before the population comes (the scripted Dark Ages used 70%), and treats the Warlord's raids as the provinces' business.
  - **Rob** is wantonly destructive, as Manish suggested: he raids every square he can reach, Province 1's and the palaces first, and makes it plain that it's his choice, not the crown's permission. That makes "the Emperor can't control the Warlord" bite from Year 1 and drains the crown.
  - Clark, Rich, Silke and Paddy keep their first-attempt notes.
- **The restart, procedure:** the pay-or-revolt prompt from Year 1, straight after the declaration and before collection, for every seat that owes tax, plus Rob. It continues every year while there's an Emperor.
- **The restart, one Storyteller's tip, to Silke, public,** straight after Year 1's tax declaration and before the pay-or-revolt prompts. It covers how a revolt is called and called off, that a called revolt stops play, that one called before the tax is paid voids it, what Chaos stops and doesn't, the sealed treasury, and that the throne then goes only by acclamation (claimants only; anyone but the deposed Emperor, a peasant or the Warlord included).
- **The restart, Year 1 (Manish): the revolt is aimed at Year 2 instead,** so the table gets a feel for the game first. The tip to Silke and the pay-or-revolt round move to Year 2; Year 1's tax runs normally. Because Year 1's auction can complete a set, **Rich's note was changed** (sent to him by `note --to` before his Year 1 auction): he loves the Gentry's seat and would rather be the man every claimant needs than the man in the chair. In attempt 1 his note ("the richest man should be the one sitting in the chair") sent him to the throne with a set.
- **The restart, Year 1, in practice:** the move to Year 2 reached the Storyteller after Year 1's tip and pay-or-revolt round had gone out (Reina taxed 50%). Both stand, and both are repeated in Year 2.
- **The restart, Year 1, factions (Manish's idea):** rivalries were added to four notes, as stances. Clark has never trusted a landlord and would sooner see the chair empty than see the Gentry or his man in it. Rich and the Official have never trusted each other. Silke's backing can't be bought for her landlord or anyone he puts forward. Paddy trusts a steady hand over a clever ledger and dislikes the way the Official counts. Why: two blocs (Clark and Silke against Rich and Paddy), with Rob leading on soldiers and Reina able only to back someone, so that no claimant leads the acclamation outright and Chaos holds. Clark and Rich got theirs by `note --to`; Silke and Paddy at their launches.
- **The restart, Year 1, a rewind (Manish, in Storyteller 4's session):** Year 1 was rewound to just after Reina's 50% declaration. The tip, the pay-or-revolt round, Rob's revolt call ("the cause is me", with Silke and Paddy backing) and Reina's answer were removed from the script and the talk log; their files are in `outtakes/year1-rewind/`. Clark collects normally, the Summer Palace stands, and the tip and the round go to Year 2 as planned. Every seat that heard the revolt gets a fresh agent, so the new launches carry the faction notes from `ledger.md`. Why: Manish wanted the table to get a feel for the game before the revolt. Worth noting for the report: given the tip and the prompt, the table revolted at once, against a 50% tax and a Warlord.
- **The restart, end of Year 1: no steering on Clark's set.** Clark completed a set at Year 1's auction (Deed 6, Jar 7, Bowl 7) and can afford to take the throne in Year 2. Left to him. If he takes it, the revolt can come against him, with Reina deposed and Rich, Paddy and Rob his rivals. Procedure only: Year 2's tip and pay-or-revolt round go out as one simultaneous round, which also saves context. Crossings are settled by the players.
- **The restart, Year 2:** the revolt was called (Rob, with Silke and Rich agreeing) and dropped after Reina's answer. Silke, Paddy and Clark believed a revolt crowns Rob; two public corrections didn't shift it. Rich then completed a second set.

Why: the "a revolt crowns Rob" belief turned three players, and the rules notes had only stated the rule, not worked it through with the table's own numbers. Paddy, because he's untipped and one of the three. The tip says nothing about sets.
- **Before Chapter 2, standing notes for a set swap.** If Clark or Rich takes the throne with a set, the new Emperor gets the hard-taxing, works-raising note and Reina gets the deposed note, sent by `note --to`. Why: in attempt 1 every set-made Emperor cut the tax and closed every cause.
- **Year 3 of the restart:** Silke and Rob called "taxes are consistently excessive" in the simultaneous round. Clark and Rich (who both hold sets) and Paddy (a fat year) refused to agree. Clark's and Silke's soldiers captured both of Rob's, so Rob's army is 0.

Why: the two set-holders won't back any revolt, so one needs all three players without a set: Silke, Rob and Paddy. Paddy named his price in public, "something built on the coast". The mid-chapter note reached her as "the Director's direction". DIRECTOR.md now says such notes are framed as "a note on playing your character", never naming the Director.
- **Before Chapter 3:** no tip, since Paddy's public deadline ("if nothing is built south of Silke's beach by Year 5, Paddy agrees the cause exists") is the lever. The pay-or-revolt round continues, and the standing notes for a set swap are kept, framed without the Director. Notes unchanged.
- **Year 5 of the restart, first play (now in `outtakes/year5-rewind/`):** Reina met Paddy's deadline with a Province 6 canal section. Rob called "consistently excessive", and Silke and Rich agreed. Reina paid Rich 37.5 (his posted price) and promised a Province 5 canal, and Silke and Rich took it back: the third revolt dropped the same way.
- **Year 5 rewound, and an actor's brief (Manish):** Manish had Year 5 rewound to the end of Year 4 and replayed with a brief for the cast, as actors. The new `direction.md` in the game folder is added by `briefs.py` to every launch prompt (only in games that have the file). It sets out the arc the cast plays towards: the revolt stands this year, Chaos holds with the acclamation split, then an acclamation and recovery. It gives each player a part:

  It forbids any mention of itself in a say, aside, whisper or diary; any reply that does is re-prompted and not filed. Why: three revolts in four years were dropped, each by a backer bought back. - **Rewound to the end of Year 1 (Manish), before the Year 5 replay began:** Years 2–5 are in `outtakes/year2-rewind/`, and Year 5's first play is also in `outtakes/year5-rewind/`. ## Attempt 1 (Years 1–6, now in `outtakes/attempt-1/`)

- **Before Chapter 1, the deck: seed 5130.** Searched seeds 1–60,000 for: land opening (a doubling) in Years 5–7; no negative bare income for any seat before it; a lean year at or right after the doubling; at most x2 until Year 13; at most one lean year in the recovery. Five passed. 5130 gives four x1 years with positive incomes (Year 2 rainflood, 3 careless, 4 chest, 5 blight), when no tax is worth revolting for. The population doubles in Year 6, then a bad Year 7 (−70 for Province 1, −30 for every province) with no land to buy under Chaos. Years 8–14 are x2, mostly average, with one bad year (13): seven years to recover. The first x3 is Year 15. Alternatives scouted: 36521 (the same shape, but x4 in Year 14 and x6 in Year 15, so a hard deadline at Year 13) and 16427 (x1 to Year 6 and a doubling in Year 7, but the share and utility cards fall inside Chaos). Rejected as harsher: 2136 and 1049 (x3 by Year 6–8) and 4292 (x3 at Year 13, fewer good recovery years).
- **Before Chapter 1, the actor's notes** (`ledger.md`, **Playing it**). Stances only, approved by Manish. Reina proud and in a hurry to build, never answering a grievance with a cut. Clark ambitious, with the revolt clauses by heart. Rich greedy, fawning while the crown is strong. Silke sharp, selling her backing dearly. Paddy hating to owe, siding with whoever feeds Province 6. Rob an opportunist who'd sit on the throne himself.
- **Before Chapter 1, the revolt line:** `REVOLT_LINE = True` in `game.py` puts the seven causes for revolt and what Chaos stops on every board card (a `briefs.py` flag, this game only). Why: in the first attempt no prompt ever offered a revolt.
