Two years, and the set is whole: Silke's Jar and Paddy's Bowl for ten apiece, and Clark's own Customs House for twenty-six, bought out from under him with a smile and a thank-you. It cost me the better part of my grain, and Rob's riders took two more fives off me. Still, I end the year with fifty-five, and twenty is all a seat change needs. Clark tried to sell my squares to the Warlord, and the Warlord was vulgar enough to say so in front of everyone. I couldn't have paid for a better turn of events, and from now on I'll treat Clark with exaggerated kindness. Silke has a set too but no grain, so I must keep the rent gentle and the lady friendly until I've moved. Next comes the throne. I'll bow to Reina all the way up the steps, and I'll want somebody else's soldier between my fields and that desert when I'm sitting on it.

## Years 3–4

I said the throne came next and it did. I bowed to Reina, thanked her for the steps, and walked straight up them with her reserve in my pocket. It wasn't quite the hundred and four I'd counted on, but my pieces went back to stock before that rider on I11 could reach them, and that was worth more than the difference. I taxed at half to keep Silke's purse under twenty and put the canal in Paddy's paddies to sweeten it. It worked for exactly one auction step, and then she took Clark's seat instead of mine. I can't say I mind watching Clark fall to the coast, and I'll keep my promise of a canal to Province 5 so that he has to thank me for it. The crown holds two hundred and the palaces are untouched. But Silke has the book, eighty-five grain, a Deed and a Bowl, and the only Dragon Jar left goes on the block next year. Whoever buys that Jar decides whether I keep this chair, so I'll pay Silke her ten to the grain, smile at her through every collection, and make sure the bidding on that Jar costs her everything she has.
