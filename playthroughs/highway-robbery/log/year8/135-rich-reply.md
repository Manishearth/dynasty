diary: I said I'd bid a grain past careful, and I did. I took Paddy's Bowl by a grain over Silke, and it felt so good I nearly forgot to thank her. Then I put it in the far corner, told the table no boots ever went there, and Rob walked his rider up to within one step of it inside two years and fined me five each way. That's the last time I describe my own fields out loud. The Jar was never mine to win: Paddy came with a hundred and eighteen and needed only that piece. I made him pay thirty-five for it, Silke made him pay forty-six, and now he's the Gentry and needs only a Bowl, and the only Bowl on the board that would do is mine. Clark is worse. He has a hundred and seventy-two, a Bowl and a Customs House, and Paddy's Scroll comes up next year, which is the only piece Clark is missing. I have fifty-nine and still need two pieces. I can't outbid him, so I'll make the Scroll cost him every grain my purse can push, bow to Reina while I do it, and remind her that the man who wants her chair isn't the one carrying her basket.
