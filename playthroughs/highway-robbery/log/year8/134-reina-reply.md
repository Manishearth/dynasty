diary: I said I'd spend the reserve as fast as it came in, and I did: a canal for Paddy, irrigation for Rich, sea land for myself, and nearly three hundred grain of other people's squares, because I promised "every square" and Clark wrote it down with the date. It left the crown at fourteen and a half one year-end, and I won't make that promise in those words again. The forty percent was my answer. Every grain I took from Paddy was one he couldn't bid on that Jar, and he bought it anyway at forty-six and changed seats with Silke instead of coming for mine. Now he's the Gentry with a Customs House and a Jar side by side in a quiet corner, and the one thing he needs is a Wooden Bowl. Rich and Clark both have Bowls on the board, and Rob's men are standing a step from each of them. So the most dangerous piece at this table is any Bowl that goes back to stock, and if one comes to the block I mean to be bidding for it myself. A hundred and forty in the reserve, and the works have still to go up. But first I need the Bowl, and then the Wall.
