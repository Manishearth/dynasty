## At the table since your last turn

Silke: "A hundred and eighteen, Paddy, less twenty for the chair, so you can go to ninety-eight, and the whole table can do that sum. I can't, but every grain I make you pay goes into my pile, and the pile is mine to count."

Paddy: "Three Jars I've lost by a single grain, friends, and I've been saving all year so it won't be four. Rich, Silke, I know you both need it and I'm sorry, but this time I'm bidding like a man who means it."

Rob: "Two grain for a Jar and still not a bid from me. I've never paid for a piece in my life, and I'm not starting on Clark's crockery. Rich, Silke, if one of you wins it, put it somewhere nice. I'll be along to admire it."

The auction: Paddy takes Clark's Dragon Jar for 46. Rich stopped at 34 and Silke at 45 (she'd gone that far only to keep it from Paddy); Rob passed. Clark's quarter is 11.5. With the Jar, Paddy holds a full set (Customs House, Land Deed Scroll, Dragon Jar) and 72 grain.

Paddy: "Well, I'll be. Four tries and a grain past careful, and there's my set. Silke, I'm sorry, truly. You've counted that pile better than anyone, but I've sat in the paddies eight years and I'd like a turn at the upper table. The Rice Peasant's seat is a good one, with three canal sections, sea land out to the corner and a hundred and ninety-five a year in an average year. I've left it in fine order for you."

Paddy puts the Jar on R23, then changes seats with Silke: he is the Gentry now, in Province 3, with her 59 and his 72, 131 in all, and he runs the Harvest Pile and the rent from next year. His Land Deed Scroll goes back to Province 3's stock, to be auctioned next year; his Customs House and Jar move to Province 3. Silke is the Rice Peasant in Province 6, with its two canal sections and new land, and draws 60 from the pile. Nobody had bought a soldier.

(Storyteller's note: Province 6 has two canal sections, not three.)

Paddy: "I'll put them side by side again, M9 and M10, in the quiet corner of the Gentry's fields. Old habits. Rob, I know your lads like a long walk, so I've given them one."

Paddy puts his Customs House on M9 and his Jar on M10 in Province 3. As the Gentry he needs only a Wooden Bowl (and 20 grain) for a set that could take any seat, the throne included.

Rob: "Rich, your Bowl's in the far corner, and W1 is one step from it now, with five out of Province 2 for the walk. Clark, two men, five grain, one step from your Customs House. I named you last year, so nobody can call this a surprise. And congratulations, Paddy. The quiet corner is lovely. I'll be up to see it."

Rob marches: W1 to H15, raiding Rich for 5; W2 to O23 and W3 to P21, raiding Clark for 5 each, 10 in all. No hire. W1 is now 1 round from Rich's Bowl on G16; W2 is 1 round and W3 2 rounds from Clark's Customs House on P23. Salaries: Reina pays Rich 10 and Rob 10.

Where Years 7 and 8 leave the table: Reina is Empress with 140.5. Clark, the Silk Peasant, has 172, holds a Bowl and a Customs House, and needs only a Land Deed Scroll. Rich, the Official, has 59, holds a Bowl, and needs a Scroll and a Jar. Paddy, now the Gentry, has 131, holds a Customs House and a Jar, and needs only a Bowl. Silke, now the Rice Peasant, has 60 and nothing on the board. Rob has 192 and still needs a piece each from Rich, Clark and Silke. Paddy's Land Deed Scroll is back in Province 3's stock for next year's auction. Works still owed: 580.
