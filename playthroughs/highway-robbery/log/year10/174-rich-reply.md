diary: I went into Year 9 with nine grain, so I couldn't make the Scroll cost Clark anything at all. He took it for thirty-one, completed his set and climbed straight past me into my own ledger chair, and in doing it he threw me onto the coast. I thanked him, and for once I almost meant it, because the coast he despised paid me four hundred and sixty in a single harvest, and the crown paid half of every square of sea I added to it. With that grain I finally bid past careful. I took the Customs House by one grain over Silke and the Bowl by one grain over Paddy, and that's the same single grain I'd lost by three times before, only this time it fell my way. I'm sorry about Paddy. He made me a decent offer, and my limits had already beaten him to it. Now I hold two of the three pieces, and both Land Deeds in China sit in Rob's sand. He's taken Clark's Scroll off him, and his men are walking my farm and fining me five for the privilege. I said nothing aloud about where my pieces went, and his rider still stands two steps from the Customs House, which shows how little my silence protects them. Rob doesn't sell, or so he says. But he never stops talking about prices, and a man who takes a raise from the Empress can be bought. Next year I'll keep my purse full, keep the crown's tax honest, and find out what figure finally makes the Warlord sell a piece out of his desert.
