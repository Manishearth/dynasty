## At the table since your last turn

Clark: "Four squares, Your Majesty, half from you as you announced, and I'm writing it down under 'promises kept' right beside Rich's canal. That's two of you on the page now, Silke, so mind you stay there. And Rob, they're on the far shore, so tell W2 to bring a boat."

Paddy: "Your Majesty, I'll take you at your word, half a square each. Four squares, then, down at the bottom of my paddies, forty from me and forty from the crown. Twice as many mouths in the south-east means twice as many hands, and I'd like them all planting rice and not out wandering the desert with Rob."

Silke buys four squares of sea for Province 1 (E23, F23 to F25) for 80. Clark buys four for Province 5 (Q26, Q27, P27, O27), 40 from him and 40 from the crown. Paddy had also asked for Q26, which went to Clark first (land is settled in table order); his other three, R25, R26 and S26, are bought, 30 from him and 30 from the crown.

Paddy: "Beaten to a square by Clark, and by a moment, not even a grain this time. Fair enough, neighbour. I'll take R27 on the same terms, Your Majesty, ten from me and ten from the crown, and the south-east will feed four times the mouths if it has to."

Paddy buys a fourth square, R27, 10 from him and 10 from the crown. The auctions follow: the three pieces Silke returned come up in order, Reina's Land Deed Scroll, Clark's Dragon Jar, Paddy's Wooden Bowl.

Rich: "An Official with an empty board and seventy-seven grain. I'm the humblest man at this table, Your Majesty, so forgive me if I bid on a pot or two. Reina, the Jar sits so nicely beside a Bowl, I know. I'd hate to make it expensive for you, but I'd hate even more to go home with nothing."

Reina: "Rich, darling, you need three pieces and I need one, so let's not make the Pile fat for nothing. You can have my Scroll and Paddy's Bowl without a peep from me, and I'll take the Jar. Bid against me on it and you'll only have less for the two you actually need."

Clark: "Rich, you need three pieces, I need three, and only one of us has a canal paying twenty a year from next harvest. I'll bid my honest number on the Scroll and the Bowl and not a grain of spite. The Jar opens at ten, Reina, and that's me being neighbourly."

Paddy: "Reina, I'll not fatten the Pile chasing a Jar with thirty-two grain in my pocket, so it's yours as far as I'm concerned. The Scroll, though, was mine until Rob's lad carried it off, and I'd like it back. I'll bid it honestly, Rich, Clark, and no spite in it."

(Storyteller's note: The Land Deed Scroll on the block is the one Silke returned to Province 3's stock; Paddy's own Scroll stays on O18 in Rob's desert.)

Rob: "Six years and not a grain spent on crockery, and I'm not breaking the habit for Clark's Jar. Reina, it's all yours if you want it, darling, but buy it knowing my W1 is three steps from your Bowl and your Customs House. A full set lasts exactly as long as it takes me to get there."

The auctions settle: Paddy wins Reina's Land Deed Scroll at 23 (Rich stopped at 22, Clark at 18); Reina wins Clark's Dragon Jar at 41 (Rich stopped at 40); Clark wins Paddy's Wooden Bowl at 16 (Rich stopped at 15). Rob bid on nothing.

Clark: "Sixteen for a Bowl, a grain past Rich, and forty-one for my Jar, Reina, so the quarter coming back to me is ten. Thank you both for being so generous to a humble silk farmer. Rob, it sits down by my new squares on the far shore, so W2 will be wading."

Paddy: "Twenty-three to twenty-two, and Rich, I'm sorry, but for once the grain went my way. That's two times in a row now. It goes on S24, right beside my Customs House, so Rob, if your lads come calling, they'll find both down the far end of a long road."

Reina: "Silke, darling, you did tell us that patience and a purse beat any promise, and I took notes. I'll have the throne back, with thanks for keeping it warm. The Gentry's chair is a lovely one; I've left the Harvest Pile in perfect order. And Rob, you won't need to walk to my Bowl now: it's going back to the stock, so your W1 can stay where it is."

Paddy puts the Scroll on S24 and Clark the Bowl on P26. Reina puts the Jar on M13, then, holding a full set and 37 grain, changes seats with Silke and takes the throne back, with the crown's reserve of 165.5: 202.5 in all. Her Customs House, Dragon Jar and Wooden Bowl go back to the stock of Provinces 2, 5 and 6, for next year. Silke becomes the Gentry in Province 3, holding nothing, and draws 60.

Rob: "Three Emperors in two years. I've never seen a chair so keen to be sat in. Welcome back, Reina; the palaces are yours again, which means every one I burn costs you twenty. Clark, "wading" is exactly right: W2 is four steps from your Bowl, and I'm telling you now so it's fair. Paddy, five for the road, and W3 is walking that long road of yours."

Rob marches: W1 raids J13 in Rich's Province 2, W2 raids M25 in Clark's Province 5, W3 raids R19 in Paddy's Province 6, 5 from each. Nobody else has a soldier to move.

(Storyteller's note: After the march, W2 on M25 is 3 rounds from Clark's Wooden Bowl on P26, not four.)

Reina: "Rich, ten for the book, the same as you were promised; I don't make the Official pay for my good fortune. Rob, you keep the cards, and I'm doubling it to ten. Call it the cost of a quiet road past my palaces, and do read me something gentle."

Salaries: Reina pays Rich 10 as the Official and keeps Rob on as the I Ching reader at 10, double his old pay.

The end of Year 6. Reina is Empress again, with 182.5 in the reserve and four new squares of sea at E23 and F23 to F25. Rich is the Official (82, nothing on the board); Silke the Gentry (60, nothing); Clark the Silk Peasant (37.5, a Wooden Bowl on P26 and four new squares); Paddy the Rice Peasant (8, a Customs House on S25 and a Land Deed Scroll on S24, needing only a Dragon Jar, and four new squares). Rob holds 172, two pieces in his desert (Paddy's old Jar on T17 and Scroll on O18), and still needs a raided piece from Rich, Silke and Clark. A Customs House, a Dragon Jar and a Wooden Bowl wait in stock for Year 7. The population is x2; 650 of the works are still owed.
