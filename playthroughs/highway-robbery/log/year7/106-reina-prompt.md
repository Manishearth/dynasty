## Reina's diary so far, in her own words

## Setup–Year 2

Two years in, and the Summer Palace stands at G26, and next year my canal will add twenty to every harvest. Those are the things that last. I embarrassed myself over the tax arithmetic, twelve instead of eighteen, and Clark corrected me in front of everyone; I'll let him enjoy it, since a proud Official is a loyal one. Rob has taken ten grain in salary from me and given back nothing but raids on everyone except me. Maybe that is exactly what I'm paying for, but I'll watch my palace all the same. What worries me is Rich: he smiles like a servant, he holds a full set, and he has the grain to change seats, so I'll be watching to see which chair he wants. Silke has a full set too but no grain, so I could cheaply buy her gratitude with a gentle tax. Ninety-four in the reserve. The Winter Palace is next, and then the coast canals.

## Years 3–4

I wrote that I was watching Rich, and I watched him take the throne in a single polite sentence, along with the ninety-two I'd saved for the Winter Palace. I didn't even get to lay its first stone. The canals I dug now feed his harvest, and he's already digging one of his own in Paddy's paddies, which tells me he understands the throne better than I'd like. As Gentry I've been honest with the pile and gentle with the rent, and I overpaid for a Customs House, thirty-nine grain, mostly to keep Paddy from finishing his set. That was the right call and an expensive one, and I'm down to forty-eight and a half. Silke did to Clark exactly what Rich did to me, which proves that patience and a purse are worth more at this table than any promise. Next year the Jar and the Bowl I need both come back to the block, and Silke wants that Jar with eighty grain behind her, so I'll have to make a deal or outbid her. Once I hold all three, the throne is the seat I'll take back.

## Years 5–6

I took the throne back, and I did it the way it was taken from me: one polite sentence, with grain in my purse. It was expensive. Nineteen for the Bowl and forty-one for the Jar, because Rich wouldn't stop until forty, and I lost the Jar to Silke at thirty-one the year before. Then Silke spent her own reign generously, and I came into a hundred and sixty-five of her reserve. The two sets I bought both went back to stock the moment I sat down, and every piece I chased is up for auction again, which means somebody else is already saving for my chair. Paddy needs only a Jar and is down to eight grain, Rich has eighty-two and an empty board, and Silke is sitting where I sat, with sixty and a grudge she's too gracious to show. With twice the mouths my palaces are barely paying their way, so the works have to start again, and I'll pay Rob his ten a year gladly rather than see him burn the Winter Palace. This time I won't save a hoard for someone to inherit: I'll spend the reserve on canals and land as fast as it comes in, and keep a close eye on whoever buys the Jar.
