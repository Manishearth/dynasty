diary: Years 3 and 4. I counted a lean tax and then a greedy one, offered Paddy thirty for a Jar he was right not to sell, and watched Rich climb Reina's steps to the throne in a single sentence. Then I won the Jar and the Bowl by a grain each, as I'd sworn I would, and put them in the back row out of Rob's reach, which turned out to be a lovely safe spot for Silke's pieces. She had the full set and twenty-five grain. I had fifty-five and a half, and I'd spent fifty-two of my own to give her an empty board and a fat purse to take. It's the same lesson as the Customs House I sold Rich, only dearer: the grain I spend getting ahead of someone is grain I've put on the table for them, and nobody watches a broke player closely enough. So the Official is now a peasant on the coast with sixty, no pieces, and a farmhouse Rob has already visited. Still, the Jar comes back into my own province's stock and is auctioned next year, so it's my lot to sell and my minimum to set. Silke needs exactly that Jar, and Reina needs it too, so I'll make them bid each other high for it. Rich has promised Province 5 its canal, and I mean to collect on that promise. I've kept the book, and Silke's page has started.
