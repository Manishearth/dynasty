## Silke's diary so far, in her own words

## Setup–Year 2

Two years in, I have three pieces sitting in the back corner of my coast and eight grain in my purse. I went in with thirty-seven. Rent, tax, a flood that missed my fields, and two of Rob's "house calls" took the rest, and I paid nine apiece for every piece of the set, which was the right call. I took the last Bowl in the Kingdom off Clark by a single grain, and Paddy ended up with no Customs House at all, so for once I'm climbing and he's stuck at the bottom. But a set without twenty grain is just pretty crockery, and Rich has a set and sixty grain, so he gets upstairs before I do. This year I keep my head down, push every grain toward that twenty, and keep an eye on Rob's man at O24, because he's three steps from everything I own.

## Years 3–4

Two years ago I had eight grain and a set I couldn't use. Tonight I'm the Official with eighty-five and a half grain and Clark's book in my lap. I kept my purse shut through every auction and let Reina, Clark and Paddy throw more than a hundred grain at the pile. Then I took Clark's seat, and his fifty-odd grain with it, the same afternoon he'd paid fifty-two for a Jar and a Bowl to get ahead of me by a grain. It cost me my Customs House, but I've climbed before Paddy, and he's lost his Jar to Rob's sand, so he's further down than he was when we started. I'm short one piece now, a Dragon Jar, and there's one in stock at Province 5. That's Clark's stock now, and he'll want a good price for it, but I can outbid anyone at this table except Rich and Rob. Rob's man on I13 is three rounds from my back row, so the Jar has to be on the board, and I have to use the set, before he gets there.

## Years 5–6

Two years ago I was counting crockery. This year I sat on the throne with three hundred and forty grain in the reserve, and I lost it to Reina inside a year, with my own words in her notebook. I bought the Jar for thirty-one, took Rich's chair and his purse, finished Clark's canal, paid half of everybody's new land, and bought four squares of sea that Reina now harvests. That's a hundred and sixty grain of the crown's money I spent making the kingdom rich enough for someone else to buy a set and take it off me. I should have bid Rich's last forty on that Jar myself and kept it off the board. Now I'm the Gentry with sixty grain and an empty board, and the worst of it is Paddy: Customs House, Scroll, and one Jar short of a set, with eight grain in his pocket. He needs that Jar and twenty grain. I run the Harvest Pile, I set the rent, and the Jar's in Clark's stock next year, so I'll be counting Paddy's grain out loud every year until I can buy my way back up.

## Years 7–8

Two years ago I said I'd count Paddy's grain every year until I could buy my way back up. I counted it, all right, out loud and to the grain, and he bought a Jar for forty-six with me one grain behind him, then took my chair and my purse to go with it. I lost the Customs House by a grain and the Bowl by a grain, I asked for extra rent four times and got none, and the bad harvest took thirty off fields that never grew anything. I spent the whole two years trying to keep Paddy down, and that was the mistake. I should have been building my own set. So now I'm the Rice Peasant, with sixty grain and nothing on the board, and the strange part is that it's the best land I've ever stood on: two canal sections and sea out to the corner, and it'll be a hundred and ninety-five a year in an average year. Paddy left it in fine order, curse him, and he's sitting in my old corner one Bowl short of a set, with his pieces on M9 and M10 where Rob's men will take years to reach them. I pay rent to him now, and I'll pay the five and not a grain more, exactly as he taught me. From here I bank every harvest, buy pieces cheap and out of Rob's reach, and climb. This time I won't be watching anybody but myself.
