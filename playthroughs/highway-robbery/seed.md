# Chance and steering in Highway Rob-bery

The Director nudges this game towards the arc in `scenario.md`. Any steering is the Director's, made before the game or between chapters through a handoff, and is listed below when it happens. A Storyteller never steers on its own.

- **Seed:** `297`, recommended in `scenario.md` and confirmed by the Director before Chapter 1.
- **How the deck is shuffled:** the twenty card keys from `cards.py`, sorted alphabetically, then `random.Random(297).shuffle(keys)` (Python 3).
- **Deck order, top first** (Year 1 draws no card, so the first of these is Year 2's):

  1. rainflood · 2. dribble1 · 3. share · 4. chest · 5. double1 · 6. careless · 7. dribble2 · 8. double2 · 9. double3 · 10. utility · 11. blight · 12. canal · 13. heaven · 14. winds · 15. half2 · 16. dribble3 · 17. breach · 18. half1 · 19. goodevil · 20. repairs

  The players are never shown this list, and the narrator never hints at it. Population cards past their caps are still drawn in order; the engine treats a capped one as a good harvest.

- **Booklets:** not drawn by chance. Each player sits in the province their name puns on: Reina 1, Clark 2, Rich 3, Silke 5, Paddy 6, Rob 8.

## Steering log

- **Before Chapter 1, the deck:** seed 297 stands. `deck_scout.py 297 --seats 1,2,3,5,6,8 --years 10`: split floods in Year 2 (bad for Clark, Silke and Rob), a bad harvest for everyone in Year 3, the Emperor's Share in Year 4 and the Imperial Chest in Year 5, ×2 in Year 6 (land opens), Careless Prosperity in Year 7 (every seat plays its first disaster; Rob's is no raids on Provinces 5–7 that year, so he can still ride on 2 and 3), a bad harvest in Year 8 (every bare income negative), and ×4 from Year 9. Rob plays no other disaster in ten years, so only soldiers and tolls stop his raids, and five years at ×1 give him time to hire and ride before land and debt take over the table. The alternatives suit this arc less: 268 and 910 reach ×6 by Year 7 and put every seat in the red, which makes the game about land rather than the Warlord; 1076 is calm until Year 5 but also reaches ×6 by Year 7. Rob's bare income is 0 in Years 2–3 and 6–7 and negative from Year 8, so his hires and tolls must come from raids and soldier sales: the campaign has to pay for itself.
- **Before Chapter 1, the actor's notes:** the **Playing it** line in each `ledger.md` entry, from `scenario.md`'s suggestions: Reina would rather pay the Warlord than build soldiers; Clark sells what he knows about everyone's squares; Rich would rather someone else's soldier stood on his border; Silke counts aloud who has paid towards stopping Rob and who hasn't; Paddy pays his fair share of a defence and no more; Rob names his targets before he rides. Stances only: no moves, numbers or results.
- **Before Chapter 1, the arc changed (Manish):** Rob takes the throne and wins, around Years 6–8, with a few years to reign; a collapse under him is fine (`scenario.md`). The notes were rewritten towards infighting rather than a coalition: Reina a builder who pays Rob off rather than buying soldiers; Clark and Rich rivals for the throne, Clark selling information to anyone, Rob included; Silke racing Paddy for the scarce pieces and counting his grain; Paddy unchanged; Rob means to have the throne itself. Silke's old note (counting who had paid for defence) was dropped, since it organises a coalition against him. No tip will give anyone bad advice.
- **Before Chapter 1, retuned for seat changes (Manish):** most players should end on a lost seat, through many swaps (`scenario.md`). Silke now means to climb as high as a set takes her, Paddy quietly means to climb and would rather spend on pieces than defence, and Rob enjoys watching the others fight for their chairs before he rides.
- **Before Chapter 4 (Years 7–8), Rob behind the path:** after six years and five seat changes Rob has one counted piece of four, and two of the three players he still needs (Rich and Silke) hold nothing. Two levers, both for Rob only:
  - his **Playing it** line changed from enjoying the knife-fight "first" to "now he means to collect, before the kingdom has nothing left worth ruling": a stance with more urgency, no move;
  - **one public Storyteller's tip to Rob** in Year 7, from what the table can see: the three players he still needs place whatever they buy on unraided squares of their own provinces, so a soldier already inside Province 2 or 3 before an auction stands close to wherever a piece lands. The other players hear it, as every tip is public.
  No tips to anyone else; the infighting is doing the rest. The tip was given in Year 7, filed with no recipient so the whole table heard it (`note --to` shows a note only to its recipient).
- **Before Chapter 5 (Years 9–10), Rob still one of four:** W1 and W2 stand a round from Rich's Bowl and Clark's Customs House, but his last target, Silke, holds nothing and few pieces are left to buy. Rob holds two raided pieces on his desert (a Jar and a Deed), and RULINGS ("Readings the playthroughs rely on") lets the Warlord sell raided realia privately, keeping the whole price; the buyer places it at once. **One public tip to Rob** in Year 9 saying so, from the rules and the board; whatever he and the table do with it is theirs. The length lever: the game doesn't stop at Year 10 if Rob is still riding; a parked spare takes Chapter 6.
- **Before Chapter 6 (Years 11–12), Rob two of four:** he raided Clark's Scroll in Year 10 and needs Rich (whose Customs House is a round from W2) and Silke, who holds nothing. No stock is left anywhere: every remaining Jar and Deed is on Rob's desert, and he refused to sell (Reina pays him 20 a year to keep the Scrolls). Two levers:
  - Rob's **Playing it** line adds "He's no hoarder: grain and spoils are only tools to him, and the throne is the one thing he means to keep." A stance, not a move.
  - **One public Storyteller's tip to Silke** in Year 11: no stock is left, and the only Jar and Land Deeds not on someone's board are on Rob's desert, which he may sell. True, from the board, and in her interest as a climber; it also gives Rob a target if she buys.
  The game runs past Year 12 if it must (the Chapter 7 spare is parked).
- **Year 11, the tip given:** filed after the I Ching card as a note with no recipient, so every player heard it in their next prompt, Silke before her first decision. In the same prompts they heard that the average harvest had sunk Paddy into selling his Jar and Customs House back to the pile, so the tip's line about Paddy's Jar was already out of date; both pieces came up at that year's auction.
- **Year 12, mid-chapter (the Director's call; Manish away):** at the end of Year 11 only the Province 3 Reservoir (80) was owed, so Year 12's works step could end the game. Rob was then three of four, with his last target, Silke, 2 rounds away: he couldn't finish before Year 13. One public Storyteller's tip to Rob, his only one in Chapter 6, before the crown's prompt: the Reservoir is the last owed work and building it ends the game before the march; any player may call a revolt if two others agree a cause exists, and a player in debt is one (Paddy is in debt); and under Chaos no works are built until a new Emperor is acclaimed by grain, soldiers and friends. All rules and board; the table decides. Sent by message to the Storyteller mid-chapter, since a handoff would come too late.
- **Year 9, the tip given:** filed after the I Ching card as a note with no recipient, so every player heard it in their next prompt. Rob refused Clark's whispered 60 for the Deed that year.
- **Steering planned:** none beyond the notes in Chapter 1. From Chapter 2, at most one public Storyteller's tip per player per chapter, about coalitions (who has paid for a soldier and who is free-riding), from what the table can see. If Rob is two pieces short with no soldiers left by Year 5, a tip to him about reinforcements and the sea. Every tip is logged here when it's given.
