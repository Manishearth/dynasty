# Clark Grafton's diary

## Setup–Year 2

Two years in, and the Official owns nothing but a ledger and sixty-seven and a half grain, which is still more than anyone below the crown. I ran a clean count, corrected the Empress on her own tax, and set the price on the last Customs House high enough that Rich paid twenty-six for it. That was a fine bit of leverage until I remembered it also handed him his full set. Paddy outbid me on the Jar by one grain and Silke on the Bowl by one, and every piece in the kingdom is now sold, so my own set can only come from someone else's board. That means Rob's soldiers, or a deal. My whisper to Rob came back at me in public, neat handwriting and all, and that page goes under "lessons", not "grievances". Rich now has his set and the grain to come for my seat, Silke has hers and none of the grain, and Rob has three men and no reason to love me. So next year I make myself too useful to replace. I'll keep counting the tax honestly, sell what I know to whoever's frightened of Rich, and keep enough grain saved that when a set comes loose, it falls to me.

## Years 3–4

Years 3 and 4. I counted a lean tax and then a greedy one, offered Paddy thirty for a Jar he was right not to sell, and watched Rich climb Reina's steps to the throne in a single sentence. Then I won the Jar and the Bowl by a grain each, as I'd sworn I would, and put them in the back row out of Rob's reach, which turned out to be a lovely safe spot for Silke's pieces. She had the full set and twenty-five grain. I had fifty-five and a half, and I'd spent fifty-two of my own to give her an empty board and a fat purse to take. It's the same lesson as the Customs House I sold Rich, only dearer: the grain I spend getting ahead of someone is grain I've put on the table for them, and nobody watches a broke player closely enough. So the Official is now a peasant on the coast with sixty, no pieces, and a farmhouse Rob has already visited. Still, the Jar comes back into my own province's stock and is auctioned next year, so it's my lot to sell and my minimum to set. Silke needs exactly that Jar, and Reina needs it too, so I'll make them bid each other high for it. Rich has promised Province 5 its canal, and I mean to collect on that promise. I've kept the book, and Silke's page has started.

## Years 5–6

Years 5 and 6. I sold my own Jar twice and pocketed seventeen and a half for the trouble, and both times the Jar went straight into a set that took the throne, first Silke's and then Reina's. So the kingdom now has had three Emperors in two years, and I've been the one supplying the pottery. Two promises go under "kept": Rich's canal and Silke's, so Province 5 now has two sections and a harvest that, by the engine's count, outruns everyone else below the crown. I also bought four squares of sea at half price, which is the cheapest leverage I've ever had. I lost the Customs House to Paddy by a grain and the Scroll to him by five, so his "honest bidding" gets a page in the book of its own, but I took the Bowl from Rich at sixteen and set it on P26, which Rob tells me W2 can reach in three. Paddy is now one Jar from a set with eight grain to his name, and the Jar is back in my province's stock, so next year I set its minimum and he can't afford it. Rich is the Official with an empty board, two pieces short of me and with forty grain more, and I mean to have his seat. I need a Customs House and a Scroll, I need the Bowl to survive Rob's walk, and I need to stop spending my grain on other people's thrones.

## Years 7–8

Years 7 and 8. The crown paid for seven squares of my sea and half of five more, so Province 5 now brings in more on a bad harvest than most of this table brings in on a good one. I've got a hundred and seventy-two grain, and I finally stopped spending it on other people's thrones. I bought Rich's Customs House for thirteen and set it on P23 beside the Bowl, and I'm one Scroll from a set. Then I watched my own Jar go to Paddy for forty-six. I took eleven and a half for my trouble, and the Jar made him the Gentry, which makes four thrones and chairs my pottery has furnished. That's the same lesson for the third time, and the page it's on is getting crowded. Paddy's Scroll is back in Province 3's stock for next year, and it's the one piece I need, so this time I bid like a man with a hundred and seventy grain and nothing left to wait for. Rob has two men one step from my Customs House, and he warned me in public. The book records that as manners, and it doesn't make me forgive him. Silke is a peasant now with sixty grain, so her page gets a footnote: the landlord who wanted twenty from me is now my neighbour on the coast. When the Scroll comes, I'll take whichever chair is weakest, and I don't plan to spend a grain making anybody else's set easier.

## Years 9–10

Years 9 and 10. I finally bid like a man with grain and got Paddy's Scroll for thirty-one. That gave me a full set, and I spent it on the chair I'd wanted since Year 4. I traded the richest farm in China for Rich's nine grain and a ledger, and Rich went home to my coast and came back with five hundred and ten. Then he bought my own Customs House off me for sixty-one, and now he's one Scroll from a set, so this time the pottery I furnished him was a whole farm. I left my Scroll beside Rob's man after he'd told me, in public and twice, exactly what he'd do, and it's in his sand at M15 now. That page goes under "lessons", in my own handwriting, because the grievance is mine as much as his. So I'm the Official with a hundred and ninety-one and a half, a Bowl, and the honest count, and I'm short a Jar and a Scroll. Every Scroll left in China is in Rob's desert, and so is the only Jar anyone's selling, if he ever sells. Rob has turned me down twice, but he's a man who loves to talk about prices, and Rich needs a Scroll from that same desert. So I'll make it worth Rob's while to sell to me and not to the coast, and I'll keep Reina's tax so clean that she can't afford to replace me. For the record, the throne is still the only chair I haven't sat in.

## Years 11–12

Years 11 and 12, and that's the end of the book. I stopped at fifty-two for the Jar, and Rich paid a hundred and thirteen for it only so Silke couldn't have it, which is how the rich play and why I'm not one of them. Then I did the cleverest thing I've done in twelve years: I called the revolt, I was the only vote that counted, and I sold that vote for a Scroll and a doubled salary. I spent it on a man who'd read my whisper aloud twice. The Scroll went back to stock the moment he sat down, so he couldn't sell it to me, and he laid the last stone in China himself before I could collect the salary either. That's five thrones and chairs I've furnished now, and this one I furnished with the throne itself, so it goes under "lessons", in capitals, with a note that Rob kept every promise he made in public and not one of the ones he made to me. I finished where I started, the Official with eighty-six and a half and an honest count, which the rules call a hold and I call twelve years of setting other people's prices. Paddy backed me when I hadn't claimed anything, so the "friends" page has one entry, and I mean to keep it. The throne is still the only chair I haven't sat in, and by my count I'm the one who handed it to Rob.
