# Paddy Doyle's diary

## Years 1–2

Two years and my cart's come home empty both times. I bid honest and small the first year and bid firmer the second, and each time somebody with a fuller purse had already decided the piece was theirs. The only thing I've got out of it is my Bowls selling dear, twelve and then fifteen, though only a quarter of that comes back to me and the rest goes into Rich's pile. Then Noah, who kept saying "bid small, bid plain," walked off with the throne, and I'll give him this: he said sorry to Reina's face before he did it. I'm sixty-one grain richer than I'd feared and I've not wasted any of it, which is something, but there's one fewer of every piece now and the gentlemen have all the rest. Next year I'll pick two pieces and go after them properly instead of trying for all five, and I'll keep twenty back for a field when the land opens, because I'd sooner own a field than pay rent on one.

## Years 3–4

Years 3 and 4. I did what I said I'd do. I picked two pieces and went after them, and I got the Customs House for fifteen and the Brush for ten, cheaper than I'd braced for. Then the land opened and I put most of my grain into three fields by the shore, two of them half paid by the crown and the third paid by me in full, so from next year I'll be feeding myself and not lying awake over it. I'd only thirty-odd left for the auctions after that, and Reina beat me to the Deed and the Jar, fair and square. Then the throne changed hands three times in one evening, Reina, then Rich, then Clark, each of them saying sorry to the last as they sat down, and I sold Rich the Bowl for twenty-one that put him in the chair for about a minute. I've thirty-six and a half grain, two pieces on the board and three fields of my own coming in. The gentlemen hand the crown round among themselves like a teapot. Next year I'll want a Deed, a Jar and a Vase, and with the fields paying I might finally have the purse to stay in when the bidding climbs.

## Years 5–6

Years 5 and 6. I'll write it down slowly, because I hardly believe it myself. The seed rotted the first year, but the crown paid three parts of every field, so I put two more squares by the shore and took the Deed and the Vase for ten apiece, with nobody fighting me for the Vase at all. Then the granaries filled, I paid Reina her ten because she asked it kindly, and I paid twenty-six for a Jar that Rich only let go at twenty-five. In one evening I went from rice farmer to Official to Emperor of China. I took Rich's chair because he'd stood against me to my face, and Clark's because a Bowl I'd sold for three years came round and finished the set. I sat on the throne with two hundred and five grain for about as long as it takes to pour a cup of tea, and then Graham bought his Vase and took it off me. I'd told him I'd understand, and I do. So the teapot came round the table to me after all, and I've ended up the Scholar with sixty-five grain, the five-grain rate and nothing on the shelf. I've lost my fields by the shore, and that's the part that stings a bit. Still, I'm better off than the empty cart I started with, and I know the way up now. Next I'll keep the book honest, read the cards plain and save towards a set again, and I'll not make anybody a promise I can't keep, Graham least of all.

## Years 7–8

Years 7 and 8. I turned the cards and read them plain, the good one for my province first, which was a joke on a man with no fields to shine, and then the one that made us three hundred million, which wasn't a joke at all. I went after the Customs House and the Deed and came up short both years, by one grain to Noah and then by a few to Rich. I've nothing on the shelf, but I've seventy-two and a half grain and I didn't throw any of it after pieces I couldn't finish. My Brushes are all sold now, the last one to Clark for thirteen, and only a quarter of that ever came home to me. The part I'm cross with myself about is the land: I let two years of crown shares go past without buying a single square, and at times three a Scholar with no fields is losing ground just by sitting still. Then this morning I watched Reina pay a hundred and one for a Bowl to keep it from Noah, and set three hundred on a Jar, and I thought that the teapot's got so hot nobody can pick it up. Next year Rich and Silke both need that Jar and it opens at two, so I'll stay out of the scalding. I'll get some land planted the moment the crown offers a share, I'll keep the book honest, and I'll wait for the gentlemen to burn their fingers.

## Year 9

Year 9. I turned up the Bad Harvest card myself, and it hit me harder than anyone: with no fields to my name the pile took seventy off me, and I sat down to the auction with two and a half grain, which isn't a purse, it's lint. I put two on Clark's Customs House and he'd set it at three, so I couldn't even buy a ticket. Then the teapot went round the table faster than I've ever seen it. Silke paid two hundred and one for a Jar and took the throne, Noah bought the Bowl and took my stool off me, and then he needed the Vase, which I suddenly had to sell, so he bought it from me and took the throne off Silke before her tea had cooled. He said sorry to her face, and he said a kind word to me as well, and I believe he meant both. So now I'm a wheat farmer with sixty-five grain and twenty squares of good land with a reservoir on them, which is more ground than I've ever stood on in this game. All I had to do for it was lose my job. Next year I'll let the land feed me, pay my rent and tax without a fuss, and keep my grain back until a piece comes up at a price a farmer can stand. It's the land I've regretted every year, and this year I didn't even have to buy it.

## Years 10–11

Years 10 and 11. The land did what I always hoped it would. Two good harvests came in, four hundred and forty the second year, and I went from lint in my pocket to four hundred and sixty-three grain, more than I've ever held in this game, and I paid every rent I was asked with a clear conscience. I bought fifteen squares at a farmer's share, half the first year and a quarter the second, so the Pile and the crown weren't left holding the whole bill. I bought a Customs House for twenty-six, and that was the one bit of the auctions that went my way. After that I lost the Deed, the Brush and the Bowl to Graham by a single grain each time, which is one grain short three times in one night, and I'm only half laughing about it. Then Rich paid four hundred for a Jar and took the throne off Noah, and Graham sat there with a full set and six hundred grain and wouldn't get up, and I've never seen a quieter man look so dangerous. Next year I'll stop bidding round numbers, because everyone at this table has learned to bid one over a round number, and I'll go after the Deed, Brush and Bowl with a few grain on top. The Jar can wait until the purses are thinner, and I'll keep my eye on Graham.

## Years 12–13

Years 12 and 13. I did what I said I'd do and stopped bidding round numbers, and Reina beat me by one grain on the Deed and again on the Brush, so it turns out I'd only taught the table a new number. I got Reina's Bowl at her opening price of forty, and it sits in the wheat beside my Customs House, which leaves me two pieces and three short when the game ended. Graham put an eight-hundred wall round his Jar and nobody could climb it, so neither he nor Reina ever finished a set, and in the end that was the piece that held all of us still. I paid Noah his fifty and I'd have paid Graham his hundred too. The fields gave me eight hundred in the floods, and I'd bought the next ring, eleven squares at a farmer's share. Then Rich laid his Pagoda under the Wall and that was the last of it, with no auction and no last chance. I finish a wheat farmer with one thousand three hundred and seven grain, more than four of the gentlemen, after starting with an empty cart and a handful of Bowls I couldn't sell dear. I never did get my set, and I was Emperor for about one cup of tea, but I kept my word to every one of them and I owned my field, which is what I said I wanted at the start. If I sat down again tomorrow I'd buy the land in the first year it was offered, and I'd watch Reina's numbers as closely as she watched mine.
