# Narration

The narrator's lines, chapter by chapter: the chapter title, each year's opening line, and the turning points, each tagged with the log number it follows. The narrator isn't part of the game, so none of this goes into `game.py` or the playthrough page; the page must make sense without it.

## Chapter 1: Whoever Waits Loses the Throne

- **(start, Year 1)** The first year of Reina's reign, and the quietest table the chronicle has kept: no army in the desert, no one to blame but each other. Sixty each, and a thousand and thirty owed in works.
- **(004)** The Summer Palace goes up in the far corner of Province 1, at J24. One work down; a thousand and ten to go.
- **(010)** Six lots and not one bid above six. The table has agreed, without anyone saying so, to be poor slowly.
- **(012)** Clark and Noah take three pieces each, Clark three times on the strength of sitting one chair earlier. Four players go home with empty shelves and full purses.
- **(start, Year 2)** The second year of Reina's reign. The card says drought and the harvest says average; the crown digs a canal and keeps the rate at thirty.
- **(022)** The bids double. Noah asks for two pieces and nothing else; Clark asks for the other two and says so out loud.
- **(025)** Two years and one auction lot after her coronation, Reina is the Scholar. Noah takes the throne with three lots unsold behind him and an apology in front.
- **(027)** Fifteen for a Bowl, fifteen for a Vase, from the newest Scholar at the table. Clark ends the year exactly where he began it: two pieces short.
- **(028)** Ten each, as declared. The crown changes hands and the salaries don't. Three peasants have now sat through twelve lots without winning one.

## Chapter 2: Four Emperors and a Vase

- **(start, Year 3)** The first full year of Noah's reign. The card is a utility levy that only the crown collects, and the crown has more grain than any two of its subjects together.
- **(039)** A reservoir for Province 7, paid in full by the crown and set in the corner where it is least in the way. Eight hundred and seventy owed.
- **(046)** Thirteen lots without a peasant winner, and then three in a row: a Customs House and a Brush to Paddy, a Deed to Graham.
- **(048)** Seventy-seven grain for a Bowl and a Vase, split between the Official and the Gentry, one each. Each now holds four of five, and each is waiting on a different peasant's stock.
- **(start, Year 4)** The second year of Noah's reign, and the first of two hundred million mouths. The harvest is good, the consumption is doubled, and the sea along the coast is for sale at twenty a square.
- **(063)** Ten squares of new land in one year, four provinces buying. Province 4 buys none: its only two squares of coast now belong to the crown.
- **(070)** Reina takes the throne back with two lots still unsold, two years after losing it the same way. The chronicle notes that the Bowl and the Vase are still on the table.
- **(073)** One lot later, Rich takes it from her. The reign of Reina the Second lasts a single Wooden Bowl.
- **(075)** And one lot after that, Clark. Four Emperors in one year, three of them in one auction; the fifteen pieces they paid for go back into stock.
- **(076)** The year ends with the crown at 280 and four of the seven chairs swapped. Eight hundred and fifty still owed in works, and every new Emperor has said sorry.

## Chapter 3: One Rung at a Time

- **(start, Year 5)** The first year of Clark's reign, and the first lean one at two hundred million. The three peasants with fields are up thirty; the three gentlemen without them are down thirty, and the crown sits on two hundred and seventy.
- **(093)** By the end of public works the crown has paid for two links of Wall, four squares of its own sea and fifteen of every twenty on nine squares for the peasants. Two hundred and ninety-seven at the start of the step; twelve at the end.
- **(102)** The cheapest auction in three years: six lots for sixty-nine grain. Graham takes three of them, and three is all he needed.
- **(103)** Five years, thirty lots, and a peasant climbs. Graham carries four pieces north to the Scholar's province; Noah, who has now held three different chairs, walks south to the wheat.
- **(104)** Salaries at the legal minimum, and the crown closes the year at two grain. The chronicle has never kept a smaller treasury.
- **(start, Year 6)** The first year of Clark's reign with a full granary. The card is fortune for all and misfortune for three, and the man who turns it was growing wheat a year ago.
- **(109)** The fourth link goes up above Province 3. Six years after the first coin was struck, Reina plants the first field under the Wall.
- **(121)** The Jar goes to Paddy at twenty-six, one grain over a keep-from. Rich has said all chapter that the Rice Peasant is the man who matters; the Rice Peasant takes his chair.
- **(126)** One lot later, Paddy's new seat needs only a Bowl, and the Bowl is the next lot. The Rice Peasant who sold Bowls for five years buys one and sits on the throne.
- **(128)** And after the last lot, Graham. Two peasants started the year at the bottom of the table; both have been Emperor by the end of it, one for a single Vase.

## Chapter 4: One Grain Over

- **(start, Year 7)** The first year of Graham's reign. Heaven has favourites, the Scholar reads, and three provinces shine while four sulk; the reserve stands at 359.5, and 690 of the works are still owed.
- **(141)** Four links of stone go up above Province 1 in one stroke, 160 from the crown. Province 2's strip, under no stone at all, stays shut.
- **(145)** Three-quarters of every square, to anyone who asks. Twenty-four squares later the crown has 3.5 grain and fifteen of salaries to pay.
- **(154)** Silke's Bowl at 29 and Vase at 32, each one grain over the last bidder standing. She said in the third year that Reina's seat was the one she could take; in the seventh she takes it.
- **(start, Year 8)** The second year of Graham's reign, and the villages are noisier: three hundred million mouths, a good harvest for all, and a crown that starts the year at half a grain.
- **(161)** Fourteen back off the top, and four links of stone over the collector's head. The Great Wall is finished above all three northern provinces, eight years after the first stone.
- **(173)** Three hundred for a Jar nobody meets, and a hundred and one for a Bowl, one grain over a number said out loud. Two players end the year one Jar short, and every Jar in China belongs to the woman whose chair one of them took.
- **(178)** Noah lends the crown three and calls Reina's old debt settled. The chronicle closes the account opened in the fourth year.

## Chapter 5: Whoever Waits

- **(start, Year 9)** The third year of Graham's reign, and the crown opens it with half a grain. Three hundred million mouths, one Jar on the table at two, and two players who need nothing else.
- **(203)** Two hundred and one for a Jar that opened at two, one grain over Rich. Silke said in the eighth year that she would buy it and then take the throne; she does both before the Bowl is sold.
- **(208)** And two lots later, Noah: wheat to Scholar on a Bowl, Scholar to the throne on the Vase he had priced himself an hour before. Silke's reign lasts a Bowl and a Vase; Noah's first ended seven years ago in the same auction-room way.

## Chapter 6: The Price Rises with the Chair

- **(start, Year 10)** The second reign of Noah, tenth year of the dynasty. Six hundred million mouths and a good harvest, and the Harvest Pile pays out eighty more than it holds.
- **(220)** Seventy for a Scholar's debt and a hundred and sixty for four sections of canal, promised in the third year. Noah pays both in the same breath and keeps the arithmetic out loud.
- **(234)** Two hundred and fifty for the Jar, at the seller's own minimum. Rich has needed only that piece since the eighth year; he takes it, and then the coast of the woman who sold it.
- **(237)** One lot later, a Bowl at twenty-six, and the rice farmer of an hour ago is the landlord he was in the first year. Three seats in two lots, and nine hundred and forty-six grain in one purse.
- **(start, Year 11)** The second year of the second doubling, and the horsemen come over the Wall. Six hundred million mouths, a good harvest, and a silk coast that alone draws a thousand and twenty from a Pile holding a thousand and eighty.
- **(258)** Four hundred for a Jar at the seller's own minimum, and the throne one lot later. Rice farmer, silk farmer, landlord, Emperor: four seats for Rich in two auctions, and a sorry at each door.
- **(260)** Graham buys the four pieces he lacks at one grain over the field each time, and then does not stand up. The chronicle notes a complete set left on the table, and seven hundred-odd in the new Emperor's reserve.

## Chapter 7: The Mortar Ready

- **(start, Year 12)** The first year of Rich's reign, twelfth of the dynasty. A doubling card turns up with nowhere left to double, so it is only a good harvest, and the Pile pays out two and a half thousand more than it holds.
- **(274)** Thirty grain of water for Clark's wheat, and the works owed fall to one: a Pagoda the crown builds alone, in a year when nothing else is built.
- **(284)** Eight hundred for a Jar, and nobody bids. Reina buys three pieces, two of them one grain over Paddy, and is one Jar short; the only Jars in China sit unsold on the silk coast.
- **(289)** Graham stands up at last, a year after finishing the set, and takes the Gentry's chair from Noah without a sorry. Noah inherits the silk coast and the four Jars, the one Graham and Reina each still need.
- **(start, Year 13)** The second year of Rich's reign, and the floods. Twenty grain of works owed, a Jar due on the counter at two, and two players each one Jar short of a chair.
- **(294)** The Marble Pagoda goes up on G20, at the crown's own cost, before the auction. Every work in the book stands, the empire survives, and the Jar stays in its box on the silk coast.
