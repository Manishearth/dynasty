## At the table since your last turn

Rich: "Customs House at two again, friends; I don't overcharge the people I tax. Paddy, you told Silke to her face you're after a Jar, so I'll be just as honest with you: I need one too, and I'm not standing aside for it. Nothing personal. You're a fine tenant and a finer bidder, and I'd sooner lose it to you dear than give it to you cheap."

Graham: "One piece, friends, and I'll say which: the Vase, and nothing else. Paddy, I won't bid against you on the Jar, and I'd ask the same courtesy back, as one man who came up from the fields to another: a set that's finished doesn't have to come for the chair I'm sitting in. My Brush goes up at eight."

Silke: "Paddy, you said it to my face and I respect that, so here it is to yours: the Jar is twenty-one this year. You're one piece from a chair, and that's what a chair costs. And Noah, you've got ninety-nine grain and an empty shelf, so I expect we'll be bidding against each other on the Customs House."

Paddy: "I'll put my cards face up, since everybody can count anyway. I'm bidding on the Jar and nothing else this year, and I'll stay in a good long while on it. Anybody who's only after it to keep it off me will be feeding the Harvest Pile, not themselves. The Bowl's at ten again, same as last year, and it's a fair price."

Noah: "Here's my arithmetic, so nobody has to guess it. I have 99 grain, I need five pieces, and I need 20 left over at the end. That leaves about 79 to spread across five lots, so you'll see me on all of them and you'll see me stop. Paddy, you told us you want the Jar, and I'll be honest back: so do I. I won't pretend it's worth more to me than it is. My Vase goes up at eight."

The minimums: Customs House 2, Land Deed 2, Brush 8, Dragon Jar 21, Wooden Bowl 10, Vase 8.

Noah: "Silke beat me to the Customs House and the Deed by one grain each, and she bid fair for both. So the Brush is the one piece I can take home this year, and I'll go to sixteen for it. Rich, if you go past that, I'll shake your hand and the Pile can have the rest."

Rich: "Noah, you said you'd stop, and I believe you, you're a man of plain numbers. Mine's plainer: there are two Brushes left in this whole empire, and an Official with no Brush stays an Official forever. With apologies, I'll be staying in a little longer."

The auctions so far: Silke takes the Customs House at 16 (Noah stopped at 15) and the Land Deed at 16 (Noah 15). In the live raise on the Brush, Noah raises to 16 and Rich to 15, so Noah takes it at 16. Paddy takes the Dragon Jar at 26 (Rich's keep-from stopped at 25). On the limits, Silke would take the Wooden Bowl at 16 and Graham the Vase at 16.

Silke: "Noah, one grain each time, and you bid them fair. I'll take the handshake you offered Rich. Paddy, twenty-six for the Jar, and a quarter of it goes into the coast, so thank you twice. And Rich, look at that: the Brush went at sixteen, an even number, and the man in front of you still won it."

Noah: "The Brush goes on Q15, in the middle of the wheat. Sixteen to the auction: four to Graham and twelve to the Pile, and that leaves me 83. Rich, you said you'd stay in and then you stopped at fifteen, and I'm not holding it against you. It's one piece, and I've got four still to find."

Paddy: "Well, I'll be. Rich, you told me to my face you weren't standing aside, and you didn't, and you lost it dear, which is just what you said you'd sooner do. So I'll be as plain with you as you were with me: I'm taking the Official's chair. There's nothing personal in it either. Graham, you kept your word on the Jar, so I've kept clear of yours. And I'll count the tax the way Rich did, twice and to the half-grain, because that's the only way I know how to count."

Paddy shows the Rice Peasant's complete set and 47 grain after lot 5 and replaces Rich as the Official. He takes Rich's 56 (103 in all), returns the Customs House to Province 2's stock and carries the Brush, Deed, Jar and Vase to Province 2; as Official he now needs only a Wooden Bowl. Rich draws 60 and plays the Rice Peasant, so lot 6, the Bowl, is now his to sell. Paddy and Rich give limits on the last two lots.

Paddy: "Rich, there's your Bowl to sell now, and you'll get a fair quarter from me if I can manage it. I'm going after it, and I'll not pretend otherwise. The Vase is Graham's to fight over. I've one already and I've no use for two."

Rich: "Well, Paddy, you were plain with me and I was plain with you, and here we are. Congratulations, sincerely; I told this table the Rice Peasant was the man who mattered, and I was right, only not the way I meant. The Bowl's yours to buy now, all of you, at ten, and I'll take whatever a humble rice farmer is offered for it. Graham, I need a Vase too now, so I'll make you earn yours."

Paddy: "Clark, I said once I'd be no enemy of yours over a Bowl, and I meant it then. It's a Bowl that's done it all the same, and I'm sorry, truly. I'll take the chair. Graham, you've the Vase coming to you and I'll not stand in the way of it. If you come for this chair I'll understand, because I came for it myself. Rich, there's twenty-two for your Bowl, the fairest money I ever spent."

(Storyteller's note: Rich keeps a quarter of the 22 for the Bowl, 5.5; the other 16.5 went to the Harvest Pile.)

Paddy takes the Wooden Bowl at 22 (Silke stopped at 21), completing the Official's set, and takes the throne from Clark after lot 6. He returns all five pieces to stock and takes the reserve, 124 (205 in all). Clark draws 60 and plays the Official, in Province 2; he gives a limit on the Vase. On the limits so far, Graham takes the Vase at 21 (Rich stopped at 20).

Clark: "Paddy, you said you'd be no enemy of mine over a Bowl, and then a Bowl took my chair. I'm not angry, I'm making a note of it. Graham, the Vase is yours. I won't spend sixty grain to protect a throne that isn't mine any more. I'll count the tax honestly, Your Majesty, because that's the job, and I'll expect the salary to show it."

Graham: "Paddy, you kept clear of my Vase and I kept clear of your Jar, and you said you'd understand if I came for the chair. I'm coming for it. Three years ago I was paying fifteen for a Deed nobody wanted. Now I'll keep the book, and I'll write it in ink. Clark, you'll get the salary the count earns, and Noah, the Scholar's chair is a good place to start from. I'd know."

Graham takes the Vase at 21 (Rich stopped at 20), completing the Scholar's set, and takes the throne from Paddy after the last lot. He returns all five pieces to stock and takes the reserve, 205 (264.5 in all). Paddy draws 60 and plays the Scholar, in Province 4.

(Storyteller's note: Paddy, not Noah, takes the Scholar's chair: the displaced Emperor plays the seat the mover left. Noah stays the Wheat Peasant.)

Graham: "Clark declared ten for the Official and five for the Scholar this year, and a declaration from the throne doesn't change because a new man is sitting in it. Clark, here's your ten, the same as you promised Rich. Paddy, five, the chair's rate, which is what I was paid in it, and it'll rise when the book says so."

Salaries: Graham pays the Official, Clark, 10 and the Scholar, Paddy, 5, as Clark declared.

End of Years 5 and 6. Seats now: Graham Emperor (249.5 in the reserve after salaries), Clark Official (70), Reina Gentry (43.5), Paddy Scholar (65), Silke Silk Peasant (81), Rich Rice Peasant (65.5), Noah Wheat Peasant (88). Silke holds a Customs House, Deed and Brush; Noah a Brush; nobody else holds a piece. 690 of the works are still owed; Province 3's Wall is finished.
