diary: Two years, and I bought nothing at the auction table. I kept my word to myself: I bid small, I paid the Harvest Pile nothing, and I got my Wall. Ten grain the first year and twenty the second, Clark's stones with my name on them, and G9 and G10 planted underneath. I'm a landlord with land at last, earning thirty-five in an average year instead of losing five. Then I sat and watched three chairs change hands in a single round. Paddy finished the rice set and took Rich's seat, a Bowl took Clark's throne, and a Vase handed it to Graham, all inside one auction. My keep-from on the Jar never mattered, because Rich was the one who fed it. Clark is the Official now with nothing, so the man who built my Wall has no Wall left to build, and I owe him and will remember it. Silke paid me five when I asked fifteen and told me exactly why, and I respect that more than I like it. Noah paid the full fifteen and told me the old debt is still open, so his name goes in the good column. Graham is on the throne now, a peasant three years ago, with two hundred and fifty in the reserve and a book he says he'll keep in ink. Next comes land before the counter doubles, rent that tracks the harvest, and a cheap Brush while there are any left. I've learned to watch the shelves, and now I'm learning to wait.
