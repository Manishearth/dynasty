diary: Years Five and Six, and the good book closed both years on a page about my own foolishness. In Year Five I emptied the treasury into everyone else's fields, fifteen grain a square, seventy for Reina's Wall, and ended the year with two grain. While I was paying for their seed, Graham bought five pieces at the wheat-price and walked off with the Scholar's chair. In Year Six I taxed properly, finished Reina's Wall and gave Rich his ten, and then Paddy, who told me once he'd be no enemy of mine over a Bowl, bought a Bowl for twenty-two and took the throne. He sat in it for exactly one lot before Graham took it from him. So I'm the Official now, with seventy grain, not one piece on the shelf, and an unbuilt Wall over my head, collecting tax for a man I gave a Reservoir and fifteen grain a square. Paddy Doyle has a fresh page in the other book. Graham has one too, though he kept my salary promise to the grain, so his name goes in the good book in pencil. Next time I don't spend a grain on anyone who can count to five pieces. I count the tax honestly, I make Graham build the Wall above me, and I collect the five pieces quietly, the way the peasants did, because it turns out this chair goes to whoever is patient, not whoever is generous.
