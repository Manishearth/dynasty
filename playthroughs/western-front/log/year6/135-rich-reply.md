diary: Two years ago I was a landlord, then I was an Emperor for one lot, then a tax-man, and tonight I'm a rice farmer on the south-east coast. That's three steps down, and every one of them was taken from me by a man who could count better than I was watching. I counted Clark's tax twice and to the half-grain, I paid my own share first where he could see it, and he wrote me down in pencil on the good page. Then Paddy took my chair, and the good page went with it. I told Paddy to his face I'd make him pay full price for the Jar, and I did, twenty-five to his twenty-six. He said I'd been plain with him, so he'd be plain with me, and he took the chair anyway. It was the fairest robbery I've ever suffered, and I find I can't hold it against him. I lost the Brush to Noah by one grain, which makes it twice now I've been beaten by exactly one, and I mean to stop bidding numbers other men can guess. I did keep one promise to myself: I made Graham pay twenty-one for his Vase instead of sixteen, and he still went from the wheat to the throne in two years, which I'd never have believed of the quiet man who paid fifteen for a Deed nobody wanted. So here I am with sixty-five grain, six squares of good shore, a Bowl's quarter in my pocket, and a landlord, Reina, who keeps a long book and likes being thanked out loud, so I'll thank her loudly. The Rice Peasant needs a Customs House, a Deed, a Brush, a Jar and a Vase, and the first man I mean to replace is whoever sits in a chair I've already sat in, because I know exactly what each of them costs now.
