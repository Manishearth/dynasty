diary: Two years ago my shelf was empty. Now I have a Customs House, a Deed and a Brush in a row down the middle of the coast, and every one of them cost me an odd number. Seven new squares went in while the crown was paying most of the price, and this year's harvest proved they were right: a hundred and sixty in, sixty-four out to Rich's count, and only five to Reina, because she charged me five in the lean year and I don't forget either. I lost the Bowl to Paddy by one grain, at twenty-one to his twenty-two, and an hour later Graham, who was a wheat farmer the year before, sat on the throne. Paddy climbed past Rich, Graham climbed past Paddy, and Clark has gone back to counting tax for whoever's sitting in the chair. I set the Jar at twenty-one to make Paddy pay for his chair, and it cost him twenty-six, and the first thing he did with the chair was lose it. What it cost me is that I'm still a peasant, two pieces short. But the Bowl and the Vase I need have both just gone back to stock, I have eighty-one grain and the biggest harvest of any peasant at this table, and every one of them has learned that the chair goes to whoever finishes the set, not whoever has been waiting longest. Next year I finish mine.
