## At the table since your last turn

Noah: "The Brush goes on Q15, in the middle of the wheat. Sixteen to the auction: four to Graham and twelve to the Pile, and that leaves me 83. Rich, you said you'd stay in and then you stopped at fifteen, and I'm not holding it against you. It's one piece, and I've got four still to find."

Paddy: "Well, I'll be. Rich, you told me to my face you weren't standing aside, and you didn't, and you lost it dear, which is just what you said you'd sooner do. So I'll be as plain with you as you were with me: I'm taking the Official's chair. There's nothing personal in it either. Graham, you kept your word on the Jar, so I've kept clear of yours. And I'll count the tax the way Rich did, twice and to the half-grain, because that's the only way I know how to count."

Paddy shows the Rice Peasant's complete set and 47 grain after lot 5 and replaces Rich as the Official. He takes Rich's 56 (103 in all), returns the Customs House to Province 2's stock and carries the Brush, Deed, Jar and Vase to Province 2; as Official he now needs only a Wooden Bowl. Rich draws 60 and plays the Rice Peasant, so lot 6, the Bowl, is now his to sell. Paddy and Rich give limits on the last two lots.

Paddy: "Rich, there's your Bowl to sell now, and you'll get a fair quarter from me if I can manage it. I'm going after it, and I'll not pretend otherwise. The Vase is Graham's to fight over. I've one already and I've no use for two."

Rich: "Well, Paddy, you were plain with me and I was plain with you, and here we are. Congratulations, sincerely; I told this table the Rice Peasant was the man who mattered, and I was right, only not the way I meant. The Bowl's yours to buy now, all of you, at ten, and I'll take whatever a humble rice farmer is offered for it. Graham, I need a Vase too now, so I'll make you earn yours."

Paddy: "Clark, I said once I'd be no enemy of yours over a Bowl, and I meant it then. It's a Bowl that's done it all the same, and I'm sorry, truly. I'll take the chair. Graham, you've the Vase coming to you and I'll not stand in the way of it. If you come for this chair I'll understand, because I came for it myself. Rich, there's twenty-two for your Bowl, the fairest money I ever spent."

On the limits so far, Graham takes the Vase at 21 (Rich stopped at 20).

Clark: "Paddy, you said you'd be no enemy of mine over a Bowl, and then a Bowl took my chair. I'm not angry, I'm making a note of it. Graham, the Vase is yours. Graham: "Paddy, you kept clear of my Vase and I kept clear of your Jar, and you said you'd understand if I came for the chair. I'm coming for it.
