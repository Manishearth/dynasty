diary: Years 5 and 6. It was the bad harvest that did it. Everyone else came to the auction broke, and I came with fifty-nine, so I got the Customs House, the Jar and the Bowl for forty grain together and sat down in Noah's chair with his forty-two in my pocket. He'd said sorry to us peasants once, and I'd written it down. A year later I paid twenty-one for my own old Vase out of Noah's hand and took the throne from Paddy, who'd held it for the length of one lot. He kept his word about the Jar and I kept mine, and then I took his chair anyway, and he said he'd understand. I believe him, and I'm writing that down too. It was three rungs in two years, and I paid this table's price for every one. Now I sit on 249 grain with 690 of works owed, and every man below me is counting the way I used to count. Silke has three pieces, Paddy knows the climb, and Noah is sitting on the wheat I fed with my own squares. I'll pay salaries in ink, keep promises to the grain, build the Wall where it buys me friends, and watch the Vase auctions closer than anything else.
