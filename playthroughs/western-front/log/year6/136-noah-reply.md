diary: In Year 5 I read a bad-harvest card and asked out loud how the crown would pay salaries out of 12 grain. Then Graham, the man I built a reservoir for, put five pieces together in one night, took my stool, and handed me his wheat. I didn't mind as much as I expected to. He came up from the fields fair and square, and I can't say the same about how I took the throne. I paid Reina the full fifteen in rent this year, but I didn't pretend it settled anything. I still owe her a "make it right", and saying so out loud is the least I can do until I find a way to pay it. At the auction Silke beat me by one grain on the Customs House and again on the Deed. I took the Brush for sixteen and put it on Q15. Then I watched the throne change hands twice in the last two lots, and both times the winner was a peasant who had counted more carefully than the rest of us. So now I'm the Wheat Peasant with 88 grain, eight squares of land and a reservoir, and I hold one piece. I still need four pieces and 20 grain. I'll hold to the slow arithmetic: fair prices, the most I'm willing to pay said out loud, and no war over a piece just to stop somebody else getting it.
