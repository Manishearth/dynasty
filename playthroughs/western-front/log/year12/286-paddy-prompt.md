## At the table since your last turn

Clark: "Customs House at three, the toll it's always been, and I won't be charging the table eight hundred for it. Graham, a Jar at that price is a wall, not a piece for sale. I'll write it down that way, and I'll leave the climbing to whoever has the boots for it."

Noah: "The arithmetic's short. I've got two hundred and thirty-eight, Graham's Jar starts at eight hundred, and there's only the Pagoda left before the end. I can't make a set this year, and buying four pieces I can't finish would only pour three quarters of my grain into the Pile for nothing. So I'm passing on all of it. My Deed goes up at two, and anybody who needs it can have it for that."

Silke: "I'm a hundred and ten under, so I pass on all five lots. You can't bid with grain you haven't got. My Brush opens at fifteen. Last year it went for forty-one, so fifteen is a fair price, and a quarter of whatever it fetches goes against my debt. Rich, thank you for the hundred and fifteen. That's the second year I've written your name in the good column."

Reina: "Eight hundred for a Jar, Graham? That's not a price, it's a locked door, and I don't think anyone here has the key this year. I'll bid on a few things for the shelf and leave the climbing to people with more grain than me. My Bowls go up at forty, and they're good wood."
