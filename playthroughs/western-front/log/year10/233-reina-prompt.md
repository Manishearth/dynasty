## At the table since your last turn

Clark: "Customs House at three, the same toll as always. Everyone at this table just got new fields from the crown, so I expect you'll all be very generous tonight. I'm only reminding you that Graham's Deed opens at two, and a man with seventy-two grain can count to two."

Graham: "Thank you, all three of you. Eighty, counted, and I've written each of you down for paying it. I won't lie to the table about the auctions: I'm back on the bottom rung with a hundred and fifty-six, and I'll bid on every piece I'm short, but nothing at three hundred. I paid the table's price once, and I can do it again."

Silke: "Six lots and I'm not bidding on any of them, because the rules won't let me, and frankly my purse wouldn't either. My Brush goes up at nine. Whoever wants it, a quarter of what you pay comes back to me, and it all goes to clearing a debt that a good harvest put on me. Noah, thank you for the eighty, I'll hold you to it at year's end. Rich, Reina, Paddy: you've all just got cheap land, so when the Jar comes round, remember that every grain over the minimum goes into Graham's empty Pile."

Rich: "I've no quarrel with any lot tonight but one, and everybody here knows which. Reina, I've lost that Jar by one grain more times than a man should admit out loud, so this year I'm not bidding a number anybody can guess. I'm bidding the whole purse, less what it costs to sit down. Everyone else, take the Customs Houses and the Deeds with my blessing."

Paddy: "I've land under me for once, so I'll start the set again from the bottom: the Customs House, the Deed and the Brush, at a farmer's prices. The Jar and the Bowl I'll leave to whoever wants to burn their hands on them this year. And the Vase opens at ten, which is fair for a piece that's put two of you on the throne."
