diary: In Year 7 I bought the Customs House, the Deed and the Jar for forty-three grain all told, and lost the Bowl to Silke by one grain on a number I'd said out loud. She took the Gentry's chair with it, and she'd earned it. In Year 8 I had two hundred grain and only one piece left to buy. I told the table I'd go to a hundred and not a grain more, and Reina paid a hundred and one, sixty-six of it only to keep the Bowl from me. It was the fourth time I'd lost by one grain, and this time I'd handed her the number myself. I told her I'd call our old debt settled, and I meant it. I took her chair once by not waiting, and she took a piece from me by counting, so I'd call that fair. I lent the crown three grain without interest so Clark and Paddy got what they were promised, because a promise made from the throne ought to be kept even when it's somebody else's. Now I've got two hundred and two grain and four pieces on the board, and Rich has two Bowls left to sell. Next year I'll still bid with my arithmetic said out loud, but I may not say the top of it until I've seen who else is bidding. Being honest is one thing, but I've learned you don't have to hand the other fellow your last grain to stay honest.
