diary: Years 7 and 8. I turned the cards and read them plain, the good one for my province first, which was a joke on a man with no fields to shine, and then the one that made us three hundred million, which wasn't a joke at all. I went after the Customs House and the Deed and came up short both years, by one grain to Noah and then by a few to Rich. I've nothing on the shelf, but I've seventy-two and a half grain and I didn't throw any of it after pieces I couldn't finish. My Brushes are all sold now, the last one to Clark for thirteen, and only a quarter of that ever came home to me. The part I'm cross with myself about is the land: I let two years of crown shares go past without buying a single square, and at times three a Scholar with no fields is losing ground just by sitting still. Then this morning I watched Reina pay a hundred and one for a Bowl to keep it from Noah, and set three hundred on a Jar, and I thought that the teapot's got so hot nobody can pick it up. Next year Rich and Silke both need that Jar and it opens at two, so I'll stay out of the scalding. I'll get some land planted the moment the crown offers a share, I'll keep the book honest, and I'll wait for the gentlemen to burn their fingers.
