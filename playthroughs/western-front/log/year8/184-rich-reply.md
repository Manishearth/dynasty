diary: Two years ago I had nothing in my hands but a Bowl's quarter and a grudge against the number one, and tonight I've got a Brush, a Customs House, a Deed and a Vase in a neat row down the middle of my rice fields, and I'm one Jar short of a chair. I won three of them at sixteen, each by a single grain, so I've paid that debt back to myself in full. I paid Silke her twenty with a smile because I said rent rises with the land, and I said it out loud in the first year from her chair, and a man who keeps his word from both sides of the counter is worth something. I made Silke pay thirty-two for her Vase, and I put a sixty-one minimum on my Bowl to keep Noah honest, and Reina paid a hundred and one for it just to stop him, which put twenty-five grain in my purse that I never had to bid for. I'm sorry for Noah, truly; he names his number every year like a gentleman, and the table eats him for it. The trouble now is plain arithmetic. There's one Jar coming up first next year at two, Silke wants it with two hundred and fifty-seven behind her, I've got ninety-five, and Reina sells it. I can't outbid the Gentry, so I'll have to outthink her, or find whatever it is Reina wants more than she wants to see Silke climb. The Emperor's sitting on half a grain and borrowing his salaries from the peasants, and a man with one piece to go ought to remember that the chair above Silke's is the one that's wobbling.
