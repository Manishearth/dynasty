## At the table since your last turn

Clark: "Three for the Customs House, as always. It's a toll. I've got twenty-seven grain and five new squares, so I'm a spectator with a notebook this year. Noah, Silke, you each need one piece, and you can fight each other for the pile's benefit. I'll be writing down who paid what."

Paddy: "I'll be honest with the table: I'm the poorest man here bar the crown, so I'm bidding like it. A small go at the Customs House and the Deed, and nothing else. The last Brush in China starts at ten, because there's only the one, and whoever buys it knows what it's worth to them."

Reina: "Silke, you told me to set my price, and you set yours high for six years, so here it is: the Jar starts at three hundred. That's not spite. It's the same lesson you taught me from the other side of the counter. And Noah, I know which piece you're after. You still owe me, and I'm afraid this is how I'm collecting this year."

Rich: "Noah, everybody at this table knows which piece you're after, and it happens to be the one I'm selling, so I'll say it plainly: the Bowl starts at sixty-one. Three-quarters of it goes to the pile, and it feeds us all. Silke, I'm wishing you luck on that Jar, Your Ladyship, though I'll stand in line for one myself."

Noah: "I'm after one piece this year and I'll tell you my number for it, same as always: a hundred for the Bowl and not a grain more. I've got two hundred and one, I keep twenty back, and everything I spend past what the Bowl is worth only goes to fill the pile. Reina, if you've got a price in mind for standing aside, say it in front of everybody, and I'll tell you honestly whether I can pay it."

The auctions, settled on the limits: Clark's Customs House to Rich at 16 (Reina stopped at 15, Paddy at 10); Silke's Land Deed Scroll to Rich at 16 (Reina at 15); Paddy's Bamboo Brush, the last in stock, to Clark at 13 (Reina at 12); Reina's Dragon Jar unsold, since nobody met her minimum of 300 (Silke's limit was 125), so it must go up again next year at 2; Rich's Wooden Bowl to Reina at 101 (Noah stopped at 100; Reina's last 66 were a keep-from, to stop Noah); Noah's Vase to Rich at 16 (Reina at 15). Rich now holds a Bamboo Brush, Customs House, Land Deed Scroll and Vase, and needs only a Dragon Jar. Noah still needs a Wooden Bowl, and Silke a Dragon Jar.

Clark: "The last Brush in China, thirteen grain, on J12, which is where my last set started. Reina, one grain short, and for once that's in my favour. Write that one down yourself. And Reina, that Bowl at a hundred and one: three hundred on your Jar and sixty-six grain just to keep Noah out. I think you've just made yourself the most popular woman in the other book."

Reina: "A hundred and one for a wooden bowl. Noah, you named your number in front of everyone, and I'm afraid I can count. That's one grain, the same one Silke has beaten you by three times now. And look at the table this morning: Silke one Jar short, Rich one Jar short, and the only Jars in China sitting with me. Funny how a coast changes a woman's prospects."

Rich: "Sixteen, sixteen and sixteen, and I won all three by a single grain, so the gods have finally paid me back for the last three times. Reina, a hundred and one for my humble Bowl, you're too kind, and the rice fields thank you for every grain of the quarter. Noah, I'm sorry. I truly am, and I'll say so again next year when I mean it twice as much."

(Storyteller's note: The unsold Dragon Jar must go up again next year at a minimum of 2 (an unsold lot reopens at 2), so its minimum can't be set higher next year.)

Graham: "Fifteen, as declared. I'm three short, and I'll say that out loud. Noah, you asked last year where the crown's grain comes from, and you're the only honest counter at this table, so I'll ask you: three now, four back before Clark carries the first bag. And everyone, look where the money went. Seven provinces planted at half, and every square will pay at times three in the spring."

Noah: "Three it is, Your Majesty, so Clark and Paddy get what you declared. Pay me back the three and keep the extra grain, because I won't charge interest so men can be paid what they were promised. Reina, a hundred and one, and you beat me by one grain fair and square on a number I said out loud myself. I'll call that "make it right" settled, and I won't bring it up at this table again."

Noah lends the crown 3; Graham pays the declared salaries, Clark 10 and Paddy 5, and the crown ends Year 8 at 0.5. The end of Year 8: Graham Emperor (0.5), Clark Official (28.5, a Brush), Silke Gentry (257; needs only a Dragon Jar), Paddy Scholar (72.5), Reina Silk Peasant (97, a Bowl), Rich Rice Peasant (95.5; needs only a Dragon Jar), Noah Wheat Peasant (202; needs only a Wooden Bowl). The Great Wall is complete; 370 of the works are still owed. Reina's unsold Dragon Jar goes up first next year at 2.
