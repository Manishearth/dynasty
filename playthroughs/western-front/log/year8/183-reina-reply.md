diary: In Year Seven I took Graham at his word and bought four squares at five grain each, the cheapest land I'll ever own. I watched Silke and told the table she was two pieces from a chair. I set my keep-froms on the Bowl and the Vase at fifteen, and she paid twenty-nine and thirty-two, and then she took my seat. She took it fairly and she'd said she would, so I'm not angry, I'm only tired of learning the same lesson. Then the coast she gave back turned out to be the richest field in China. I paid her ten in rent, exactly what she paid me, and bought eight squares of sea. I'd gone from fifty grain to nearly three hundred in a single harvest. In Year Eight I spent a hundred and one on a wooden bowl to keep Noah a peasant, and he called his old debt to me settled, which I didn't expect and will miss a little. Clark beat me to the last Brush by a grain, so I can never finish a set unless one comes back to the shelves. That makes me a seller now, not a buyer. The Jar I priced at three hundred has to go up at two next year, and Silke and Rich each need only that Jar. Whoever buys it takes a chair, and unless I hold the auction right, the chair goes to whoever has the most grain left. Next year I'll need to talk before I bid: work out which of them I'd rather see climb, and what that's worth to me in public.
