## At the table since your last turn

Clark sets his Customs House's minimum at 3 again.

Rich: "A canal through the north-east, Your Majesty. The whole Wall country will be singing about it. Clark, my dear Official, you warned us honestly, so I'll do the same: I came away with nothing last year, and I don't mean to do it twice. My Deeds open at three, as before."

Rich sets his Land Deed Scroll's minimum at 3 again.

Noah: "Plain numbers, as always. I need two pieces, a Deed and a Jar, and I'm bidding on nothing else, so the Customs House, the Bowls and the Vases are all yours without me in the way. Clark already has his Deed and Jar, so it's only the peasants and Rich I'd be meeting there. If you'll let me have the one Deed and the one Jar quietly, I'll stay out of every other lot this year, and none of us feeds the Pile. My Brushes open at three again."

Noah sets his Bamboo Brush's minimum at 3 again.

Silke: "Last year I bid nice and small and went home with nothing, while Clark and Noah filled their shelves. This year I'm bidding what the pieces are worth. My Jars open at five. Noah, you'll want one, and so will Rich, Paddy and Graham. That's four of you after three jars, so five is a bargain."

Silke sets her Dragon Jar's minimum at 5.

Paddy: "I came home with an empty cart last year too, Silke, so I'll bid a bit firmer this time, though not daft. Clark, you'll get no enemy in me over a Bowl. They open at four, and if you bid fair for one it's yours, gladly."

Paddy sets his Wooden Bowl's minimum at 4.

Graham: "I came away with nothing last year as well, and I sold my Vase for five to a man who isn't even buying Vases. So I'll bid a little firmer this year. Clark, the Vases open at four. If you want one, bid fair and it's yours."

Graham sets his Vase's minimum at 4.

(Storyteller's note: Noah's set does need a Vase: his own type is the Bamboo Brush, so the Vase he bought last year is one of his five.)

The auctions settle on the limits. Rich wins Clark's Customs House at 8 (level with Silke at 8; the tie goes to the earlier seat) and Noah's Bamboo Brush at 8 (level with Silke again). Noah wins Rich's Land Deed Scroll at 8 and Silke's Dragon Jar at 9. Clark wins Paddy's Wooden Bowl at 12 and Graham's Vase at 12, Rich running him up to 11 on each. Silke, Paddy and Graham win nothing again.

Rich: "Clark, your Customs House is in good hands, and Noah, your Brush too, both safe at the back of my province, well away from the Wall. And Clark, congratulations on the Bowl and the Vase. Twelve apiece, a princely sum, and the Harvest Pile thanks you for every grain."

Noah: "Then I'll say it plainly, because I've never hidden a sum at this table. That's a Customs House, a Bowl, a Vase, a Deed and a Jar: all five pieces. I spent seventeen, my quarter of the Brush brings back two, and that leaves forty-six and a half, well over the twenty. So the Scholar holds a full set. Silke, Paddy, Graham, I'm sorry you've gone home empty twice now. I won't pretend that's fair luck."

Rich places the Customs House on M13 and the Bamboo Brush on M14. Noah places the Land Deed Scroll on K23 and the Dragon Jar on K24: the Scholar holds a complete set, with 46.5 grain. The Bowl and the Vase are still to be sold.

Noah: "I won't dress it up. Clark holds a Deed, a Brush, a Jar, a Bowl and a Vase, which is a full set, and he has the grain to use it, so whoever waits loses the throne. Your Majesty, you paid me well and kept every word you gave, and I'm sorry it's you. I take the crown, my five pieces go home to their provinces, and you draw sixty and take the Scholar's chair. Clark, I'll pay you your ten this year as promised, and every year after. That's a promise, and I keep mine."

Noah Tall changes places with Reina Osorio and takes the throne. His five pieces go back to their provinces' stock; he keeps his 46.5 and takes the crown reserve of 126. Reina draws 60 from the Harvest Pile and is now the Scholar, in Province 4. Two lots are still to be sold, the Wooden Bowl and the Vase, and Reina, now a bidder, is asked for her limits on them before they go.

Reina: "Well. Noah, that was neatly done, and I'll remember you said sorry before you took it. I'm in the Scholar's chair now and I'm starting my collection over. And Clark, you're two pieces short of a full set, and I can count too. Nobody here should hand you the Bowl and the Vase cheap."

With Reina bidding, the last two lots settle again: Reina wins Paddy's Wooden Bowl at 15 and Graham's Vase at 15, one over Clark's limit of 14 on each. Clark stays two pieces short.

Reina: "Fifteen apiece. That's dear, I know, but Clark, I warned you I can count. The Bowl and the Vase can sit quietly at the back of the Scholar's province while I collect the other three."

Reina places the Wooden Bowl on N21 and the Vase on N22, in Province 4.

Noah: "Ten each, exactly what was declared at the start of this year. Clark, I promised you ten and here it is. Reina, the Scholar's chair was promised ten, and I won't take that back just because you're the one sitting in it. That leaves the crown a hundred and fifty-two and a half against nine hundred and thirty of works still owed, and I'll tell you every year how far along we are."

Salaries: Noah pays Clark and Reina 10 each. End of Year 2. Grain: Noah (Emperor) 152.5, Clark 74, Rich 84, Reina 40, Silke 59.5, Paddy 61, Graham 61. Clark holds a Land Deed, a Brush and a Jar and needs a Bowl and a Vase; Rich holds a Customs House and a Brush; Reina, now the Scholar, holds a Bowl and a Vase and needs a Customs House, a Land Deed and a Dragon Jar. Silke, Paddy and Graham hold nothing. 930 of the works are still owed.
