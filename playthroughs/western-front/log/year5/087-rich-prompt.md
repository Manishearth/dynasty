## Rich's diary so far, in his own words

## Years 1–2

Two years, and the throne has already changed hands while I was busy watching the wrong man. I spent the whole of Year 2 pushing Clark up the ladder on Bowls and Vases, and the quiet Scholar who talked about nothing but "plain numbers" walked off with the crown. I won't forget that lesson, and I won't forget Noah's nice manners either. Still, I've kept every word: rent at five, the Pile paid out to the grain, and the peasants know Rich Baron as a fair landlord, which will matter when the fields open and the rent goes up as I said it would. I hold a Customs House and a Brush and eighty-four grain, the most anyone has below the throne. Next year I want a Jar, a Bowl and a Vase, and I'll keep Clark from his last two pieces even if Reina won't. Noah sits up there with a hundred and fifty grain and nine hundred and thirty of works owed. A man who promises to pay everyone has made a great many promises, and I'll be watching to see which one he breaks first.

## Years 3–4

One lot. I sat on the throne of China for exactly one lot, and I'd told myself all evening that the danger was Clark and his Vase. I was right about that and still didn't act on it. I had seventy-six grain when I sat down, and I could have kept forty of it back to run the Vase up against him, but I took the chair the moment it was offered, like a farmer grabbing the first fat goose he sees, while the man with one piece missing was still bidding. Reina did the same thing to Noah, and I did it to her, so I should have known it would be done to me. I paid forty-one for a Vase to keep it from Clark in Year 3 and then left the next Vase on the table for him at twenty-three, and that's the whole lesson in two numbers. I kept my word to the end: the rent stayed at five and the Pile was paid to the grain, and Graham called me on the letter of it, and he was right to. Now I'm the Official on five a year, collecting Clark's tax in a province with no new land until the Wall goes up, and the first thing I'll do is be the cleanest tax-man this empire has ever seen, because Clark keeps a book and I mean to be in the good part of it. Then I'll start again from nothing, piece by piece, and next time I'll keep back enough grain to stop the man behind me before I sit down.
