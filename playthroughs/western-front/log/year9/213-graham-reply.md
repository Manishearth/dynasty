diary: Year 9. I cut the tax to thirty on a lame-ox year, built Rich his canal, paid Clark fifteen a square because he'd once paid fifteen on mine, and put eighty into my own ground under the Wall. Then Silke paid two hundred and one for a Jar, and before the auction was over the throne had changed hands three times. I hadn't even sat down in the Gentry's chair when Noah took it from her, and he said sorry to her face, which is the third time I've written down Noah saying sorry and the first time it was said to someone other than me. The crown went over with four hundred and forty-one and came back to Noah with five hundred and fifty, and most of the difference is fields I paid half of. I have sixty grain and no pieces, the same as Year 1. But I run the Harvest Pile now, it holds eleven hundred, and every peasant at this table owes me rent on land the crown paid for when I was the crown. Next year I'll ask for the rent, and I'll ask for all of it. Then I'll buy the cheap pieces first and keep twenty back, the way I did last time.
