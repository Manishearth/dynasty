## Graham's diary so far, in his own words

## Years 1–2

Two years of auctions and I've bought nothing. Twice I bid what was sensible, and twice the men above me paid twelve and fifteen for what I'd have called seven. My Vases went for good prices, and I got a quarter of each while the Pile took the rest, and the upper class used them to swap chairs among themselves. Noah said sorry to Reina and then took her throne, and he said sorry to us peasants too, which cost him nothing. I have sixty-one grain, the same as Paddy, which is more than Reina has now, and I'm writing that down, because the cheap pieces are gone and next year sensible won't be enough. There are three of each left and two years of auctions. I'll pay what it takes for the Jar and the Deed first, keep twenty back for the set, and keep quiet about the rest until I'm standing on the rung.

## Years 3–4

Years 3 and 4. I paid fifteen for a Deed and twenty-four for the last Brush in the game, and I've set them side by side in the middle of my land. I bought two squares on the southern edge with the crown paying half, and I told Rich no when he tried to charge me rent on a reservoir he never paid for. That was five grain, and it was worth saying. Then in one auction the throne went round the table three times, Reina, then Rich, then Clark, and all their pieces went back into stock, so the Brush I paid top price for as the last one isn't the last one any more. Noah has gone from Emperor to Scholar with sixty grain and nothing in hand, so he's starting from the same rung I'm on, and I'm two pieces ahead of him. I have fifty-eight grain, and with forty a year of new land and the Reservoir coming in, the south will be the best-fed province at this table. I still need the Customs House, the Jar and the Bowl. They'll cost what this table charges, and I'll pay it, and I'll go on writing down who said sorry.

## Years 5–6

Years 5 and 6. It was the bad harvest that did it. Everyone else came to the auction broke, and I came with fifty-nine, so I got the Customs House, the Jar and the Bowl for forty grain together and sat down in Noah's chair with his forty-two in my pocket. He'd said sorry to us peasants once, and I'd written it down. A year later I paid twenty-one for my own old Vase out of Noah's hand and took the throne from Paddy, who'd held it for the length of one lot. He kept his word about the Jar and I kept mine, and then I took his chair anyway, and he said he'd understand. I believe him, and I'm writing that down too. It was three rungs in two years, and I paid this table's price for every one. Now I sit on 249 grain with 690 of works owed, and every man below me is counting the way I used to count. Silke has three pieces, Paddy knows the climb, and Noah is sitting on the wheat I fed with my own squares. I'll pay salaries in ink, keep promises to the grain, build the Wall where it buys me friends, and watch the Vase auctions closer than anything else.

## Years 7–8

Years 7 and 8. I built eight links of Wall, both strips, mine and Clark's, and I paid for every stone with crown grain. I put six hundred and ten into other men's fields and a hundred and twenty into my own, and twice I ended the year with half a grain, borrowing from the men I was paying. Clark lent me twelve and took fourteen back. Noah lent me three and wouldn't take the fourth, and that's the second time I've written Noah down for something, and this time it's to his credit. Silke climbed into Reina's chair, and Reina asked three hundred for a Jar so nobody could climb into hers, and now that same Jar goes up first next year at two grain, with Silke and Rich each one Jar short and Silke sitting on two hundred and fifty-seven. Every field in China is wider than it was, and every one of them pays my forty percent at times three, so the crown will be rich again by spring. I think it's a good trade, but it only counts if I'm still in the chair when the harvest comes in. There are three hundred and seventy of works left. I'll watch that Jar lot closer than I've watched anything since my own Vase, and I'll remember who still owes this crown a kindness and who doesn't.
