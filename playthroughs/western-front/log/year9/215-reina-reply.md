diary: Year Nine I watched three thrones change hands in a single auction, and none of them was mine to take or to lose. Silke kept her word to the letter: she bought my Jar for two hundred and one, took Graham's chair and not mine, and then sat on it for one lot, the same lot-and-a-half lesson I had carved for myself in Year Four. My keep-from made Noah pay a hundred and fifty-one for a wooden bowl, and it still wasn't enough. I'd forgotten that the Scholar collects the Vase, he had, and I stopped one grain short on it at twenty, the same single grain that beat me to the Brush last year. So Noah sits on the throne for the third time, with five hundred and fifty in the reserve, and he says sorry to everyone he passes on the way up, and somehow it still works. What it cost me is mostly pride: three grain for a Customs House, thirty in rent, a hundred on ten squares of sea, and fifty-two of my Jar money. I end the year with three hundred and fifty-seven, a Bowl and a Customs House, the Deed opening at two next year, Brushes back on the shelf, and a coastline that pays seven hundred in an average year. For the first time a set is within reach if I buy the cheap pieces before anyone notices I'm buying them. Rich needs only a Jar and I hold four of them, so the next time a Jar goes on the counter I decide who climbs. Graham collects the rent now, a man who's just lost a throne, so I'll pay him fairly and see whether he remembers who did.
