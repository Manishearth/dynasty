diary: Beaten by exactly one grain for the fourth time, and this time I'd chosen the number myself, two hundred, every grain I could spare, and Silke paid two hundred and one and sat on the throne for exactly one lot, same as I once did. So I watched her learn my own lesson from the other side of the table. Noah, the gentleman the table used to eat, bought my Bowl at a hundred and fifty-one, slid into the Scholar's stool, and then took the crown from her with a Vase, and I'll admit I clapped inside. I paid Silke her thirty because I'd said rent rises with the land, and I'm still glad I did, even though she bid that thirty back at me. Losing put nothing in the Pile and left me with two hundred and fifty-eight, the canal running through my shore, eighteen squares, and a Bowl's quarter of thirty-seven I never bid for. Silke's got her Jar now and is down to Scholar on seventy grain, so the only person left who needs a Jar with a real purse is me, and Reina has four of them on her shelf. Next year the Jar should come cheap, and the chairs worth watching are the Scholar's and the Gentry's, held by people with seventy and sixty grain who can't stop me once I sit down.
