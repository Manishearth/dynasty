diary: Year Nine. I collected three hundred and fifty-one on a lame ox, honestly, my own nine first, and Graham paid fifteen a square on my four new fields, the rate I once paid on his. That puts him in the good book twice in ink, and I'm up to a hundred and thirty-five a year in an average harvest. Then I sat with twenty-nine grain while the table spent nearly six hundred, and watched the throne change hands three times in one auction: Silke for one lot, Noah for the rest, and Graham down to the Harvest Pile with sixty. I won nothing, I didn't expect to, and the Deed went unsold and opens at two next year, which is the most useful news I heard all night. Noah sits in the chair for the second time and paid me ten again without being asked, so he stays in the good book; he's kept that promise for seven years now, which is more than anyone else here can say. The other book gets a line for the whole table, though: four hundred and odd grain thrown into the pile for a Jar and a Bowl. Next year the fields fill my granary at last, the Deed starts at two, and I mean to be the man with grain in his pocket when everyone else has spent theirs.
