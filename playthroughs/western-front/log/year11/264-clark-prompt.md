## At the table since your last turn

Silke: "Debt again, so I'm only selling. The Brush opens at eleven, and every grain of my quarter goes back to Noah's kindness. Thank you, Noah. Twice in two years is a habit, and I'll put it in my book next to Clark's. Graham, you hold the Jars, and the Pile is eight hundred and fifty-seven short. Rich has a thousand and sixty-seven and needs exactly one of them. You know what a chair costs. Set the price that way."

(Storyteller's note: The Harvest Pile stands at -317.5 now, after the Reservoir and the land; it was -857.5 after the works.)

Rich: "Reina's arithmetic is quite right, and I won't insult anyone by pretending otherwise: I want that Jar, and I'll pay for it at the counter where you can all watch. Graham, set whatever minimum your conscience lets you, and a quarter of it's yours. My Deed opens at forty; that's a landlord's price for a landlord's paper."

Graham: "The Jar opens at four hundred. Rich, you told the table last year the rent rises with the land, so here's the same measure: the price rises with the chair. Everyone else, I'll be bidding on the Customs House, the Deed, the Brush and the Bowl, and I'll be bidding to win them."

Reina: "I'm starting over from the bottom, so I'll bid like a farmer: a fair price on the cheap pieces and nothing at all on the Jar. Rich, there's a thousand grain in your purse and one Jar on Graham's shelf, and I'm not going to pretend I can stand in that doorway. Everyone else, if you want to stop him, now's the time to say it out loud, not afterwards. My Bowl goes up at ten."

Paddy: "I'll say my pieces out loud again, the way I did in Year 5, and it went well for me that year: the Deed, the Brush and the Bowl. I'll pay a fair price at the counter for each, but I'll not put a farmer's whole harvest on a pot. Rich, the Jar's between you and Graham, and I'll stand well back from that one."

The auctions, settled on the limits: Clark's Customs House to Graham at 31 (Reina stopped at 30); Rich's Land Deed Scroll to Graham at 46 (Paddy stopped at 45; Clark's 35 and Reina's 30 were under Rich's minimum of 40); Silke's Bamboo Brush to Graham at 41 (Paddy 40, Reina 30); Graham's Dragon Jar to Rich at Graham's minimum of 400 (Clark's 20 was under it). The Bowl and Vase are still to come.

Graham: "The Customs House, the Deed and the Brush go round the Vase, where anybody can count them. That's four. Rich, four hundred, paid at the counter as you said it would be, and I've written down that you kept your word."

Rich: "Four hundred, Graham, paid at the counter as promised, and the Jar sits at K16 with the other four where everyone can count them. Noah, Your Majesty, I'm not sitting down quite yet. A man who's been unseated from behind once learns to look over his shoulder before he takes the chair, and Graham, you've got four pieces round a Vase and a Bowl coming up at ten."

Rich: "Then I won't sit here waiting to be taken from behind. Noah, Your Majesty, you've been the most honest Emperor this table has seen, paying salaries out of your own reserve and building canals seven years late but building them, and I'm terribly sorry. I'm taking the throne. The Gentry's chair is yours, with the Pile and the rents, and I'll pay my Official and my Scholar the way you taught us all."

Rich took the Jar on K16, completing the Gentry's set, and took the throne from Noah after lot 5. He returned all five pieces to stock and took the reserve, 229 (907.5 in all). Noah drew 60 from the Pile and is now the Gentry, in Province 3.

Noah: "Rich, you said sorry to my face before you took it, and that's the same courtesy I gave Reina and Silke, so I'll put you in the same column they put me in. You've earned the chair. I've got sixty grain and an empty shelf, so I'll bid a farmer's price on the Bowl and the Vase, and I'm not going to fight anybody for either. Graham, if the Bowl is your fifth piece, you'll be paying more for it than I've got."

The Bowl, settled on the limits: Graham buys it at 41 (Noah stopped at 20, Clark at 25, Paddy at 40); Reina keeps a quarter. The Vase is still to come.

Graham: "The Bowl goes on P23, under the Brush. That's five pieces and six hundred grain, and anybody can count them from their chair. I'm not getting up tonight. Rich, you said sorry to Noah, and to Reina, and to me. I've written all three down, and I'll tell you when I've finished counting."

Graham placed the Bowl on P23 and holds his complete Silk set (607 grain) rather than change seats. The Vase, settled on the limits: Reina buys it at 26 (Noah stopped at 20, Clark at 25); Paddy keeps a quarter.

Reina: "One Vase, in the corner of the rice fields, and I won't line it up for anybody to count this time. I've learned that much. Graham, a complete set and six hundred grain, and you're not getting up. That's the most dangerous thing anyone's said at this table all night, and I mean it as a compliment. Rich, congratulations on the throne. Watch the silk coast."

Rich: "Noah's word stands, friends, because a throne that breaks the last man's promises the night it changes hands isn't worth sitting on. Clark, your ten, as every year. Silke, a hundred and thirteen and a half: a hundred and three and a half clears your debt and ten is your salary, so nobody at this table goes to bed in debt under me. And Graham, I heard you. I'll be counting too."

Rich pays the salaries Noah declared: Clark 10, Silke 113.5 (103.5 of it pays her debt; she ends the year with 10, out of debt). The end of Year 11: Rich Emperor (784 in the reserve), Clark Official 177 (holds a Brush), Noah Gentry 60 (holds nothing), Silke Scholar 10, Graham Silk Peasant 607 (holds a complete set: Customs House, Deed, Brush, Bowl, Vase), Reina Rice Peasant 336.5 (holds a Vase), Paddy Wheat Peasant 463.5 (holds a Customs House). The Harvest Pile holds 62; 50 of the works are owed (the Province 2 Irrigation System and the Marble Pagoda, each in a year of its own).
