diary: Years 10 and 11. The land did what I always hoped it would. Two good harvests came in, four hundred and forty the second year, and I went from lint in my pocket to four hundred and sixty-three grain, more than I've ever held in this game, and I paid every rent I was asked with a clear conscience. I bought fifteen squares at a farmer's share, half the first year and a quarter the second, so the Pile and the crown weren't left holding the whole bill. I bought a Customs House for twenty-six, and that was the one bit of the auctions that went my way. After that I lost the Deed, the Brush and the Bowl to Graham by a single grain each time, which is one grain short three times in one night, and I'm only half laughing about it. Then Rich paid four hundred for a Jar and took the throne off Noah, and Graham sat there with a full set and six hundred grain and wouldn't get up, and I've never seen a quieter man look so dangerous. Next year I'll stop bidding round numbers, because everyone at this table has learned to bid one over a round number, and I'll go after the Deed, Brush and Bowl with a few grain on top. The Jar can wait until the purses are thinner, and I'll keep my eye on Graham.
