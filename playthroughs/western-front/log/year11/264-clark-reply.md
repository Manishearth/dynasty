diary: Years Ten and Eleven. I collected two years of tax honestly, my own grain first both times, and I watched Rich Baron go through three chairs in two nights and say sorry before every one of them. In Year Ten I lost the Deed to Reina by one grain, thirty-one to my thirty, which takes the count of single grains at this table to everybody else eleven and me one, and I came away with nothing but four free squares from Noah. In Year Eleven I bought the last ring of fields above my wheat for five grain a square and stood up in public to tell the table to stop Rich. Graham listened and priced the Jar at four hundred, Rich paid it without blinking, and then took the throne from Noah anyway. Noah paid my ten for seven years without once being asked, so he goes into the good book in ink, on the first page, as a farmer with sixty grain. Rich kept the salary and goes in pencil, but the other book has a page for him too: four grain of tax on a two-chair night. Graham is sitting on a complete set with six hundred grain and won't get up, and that's the most dangerous man at the table, so I'll be watching him rather than the throne. I have a hundred and seventy-seven grain, one Brush, and a harvest that is finally worth something: one seventy-five a year, the best I've had. Next year my Irrigation System is the last proper work still owed and the Wall's built over my head, so I let the granary fill, I keep my mouth shut about which pieces I want, and I wait for everyone else to spend themselves stopping Graham.
