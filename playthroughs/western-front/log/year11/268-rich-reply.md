diary: Four chairs in two years: rice farmer, silk farmer, landlord, and now the throne of China, and I said sorry to every one of the people I took them from, and meant it every time, which I suppose is the oddest part. Two hundred and fifty for one Jar and four hundred for the next, so the Jars alone cost me six hundred and fifty grain, and I paid it at the counter like I said I would. Taking Reina's chair wasn't the clever part. She told the whole table she was one piece away and had five hundred and sixty-six in her purse, so I knocked over the one person standing right behind me and kept her purse. That's the Year Four lesson, finally learned and paid back with interest. I wanted to hold the Gentry's chair one more lot and run Graham's Bowl up out of his reach, but the limits were sealed, so I sat down the moment he could have taken my grain. He bought the Bowl for forty-one, and now he's sitting on a full silk set and six hundred grain, and he won't get up. Graham's the quiet man who paid fifteen for a Deed nobody wanted and went from the wheat to the throne in two years, and he told me he'll say when he's finished counting. So I've kept Noah's word to the half-grain, Silke goes to bed out of debt, Clark has his ten, and the reserve holds seven hundred and eighty-four. Next year I need every upper chair too comfortable to give up, and Graham will need one more climb before he can reach mine. I mean to make that climb very expensive for him.
