diary: Year Ten I had the richest coast in China, eight hundred grain and four pieces in a tidy row, and I told the whole table I was one Vase short because I thought honesty would keep me safe. Rich listened, paid my two hundred and fifty for the Jar, said sorry, and took the coast, the grain and the chair all at once. So I've now been unseated for being honest as many times as for being careless, and I can't decide which lesson stings more. I paid half my own land to spare the reserve, and the reserve went to Rich anyway. In Year Eleven I began again as a rice farmer with sixty grain, paid my rent, took three cheap squares, and bid thirty a piece while Graham bid one grain more on three of them and walked off with a whole set. Now he sits on it with six hundred grain, not getting up, the most dangerous man at the table, and Rich is on the throne with Noah's reserve and the habit of saying sorry. I have three hundred and thirty-six, a Vase tucked in a corner where nobody's counting, and a shore that pays four hundred and fifty a year. Next year I keep quiet, buy cheap and spread out, and let everyone keep their eyes on Graham. If he climbs, I want to be the one he passes over, not the one he takes.
