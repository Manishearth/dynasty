diary: Two years on the Scholar's stool, and twice I went to bed in debt for a harvest I had no fields to bring in. Twice an Emperor cleared it for me, first Noah and then Rich keeping Noah's word, and I've written both of them down, because the last thing a squeezed woman can afford is to forget who was kind to her. I couldn't bid on a single lot in either year. All I could do was sell my Brushes and watch, so I watched Rich take three chairs in two nights, the Silk coast, the Gentry's Pile and finally the throne, and each time he said sorry first, which is a courtesy I taught this table myself. The price of a chair kept climbing, two-fifty for one Jar and four hundred for the next, and every grain of it went into the Pile instead of anyone's purse. Now I have ten grain, no land, no square left in my province to buy, and an empty shelf, while Graham sits on a finished set and six hundred grain and says he isn't getting up. He's the one to watch. A full set he hasn't used can take any chair at the table, and the longer he waits, the more of us start counting him. Next year I can't buy my way back in, so I'll do what I did in the first two years: read the card, count every grain, and keep track of who owes the Scholar a favour.
