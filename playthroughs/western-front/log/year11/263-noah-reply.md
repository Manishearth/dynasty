diary: In two years on the throne I built the canals I'd promised Silke and Paddy back in the third year, and I built Graham's old reservoir too. Twice I cleared Silke's debt out of the reserve, and I paid on more new land than I can count without a pencil. Every sum I said out loud was right this time, and I checked the map before I put the reservoir down. None of it kept Rich off the throne. He bought the Jar at four hundred, the price on it, said sorry to my face, and took the chair, which is exactly how I took it three times myself. So I've no complaint coming, and I haven't made one. What I'm proudest of is that Rich paid the salaries I'd declared, to the grain, on the night he unseated me, so a promise made from the throne outlasted the man who made it. Now I'm the Gentry with sixty grain and an empty shelf. The Pile has sixty-two in it and a ledger of shortfalls behind it. Graham is sitting on a full set and six hundred grain and won't say what he means to do with them. Fifty of the works are still owed, so it's two more years to the end at the earliest. Next year I'll ask a fair rent and pay out every bag the Pile owes. I'll buy cheap and say my sums out loud, and I'll keep an eye on the silk coast like everybody else.
