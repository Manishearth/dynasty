diary: Four years and my shelf is still empty, and this time I picked it that way. I put forty grain into three fields on the coast while the crown was paying half. That left me too thin to fight Graham for the last Brush, and he got it at twenty-four. Then I sat and watched the throne change hands three times in one auction: Reina, then Rich, then Clark, each of them saying sorry and none of them meaning it. What I learned is that a finished set gets you a chair, and the person sitting in it only keeps it until the next lot is sold. My Jar went to Reina for eighteen, and I kept four and a half of it. Those three fields pay sixty a year from now on, and every piece I need has gone back to stock, the Brushes included, so the market I was shut out of is open again. Clark has two hundred and eighty in the treasury, Paddy is down to thirty-six and a half, and Graham has spent his purse on a Deed and a Brush. Next year I'll have the steadiest income of any peasant at this table, and when the population doubles again I won't be the one going hungry.
