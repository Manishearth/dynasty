diary: Year Three I bid fourteen a piece and came home with nothing, while Clark and Rich spent forty apiece and ended one piece short each. I should have seen then that the shelves were about to tip. Year Four I bought the Customs House, the Deed and the Jar in a row, took the throne back from Noah, and said sorry to his face. Then I sat in that chair for one lot. Rich bought a Bowl and threw me out, and Clark bought a Vase and threw him out. The man who keeps the book now wears the crown, and he's sitting on two hundred and eighty grain of reserves I'd saved twice over. I'm the Gentry now, with sixty grain, nothing on my shelf, and a province under an unbuilt Wall, so I can't buy land until Clark builds it. What I learned: having a set isn't safe while two other people are one piece from theirs. I won too early, or I didn't block enough. But I run the Harvest Pile and the rent book now, so every peasant at this table deals with me once a year, and I'll be generous with them and keep count. Clark owes me nothing yet. He'll owe me a Wall.
