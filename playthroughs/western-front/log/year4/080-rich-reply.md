diary: One lot. I sat on the throne of China for exactly one lot, and I'd told myself all evening that the danger was Clark and his Vase. I was right about that and still didn't act on it. I had seventy-six grain when I sat down, and I could have kept forty of it back to run the Vase up against him, but I took the chair the moment it was offered, like a farmer grabbing the first fat goose he sees, while the man with one piece missing was still bidding. Reina did the same thing to Noah, and I did it to her, so I should have known it would be done to me. I paid forty-one for a Vase to keep it from Clark in Year 3 and then left the next Vase on the table for him at twenty-three, and that's the whole lesson in two numbers. I kept my word to the end: the rent stayed at five and the Pile was paid to the grain, and Graham called me on the letter of it, and he was right to. Now I'm the Official on five a year, collecting Clark's tax in a province with no new land until the Wall goes up, and the first thing I'll do is be the cleanest tax-man this empire has ever seen, because Clark keeps a book and I mean to be in the good part of it. Then I'll start again from nothing, piece by piece, and next time I'll keep back enough grain to stop the man behind me before I sit down.
