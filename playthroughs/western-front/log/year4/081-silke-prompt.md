## At the table since your last turn

Clark: "Customs House at three, as always. Reina, I've written it in the book, the throne buying up your coastline. That entry isn't under your name, for once. Graham, I'll say it plainly this year, since there's no secret left to keep: I want your Vase, and I'll pay a good deal more than fifteen for it."

Rich: "Graham, five it is, and you were quite right to hold me to the letter. When your two southern fields come in I'll be asking, and you've told me yourself you'll give me an answer. Reina: "My last Brush goes up at 2. Paddy already has one, so whoever's still short, it's yours cheap. I'm bidding on the Customs House, the Deed and the Jar this year and nothing else. Silke, Graham, Paddy, every grain we push each other up goes three quarters into the Pile. If you let me have one of these cleanly, I'll stand aside for you on the next one, and I'll say so in front of everybody. Your Majesty, I'm holding you to 'make it right.'"

Paddy: "My purse went into the ground this year, so I'll be bidding small and plain, a Deed if Rich will part with one fair, and a Jar if it comes cheap. Rich, the Bowl's at fifteen, the same price everyone at this table seems to agree on. Clark, I'll stay well out of your way on the Vase."

Graham: "One Brush left in the game, and two of us need it. Silke, I'll say it plainly: I'm going high for it, so if you want to save your grain, take the Bowl and the Customs House instead. Clark, the Vase opens at twenty. You paid thirty-six for a Bowl, so I don't think twenty will trouble you."

Lots 2 to 5: Reina takes Clark's Customs House at 17, Rich's Land Deed at 15 and Silke's Dragon Jar at 18; Graham takes Reina's last Bamboo Brush at 24. The Wooden Bowl and the Vase are still to be sold.

Reina: "Customs House, Deed, Jar, next to my Bowl and Vase. That's five, with grain to spare. Noah, two years ago you took this chair in the middle of an auction and said sorry while you did it. I'm taking it back the same way, and I'm sorry too. The Scholar's seat is a good one: the Emperor pays you at least five, and I'll pay you ten, because I've always paid loyal service double. The province is a little short of coastline, but you know that better than anyone. Clark, Rich, the Bowl and the Vase are all yours. Bid carefully, because the throne isn't empty any more."

Graham: "Twenty-four. Thank you, Reina, that was the last one in the game, and it was a fair price. Silke, I'm sorry. I did tell you plainly beforehand."

Reina completes her set after the fifth lot and takes the throne back: her five pieces go back to stock and she takes the reserve, 104.5, for 149.5. Noah draws 60 and becomes the Scholar, in Province 4.

Noah: "Well played, Reina. You said sorry to my face, the way I did to you, and I'll take it the same way. Here's my arithmetic. Clark, Rich, if either of you wants it more than that, it's yours, and I won't push your price up just to spite you. And Reina, ten a year is very fair, and I'll hold you to it, the same way I'd have held myself."

Rich: "Paddy, thank you, a beautiful Bowl at an honest price. Reina, my dear, you held that chair for a whole lot and a half, and you sat in it beautifully. I'm terribly sorry, but a landlord who has his whole set in hand doesn't leave it lying on the table while Clark is bidding on a Vase. You'll find Province 3 a well-kept house, and the rent is five, just as I always promised."

Rich takes the Wooden Bowl at 21 (L14), completes his set and takes the throne: his five pieces go back to stock and he takes the reserve, 149.5, for 226. Reina draws 60 and becomes the Gentry, in Province 3. The Vase is still to be sold.
