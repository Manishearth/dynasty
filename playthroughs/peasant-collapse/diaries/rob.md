# Rob's diary

## Setup–Year 2

Rob's diary, end of Year 2:

Two years in and I've worked out that I'm the villain. I've never enjoyed a board game more. I've paid no tax, I've had ten grain off Silke and Paddy, and I've spent twenty on a third lad to stand at the bottom of Graham's garden. I've still got my sixty, and nobody's bought a single one of my soldiers off me. The three farmers have spent two years making solemn promises to each other and then fighting over a bowl. Silke paid twenty-five for it and she's down to twenty-seven and a half, so she can't buy off more than one of my boys. Reina keeps writing my name in her little book and telling me walls have long memories. She's right, but they don't have legs, and mine are healing nicely. Next year W2 goes for Paddy's jar, W3 for Graham's brush and W1 for Silke's scroll. They're all two or three steps away, so they'll get there at roughly the same time, and I'll be grinning the whole way.

## Years 3–4

Rob's diary, end of Year 4:

Four years in and still not a grain of tax, and Reina's tally of my sins is long enough to wallpaper the Winter Palace. Year three was a proper soap opera. Paddy outbid Silke for the Vase at twenty-nine, finished his set and took her coast off her, so poor Silke ended up in the rice fields wondering what she'd done. Then Graham beat her to the Jar at twenty-three, and she's sitting on the only Wooden Bowl in China like a dragon on a hoard. I passed on every lot again. I don't need their pots. I need them to keep buying pots and putting them down where my lads can walk over and pick them up. My boys took their five a head off each farmer on the way in, and this year W3 finally walked through Graham's gate and lifted his bamboo brush off R14, which is the first name crossed off my list. W1 is one step from Paddy's customs house, and W2 is two from the scroll Silke so kindly bought for two and put out in the open. The farmers spend all their time correcting each other's minutes and swearing on Reina's book. They're so busy keeping their promises that nobody's noticed the desert never made any.

## Years 5–6

Rob's diary, end of Year 6:

Six years without paying a grain of tax, and tonight I'm the man who collects it. Year five the committee fought over the only bowl in China, and Paddy kept his word for precisely one year, which I believe is a record. My W1 walked off with his customs house while they were still arguing. Year six, Population Doubled and everyone went hungry at once. Silke spent twenty of her last grain on one of my own lads to guard her scroll, and my W2 stepped onto it, took the scroll and took her lad as well. Graham paid me thirty for his own brush back, which is the best deal I've ever made: a lovely thing to own and a lovely thing to sell. With all three names crossed off I walked into the Winter Palace and asked Reina for the book. She gets the desert and my boys now, and I get the throne, a famine and three hundred and sixty grain of canal I've spent six years teaching everyone not to pay for. I was told quite firmly that the auction money goes to the Harvest Pile and not into my pocket. I'm beginning to suspect the villain's seat was the comfortable one, but the view from up here is marvellous.

## Years 7–8

Rob's diary, end of Year 8:

Two years on the throne, and the throne turns out to be the hungriest seat in China. In Year 7 a Bad Harvest took a hundred and ten off me, and I did something I'd sworn I'd never do: I paid for a public work. Forty grain finished Silke's canal, and I gave Graham his five for reading the cards, partly to keep the committee from agreeing on a revolt. Graham bought nearly every piece on the table and got within one Vase of my chair. Then in Year 8 the Emperor's Share card came up, and every one of us went into the red at once, me included. Graham sold all four of his pieces back for two each, and the board ended up emptier than the desert. Silke proposed we give up, the committee voted yes one after another, and I voted yes with them, because the sum never came right and I'd spent six years making sure it wouldn't. Three hundred and twenty grain of works were still owed when we stopped. My only monument is the canal on the Rice coast, and it was the only thing on the board still paying anyone at the end. I was the villain who won the empire and the Emperor who couldn't afford it, and I'd sit in either seat again tomorrow.
