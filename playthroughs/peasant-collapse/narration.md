# Narration

The narrator's lines, chapter by chapter: the chapter title, each year's opening line, and the turning points, each tagged with the log number it follows. The narrator isn't part of the game, so none of this goes into `game_pilot.py` or the playthrough page; the page must make sense without it.

## Chapter 1

**Chapter One: The Only Bowl in China**

- **(002) Year 1 opens:** The first year of Reina's reign. No card is turned, the sixty is the harvest, and forty in every hundred of it goes north to pay for a palace in the far corner of Province 1. Two soldiers watch from the sand.
- **(Jar, after 006):** Seventeen for the only Dragon Jar in the empire. Silke keeps four; the Harvest Pile, as at every auction, keeps the rest. Paddy offered a favour, Graham offered a favour, and both bid anyway.
- **(016):** The desert collects its first two fives, from the coast and the rice, and buys itself a third soldier with the change. Three soldiers, three pieces, and every distance is two or three.
- **(016, Year 2 opens):** The second year of the reign. The first card says the canal gives way, though no canal has yet been dug to give way; the harvest is average, Graham's wheat takes the hail, and the Warlord's legs fail him for a year. Six pieces on the board by the end of it, and three soldiers who may not touch any of them.
- **(028):** Twenty-five for the only Wooden Bowl in the empire, the dearest lot of the reign. Paddy keeps six; the Harvest Pile keeps nineteen. Graham lets it go, and keeps the Vase.
- **(033):** Paddy holds three of his four. The fourth is the Vase, the last piece in Graham's stock, and it goes up next year, after Silke's last Customs House, which Graham needs.

## Chapter 2

**Chapter Two: A Lovely Thing to Lose**

- **(033, Year 3 opens):** The third year of the reign, and the villages are noisier than they were: a good harvest, and half as many mouths again to eat it. Three pieces wait beside the board, each wanted by exactly one other peasant.
- **(046):** The Customs House goes for two, the Brush for two, and the Vase for twenty-nine. Every promise of the last two years is kept at the minimum, and the one piece nobody promised is the one that costs.
- **(047):** Paddy Doyle, who drew the rice, sits down on the silk coast with seventy-nine grain. Silke Weber, who drew the silk, stands up with sixty and none of her pieces, and takes the rice. The first seat in the dynasty changes hands.
- **(048):** The desert takes its five from all three coasts in one night, the wheat's first. Rob still holds no piece of anyone's; W3 stands one square from Graham's Brush.
- **(048, Year 4 opens):** The fourth year of the reign. The rains come late, and for the first time every granary in the empire, the Emperor's included, pays the Harvest Pile rather than the other way round. Four pieces wait beside the board, and three peasants who each need two of them.
- **(053):** Twenty-three for the Dragon Jar, and Graham is one Bowl from a set. The Bowl sits in the stock of the woman he outbid, and she sells the Brush first.
- **(061):** Every promise of the year is kept at two. Graham and Silke trade a Scroll now for a Bowl next year, and Paddy, who could finish with that Bowl himself, promises in public to leave it alone.
- **(062):** On the last night of the fourth year the desert takes its first piece: Graham's Brush, from R14 to the sand at T14. The empire has no Brush left to sell him, and the Bowl he was promised for two no longer finishes anything.

## Chapter 3

**Chapter Three: Next Year's Another Year**

- **(062, Year 5 opens):** The fifth year of the reign. A good harvest, no new mouths, and one piece left beside the board: the only Wooden Bowl, which since last night finishes a set that reaches the throne.
- **(067):** Two for the Bowl, as promised a year ago. The one man at the table it would have crowned sits on his hands, and says so.
- **(068):** That night the desert takes Paddy's Customs House from O22. The set that could have reached the throne is now two pieces short, and Rob is one square from his third name.
- **(068, Year 6 opens):** The sixth year of the reign. The harvest is good and the mouths have doubled, so every granary in the empire, the crown's included, pays the pile; no tax is raised, no work is built, and nothing is left beside the board to sell.
- **(069, a correction):** The chronicle misspoke at (068): the Silk set has no use for a Customs House. Paddy is still exactly one Bowl from the throne, and Graham holds it.
- **(073):** Thirty grain buys Graham back his own Brush, and the same night the desert steps onto Q25 and takes the guard Silke paid twenty for and the Scroll beneath it. One piece from every peasant: the Warlord's list is done.
- **(074):** Graham Garner, who drew the wheat, sits down on the silk coast with ninety-nine and a half. Paddy Doyle, who passed on a throne a year ago to keep his word, takes the wheat with sixty and an empty field.
- **(075):** Robert Land, who never paid a grain of tax, walks into the Winter Palace with 185 and the book. Reina Osorio takes the desert, his three soldiers and sixty from the pile; 360 of the works are still owed.

## Chapter 4

**Chapter Four: A Lovely Thing to Owe**

- **(080, Year 7 opens):** The seventh year of the reign, and the first of Rob's. The seed rots, every granary in the empire pays the pile, and two of the three peasants finish the harvest owing it grain; at the auctions only Graham and the desert can lift a hand.
- **(089):** Ten for a Scroll, and nobody bids against it. Graham holds three of the four pieces that reach the throne and exactly the twenty it asks for; the fourth is the Vase, and Paddy keeps it in his stock.
- **(090, Year 8 opens):** The eighth year of the reign. The Emperor's Share comes up on an average harvest, and with three mouths for every one the dynasty began with, every granary in the empire ends the harvest owing the pile. Graham's four pieces go back to it at two each, eight grain for the set that stood one Vase from the throne.
- **(093):** The banker proposes that the table give up. Nothing stands on the board, and nobody may bid on what waits beside it.
- **(097):** Five votes, five yeses, the only unanimous decision of the reign. The dynasty ends in its eighth year, in collapse, with 320 of its works unbuilt and every seat in debt to the pile.
