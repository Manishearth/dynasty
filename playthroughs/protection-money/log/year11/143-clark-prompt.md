## Clark's diary so far, in his own words

## Years 1-2

Years One and Two, entered in my own hand. Reina taxed us at half in the first fat year, and I noted it. Rich bought three lots by one grain apiece over me and took her throne within the year, and I noted that too, along with the fact that nobody lifted a finger to stop him. This year I spent thirty-five on a Scroll, a Jar and a Bowl and outbid Reina for the last two. It was dear, but it bought the only thing at this table that's worth more than grain: a loaded set that nobody knows when I'll fire. It's already paying. Rich went from ten a year to thirty, publicly, and gave his word in private: "thirty a year, every year, not one grain short and not one day late." That promise is copied here word for word, so I can quote it back to him the day he breaks it. Next year I'll watch three things: whether Rob's soldiers start walking toward I15, whether Reina comes calling with a better offer than Rich's, and whether I stay above twenty grain, which keeps the set loaded. Loyalty is a fine thing, and so far it has a price of thirty.

## Years 3-4

Years Three and Four, in my own hand. Rich kept his word to the letter both years, thirty and thirty, and even quoted my own book back at me, which tells me he knows exactly what the book is for. I spent forty-four on a Jar and a Bowl I didn't need, outbidding Reina by a single grain each time. It was dear, but she still has no set, I'm the only loaded gun at this table, and I've got spares now that Rob's man is standing on H13, one square from my Jar. Things to note: Reina's "the coast's business" lasted one year. Silke carried the empire through a lean harvest. Rob has taken ten from my fields and smiled each time. And worst of all, the crown's reserve is down to nineteen and a half after Rich paid my thirty and kept building pagodas. A reserve under twenty and a thirty-a-year promise can't both last, so next year I'll find out whether Rich would rather break his word to me or find the grain somewhere. I'll be watching Reina's purse for the moment she offers a better rent than his. Ninety-nine grain, set complete, and my loyalty is still rented by the year.

## Years 5-6

Years Five and Six, in my own hand. Rich kept his word for a fourth and fifth year, thirty and thirty, to the grain. I've written that down honestly, because a book that only records the sins is no use as evidence. Look at what it cost him, though: the crown finished Year Five on four and Year Six on nothing at all. Four years in a row I've wiped out his reserve with his own promise. Item: the works stood still for a year, and then one Canal section went to Paddy and not to Silke, who pays most of the tax and said so to the whole table. Item: Rob walked off with my spare Jar and then stood on my Scroll and called it restraint, and Rich hasn't moved a single soldier against him, which makes two causes for revolt in one man. Item: Reina has started counting out loud as well, and Silke is carrying the empire and knows it. My set is complete, I'm sitting on well over a hundred, and I'm the only loaded gun at the table, so the throne is mine to take whenever the price is right. That point is coming close now, with an empty treasury, a Warlord nobody controls and the coast feeling unrewarded. Next year I'll watch whether Rich can pay thirty from zero without breaking his word, and if he does break it, I'll read his promise back to him word for word, with two friends standing beside me. Loyalty is still rented by the year, and the landlord's credit has just run out.

## Years 7-8

Years Seven and Eight, in my own hand. Rich kept his word for a sixth and seventh year, thirty and thirty, to the grain, and I've written that down honestly. He also did what I'd stopped expecting and put two links of Wall on the board, and I've written down that both of them stand over Province 1, which leaves my own strip as shut as it was in Year One. The coast is fat now, and Silke paid a hundred and five in one year and said out loud whose harvest built the north's roof. That makes her the one I'd want standing next to me when I read Rich's promise back to him. The crown finished on fourteen and then forty-eight, which I'd call thin but not empty, with five hundred and ninety still owed in works. The new item, and the one that matters most, is Rob. He holds three pieces, his only missing one is Silke's Scroll, and W2 is standing one square from it. The day he takes it, I stop being the only loaded gun at this table, and every grain of my leverage gets cut in half. So next year I'll watch Q26 harder than I watch the treasury, and I may have to decide whether the Scroll is worth more in Silke's hands than my patience is worth to Rich. A hundred and ninety-four grain, set complete, and my loyalty is still rented by the year, though I'm now pricing in a competitor.

## Years 9-10

Years Nine and Ten, in my own hand. Rich kept his word for an eighth and a ninth year, thirty and thirty, and both times he paid it out of a treasury that couldn't spare it. In Year Nine he finished the Wall over his own roof, all four links. In Year Ten, after three years of my blank line, he put two links over mine, and I've written that down as plainly as I wrote down the delay. A book that won't credit a payment isn't worth quoting. The river took thirty of my grain, a gale took ten, and I paid nothing in tax in a flood year, so I end on two hundred and twenty-eight with the set complete. Silke paid nearly three hundred and fifty in one year and asked me to file it under the people who pay for the Wall, so I have, and her name is the first I'd call on. The main event was Silke's soldier taking W2 off the board, which made me the only loaded gun at the table again. Rob hired another man on T24 straight away, so now two soldiers are four rounds from that Scroll, and my monopoly has a clock on it. Next year I watch three things: whether the last two links over Province 2 go up, whether W2 and W3 close on P25 together, and whether Rich's thirty survives the first lean year since the treasury filled. Loyalty is still rented by the year. The landlord has paid on time for nine years and has started fixing my roof, and the rent goes up the day a second gun appears.
