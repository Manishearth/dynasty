## Rich's diary so far, in his own words

## Years 1-2

Two years, and I went from keeping the Pile to sitting on the throne, for the price of nineteen grain at auction and a lot of smiling. Reina was gracious about it, which worries me more than shouting would have. She bid hard for her Customs House, and she'll be back. Clark is the real thorn now. He bought his set out from under her and sold me his loyalty for thirty a year, and I've promised it to him to the letter: thirty while he's Official and I'm Emperor, no more and no less. It's cheap next to the reserve, but every grain I give him keeps his set alive, so I'm counting on Rob's soldiers and the auction house to thin his purse for me. The canal starts paying next year. I'll keep the tax kind, the works rising and the peasants grateful, and I'll keep watching Clark's grain, Reina's purse and Silke's sharp tongue.

## Years 3-4

Two years of mercy, and the reserve has gone from a hundred and ninety-two to nineteen and a half. I don't regret it, but I feel it in my stomach. I've built the Winter Palace and the Marble Pagoda, put two fields into the sea and paid half of every coastal field Silke and Paddy wanted, so the coast is grateful and nobody can call me a miser. Clark got his thirty both years, to the letter, and he spends it on Jars and Bowls he doesn't even need. That used to look like folly. Now it looks to me like a man shopping for allies. Rob has taken Reina's Customs House, which takes her down a peg, but his soldier is one square from Clark's Jar, and I'd sleep better if he'd hurry up and take it. Next year I need a good harvest and fat taxes from Silke's fields. I'm not raising the rate, though, not yet. A thirty-percent Emperor with an empty purse can still sit on his throne, but a fifty-percent Emperor with enemies can't. If the population card comes up x4 before I've bought more land, I'm in real trouble, so the first spare grain goes into the sea.

## Years 5-6

Two years, and both of them ended with the treasury at exactly zero. Nobody can call that carelessness, because every grain went where I'd said it would, but I don't like how it feels. Clark got his thirty both years, to the letter, and wrote every grain of it in that book of his as if he were drawing up the charges for a trial. A bad harvest left me nine and a half. A good one, with the utility levy and thirty percent from Silke's fields, paid for the first section of Paddy's canal and some help on the coast's land, and I spent all of it again. Paddy is the one I'm glad of. He waited a year without complaining and thanked me when I kept my word, and if anyone calls a revolt, his is the hand I most need to stay down. Silke worries me more. She has ten sea squares now, pays most of the crown's tax and counts it all out loud, and she's told the table the next canal had better be hers. I didn't promise it, but I'll probably have to give it to her. Rob has taken a piece from every one of us except Silke and me, and he complimented my canal, which I don't trust at all. His soldiers are three steps from my land. What comes next is simple and hard: I have to stop ending the year on nothing. The rate stays at thirty, because I said so and they believe it. But the reserve needs a cushion before the Wall and the x4 card catch up with me. If that card comes while I'm sitting on zero, I'll need a lot more than smiles.

## Years 7-8

Two good harvests, and I've finally finished a year with something left in the jar: fourteen after the first, forty-eight after the second. That isn't a fortune, but it isn't zero either, and I slept better for it. I gave Silke her first canal section, and she thanked me in front of Clark's book, which is worth more than the forty it cost. Then I spent eighty of her grain on the Wall over my own province, and she made sure the whole table knew whose grain it was. She now farms twenty-four sea squares to my four, she pays most of the crown's tax, and she has started saying "I'll remember." I don't like the sound of that. Clark paid for the empty Wall line in breach damages, and now that it has something on it he's written my province number next to it, which tells me he's still building his case. Paddy is still my quiet friend, but he's talking to Rob about a Customs House, and that worries me. What really worries me is Rob. He has three pieces and only needs Silke's Scroll, his W2 is one square from it, and W3 is two steps from my Winter Palace. Next year the remaining two links of the Wall go up, the coast keeps its five a square, Clark gets his thirty to the letter, and I keep more in the jar every year. Then I'll quietly start working out what it would cost to make Rob look somewhere other than Silke's beach.
