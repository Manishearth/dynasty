## At the table since your last turn

Reina: "Well played, Clark. Eleven years of ink, and the last page turns out to be a deed. I'll put the Scroll up from Province 3 at ten, which is a fair floor for a piece that's just made an Emperor, and I won't be bidding on the Jar or the Bowls. I've no Customs House, and I'm not going to pour the Gentry's grain into a set I can't finish before the table stops. Silke, Paddy, whatever you do with those Bowls tonight, do it out in the open, the way this coast has done everything."

Silke: "Well played, Clark, and I mean it: you counted better than any of us, and I'll bow when the night's over. Paddy, I'm bidding on your Bowl, and anyone who wants to bid against me should know I'll put up every grain in my purse except the twenty the set needs, all of it going into the Pile. The Jar goes up at two, and whoever wins it gets it honestly."

Paddy: "Well, Clark, you've done what you said you'd do, and you did it in the open, so I can't fault you for it. That puts the Scroll and the Jar back on the table, and I'll bid on both of them plainly. I've wanted those two since Year 2, and tonight I've got the grain to mean it. The Bowl goes up at two, the same as anybody's. I won't make a man pay a toll because he's coming up behind me."

The auctions: Reina's Land Deed Scroll (minimum 10) goes to Paddy at 13 over Rich's 12; Silke's Dragon Jar to Paddy at 13 over Rich's 12; Paddy's Wooden Bowl to Silke at 17 over Rich's 16. Each seller keeps a quarter. Paddy now holds a Customs House, a Land Deed Scroll and a Dragon Jar, and Silke a Land Deed Scroll, a Customs House and a Wooden Bowl: both sets are complete.

Paddy: "Thirteen and thirteen. After all these years, I can't quite believe it. Rich, you kept every word you ever gave a rice farmer, and I'm sorry it's your chair. But the crown's already gone from you tonight, and I won't knock Reina out of a seat she's kept honest for eleven years. The Customs House goes back to Province 2's stock with my thanks to Rob, and I'll collect Clark's taxes the way he collected ours: counted twice, and not one grain skimmed."

Paddy places the Scroll and Jar, then, with his complete set and 20 grain, replaces the Official: Paddy becomes the Official in Province 2 with his grain and Rich's (833); the Customs House goes back to Province 2's stock, and the Scroll and Jar move with him to G13 and I13. Rich takes the Rice Peasant's seat in Province 6, holds nothing and draws 60 from the Pile.

Silke: "Paddy, count it with me, because it's the kindest sum on the board: you drew the Rice Peasant, so the Silk Peasant's seat is still a step up for you, with fifty-four sea fields, two canal sections and a soldier on the Scroll. Reina stood aside for me on the Customs House, so her seat stays hers, as I said I'd remember. I'll take the Official's book, Clark, and I'll count your taxes out loud, every grain, the same as I've counted everyone else's for twelve years."

Silke places the Bowl, then, with her complete set and 20 grain, replaces the Official: Silke becomes the Official in Province 2 with her grain and Paddy's (2298); the Customs House goes back to Province 2's stock, and her Scroll, Jar and Bowl move to G12, G14 and I12. Paddy takes the Silk Peasant's seat in Province 5 with its soldier on P25; his Scroll and Jar go back to stock, and he draws 60. The seats now: Clark Emperor, Silke Official, Reina Gentry, Paddy Silk Peasant, Rich Rice Peasant, Rob Warlord.

Rob marches: W1 onto H19 in Province 1 (10 from the crown), W2 onto R24 in Province 6 (5 from Rich), W3 stays on M22. The 15 pays down his debt: Rob is 28.5 in debt.

Rob: "Your Majesty! Three new seats in a single evening, and every one of them taken in the open. I've never been prouder of a table of honest people. Clark, I'll be stopping by the garden for ten on the way to congratulate you properly, and Rich, I'm sorry, old friend, but the rice is yours now, and so is the five."

The provinces' soldiers: none is in reach of a Warlord soldier, and Province 5 has no pieces to guard; Paddy's soldier stays on P25.

Clark: "Silke, the Official's salary is thirty, the same thirty Rich paid me for eleven years without missing a grain or a day. A crown that changes the rate the moment it changes hands isn't worth bowing to. I'm told a man once said that at this very table. Rich, I've written your eleven payments on the last page in my best hand. A book that won't credit a payment is no use to anyone, and nobody at this table paid more honestly than you. Rob, the garden's ten is in the book as well, under your debt."

Salaries: Clark, the new Emperor, pays Silke, the new Official, 30. The crown ends the game with 930.

The table stops after Year 12, as agreed, and scores from where everyone sits. Clark drew the Official's seat and ends on the throne; Silke drew the Silk Peasant's and ends as the Official; Reina drew the throne and ends as the Gentry; Paddy drew the Rice Peasant's and ends as the Silk Peasant; Rich drew the Gentry's and ends as the Rice Peasant; Rob drew the Warlord's and ends there. Grain: Silke 2328, Clark 930, Reina 107, Paddy 60, Rich 55, Rob 28.5 in debt. 310 of the works were never built. No revolt was called in twelve years.
