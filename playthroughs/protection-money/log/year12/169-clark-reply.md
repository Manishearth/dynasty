That's the last tax I'll collect, and it went in honest. Rich, I promised you a total on the last page, so here it is. Eleven years of thirty, every one paid, and every one credited in ink. Seven years before a single link of Wall over my roof. A Warlord who's taken three provinces' grain, walked off with my Jar and now sits forty-three and a half in debt to the Pile, which this book lists as a cause for revolt, and not one soldier ever sent after him. You paid the rent beautifully, my friend, but I never sold you the house. The set's on the board and the grain's in my hand: I'm taking the throne."
aside: "The deal was always rented by the year, and this is the one year where nobody comes back to pay next year's rent. I drew seat two and the throne is seat one, so tonight the paper trail turns into a coronation."

Ruling: the tax is collected as declared, then the seat change stands (§11; the engine's swap checks the set, the 20 grain and the seats). A seat change may be made at any step (precedent Y2: Rich took the throne at the rent step). Clark becomes Emperor: he returns every piece he holds to its province's stock (the Land Deed Scroll to Province 3, the Dragon Jar to Province 5, both Wooden Bowls to Province 6) and takes over the crown's reserve with his own grain. Rich becomes the Official in Province 2, holds nothing and draws 60 from the Pile. Each seller must auction a piece this year: Reina the Scroll, Silke the Jar, Paddy a Bowl. Clark and Silke answered at the same time; the collect step comes before the land step, so the throne changed first, and Silke's renewed 500 for the Bowl reached a Clark who no longer held it (an act line says so).
