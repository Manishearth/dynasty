diary: Years Eleven and Twelve, in my own hand, and these are the last lines in the book. Rich kept his word for a tenth year and for most of an eleventh. He put up the last two links over my roof, at the crown's whole cost, and I've credited every grain of it, because the book was always meant to be evidence and never just a list of grudges. The total on the last page was what I'd said it would be. On the last night, with a Warlord in debt to the Pile and nobody coming back to pay next year's rent, I collected his four hundred and fourteen honestly and then took the throne with the set I'd carried loaded since Year Two. I'm not ashamed of it. I told him the loyalty was rented by the year, and I said it with a smile every single year. It cost me Silke's three hundred and then her five hundred for a Bowl. The second offer came with an oath, but it came one move too late, and I'd turned the first down because no sum is worth handing someone the key to your own chair. What I didn't count on was the coast climbing straight up the ladder behind me, Paddy into my old book and Silke over him, so the Official who signs for my taxes now holds two thousand three hundred grain and three pieces of a set. If there were a Year Thirteen, she'd be the one keeping a paper trail on me, and I've no doubt she'd keep it as well as I did. The table stopped, the throne is mine, and I drew seat two, so I'll call it a win and close the ledger. I paid her thirty, the same as I was paid, because a crown that changes the rate when it changes hands isn't worth bowing to, and I'd hate for anyone to find that line missing from my book.
