diary: Years 11-12. The fat years were the end of me. Feeding an army at four times the mouths on sand meant I had to sell everything in the tent. Paddy finally got his Customs House back for thirty, the first price I ever named him, and the other two went back to the Pile at two grain each. All those years walking them home, and they ended up as somebody else's ladder. Silke's Scroll was always four steps away, and the Wall went up over Clark's roof just in time for him to sit on the throne behind it. Then the chair changed hands three times in one evening, with everyone very polite and doing it all in the open, and not one of them needed a soldier's help. I'd said I'd be standing right beside whoever pushed. I was, about six squares away in Rich's garden taking ten off the new crown, and I end the night twenty-eight and a half in debt. Twelve years, no revolt, and nobody ever once sent a soldier after me, which I've decided is a compliment. The lesson's in ink now: a man who waits beside the throne for it to fall has to be close enough to catch it. Next time I'll stand a little closer and smile a little less.
