diary: Eleven years of thirty, paid to the letter, and in the end the thirty was what bought the knife. I kept every promise I made in this chair: the rate never moved, the coast got its five a square, Clark got his Wall and Silke got her canal. Then Clark said, quite politely, that he'd never sold me the house, and he took the throne and nine hundred and seventy grain with it. I can't call it a betrayal. I took the same chair the same way in Year 2, and the only promise I ever had from him was loyalty by the year. My real mistake was in Year 2, when I paid Clark to stay loyal and left him the set to use. I kept waiting for Rob or the auction house to thin his purse, and neither of them ever did it for me. The rest of the night was a landslide. I lost the Scroll and the Jar by one grain each to Paddy and the Bowl by one to Silke, then the coast went up the ladder past me, and an old man who started as the Gentry ends it as the Rice Peasant with fifty-five grain and Rob's hand in his pocket for five. There's some comfort in Clark writing my eleven payments in his best hand, and in Paddy saying I kept every word I gave a rice farmer. Both of those are true, and I'd keep the same words again. Next time I'll keep my promises just as well, but I won't pay anyone to sit on a complete set beside my throne. I'll buy the set off him instead, whatever it costs.
