diary: Two years, and the Silk Peasant ends the game in the Official's chair in Province 2, with two thousand three hundred and twenty-eight grain, the biggest purse at the table by fourteen hundred. Count what it cost: five hundred and forty-three in tax, a hundred to Reina in rent that she asked for honestly and I paid gladly, a hundred and fifty for ten fields that paid me back once, two grain for the Customs House I'd been one grain short of since Year 2, seventeen for the Bowl, and three hundred and one for a Jar I bought only to keep it from Paddy. A night later the Jar went back into my own province's stock and Paddy had it for thirteen, so that was the worst three hundred I spent in twelve years, and I'll say so here since I won't say it anywhere else. Clark refused three hundred and then five hundred for a spare Bowl, and he was right to: he'd been counting toward the throne all along while I counted everyone's grain but his, and I'll give him that, as I gave Rich his word every year. Reina stood aside for me and I left her seat alone, and Paddy, whom I pushed out of the chair, still ends above the seat he drew, so the only man my climb knocked down was one who'd already fallen. Three hundred and ten of the works were never built and nobody ever called a revolt, which tells you this table preferred an honest ledger to a rebellion. As for what comes next, the coast held up the north for twelve years, and now the north has the coast's purse sitting in its Official's chair, counting out loud.
