diary: Rob finally named a number in Year 11, thirty grain, and I paid it gladly, and the Customs House came home to R20 after five years in his tent. I paid Reina her twenty-five both years because she'd earned it, and I watched Silke pay three hundred and one for a Jar she didn't need, only to keep it out of my hand. I'd have done the same in her place. Then Clark took the throne on the last night, and all his pieces went back to the stock. I won a Scroll and a Jar for thirteen grain each, the cheapest pieces I've won in twelve years, and for one minute I was the Official in Province 2 with eight hundred and thirty-three grain beside me. Silke counted better than I did and took that seat from me straight away, and every grain I'd saved since Year 1 went with it. So I finish in the Silk Peasant's seat, one step up from the one I drew, with sixty grain, a soldier I never bought and fifty-four fields I never planted. That's still a step up, and I'll take it. I owe nobody a single grain, and every rent and tax I was asked for was paid. If we ever sit down to play again, I'll remember that whoever chooses last gets to choose against you, and I'll keep a little more back than twenty.
