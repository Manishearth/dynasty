# Robert Land's diary

## Years 1-2

Two years in, and I've already put one Emperor out of a job without ever going near the palace. One five-grain visit to Rich's garden took his set away for a single harvest, and that was enough to make him panic. He grabbed the chair the moment the harvest made his set whole again, and poor Reina ended up running the Pile. I've paid my taxes both years, smiled every time, and Clark has it in writing, which will look very nice on my character reference later. It cost me twenty for the third soldier, and he's worth every grain: he's standing one step from Reina's Customs House, W1 is walking toward Clark's pretty little set, and W2 is going down the coast to Silke's Scroll. Clark is the one to watch now. He has a full set and sixty-three grain, and Rich just tripled his pay to keep him sweet, which tells me Rich is frightened. Whoever ends up in that chair next, I'll be standing close by with a soldier and a smile, and I won't need to push. Just help them along a bit.

## Years 3-4

Years 3-4. I passed on every lot, sat back, and watched Clark spend forty-four grain on a Jar and a Bowl he didn't even need, just to stop Reina finishing her set. Then I strolled onto M14 and took her Customs House anyway, so now it's sitting outside my tent on N14 and her set's broken for a good long while. The bad harvest cost me twenty into the Pile, but the walk paid for itself: five here, five there, and I'm still up to forty-two. Rich is the one I'm smiling at now. He's down to nineteen and a half in the reserve after the palaces, the pagoda and all those half-price fields, and he still owes Clark thirty a year. Clark has ninety-nine grain and a full set, and he writes down every step I take, which tells me exactly who's measuring the chair. W1 is one step from his Jar, W2 is two from Paddy's Customs House, and W3 is going for the Bowl. When Rich's purse runs dry, someone will push, and I'll be standing right beside them holding a piece of everybody's set, ready to offer a little help.

## Years 5-6

Years 5-6. Two years, two more pieces in my tent: Clark's spare Dragon Jar and Paddy's Customs House, so now I hold something of Reina's, Clark's and Paddy's, and the only one left is Silke's Scroll, three steps from W2. The bad harvest and the new utility levy took a bite out of me, but five grain here and five there off the road kept me at about twenty, which is all a man with three soldiers needs. I'll admit I got clever with Clark's Scroll. I stood on it and took the five to look restrained, and now the square's been raided, so it's safe from me for good. That's a lesson for the night: a threat only works while you can still carry it out. The real news is Rich. He's ended two years running on zero, he pays Clark thirty every year without fail, and the Wall's still waiting, while Clark writes every grain of it down in ink. Reina's on her last few grain, and she's started dropping hints about counting carefully. Somebody is going to call it soon. When they do, W3 will be standing near the Winter Palace looking helpful, and I'll be holding a piece of everybody's set and smiling at whoever counts the votes.

## Years 7-8

Years 7-8. Two good harvests and I didn't get a grain from the Pile either time. The desert grows sand and sympathy, and the gods sent me bad legs one year and a runaway lieutenant the next, so for two years I could hardly raid anybody. I still got ten out of it, five off Clark and five off Reina, before the lieutenant ran off. I'm on thirty-five, which is poor, but it's enough. The real work was walking. W2 waded round the coast and now he's standing in the sea at Q26, one step from Silke's Scroll, the last piece I need. Once he takes it I'll have something from every set at the table. W3 is in empty Province 4, two steps from the Winter Palace, and Rich has only forty-eight in the treasury. He's finally started paying for the Wall, so a palace raid costing the crown twenty would hurt him now. Paddy wants to buy back his Customs House, and I'll keep smiling at him and pouring the tea, but nothing leaves my tent until the collection's complete. Silke is the rich one now, and Clark has nearly two hundred and still writes everything down. Next year the lieutenant's gone, my legs are fine, and every soldier I've got is standing exactly where I want him.

## Years 9-10

Years 9-10. The desert had a bad two years. The sandstorm took half my grain, and then I sold Silke a soldier and walked W2 right up next to him, and she took him off the board with my own twenty in her pocket. I'll admit that one was funny, even from where I was standing. Rich tried the same thing, bought a lad off me and parked him straight beside W1, so I captured him and took ten from the garden on the way. That paid for a new W2 on T24, so the crown's twenty bought me a fresh soldier. I'm down to about seven grain, which is thin, but I've got three men again, and the lesson is in ink now: one raider can't beat a guard, so W2 and W3 will go for the Scroll together from four rounds out, and Silke will have to pick which one to stop. Rich has a hundred and eighty-eight, the Wall's going up over Clark's roof at last, and Clark is still counting. W1 is sitting in Rich's garden three steps from both palaces. Paddy keeps bringing his purse round and I keep pouring the tea, but nothing leaves the tent until Silke's Scroll is in there with the rest.

## Years 11-12

Years 11-12. The fat years were the end of me. Feeding an army at four times the mouths on sand meant I had to sell everything in the tent. Paddy finally got his Customs House back for thirty, the first price I ever named him, and the other two went back to the Pile at two grain each. All those years walking them home, and they ended up as somebody else's ladder. Silke's Scroll was always four steps away, and the Wall went up over Clark's roof just in time for him to sit on the throne behind it. Then the chair changed hands three times in one evening, with everyone very polite and doing it all in the open, and not one of them needed a soldier's help. I'd said I'd be standing right beside whoever pushed. I was, about six squares away in Rich's garden taking ten off the new crown, and I end the night twenty-eight and a half in debt. Twelve years, no revolt, and nobody ever once sent a soldier after me, which I've decided is a compliment. The lesson's in ink now: a man who waits beside the throne for it to fall has to be close enough to catch it. Next time I'll stand a little closer and smile a little less.
