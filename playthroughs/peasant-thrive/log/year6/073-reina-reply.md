diary: Two years of paying for everyone's fields, and the man I was watching took the throne with them. I taxed forty twice, ran the crown down to five grain once, and paid Graham his fifteen and a half before I laid a stone, exactly as I promised, and none of it mattered once Graham decided Paddy's hundred and twenty was worth more than his grudge. I saw it coming in my Year 4 entry and I still couldn't stop it, because a crown that has to buy land for its own hungry province has nothing left over to outbid a rich peasant. Still, Paddy was gracious about it, the coast he left me has my own canals on it and four good squares, and I'm sixty-five grain richer than the crown ever let me be. Now I'm the one collecting pieces: I need a Deed, the Vase, a Brush and the Bowl, and every one of them is sitting in Graham's or Silke's stock. Silke is one Bowl from the throne and down to twenty-nine grain, so the Bowl is the piece the whole table will fight over next. I'll be patient, I'll read the I Ching honestly, and I'll remember that I promised to see the Wall finished, not that I'd be the one wearing the crown when it is.
