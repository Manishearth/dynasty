diary: Years 5–6. I bought five squares of desert while the crown was still paying five a square, so when the next card doubles the mouths I'll be the one with enough to eat. I took the Customs House off Graham for nineteen, after he'd offered me the Jar if I'd stand aside, and then I took the Jar off him for fifty-three because a promise from Graham is only worth what he's written down. It left me with twenty-four grain, the poorest peasant at the table, and that was deliberate, because at this table a full jar draws the knife. Paddy got his set and, to his credit, went up to the throne and not into my seat, and Reina took her fall with more grace than I managed in Year 3. So I'm one Wooden Bowl from a set of my own, and the Bowl is back in Graham's stock. He's the man I outbid twice this year, and he's sitting on a hundred and nine grain. He'll ask me a hundred and twenty, the same as he asked Paddy, and he'll enjoy every grain of it. So I'll let the fields fill my jar for a year or two, keep the tax book honest, and when he puts the Bowl up I'll be the only one at the table who needs it.
