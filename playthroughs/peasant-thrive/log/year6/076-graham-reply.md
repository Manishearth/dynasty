diary: Year five I bought three fields on the crown's promise of ten a square and got less than five. Reina paid the fifteen and a half back the next spring, first thing, as she'd said, and I've crossed it out. Paddy paid seventy for the Brush and a hundred and twenty for the Bowl without a word of haggling. That's a hundred grain to me from the man who took a hundred and eighty-three off me, so I've got some of it back, if not all, and he took the throne instead of my seat, which I'll write down to his credit. I lost the Jar to Silke at fifty-three. She paid every grain she had for it to settle Year One, and I let her, because I had nothing left to answer with. Now I've a hundred and nine and a half, five fields on the coast, a Deed on R22, and the Brush and the Bowl back in my stock. Silke holds everything but the Wooden Bowl, and I'm the only one who sells it. She has twenty-nine grain, and the nearest seat she could take is mine. So the Bowl won't go up next year. The Brush will, and Reina needs it. The Bowl waits until I've counted exactly what Silke would do with it, and what it would cost me.
