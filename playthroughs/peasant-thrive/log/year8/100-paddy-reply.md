diary: I kept the tax at thirty percent both years, finished Reina's canal in my own province and then raised the whole Wall in one go, four links and a hundred and sixty grain, so only the little Marble Pagoda is still owed. I paid half of every square anybody bought, and I don't regret a grain of it, because Graham was right that ten that comes back as twenty is the cheapest sum of the year. My Prince broke a five-hundred-year-old ivory figure and it cost me ten. I'd sooner not say what I told him. Then the auctions went the way they always seem to go at this table. Reina lost the Bowl by a grain, the same way Graham once beat me to the Jar. Silke got her whole set at last and took Graham's seat and every grain he had, which I'll admit I saw coming and couldn't have stopped. Now she's sitting on three hundred and seventy-five grain with the Bowl back in her own stock, and Reina needs only that Bowl to come for my throne. I've 151 in the reserve, and the Pagoda costs twenty, which is all that's left to end my reign. Next year I mean to build it and finish what Reina started, and I hope I'm still the one wearing the crown when it goes up.
