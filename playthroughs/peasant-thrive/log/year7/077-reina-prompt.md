## Reina's diary so far, in her own words

## Years 1–2

Two years in and the house is quiet, which is how I want it. I gave each of them a job so each has a reason to keep the peace, I taxed thirty both years, and I put the Reservoir first because it feeds Graham and so feeds the treasury. The Winter Palace came second; it's a cheap gate, and the canals for Silke and Paddy go through it. That tribute horse cost me four, and Paddy's salary costs five a year, and I mind neither. The reserve stands at a hundred and twenty, which is enough cushion for when the population doubles and land starts costing twenty a square. Still 440 owed and I mean to see every bit of it built. Canals next, a section a year, and I'll watch the realia: they've been gentle with each other over the duplicates, but the Jar, the Bowl and the Vase are one of a kind, and that's where the table's good manners will be tested.

## Years 3–4

Two years of keeping my word, and it went exactly as I said it would: both canals in Province 5 and both in Province 6, a hundred and sixty out of the reserve, plus the wall bill and Paddy's salary. And at the end of it neither of the two I promised still lives where the water goes. The good manners over the realia lasted one auction. Graham bought the whole set and threw Silke off her coast, then Paddy did the same thing to Graham a year later with the Jar Graham had sold him without a fight. So the provinces change hands, but the canals stay where I dug them, and I take thirty of whatever they grow. That's some comfort. What worries me is Paddy, now the Silk Peasant, with two hundred and forty-two grain. He holds a Customs House, a Deed and the Vase, and he needs only a Brush and the Bowl, and both of those are sitting in Graham's stock. Graham has sixty-five grain and a grudge, and he'll sell to whoever pays. My reserve is down to a hundred and six, less than any of them except Graham. So next year I build the Summer Palace cheap, I keep the cushion, and I make sure Graham knows the crown remembers its friends before Paddy's money reaches him.

## Years 5–6

Two years of paying for everyone's fields, and the man I was watching took the throne with them. I taxed forty twice, ran the crown down to five grain once, and paid Graham his fifteen and a half before I laid a stone, exactly as I promised, and none of it mattered once Graham decided Paddy's hundred and twenty was worth more than his grudge. I saw it coming in my Year 4 entry and I still couldn't stop it, because a crown that has to buy land for its own hungry province has nothing left over to outbid a rich peasant. Still, Paddy was gracious about it, the coast he left me has my own canals on it and four good squares, and I'm sixty-five grain richer than the crown ever let me be. Now I'm the one collecting pieces: I need a Deed, the Vase, a Brush and the Bowl, and every one of them is sitting in Graham's or Silke's stock. Silke is one Bowl from the throne and down to twenty-nine grain, so the Bowl is the piece the whole table will fight over next. I'll be patient, I'll read the I Ching honestly, and I'll remember that I promised to see the Wall finished, not that I'd be the one wearing the crown when it is.
