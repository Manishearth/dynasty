## Silke's diary so far, in her own words

## Years 1–2

Two years in, and I have the Brush and the last Land Deed for fourteen grain between them. The only fight was the first Deed, which Paddy took off me by a single grain, and then he kept his word to stay out of the second, so I'll give him that. Reina is playing the gentle Empress: thirty percent, waterworks for Graham, canals promised to me. She's sitting on a hundred and twenty grain, though, and a gentle crown can still be a fat one. The problem now is that everyone is short exactly two pieces, and each of us holds the last copy of something the other two need. Paddy holds my Bowl, Graham holds my Vase, and my Dragon Jar is the one thing both of them want. Next year that Jar is the most valuable thing on the board, and I mean to sell it last, slowly, and to whoever pays me with the piece I'm missing. Keep seventy grain in reserve, keep being the reasonable one, and let the men drive the price up for each other.

## Years 3–4

Years 3–4. I set out to sell the Jar slowly and let the men push the price up for each other, and they did, only Graham was the one who won all of it. He took the Jar and my Bowl in the same round, and then he took my seat. Two years of careful buying were gone in one evening, and he said it was for a single grain in Year One, which he had written down. So I'm the Wheat Peasant now, and next year Paddy took Graham's seat in exactly the same way, which taught me something: at this table a finished set is a knife, and whoever holds one uses it on the richest peasant nearby. My Deed went to Graham for sixty, and fifteen of that came back to me. The Brush cost me two grain. I have 111.5 grain now, the Reservoir, one Brush, and I'm the only one of us who hasn't been robbed yet this year. Paddy has 242 and needs only a Brush and the Bowl to take the throne. Graham will be auctioning the Bowl and a Brush, and I already have a Brush, so whatever happens I must not let Paddy have both. Reina should be frightened, and next year I mean to make her pay me to stand in Paddy's way.

## Years 5–6

Years 5–6. I bought five squares of desert while the crown was still paying five a square, so when the next card doubles the mouths I'll be the one with enough to eat. I took the Customs House off Graham for nineteen, after he'd offered me the Jar if I'd stand aside, and then I took the Jar off him for fifty-three because a promise from Graham is only worth what he's written down. It left me with twenty-four grain, the poorest peasant at the table, and that was deliberate, because at this table a full jar draws the knife. Paddy got his set and, to his credit, went up to the throne and not into my seat, and Reina took her fall with more grace than I managed in Year 3. So I'm one Wooden Bowl from a set of my own, and the Bowl is back in Graham's stock. He's the man I outbid twice this year, and he's sitting on a hundred and nine grain. He'll ask me a hundred and twenty, the same as he asked Paddy, and he'll enjoy every grain of it. So I'll let the fields fill my jar for a year or two, keep the tax book honest, and when he puts the Bowl up I'll be the only one at the table who needs it.
