## At the table since your last turn

Silke collects the tax at 30%: 16.5 from Silke, 10.5 from Paddy, 16.5 from Graham, 43.5 to the crown.

Reina digs both sections of the Grand Canal in Province 6, Paddy's, the crown paying the 80, as she promised last year. Each adds 10 to Paddy's harvest from next year. 280 of the works are still owed.

Graham: "The Jar's the last one in the world and it finishes a set, so it goes up at sixty. Anyone who thinks that's dear can wait a year and see what it costs then. Silke, I'll want your Deed, and I'll pay a fair price for it, more than a fair one if I have to."

(Storyteller's note: By the rules, a lot that goes unsold this year must go up again next year with its minimum at 2.)

Paddy: "Graham, sixty's steep and you know it, but it's the last piece I need, so I'll meet you there and not run off into the clouds after it. The Brush goes up at 2 again, same as the Bowl did. Silke, it's yours for a song if you want it. And Majesty, thank you for the canal. You said you'd dig it and you did."

Silke: "Well. Year One gets written down at this table and paid back in Year Three. I'll remember that, Graham, I've a long memory of my own. The Deed goes up at sixty, the same price you put on the Jar, so you can hardly call it dear. Paddy, thank you for the Brush at two. That's the first kind price I've had all night."

The auctions. Graham's Dragon Jar (minimum 60): only Paddy bids, and takes it for 60. Paddy's Bamboo Brush (minimum 2): only Silke bids, and takes it for 2. Silke's Land Deed Scroll (minimum 60): only Graham bids, and takes it for 60. Each seller keeps a quarter; the rest goes to the Harvest Pile.

Paddy: "The Jar goes on Q22, in a row with the others: Customs House, Deed, Vase and Jar. I didn't think I'd ever say it, but that's my whole set. Graham, you'll understand the Vase isn't going anywhere now, since it's part of what I hold, and I'd sooner everyone heard me say that than have any of you hoping otherwise."

Silke: "S18, where Graham's Brush used to stand. Two grain for it, and it's the first thing I've owned in Province 7. Graham, enjoy the Deed. Fifteen of that sixty is mine, so I'll thank you for the price you named."

Graham: "Sixty for sixty, Silke. That's fair, and I'll write it down as fair. Paddy, you've got your set now, so I'll shake your hand the way you shook mine, and I'll ask you to remember who sold you that Jar without a fight."

Paddy puts the Dragon Jar on Q22, Silke the Bamboo Brush on S18 and Graham the Land Deed Scroll on P21. Paddy holds the Rice Peasant's whole set (Customs House, Land Deed Scroll, Vase, Dragon Jar) with 58.5 in hand. Graham, the Silk Peasant, now needs only the Vase.

Paddy: "Graham, I won't pretend this is easy to do to the man who sold me the Jar without a fight, and I'll not forget that. But the set's made to be used, and I'd be a fool to sit on it. I'm taking the Silk Peasant's seat. Province 6 is yours now, and Reina's canal goes with it, so you'll have good water to start on. And the Jar goes up from Province 5 at the fairest price I can put on it."

Paddy Doyle replaces Graham Garner as the Silk Peasant of Province 5, with 242 grain (his 58.5 and Graham's 183.5). The Dragon Jar goes back into Province 5's stock, for Paddy to auction; his Customs House, Land Deed Scroll and Vase go on P24, P23 and P22. Graham returns his Customs House (to Province 5's stock), his Bamboo Brush and the Wooden Bowl (to Province 6's) and his Land Deed Scroll (to Province 7's), draws 60 from the Harvest Pile and becomes the Rice Peasant of Province 6, with its two new canal sections. The table jobs stay with the players: Graham banks the Harvest Pile, Silke collects the tax, Paddy reads the I Ching.

Reina pays Paddy 5 for reading the I Ching.

End of Year 4. Grain: Reina 106.5, Silke 111.5, Paddy 242, Graham 65; the Harvest Pile 1975.
