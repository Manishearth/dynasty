diary: Two years of keeping my word, and it went exactly as I said it would: both canals in Province 5 and both in Province 6, a hundred and sixty out of the reserve, plus the wall bill and Paddy's salary. And at the end of it neither of the two I promised still lives where the water goes. The good manners over the realia lasted one auction. Graham bought the whole set and threw Silke off her coast, then Paddy did the same thing to Graham a year later with the Jar Graham had sold him without a fight. So the provinces change hands, but the canals stay where I dug them, and I take thirty of whatever they grow. That's some comfort. What worries me is Paddy, now the Silk Peasant, with two hundred and forty-two grain. He holds a Customs House, a Deed and the Vase, and he needs only a Brush and the Bowl, and both of those are sitting in Graham's stock. Graham has sixty-five grain and a grudge, and he'll sell to whoever pays. My reserve is down to a hundred and six, less than any of them except Graham. So next year I build the Summer Palace cheap, I keep the cushion, and I make sure Graham knows the crown remembers its friends before Paddy's money reaches him.
