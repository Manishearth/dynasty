diary: Year nine was the last spring, and I never bought a square or set out a piece in it. An average harvest, two grain of charity, thirty percent to the crown, and Paddy laid the Pagoda between his two palaces for twenty grain. That was the last of the works, and the game ended there. I finish on two hundred and seventeen and a half, in the Wheat seat I drew, so it goes down as a hold. That's the right word for it, because I held it twice and lost it twice, and I'm only sitting in it now because Silke put me back. Silke has five hundred and seventy and Paddy's crown has four hundred and fifty-four. The one with the most is the one I never counted, and the one on the throne is the one who sat in my chair in year four. It cost me the Jar at sixty, the Bowl at a hundred and eleven, and ninety for a Deed I didn't need, and every one of those prices looked fair when I named it. Paddy said "an awful lot of fair dealing," and he's right, it was all fair. My trouble was never the price. I sold to whoever paid it without asking what they'd do with the piece. Nothing comes next for this table, because the dynasty survived and the works are finished. I'm glad of that. I said at the start I'd rather we finished the works than squabbled, and we did. If there's another table I'll do what I've written twice and done once: count all of them, every year, before I take a single grain for anything.
