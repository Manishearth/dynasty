## Paddy's diary so far, in his own words

## Years 1–2

Two pieces in the first year. I never expected that, and it cost me seventeen grain I could hardly spare, but the good harvest put it back and I've kept twenty in hand every year since. I told the table I'd stand aside on the second Customs House and the last Deed, and I did. Silke thanked me for it to my face, and that's worth more to me than any piece. Now comes the hard part. There's one Jar, one Vase and one Bowl left, and each of the three of us needs the two pieces we don't own, so no handshake can give everybody what they want. Graham will want the Jar as badly as I do, and Silke the Vase. I'll have to decide whether to fight for it or make a fair trade, and I mean to have that out in the open before a single grain goes on a bid. I'm sitting on seventy-eight grain and I'll guard every one of them until those two lots come up.

## Years 3–4

I said I'd have it out in the open, and I did, but the open turned out to be a rough place. Graham beat me to the Jar by one grain and took the Bowl as well, and he had his whole set and Silke's seat before I could catch my breath. I paid thirty-seven for the Vase and thought that was the end of my hopes. Then the Jar came back into the world at sixty. I paid it, and a whole set was sitting on Province 6, my province, with a canal Reina had promised me and actually dug. After that it was Graham who was one Vase short of the throne, with me holding it, so I took his seat. He sold me the Jar without a fight and I took his grain the same night. I'll carry that, and I mean to treat him fairly at every auction from here. Now I'm the Silk Peasant with 242 grain, more than I've ever held, and I'm still missing a Brush and the Bowl, both in Graham's stock. He'll know exactly what they're worth to me. Silke whispered that she'd back me. I'd sooner nobody owed anybody, but I'll remember she offered. What comes next is the throne, and I'm still a bit surprised to be writing that.

## Years 5–6

I read out the card that doubled the people and told everyone to buy land before the next one, so I bought four squares myself. I'd have felt a fraud otherwise. Graham asked seventy for the Brush and a hundred and twenty for the Bowl, and I paid both without a word, because I'd said I would and because he'd let me have the Jar without a fight. A hundred and ninety grain for two pieces is the dearest thing I've ever bought, and most of it went into the pile, not to him. Silke spent everything she had on the Jar to keep Graham off it, and now she's the poorest of us and needs only the Bowl, which went straight back into Graham's stock the night I climbed. I could have taken Graham's grain or Silke's seat, but they'd both dealt fairly with me, so I took the throne instead, and Reina went down to the coast with good grace. Now I've a hungry province, 103 grain, 220 of works owed and three neighbours who all know exactly what I paid to get here. I mean to keep the tax honest, pay my people even when nobody owes it, and buy enough land before the x4 card comes. I'm still a bit surprised to be writing "Emperor" at the top of this page.

## Years 7–8

I kept the tax at thirty percent both years, finished Reina's canal in my own province and then raised the whole Wall in one go, four links and a hundred and sixty grain, so only the little Marble Pagoda is still owed. I paid half of every square anybody bought, and I don't regret a grain of it, because Graham was right that ten that comes back as twenty is the cheapest sum of the year. My Prince broke a five-hundred-year-old ivory figure and it cost me ten. I'd sooner not say what I told him. Then the auctions went the way they always seem to go at this table. Reina lost the Bowl by a grain, the same way Graham once beat me to the Jar. Silke got her whole set at last and took Graham's seat and every grain he had, which I'll admit I saw coming and couldn't have stopped. Now she's sitting on three hundred and seventy-five grain with the Bowl back in her own stock, and Reina needs only that Bowl to come for my throne. I've 151 in the reserve, and the Pagoda costs twenty, which is all that's left to end my reign. Next year I mean to build it and finish what Reina started, and I hope I'm still the one wearing the crown when it goes up.
