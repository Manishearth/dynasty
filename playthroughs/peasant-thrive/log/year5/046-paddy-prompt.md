## Paddy's diary so far, in his own words

## Years 1–2

Two pieces in the first year. I never expected that, and it cost me seventeen grain I could hardly spare, but the good harvest put it back and I've kept twenty in hand every year since. I told the table I'd stand aside on the second Customs House and the last Deed, and I did. Silke thanked me for it to my face, and that's worth more to me than any piece. Now comes the hard part. There's one Jar, one Vase and one Bowl left, and each of the three of us needs the two pieces we don't own, so no handshake can give everybody what they want. Graham will want the Jar as badly as I do, and Silke the Vase. I'll have to decide whether to fight for it or make a fair trade, and I mean to have that out in the open before a single grain goes on a bid. I'm sitting on seventy-eight grain and I'll guard every one of them until those two lots come up.

## Years 3–4

I said I'd have it out in the open, and I did, but the open turned out to be a rough place. Graham beat me to the Jar by one grain and took the Bowl as well, and he had his whole set and Silke's seat before I could catch my breath. I paid thirty-seven for the Vase and thought that was the end of my hopes. Then the Jar came back into the world at sixty. I paid it, and a whole set was sitting on Province 6, my province, with a canal Reina had promised me and actually dug. After that it was Graham who was one Vase short of the throne, with me holding it, so I took his seat. He sold me the Jar without a fight and I took his grain the same night. I'll carry that, and I mean to treat him fairly at every auction from here. Now I'm the Silk Peasant with 242 grain, more than I've ever held, and I'm still missing a Brush and the Bowl, both in Graham's stock. He'll know exactly what they're worth to me. Silke whispered that she'd back me. I'd sooner nobody owed anybody, but I'll remember she offered. What comes next is the throne, and I'm still a bit surprised to be writing that.
