# Peasant thrive: narration

The narrator's lines, by chapter, each tagged with the log number it follows. Not part of the game: never in `game.py`.

## Chapter 1: Three Deals Honoured at One Table

- **(after 001)** Year one of Reina's reign. Every peasant at the table now holds a job from the crown, and only one of the jobs is paid.
- **(after 002)** The crown's first work is not a palace but a reservoir in someone else's wheat. Sixty grain into the ground, and 460 still owed.
- **(after 008)** Three peasants each propose that nobody fight over the first copy, and then all three bid anyway. Twenty-five grain changes hands; the Harvest Pile keeps nineteen of it.
- **(after 010)** Paddy ends the first year with two pieces, Silke with one, Graham with none and the Reservoir. The Dragon Jar has not left the coast.
- **(after 011)** The second year of the reign, and the first card: storms, a fine crop, and a horse that throws the Empress. Four grain, the chronicle notes, and no bones.
- **(after 012)** The Winter Palace goes up for twenty. It feeds no one; it is a door, and the canals of the coast are behind it.
- **(after 018)** Last year everyone offered to stand aside and nobody did. This year everyone stands aside. Fourteen grain buys three pieces.
- **(after 020)** Two years, two works, 440 still owed. Three peasants, each two pieces short, each holding the one piece the other two cannot do without.

## Chapter 2: The Set's Made to Be Used

- **(after 025)** The third year of the reign. The horsemen come through a Wall that was never built, which saves the crown three grain; the coast pays for a shrine, a buffalo and a feast. This is the year the Jar, the Bowl and the Vase must go up.
- **(after 026)** Eighty grain into the coast's water. The Empress names next year's work out loud, which at this table is as near to a contract as anything gets.
- **(after 031)** Three lots, three contests, and each settled by a single grain: forty-six for the Jar, thirty-nine for the Bowl, thirty-seven for the Vase. Silke bids on two and goes home with neither.
- **(after 032)** The first seat of the dynasty changes hands. The Wheat Peasant walks to the coast with 190 grain and one grudge settled; the Silk Peasant walks inland to the Reservoir with sixty and nothing on the board.
- **(after 033)** The fourth year of the reign, and a drought: average harvests, no disasters, nothing to blame but the locusts. The Jar is back on the market, sold this time by the man who bought it.
- **(after 034)** Both canal sections in Province 6, as promised a year ago in front of everyone. Four sections dug in two years, 280 still owed.
- **(after 040)** Three lots, three single bidders, and the two that matter go at sixty. Graham collects fifteen grain for the Jar.
- **(after 041)** The second seat in two years. Paddy takes the coast and 242 grain; Graham walks inland to the rice with sixty and the canals he watched being dug for someone else. Every peasant at this table now sits in a seat they did not draw.

## Chapter 3: Paid as I Said I Would

- **(after 046)** The fifth year of the reign, and the first doubling: two hundred million mouths, a fat harvest, and the sea and the desert open for sale at twenty a square.
- **(after 052)** Thirteen new squares in a single year, three of them the crown's own. The crown pays its five a square in public and runs out at the salary; one promise made in a whisper goes into Graham's book as a debt.
- **(after 058)** Seventy for the Brush, at the seller's minimum. Paddy now holds every piece he needs but one, and the one is the Wooden Bowl, in Graham's stock. The crown ends the year with nothing in its reserve.
- **(after 059)** The sixth year of the reign, and the Emperor's Share: every ditch the crown dug sends back half its water. The new fields yield their first harvest, and the crown, empty a year ago, has forty-five.
- **(after 060)** The crown repays a whispered debt in public, to the half grain, before a stone is laid, and digs the first canal under the Wall.
- **(after 068)** Forty-five each for the Jar, then one raise apiece: fifty-two, seventy-five. Silke pays fifty-three and goes home with twenty-four grain.
- **(after 070)** A hundred and twenty for the Wooden Bowl, at the seller's own minimum. One hundred and ninety grain for two pieces in two years; the Harvest Pile keeps three quarters of it.
- **(after 071)** The first change of throne in the dynasty. The Rice Peasant of Year One sits in Province 1, and the Empress of six years walks to the coast with sixty grain and her own canals.

## Chapter 4: I've Written Mine Down Too

- **(after 077)** The seventh year of the dynasty and the first of Paddy's reign. The Utility Tax sends four grain from each coast to the throne, and the woman reading the card built every work it charges for.
- **(after 081)** The Grand Canal is finished, two sections in each of three provinces. Twelve new squares this year, the crown paying half of every one; 180 owed, all of it the Wall and the Pagoda.
- **(after 086)** Three lots, three single sales at the seller's price, and one contest: forty-one for the Vase, one grain over Graham's word. The Deed and the Wooden Bowl stay beside the board: Reina needs both, Silke needs the Bowl.
- **(after 087)** The eighth year, and Careless Prosperity: the fattest harvest of the reign, and then a broken ivory, a washed-out ditch, a burnt house and a cold furnace. Thirty-five grain to the Harvest Pile, and nobody hurt.
- **(after 088)** All four links of the Great Wall in a single year, a hundred and sixty grain from the crown. Of the 520 owed at the start, twenty remain: the Marble Pagoda.
- **(after 094)** A hundred and eleven for the last Wooden Bowl, one grain over Reina's limit, and ninety-one for a Deed that Graham, by his own account, had no use for.
- **(after 097)** The fourth seat change of the dynasty. The Wheat Peasant walks to the rice coast with 370 grain; Graham, who taught the table what a set is for, walks inland to the desert with sixty.

## Chapter 5: An Awful Lot of Fair Dealing

- **(after 102)** The ninth year of the dynasty, and Imperial Charity: an average harvest and two grain each from the crown. One work is owed, the Marble Pagoda, twenty grain, and one Wooden Bowl sits beside the board.
- **(after 103)** Thirty percent, as promised, and twenty grain for the Pagoda between the two palaces. Of the 520 owed in Year One, nothing remains: the dynasty survives, in the ninth year, before the Bowl ever reaches the auction. The Rice Peasant of Year One ends on the throne; the Empress of Year One ends on the coast, one piece short.
