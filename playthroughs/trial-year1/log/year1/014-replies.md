# 014 — Lot 3, the Scroll, first bids

## Paddy (8,722 tokens)

move: bid 6; square R20
say: Six for the Scroll, Graham. Silke, there's two of them, so whichever of us gets this one, the other gets next year's. No sense us burning grain over it after the day we've both had.
aside: There are two Scrolls and only two of us need one, so I'm not going to start a bidding war over it. I've got twenty-one and I need twenty at the end, so I'm keeping it cheap and the harvest can carry me from here.
whisper: none

## Rob (8,797 tokens)

move: bid 8
say: Eight for the Scroll, Graham. Silke, Paddy, you've both got about twenty grain left and a whole coastline to look after. Bid carefully, now.
aside: There are two Scrolls, so even if I win this one it's only a cheap way to make one of them wait a year, and squeezing two peasants who are both down to twenty-odd grain suits me fine.
whisper: none

## Silke (7,954 tokens)

move: bid 5; square L25
say: "Five for the Scroll. There are two of them, Paddy, so I'm not fighting you for this one. Take it if you want it, and I'll have the other next year at whatever price Graham can still get."
aside: "I'm down to twenty-three and a half after the Bowl, and with two Scrolls on offer there's no reason to bleed for this one. A cheap bid in case nobody cares; if Paddy wants it, he can drain his purse instead of mine."
whisper: none

---
Storyteller's ruling: all legal. **Rob leads at 8.** Silke and Paddy are asked to raise or pass.
Note on Rob's reading: if Rob wins a Scroll it stays his until he trades or sells it back (Section 10, Placement), and only one more Scroll exists; Silke and Paddy both need one. So a Scroll on Rob's desert doesn't just make one of them "wait a year": one of them would need it from Rob. This is his aside, not table talk, so no note goes out; but the peasants' prompts state the count plainly.
Asides: all two sentences.
