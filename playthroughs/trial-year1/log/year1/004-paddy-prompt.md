## The table

Five players, Year 1 (a game runs about nine years). Reina Osorio is the Emperor (Province 1, the north-east, under the Great Wall). Silke Weber is the Silk Peasant (Province 5, the east coast). Paddy Doyle is the Rice Peasant (Province 6, the south-east coast). Graham Garner is the Wheat Peasant (Province 7, the south centre). Robert Land ("Rob") is the Warlord, holding the western and southern desert. Provinces 2, 3 and 4 are empty, so nobody pays rent.

## You, the Rice Peasant

- **Objective:** collect one Customs House (Silke sells them), one Land Deed Scroll (Graham), one Vase (Graham) and one Dragon Jar (Silke), and hold 20 grain. Then you may change seats with another peasant. You win by finishing in a lower-numbered province than you started in; staying put is a hold.
- **Your stock:** one Wooden Bowl (your own; Silke and Graham both need it) and two Bamboo Brushes (you never need them; Silke and Graham each need one). It waits beside the board until sold. Every round you must auction one piece of it until it's gone; you choose which, and you may set a minimum bid (at least 2). You keep a quarter of the price; the rest goes to the Harvest Pile. The Emperor doesn't bid; Rob may.
- **The pieces at this table:** each peasant holds one piece of their own type and a pair of a type they'll never need. The pairs (Silke's two Customs Houses, Paddy's two Bamboo Brushes, Graham's two Land Deed Scrolls) are enough for both peasants who need them. The singles (Silke's Dragon Jar, Paddy's Wooden Bowl, Graham's Vase) are wanted by both other peasants, and there's only one of each.
- **Placing:** a piece you buy goes at once on an empty, unraided square of your own province.
- **Grain:** 42 now (60 less 18 tax). From Year 2 your harvest is 100/75/50 and your people eat 40, before tax.
- **The Warlord:** Rob's soldiers move one square a round, after the auctions. One that steps onto a square of your province raids it for 5, or takes the piece standing there instead; a piece taken counts toward his objective (one piece from each peasant). You may buy soldiers from him at 20 each. They stand in your province, but can't move until he has raided it. A soldier standing on a piece guards it.
