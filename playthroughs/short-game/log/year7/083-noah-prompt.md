## Years 1–2

Years one and two. I lost the first Jar and the first Bowl by a single grain each, because I'd decided ten was a fair price and stuck to it. It was the right decision, and I'd make it again: the second copies came round this year and I got both at their minimums, 10 and 8, with nobody bidding against me. I also miscounted my own grain out loud in front of the whole table, 56.5 when it was 59. The Storyteller corrected me, and I deserved it from a man who calls himself the one who counts. Now the honest arithmetic. Clark, Rich and I each hold two pieces and each need two more, and each of us needs a piece that one of the other two is selling. The Customs House, the Land Deed and the Brush all go up next year, one copy of each. Clark sells the Customs House that Rich and I both need. Rich sells the Land Deed that Clark and I both need. I sell the Brush that both of them need. All three of us need the same things from each other, and all three of us know it. I have 66 grain, the most of the three, and I must keep 20 back at the end. That leaves me about 46 to fight with, and two last copies to fight for. Reina pays me seven and Clark five, and Clark has written that down. I suspect next year someone proposes a three-way trade, and I'd rather it were me, in public, with the numbers said out loud.

## Years 3–4

Years three and four. I said my arithmetic out loud, and Clark used every number of it. I told the table I had 88 to spend and split it 46 and 42, and he took the Deed at 43, one grain over my limit, for the third time in this game. Then he took my Brush at the minimum and the throne with it. Province 1 from a draw of 2 is a win if he keeps it, and he has 221 grain to keep it with. I did get the Customs House, so I hold three of the four pieces now, with the most grain of any of us who aren't on the throne. I also got a rule wrong in public, the hold-back rule this time. The Storyteller was right to correct me, and I wrote it down. Next year Rich's Deed has to come up, and so does my Brush. Rich has told me, politely, that a Brush means nothing to him without the Customs House I'm sitting on, so he doesn't need my piece, and I need his. Reina needs the Deed and the Brush as well, and she has 62 grain against my 115.5. My plan is to make one fair offer in public. If Rich asks for more than the Deed is worth, I'll pay the price anyway and say exactly what it cost me. A late seat on the throne is still a seat, and four years is enough time to take it from Clark.

## Years 5–6

Years five and six. I paid Rich ninety-five for the Deed without a single bid against me, which is what I said I'd do. I had four years of losing by one grain, and in the end the price was simply the price. With the set complete I took Reina's chair rather than Clark's, and I'd make the same choice again. The throne would have handed her a road straight back to me, and Province 2 beats the 4 I drew, so that's a win if I can keep it. It was not kind. She let me have the Deed and I took her seat a minute later, and I said it to her face because the arithmetic deserved saying, but I won't pretend it felt good. Then I lost Reina's Vase to Rich at twenty-one when my limit was twenty. That's the fourth time this game I've lost by one grain, and the second time Rich has thanked me for my figures. I'm beginning to think saying my limit out loud is a gift to everyone except me. Now the count for what comes next. I have 98.5 grain and lose about five an average year. My Customs House and Rich's Bowl both open at 2 in Year 7, Reina's Brush is still in her stock, and two years are left. I can't keep the Customs House out of anyone's hands a second time. So this coming year my job isn't to collect pieces. It's to watch who is closing in on my chair, and if someone's one piece away, bid against them for it, whatever it costs.
