## Clark's diary so far, in his own words

## Years 1–2

Years One and Two, closed and balanced. I collected eighty-five and a half for the crown and didn't skim a grain, and for that Her Majesty pays me five "for now" while the Scholar gets seven for reading a card aloud. Entered on page two, with interest. I paid twenty-three for the Bowl and the Vase, so I'm halfway to the set, and I have 58.5 grain, less than Noah and more than Rich. Very pleasing. Better still, they each need the Customs House and I own the only one. Next year it has to go up, and I mean to watch Rich and Noah decide how much they love each other, while I quietly go after the Land Deed and the Brush. Every gentleman at this table has been very polite. I've written all of it down.

## Years 3–4

Years Three and Four. I sold the Customs House to Noah for forty-one and bought the Deed and the Brush for eighty-three between them, leaving myself twenty-seven and a half, just enough. Then I declared the set and took the throne. Sixty-three for a crown is the best bargain ever entered in this ledger. I'll admit, here and nowhere else, that I told the table nobody else could finish, and then all four of my pieces went back into stock, which I hadn't read in the rules. I had to start the next page in a hurry. So the tax went to half, the crown's reserve is up to 209, the Winter Palace is built, and Rich has taken the Reservoir promise in exchange for keeping the Deed home for a year. I intend to honour it, give or take a split. Reina is keeping a ledger of her own now, which flatters me. The problem is Noah: he needs one Deed, he has 115 and a half, and next year Rich has to put it up. That gives me one year to make Rich love the Reservoir more than he loves Noah's grain, or to make Noah's purse much thinner. All of it is written down.

## Years 5–6

Years Five and Six. I taxed at sixty and paid fifty-five toward Rich's Reservoir, which he accepted with both hands and a full heart. Then I promised to bid against Noah for the Deed and was told, in front of everyone, that the Emperor doesn't bid. That's page three, and it's mine. Noah paid ninety-five for the Deed and then didn't come for my chair at all. He took Reina's, and stripped her down to nothing, which handed me the most grateful Scholar in the history of the empire, for seven a year and a few roofs. The doubling cost me dearly: the Pagoda, four squares of sea, sixty for Reina's land, salaries every year, and the reserve dropped from 258 to 106. Fat years make large families, and apparently they're all mine. But Rich paid twenty-one for a Vase he can't use, just to keep Noah off my throne, and Reina has promised to warn me early. So I have two informants and one Official who's two pieces short. The Customs House and the Bowl come back up at two next year, and that's where the danger is. Two more years. Keep the chair, keep Rich hoping for his Wall link, and write all of it down.
