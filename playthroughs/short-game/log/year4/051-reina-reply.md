diary: I said I'd stand well back from the knife fight, and I did, far enough back that I never saw Clark walk round behind me. He bought the Deed and the Brush in one afternoon, declared his four pieces with twenty-seven and a half grain, and took my throne, my canal and my seventy-seven and a half in reserve, and then had the nerve to pay me seven, "the going rate." The ledger I let him keep wasn't a pistol after all, it was a receipt, and I should have read it. The first thing he did with the crown was tax everyone at half, which tells me how long the table's goodwill will last him. I've started again from nothing and bought the Bowl and the Vase cheap while nobody else wanted them, so I'm two pieces from a set with sixty-two grain. Next year the Deed and the Brush go up, Noah wants the Deed as badly as I do, and he has twice my purse, so whatever I can't outbid I'll have to out-talk.
