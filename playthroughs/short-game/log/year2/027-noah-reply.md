diary: Years one and two. I lost the first Jar and the first Bowl by a single grain each, because I'd decided ten was a fair price and stuck to it. It was the right decision, and I'd make it again: the second copies came round this year and I got both at their minimums, 10 and 8, with nobody bidding against me. I also miscounted my own grain out loud in front of the whole table, 56.5 when it was 59. The Storyteller corrected me, and I deserved it from a man who calls himself the one who counts. Now the honest arithmetic. Clark, Rich and I each hold two pieces and each need two more, and each of us needs a piece that one of the other two is selling. The Customs House, the Land Deed and the Brush all go up next year, one copy of each. Clark sells the Customs House that Rich and I both need. Rich sells the Land Deed that Clark and I both need. I sell the Brush that both of them need. All three of us need the same things from each other, and all three of us know it. I have 66 grain, the most of the three, and I must keep 20 back at the end. That leaves me about 46 to fight with, and two last copies to fight for. Reina pays me seven and Clark five, and Clark has written that down. I suspect next year someone proposes a three-way trade, and I'd rather it were me, in public, with the numbers said out loud.
