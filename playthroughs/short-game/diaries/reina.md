# Reina Osorio's diary

## Years 1–2

Two years in, and the crown is down to 78.5 from 99. I spent it on a palace and a ditch, and both will outlast the men at that table. The Summer Palace opens the Wall and the Canal, and the first stretch of canal pays me ten a year until the end. Clark keeps his ledger of my "for now" like a loaded pistol, and I let him: a grudge he writes down is a grudge he isn't acting on. The trouble is that they've sorted the auctions out among themselves far too politely. Each of them sits at two pieces and needs exactly two of the three that go up next year: the Customs House, the Land Deed and the Brush. Next year is the knife fight, and I mean to stand well back from it, keep my tax gentle and my salaries a little uneven, and see who comes out of that auction looking closest to my chair.

## Years 3–4

I said I'd stand well back from the knife fight, and I did, far enough back that I never saw Clark walk round behind me. He bought the Deed and the Brush in one afternoon, declared his four pieces with twenty-seven and a half grain, and took my throne, my canal and my seventy-seven and a half in reserve, and then had the nerve to pay me seven, "the going rate." The ledger I let him keep wasn't a pistol after all, it was a receipt, and I should have read it. The first thing he did with the crown was tax everyone at half, which tells me how long the table's goodwill will last him. I've started again from nothing and bought the Bowl and the Vase cheap while nobody else wanted them, so I'm two pieces from a set with sixty-two grain. Next year the Deed and the Brush go up, Noah wants the Deed as badly as I do, and he has twice my purse, so whatever I can't outbid I'll have to out-talk.

## Years 5–6

I told myself I would out-talk what I couldn't outbid, and then I had seventy-four grain against a ninety-five scroll and nothing to say. I bought the Brush to keep it from Rich, and congratulated Noah on his Deed as though he owed me something. He didn't, and he said so to my face before he took my chair, my pieces and my thirty-four grain. It was the cleanest robbery of the night, and he did his sums out loud while he did it. So I've been an Empress, then an Official, and now I'm a Scholar in Province 4 with nothing on the board. Oddly, it's the first seat where somebody else pays for my roof: Clark bought three quarters of four squares of sea, and he wants an informant against Noah, which is a fair price for being fed. The table has stopped racing for sets and started hoarding them behind hundred-and-fifty minimums. Now Noah wants the Vase Rich has just taken off me, and Noah and Rich both want the Brush I'm holding. The Brush is the last card I have, so it goes up when it does the most good, and to whoever has done the most for me. Two years are left. I won't get back to the throne by Year 8, so I'll play for a full granary and decide who sits on it.

## Years 7–8

I said I'd play for a full granary and decide who sat on the throne, and I got half of that. I finished the night with a hundred and sixty-five, the most grain at the table, all of it from eight squares of sea that Clark paid for and then taxed at forty percent. Deciding who sat on the throne was beyond me. I sold the Brush to Noah for thirty-two and told myself I was watching the Vase, and the whole time the real threat was the Brush itself going straight across to Rich for sixty. I did warn Clark, out loud and in good time, and much good it did him: a warning only helps a man who has some way of acting on it. Rich's first act as Emperor was to cut my salary to five, which is about what an Empress who started in the chair and finished as the Scholar of Province 4 ought to expect. I drew the throne and I lost it, and I lost it in Year 3, not tonight. My granary is the largest at the table, and nobody has to pay for my roof, but none of that counts for much when I'm scored from the bottom seat.
