# Richard Baron's diary

## Years 1–2

Two years in and I hold half a set: Clark's Jar for eleven and Noah's Vase for ten, both sitting safe at L12 and L13. I let Clark have the first Vase rather than throw grain into a pile I pay out of myself, and I passed on the second Jar so that Noah owes me a kindness. Every one of those smiles cost less than a single raise would have. I'm down to fifty grain after two rounds of tax and two pieces, which is lean but enough. Next year the last three pieces all go up at once. I need Clark's Customs House and Noah's Brush, and they each need my Scroll, the only one there is. So the Scroll gets sold for the throne's price, to whichever of them makes me the sweeter promise on the piece I need, and I'll keep my end of it exactly as worded.

## Years 3–4

Two years, and the board has turned upside down. I stopped at forty on the Customs House, Noah took it for forty-one, and that was the end of my set, because there was only ever one. Clark then bought my Scroll and Noah's Brush, finished his set before any of us had checked our sums, and took the throne from Reina. That sent my Deed and Bowl home again, and I sold the Deed twice for the price of once, which is the kind of accident I can live with. Reina paid thirty for the Bowl, so I'm sitting on 103 grain, more than I've had all game, and I don't owe anyone a piece I can't deliver. I promised Clark the Deed wouldn't go up this year, and it didn't. I said nothing about next year, and next year it has to go up. Noah needs that Deed and nothing else, and once he has it he can come for my seat as easily as Clark's throne. Clark will pay well to keep it from him, and Reina wants it too. So the Deed goes up next year with a minimum high enough to make Noah sweat and the Emperor generous. Before it does, I'll have the Reservoir in Province 3 promised in public, and I'll smile at everybody while it's being built.

## Years 5–6

Two years, and I've gone from the man with the Scroll to the man with two Vases and a Bowl nobody can afford. Noah paid my ninety-five without a murmur, as he'd said he would, and then he used it on Reina instead of on me or the crown. That was the cleanest knife-work I've seen at this table, and I had a hand in sharpening it. The Reservoir cost me twenty-five and paid me twenty in its first harvest, which is the best bargain I've made all game. The second Vase cost twenty-one, bought only to keep Noah a piece further from the throne, and I've made sure Clark knows whose grain that was. Province 3 is safe, a hundred and sixteen grain in the barn, and I hold the seat I drew. Next year the Customs House and my Bowl both come up at two, and the Brush is in Reina's box. A cheap House and a Brush would give me a set and the throne in Year 8, so I'll go on smiling at Noah while I count what Reina's Brush will cost me.

## Years 7–8

Two years, and I finished on the throne, which is two seats higher than the one I drew. The Customs House cost me two grain because Noah had priced it out of everyone's reach for a year, and then I lost the Brush to him by one grain, the same one grain he'd been losing by all game. I thought that was the end of it. Then the Storyteller reminded us that pieces on the board can change hands, and Noah was sitting on twenty-two grain and a Brush he couldn't use. Sixty bought it, and I promised him the throne and nobody else's chair. I kept that promise to the letter, so he's the Official now, richer than when he drew the Scholar. Clark wrote my two Wall links in his book under favours owed, and I'm sorry he never got to collect, though not very sorry. I paid the salaries at the minimum because Clark's seven had been Clark's promise and not mine. I'm leaving with a hundred and thirty-four and a half in the crown's reserve, an unsold Bowl, and the throne. Nothing comes next, since the lamps are out, and that suits me: an Emperor nobody can depose is the best kind.
