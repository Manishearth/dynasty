# Narration

The narrator's lines, chapter by chapter: the chapter title, each year's opening line, and the turning points, each tagged with the log number it follows. The narrator isn't part of the game, so none of this goes into `game.py` or the playthrough page; the page must make sense without it.

## Chapter 1: Good Things Come to Those Who Wait

- (opens Year 1) The first year of Reina's reign. Four chairs, sixty grain apiece, and on the side table three pieces that each have exactly two buyers.
- (after 002) Fifty-four grain to the crown, twenty of it straight back out again as a palace on the eastern shore. The Customs House stays in the back room.
- (after 010) Twelve and twelve for the Vase, and neither man moves. Seat order settles what manners would not.
- (after 012) The first year ends with three pieces on the board and the three pieces that matter still in stock: one Customs House, one Deed, one Brush, each wanted by exactly two people.
- (opens Year 2) The second year of the reign. The Emperor's Share is drawn in a kingdom with nothing yet to share, and the crown digs its own ditch.
- (after 018) Three sellers, three second pieces, and three single pieces still in their cellars for one more year. The patience is unanimous.
- (after 023) Three lots, three lone bidders, three minimums. Two of four for everyone; next year the cellars open.

## Chapter 2: The Most Expensive Receipt in the Empire

- (opens Year 3) The third year of Reina's reign. Heaven smiles on the Official and the Scholar, frowns on the Gentry and the crown, and the three cellars must finally open.
- (after 033) Forty, forty, forty. Three sellers name the same price, and each of them is also a buyer at it.
- (after 036) Forty-one for the Customs House, forty-three for the Deed, forty for the Brush. The Harvest Pile takes ninety-three and a half of it.
- (after 038) Four pieces in a row on J13 to J16. Noah has now lost three lots by one grain apiece.
- (after 039) And so the third year of Reina's reign is the last. The Official's little book turns out to be a claim on the throne, and the four pieces that bought it go straight back to the cellars.
- (opens Year 4) The first year of Clark's reign. The Wall that was never built is breached anyway, the crown asks for half, and both cellars hold two pieces again.
- (after 045) The Deed and the Brush both stay home. The two pieces everyone is talking about are the two that aren't for sale.
- (after 048) Two lots, one bidder, two minimums: thirty and ten. The new Official starts her collection on the old Official's squares.
- (after 049) The fourth year closes with the crown at 209 and every other purse near a hundred. Next year the Deed and the Brush both have to come up, and three people want one or the other.

## Chapter 3: Ninety-Five Fits

- (opens Year 5) The second year of Clark's reign. The card is a levy the crown never asked for and will gladly keep, and the Deed and the Brush finally have to leave the cellars.
- (after 057) The crown keeps a promise in public: fifty-five grain of Reservoir for Province 3. The Deed goes up at ninety-five.
- (after 061) Ninety-five for the Deed, from the one bidder who said his number first. The Harvest Pile takes seventy-one and a half of it.
- (after 064) And so the Scholar declares his set and walks past the throne to the Official's chair. Reina, three pieces into her second collection, is back to none.
- (after 065) The fifth year closes with a new Official, a new Scholar, and the Customs House, the Bowl, the Vase and the Brush all back in the cellars.
- (opens Year 6) The third year of Clark's reign. The families double, the plates double with them, and for the first time in the dynasty the sea is for sale.
- (after 069) Four squares of sea for the crown, two for the Scholar at a quarter of the price. Provinces 2 and 3 can only watch the water.
- (after 072) A hundred and fifty, and a hundred and fifty. The Official and the Gentry name the same price for the same reason, and say so.
- (after 076) Two lots unsold for the first time in six years, and a Vase bought for twenty-one by a man who already owns one. Next year both cellars open at two.

## Chapter 4: Nothing but the Throne

- (opens Year 7) The fourth year of Clark's reign, and the second-last of the evening. The seed rots everywhere at once, and three cellars must open.
- (after 085) Thirty and ten becomes thirty-five and five. The first stone of the Wall over Province 3 goes up, seven years after the Summer Palace made it possible.
- (after 089) The Customs House, priced at a hundred and fifty a year ago, goes for two. The Brush goes for thirty-two, one grain past Rich's thirty-one; this time the single grain falls on Noah's side. The Bowl, at two, draws nobody at all.
- (after 091) The seventh year closes with every seller's cellar empty but Rich's, which holds the one Bowl nobody wanted at two. Noah needs a Vase, Rich needs the Brush, and each is holding what the other wants.
- (opens Year 8) The fifth year of Clark's reign, and the last of the evening. The villages grow by half, the harvest is good, and every plate at the table gets bigger.
- (after 099) The Bowl goes unsold for a second year. The last auction of the dynasty draws no bid at all.
- (after 100) Sixty grain crosses the table, not one of them to the Pile. The Brush that cost Noah thirty-two last year sells to Rich for sixty this one.
- (after 101) And so the Gentry, who drew the Gentry, takes the throne in the last hour of the last year. Clark, who took it in Year 3, goes to Province 3 with sixty grain from the Pile.
- (after 102) The table stops, as it agreed eight years ago. The throne has changed hands three times, and the last change came in the last hour. The works are still 510 short.
