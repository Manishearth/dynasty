## Storyteller's rulings

- **A palace placed without a square (002):** with no Warlord the square changes nothing; the Storyteller placed the Summer Palace on G20. Likewise the Province 7 Reservoir on O16:P17 (027).
- **Aside misstatements** are corrected by a note to that player in their next prompt: Silke's "nobody needs the Jar" (003) and "Paddy and Graham paid thirty-three" (010). Silke's "four of the five" (028) needed none: her next aside stated the set correctly (031).
- **Ties at the same limit** go to the earlier seat (the Scroll, Silke over Paddy at 6).
- **Both canal sections in one year** are one project (N13) (012).
- **Bidding only to block** is legal: any player may bid on any lot. A "pass; keep from X up to n" is a bid against X alone (015).
- **A public offer not to bid** isn't binding (§5): Graham's limit on the Customs House stood as given (017).
- **Year 2 lot choice:** Silke held only Customs Houses, so she wasn't asked which piece; she set the minimum in her bids. **Year 3:** each seller held one piece, so none was asked.
- **A crown work shared by agreement (027):** the Province 7 Reservoir is the crown's; Graham chose to pay 20 of it.
- **An unmet minimum (030):** Silke's Customs House at 15 drew only Paddy's 8, so it stays in stock and must go up next year at 2.
- **The Silk Peasant takes the throne (032):** offered as a short `free` choice with what each change returns. The engine's `swap()` refuses 5 → 1 at engine.py line 614, a bug (line 612 allows it at a table with no upper class). On the Director's ruling the Storyteller checked the set, the 20 grain and debt by hand and ran `swap(5, 1, ..., check=False)`. The main session has since fixed the engine, and the Director switched the line back to the normal checked `swap()`, which runs clean. Everything else swap() does applied: every piece returned to stock, the reserve to Silke, a 60 draw for Reina.
- **A new Emperor's jobs (033):** the table jobs are the new Emperor's to hand out, and the year's salaries hers to pay. In Year 4 Silke paid Graham his promised 5 as a plain act, with no prompt.
- **A live raise (041–042):** the Vase was CLOSE (22 to 21), so Reina and Paddy each sent one sealed raise; the higher limit won at one step over the other's (Reina 26 to Paddy's 22.5: 23.5).
- **Year 4 lot choice:** Reina's Customs House had to go up at 2 (unsold in Year 3); Paddy and Graham each chose their piece in a short free prompt.

The rulings below were made in the first take of Years 7–10 (set aside; `outtakes/years7-10-first-take/`). They are still true of the rules and stand as precedents.

- **A word before the harvest (Year 7):** after the card is read and before the harvest is paid, each player gets one prompt to give, lend, ask or trade (§5: any time), since the harvest forces sell-backs.
- **Unsold stock is sold only at auction (Year 7):** a piece left beside the board can't be sold privately; it goes up at that year's auction.
- **Sell-back before the harvest (Year 7):** a payment that would take a player below zero is first covered by selling realia on the board back at 2 a piece; only the shortfall becomes a debt. Scripted as `sell_back` just before `harvest()`.
- **Seat changes mid-year (Year 7):** a set may change seats as soon as it's completed, as in Year 3, offered as a short free choice with what each change does (from a dry run of `swap`). The player taking a seat takes its debt too. The tax falls on the province: it is taxed to whoever held the seat when the tax was declared.
- **Crown in debt (Year 7):** no work, no land shares and so no land for anyone, and no land prompts sent; tax collected pays the debt first; the salary is the Emperor's choice.
- **Pieces placed for a player (Year 7):** a player who names no squares has them placed by the Storyteller in their own province, the palace precedent.
- **The Emperor's Share (Year 8):** scripted after the harvest: only Province 7's Reservoir is a work outside the crown's province, so its holder pays the Emperor half its 20, 10.
- **An appointee's pay (Year 8):** at this table every appointee's pay is the Emperor's choice (N3). A reader's prompt that says "at least 5" (a seated Scholar's due) gets a note saying so.
- **The minimum named first stands (Year 8):** a seller who names one minimum in the lot prompt and another in the bids is held to the first, and a conditional offer to drop it doesn't reopen the sealed limits.
- **A standing fallback covers a better offer (Year 8):** if a player has said they'll buy even at a smaller crown share, a larger share needs no further prompt.
- **A piece held back can't be held back again (Year 9):** a piece held back one year must go up the next, even if the seat has changed hands. A seller with one type isn't asked which piece; they set the minimum in their bids.
- **A capped crown share (Year 9):** a crown share limited to some number of squares in all is a standing public offer; a peasant asking more goes back to the crown as a short free prompt.
- **Loans repaid from the tax (Year 10):** scripted as a deal act right after the tax.
- **A minimum equal to a sealed ceiling (Year 10):** the bidder buys at that price; the seller keeps a quarter, rounded down to the half.
- **A seat change at the auction (Year 10):** offered as a short free choice with what each change returns, from a dry run of `swap`; a new Emperor then settles the year's salaries and next year's jobs in a short free prompt.
- **History misstated (Year 10):** a misstatement of the table's history in an aside or a line gets no note: only the board, the cash and the rules are corrected.
