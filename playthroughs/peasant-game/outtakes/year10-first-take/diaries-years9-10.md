# The Years 9–10 diary entries (Year 10's first take)

## Silke

I spent two years sitting on the last Scroll in China while Reina circled it. In year nine I lent the crown thirteen to keep Graham's tax off my wheat. He kept the rate at thirty and paid me back to the grain, so he's as honest as I feared. I got the Jar for two. Reina paid sixteen for a Brush she didn't need just to keep it from me, and I gave her the Vase for two, which may be the kindest and stupidest thing I've done at this table. I put the rest into the ground: three fields of the Warlord's land, and Province 7 now earns more than a hundred a year in an average one. Then I set the Scroll at seventy-five, she stopped at seventy, and I bought one year. Next year it goes up at two, I can't bid on my own lot, and Reina has ninety in her chest, so unless Paddy's Scroll or some new trick turns up, she climbs. My answer is to be the richest peasant in China when she does. Whoever she climbs over, I'll have the grain to climb back after her.

## Reina

Years 9–10. I counted every shelf at the table and still got one wrong. I paid Paddy sixteen for a second Brush to stop Silke finishing a set, and she was four pieces short of one. Twelve of that sixteen went into the Pile because I was afraid. Then Silke let me have the Vase for two, and a year later she put her Scroll at seventy-five, five over everything I could spend and still claim. She'd learned that from me, and I watched her do it with ninety grain in front of me and nothing I could spend it on. I'm not angry. I lent the crown twenty-nine and got thirty-one back the same year, I kept the coast out of debt, I turned down two canals that would have cost me the claim, and I'm the richest woman at the table, one piece short of a set. But there are two Scrolls in China. Silke's has to go up at two next year, unless she finds a way to keep it back, and Paddy has the other one on R23 and reminded me tonight that he keeps his word. So next year I'll ask Paddy before I ask Silke. Nothing has been built for two years, so the game runs on, and every year it runs gives me another chance at the crown.

## Paddy

Two more years, and I've spent nearly every grain I had on mud and sea: Q27 the first year, when Graham found me four, and R27 the second. Both times I went down to nothing or close to it, but the rice will bring in seventy-five in an ordinary year now, which is more than I ever thought these paddies could feed. I read the cards for the first time, and I couldn't turn up anything but rot for my own fields, while Reina's coast had a fine year. The Jar went to Silke for two and the Vase to Reina for two, and I had nothing to bid for either one. Reina did pay me sixteen for my last Brush, though, only to keep it away from Silke, and that's four grain I didn't expect. Then Silke asked seventy-five for the last Scroll and Reina wouldn't go past seventy, so it's still sitting on Silke's shelf. Everybody at that table forgot there's another one, and it's mine, on R23. Reina needs one Scroll to finish her set, and she's got ninety grain. I'm not going to sell the thing I've held since the first year cheap, but I hate the thought of standing in anyone's way either, so I'll have to think hard about what it's worth to me and to her. Fourteen and a half grain, no debts, and I'm still the Rice Peasant.

## Graham

Years 9 and 10. Both years the crown's harvest came in at minus thirty, and the crown has no fields of its own, so twice I had to borrow just to stay out of debt. Silke lent me thirteen and Reina lent me twenty-nine, and I paid back fifteen and thirty-one, every grain on the day I said I would. I named the forty percent before the harvest, so nobody can say it surprised them. I ended the reign at nothing and owing nobody. That's poor, but it isn't in debt, and I've learned at this table that the difference matters. I put four of the crown's grain into Paddy's first square of sea and a grain or a half on every other square, and the table now holds twenty-one squares that I helped pay for. Nobody would dig the canal at thirty-nine of their own, so no work was built either year. The Wall and the Canal still stand between this table and the end, and I can't pay for either one. Silke asked seventy-five for the last Scroll and Reina stopped at seventy. Next year it goes up at two, and Paddy has a second Scroll on R23, so I'll watch who works that out first. Whoever takes this chair from me will find the books balanced. I mean to keep it long enough for somebody else to pay for the Wall.
