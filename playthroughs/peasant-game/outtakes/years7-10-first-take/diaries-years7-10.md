# The Years 7–8 and 9–10 diary entries (the first take of Years 7–10)

## Silke

### Years 7–8

Two years ago I had a throne, and tonight I have a ditch full of wheat and a Reservoir someone else paid for twice. Year seven was the one that broke me. The rains failed, my chest was already empty because I'd put it into everyone's fields, and when I asked Graham for thirty he bought Paddy's Jar instead and took the coast with it. I taxed Reina at ninety percent to get out of the red. I knew it was hard when I did it, and a year later she paid me back for it in full. Then Graham walked onto my throne with three short in the chest, and I learned what it feels like to be told I'd be forgiven for not being paid. I fought Reina for the Bowl and lost by one grain, after warning the whole table about her out loud, and then I gave her the reason myself. She took the coast, my nineteen and a half, and the two squares of sea I'd only just paid thirty for. What I've kept is the best land at this table, fifty-five a year, the bank, and the Vase and Scroll sitting on my own shelf, which nobody but Reina and Graham's old seat will want. The throne goes through the Silk seat, and the Silk seat now belongs to the woman who knows exactly how I play. So next year I keep my grain dry, sell the Vase and Scroll dearly to whoever needs them, and build the Wheat set again quietly, piece by piece, until the coast is in reach. This time I won't announce it first.

### Years 9–10

Years nine and ten were the years I stopped trying to win the race and started charging the toll on it. I lent the crown thirteen and got fifteen back. I sold Reina the Vase for two and the Brush cost her sixteen out of fear, and in the end she bought my Scroll for fifty-three, the most she could pay and still climb. She walked onto the throne with twenty grain and a smile and said she'd have done the same, and I believe her, which is the trouble with Reina. It cost me what I'd let myself want: the Vase and the Scroll are back on my shelf, but they're stock to sell now, not pieces of a set. All I hold is one Jar, and I'm three pieces short of anything. What I have instead is seventy-three and a half grain, the most at this table. I have two new squares of the Warlord's land, wheat that earns a hundred and fifteen in an ordinary year, and the bank. Reina's crown has fifteen and a half in the chest and three hundred and twenty of works still owed, and Graham is back on the coast with sixty and a long memory. So next year I sell the Vase and Scroll dearly again, I buy the Bowl and the Brushes when they come round in Province 6, and I keep enough grain dry that when the population card doubles, I'm the one who still has a field. I'm not announcing anything this time either.

## Reina

### Years 7–8

Years 7–8. The bad harvest took everything I had. The Vase and the Scroll went to the Pile for two each, and Graham passed on my Vase, bought Paddy's Jar instead and took my seat. That left me twenty-five in debt and then holding sixty and a wheat field I'd built for him myself. It's the strangest kindness anyone has done me at this table. I used it the way I should have used the throne: I took the Jar at his minimum and the Brush at Paddy's two, and then I counted everyone's shelves before I counted my own. When Graham finished the Silk set and took the crown, I saw that the only door back up was the Silk seat, and Silke was standing in it with a Scroll in her hand. So I bought the Customs House and the Bowl, made her a fair offer in public, which she turned down, and took the coast back from her. I'm sorry about that, because she played well, but she was the next one coming for Graham, and now she's on the wheat and that road is shut to her. I have sixty-two and a half grain, the Bowl and the Brush, and the Vase and the Scroll are both on Silke's shelf, so whatever minimum she sets is the price I'll pay to go home. The game could end next year, so there's one auction left that matters, and this time I'm not going to be the one who wasn't paying attention.

### Years 9–10

Years 9–10. I've got the crown back, and it cost me nearly everything I'd saved to do it. In Year 9 I threw away sixteen on a brush I didn't need, because I'd miscounted Silke's shelf, and she let me know it in front of the whole table. Then in Year 10 she priced the Scroll at fifty-three, which left me exactly twenty, the amount I needed to claim. That was the Vase at two coming back to me with interest, and I can't say I didn't earn it. The one thing I did right was lend Graham twenty-nine before the harvest. A crown sunk in debt would have been an empty prize, and he paid me back every grain of it the same evening, so I came into this crown with a bit more than nothing in it. Now I'm sitting on fifteen and a half, with a north that loses five in an average year, a Wall and a Grand Canal still owed, and all three of them richer than me: Graham with sixty on my old coast, Silke with seventy-three and every reason to come after me again, and Paddy, whom I'm paying to keep on side. Every piece of every set has gone back into stock, so the whole race starts over. This time I've seen how it's lost, and I'm going to count every shelf at this table every year until the last canal is dug.

## Paddy

### Years 7–8

Two rotten harvests, and the second one nearly took everything I own. I had four and a half grain and owed ten, and the Pile would have had my Jar, my Deed and my Customs House for two apiece. Graham wouldn't lend me six, but he gave me ten for the Jar. I took it and shook his hand, and that same night he finished his set with my Jar and walked up to the throne. I can't call it unfair. He was the only one at the table with grain to spare, and he spent it. Then the Bowl and the Brush I'd sold him came back to me, I sold them both to Reina, and with them she finished the wheat set and took the coast back off Silke. So my old pots have put two people in new seats this year, and neither of them was me. I kept my word on the Brush, I bought a square at Q26 with the crown paying five, and I've nine grain and no debts. I'm still the Rice Peasant, which this game hasn't let everyone say. The Jar's on Reina's shelf now and the Vase is on Silke's, and I need both of them again, so it's further off than ever. But next year I'm reading the cards as well as collecting the tax, the rice will bring in thirty-five in an ordinary year, and I mean to save every grain of it.

### Years 9–10

Years 9 and 10, and I read my first two cards, and both were bad for the rice. Even so I've come through them with fifteen grain, no debts, and two more squares of sea at Q27 and R27, which is more paddy than this province has ever had. I sold my last Brush to Reina for sixteen, though really Silke made her pay that, and it was the last of my stock. Then I sat and watched Reina gather every piece of the silk set, finish it with Silke's Scroll at fifty-three, and climb back onto the throne. That makes three different people up there in front of me, and none of them ever needed my Vase as badly as I did. I'm not bitter about it. Reina's paid me the six she owed me, and she's kept me on the tax and the cards, and a crown that keeps its word is worth more to a rice farmer than a crown that doesn't. What I keep turning over is that Reina's set has gone back on the shelves: the Bowl and both Brushes to mine, the Vase and the Scroll to Silke's, and Silke's sitting on the Jar. With the Wall and the Canal still owed, there may only be a year or two left, and fifteen grain won't buy the Jar and the Vase and keep twenty back besides. So I'll do what I've always done. I'll sell the pots that have come home to me at a fair price, save every grain the new fields bring in, and if the set's out of reach, I'll at least finish this game the way I started it, as the Rice Peasant who never owed anybody a thing.

## Graham

### Years 7–8

Years 7 and 8. I started Year 7 as the Wheat Peasant with thirty-two and a half grain and finished it on the throne. Paddy sold me his Jar for ten because I'd stood aside for his Customs House, and that finished the Wheat set. I used it to take Reina's seat, which cost me twenty-five of her debt and the Jar itself. Then the Pile sold me the Vase for seven and the Scroll for two, and with those I held the Silk set and twenty and a half grain. I paid three to clear the crown's debt and sat down in Silke's chair. She had paid six for a brush she didn't need to keep me from a set. It cost me about forty grain all told, and that's the cheapest thing I've bought at this table. As Emperor I taxed at thirty and paid five a square to everyone, which was what the last crown paid. I paid Reina exactly what I promised her. Silke wouldn't put twenty into her own canal, so nothing got built, and everyone heard her refuse. Now Reina is back on the coast with sixty-two grain and two of the four Silk pieces, and the Vase and the Scroll she needs are on Silke's shelf. Next year I watch what Silke asks for them, I keep the tax low enough that nobody has a reason to revolt, and I count every grain before it leaves the crown's chest.

### Years 9–10

Years 9 and 10. I sat on the throne with one and a half grain and borrowed to keep it out of debt both years, thirteen from Silke and then twenty-nine from Reina. I paid every grain back in full and on the day I said, which meant taxing at forty and building nothing. The north never got its square, and I'll be counting that against me until the population card comes. Then Silke sold the last Scroll to Reina for fifty-three, which was the exact price of the Vase she'd let go at two, and Reina walked into my chair with twenty grain and the Silk set I'd built for her by keeping the crown solvent. I'm on the coast again with sixty grain, the same seat I took from her in Year 7, and the board has gone all the way round once. Reina paid my appointees what I'd promised them, and I'll give her credit for that, but I lent to no one, broke no word, and still lost the throne to a woman who borrowed nothing from me. Next year I buy sea squares for Province 5 before the population doubles, I watch what the Bowl and the Brushes cost out of Paddy's stock, and I keep track of who at this table prices things to the grain.
