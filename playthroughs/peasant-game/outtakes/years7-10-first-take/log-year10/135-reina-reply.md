diary: Years 9–10. I've got the crown back, and it cost me nearly everything I'd saved to do it. In Year 9 I threw away sixteen on a brush I didn't need, because I'd miscounted Silke's shelf, and she let me know it in front of the whole table. Then in Year 10 she priced the Scroll at fifty-three, which left me exactly twenty, the amount I needed to claim. That was the Vase at two coming back to me with interest, and I can't say I didn't earn it. The one thing I did right was lend Graham twenty-nine before the harvest. A crown sunk in debt would have been an empty prize, and he paid me back every grain of it the same evening, so I came into this crown with a bit more than nothing in it. Now I'm sitting on fifteen and a half, with a north that loses five in an average year, a Wall and a Grand Canal still owed, and all three of them richer than me: Graham with sixty on my old coast, Silke with seventy-three and every reason to come after me again, and Paddy, whom I'm paying to keep on side. Every piece of every set has gone back into stock, so the whole race starts over. This time I've seen how it's lost, and I'm going to count every shelf at this table every year until the last canal is dug.
