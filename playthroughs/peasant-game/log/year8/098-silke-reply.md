diary: Years seven and eight were the years I learned what an empty chest feels like. The rot came the year after I'd sunk every grain into the ground, and the throne went into debt for the first time. I had to go to Graham with my hand out, and he gave me twenty, not thirty, and made me feel every grain of the difference. Then I taxed him at fifty percent twice, because he'd just bought the Jar that finished his set and I didn't like how much grain he was holding. He wrote it down. I believe him. I owe him thirty now, and his Wheat set can't take my chair, but it can take Paddy's or Reina's, and twenty of his twenty-two is "spoken for." Reina is thirty in debt with an empty shelf, so for now the only person who could unseat me has nothing to do it with, and I won't pretend that doesn't help me sleep. Every grain I raised went back into land: one square for the north, which finally pays its own way, and a half-share each for Paddy and Graham. Nobody built a stone of the Wall or the Canal, and I won't lose sleep over that either, because the game ends when the last one goes up and I'm in no hurry. Next year Graham gets paid first, before anything else, because a creditor who's been paid is a friend, and an unpaid one is the first to call for a revolt. After that the coast gets its canal, so Reina remembers who dug it for her.
