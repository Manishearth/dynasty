## Paddy's diary so far, in his own words

## Years 1–2

Two years in, and I've spent thirty grain on pots and paper, more than I've ever spent on anything I couldn't plant. But the Dragon Jar and the Land Deed were the only ones left in all of China, and now they're both sitting on my paddies at R22 and R23. Graham shook on it fair and square: the last Customs House is mine next year, and I mean to keep my side of it as carefully as he keeps his. Silke worries me a bit. She bid twelve on a scroll she didn't need, and now she wants Graham's Vase same as I do, and my Wooden Bowl as well. I've only 29 and a half grain, so I'll have to be careful. I'll save what I can, I won't owe anyone a thing, and I'll keep the Bowl back until it's worth something to me.

## Years 3–4

Two years, and I came close twice, and both times somebody beat me to the Vase: Silke by one grain, then Reina by one step. I can't hold it against either of them. I told the whole table it would finish my set, and Reina's just lost a throne, so of course she wasn't going to let me have it. Silke, meanwhile, played her game quietly the whole way through and now she's sitting on the throne, which taught me that you don't have to shout to win. The Customs House is finally mine, and I paid only two grain for it, so I've got the Jar, the Deed and the House in a row on my paddies with 42 and a half grain, and I don't owe anyone a thing. Next time I won't say out loud what a piece is worth to me. I'll just keep my twenty back, save the rest, and wait for the Vase to come round again, and I'll be ready when it does.

## Years 5–6

Two years ago I had forty-two and a half grain and was dreaming about the Vase. Now I've four and a half, and for once I think I've done the right thing with it. Graham's card doubled the mouths in every province, and the paddies would have eaten me hollow, forty-five grain a year. So I put forty-five into three squares of new land at R25, R26 and S26, and now the south-east just about feeds itself. I sold a brush to Graham for thirteen, and the other one's still on my shelf, since nobody had two grain to spare for it, and I've half promised it to Reina. What I keep thinking about is Graham telling the whole table he's waiting on my Jar, and that he'll be the one with the grain. I didn't give him an answer, and I won't sell the Jar cheap, not after all it's cost me. The Vase feels further off than ever, but I'm not in debt and I don't owe anyone a thing, and next year's harvest finally comes in my favour. If the game runs on, I'll save every grain the new fields give me and be ready when the Vase comes round.
