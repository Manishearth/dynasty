# Narration

The narrator's lines, chapter by chapter: the chapter title, each year's opening line, and the turning points, each tagged with the log number it follows. The narrator isn't part of the game, so none of this goes into `game.py` or the playthrough page; the page must make sense without it.

## Chapter 1

**Chapter One: You Already Had a Brush**

- **(002) Year 1 opens:** The first year of Reina's reign. No card is turned; the sixty is the harvest, forty in every hundred of it goes north, and a Summer Palace rises on G20. Nobody watches from the sand this time.
- **(Jar, after 008):** Seventeen for the only Dragon Jar in the empire, one grain over Graham. Silke keeps four; the Harvest Pile, as at every auction, keeps the rest.
- **(010):** Silke ends the first year with two of her four pieces and paid eleven for them. Graham ends it with none, and the most grain of any peasant.
- **(010, Year 2 opens):** The second year of the reign. The first card says the canal gives way, though no canal has yet been dug to give way; the harvest is average, and the frost finds Graham's wheat.
- **(012):** Eighty for the whole northern canal in one season, both sections at once. 420 of the works are still owed, and the crown has 84 to owe them with.
- **(017):** Silke, who needs neither, bids on the last Brush and the last Scroll. She gets the Brush for six; Paddy pays thirteen to keep his Scroll from her.

## Chapter 2

**Chapter Two: The Mandate Moves to the Coast**

- **(024, Year 3 opens):** The third year of Reina's reign. The villages are noisier by half, the harvest is good, and three pieces wait beside the board, one in each peasant's hand.
- **(027):** The crown asks fifty in every hundred, and spends it where it promised: the Province 7 Reservoir, forty from the throne and twenty from Graham. 360 of the works are still owed.
- **(030):** Graham keeps his word on the Customs House; Silke's minimum keeps it anyway. Fifteen asked, eight offered, and the last Customs House in China stays on the shelf.
- **(030):** Thirteen for the Bowl, twenty-five for the Vase, both to the coast. The girl who reads the cards now holds every piece her booklet asks for, and thirty-seven grain besides.
- **(032):** And so, in the third year, the Mandate moves to the coast. Silke takes the throne and a reserve of 129; Reina takes Province 5, sixty from the pile and a Customs House nobody would buy. Every piece Silke held goes back on the shelf.
- **(033):** The new crown's first act: Graham, who holds the pile, now holds the cards as well.
- **(033, Year 4 opens):** The fourth year, and the first of Silke's reign. The seed rots and the ox goes lame; only Graham's wheat, behind its new Reservoir, earns anything at all.
- **(035):** Thirty in every hundred, and only Graham owes it: three grain. The Winter Palace rises on H21 for twenty, and the coastal canals may now be dug. 340 still owed.
- **(042):** The Vase again, and close again: twenty-two to twenty-one, then one raise each. Paddy goes to every grain he can spare; Reina goes one and a half past it. Twenty-three and a half, and the Vase returns to P22, the same square, a different owner.
- **(045):** End of the chapter. Paddy holds three of his four; Graham two of his; Reina one, and the only one of its kind.

## Chapter 3

**Chapter Three: Every Grain into the Ground**

- **(049, Year 5 opens):** The second year of Silke's reign. The villages are no noisier than last year, the card having spent itself once already, but the harvest is good everywhere and Graham's, behind its Reservoir, is the best of it.
- **(051):** Forty in every hundred, and the crown builds the one work that must rise alone: the Marble Pagoda, on G22, for twenty. 320 still owed, and a reserve of 180 to owe it with.
- **(056):** Thirteen for a brush, one over Reina; six for a scroll nobody else wanted. Graham now lacks only the Jar, and there is one Jar in China, on Paddy's paddies.
- **(056, Year 6 opens):** The third year of Silke's reign. Fat years make large families: the harvest is good everywhere, the mouths are three hundred million, and for the first time in the dynasty a province may buy the sea.
- **(058):** Forty in every hundred, of nothing. No work rises this year; the crown buys four squares of water instead, and offers five a square to anyone who follows.
- **(065):** Eight a square for the coast, five for the south-east, one for the south centre, at the asking. Thirteen squares in a single season, and the crown's chest, which held a hundred and eighty a year ago, holds five.
- **(068):** The last brush in China opens at two, and nobody at the table can spare two for a thing they cannot eat. End of the chapter: the crown at nothing, nobody in debt, and two provinces of four still five short of bread in an average year.

## Chapter 4

**Chapter Four: On the Slate**

- **(072, Year 7 opens):** The fourth year of Silke's reign. The seed rots, the rains come late, and only the south centre has a harvest worth the name. Three hands go out across the table before the grain is counted, all of them towards the same man.
- **(078):** Twelve for the Dragon Jar before the harvest, six more after it. Graham's shelf is full, and he sits down again.
- **(078, the harvest):** The crown goes to minus ten, the coast to minus twenty-five, and the Vase and the Scroll go back to the Pile at two apiece.
- **(080):** Fifty in every hundred, from the one province with anything to tax. The crown climbs out of debt with five grain to spare, and nobody plants.
- **(085):** Two for the Vase, which comes back from the Pile to the south-east, to the square where the Jar used to sit. End of the year: thirteen squares, 320 owed, and the coast in debt.
- **(085, Year 8 opens):** The fifth year of Silke's reign. An ordinary harvest, and a card that reminds the provinces who paid for the ditch: ten of the Reservoir's twenty goes north.
- **(090):** Thirty asked for the coast, twenty offered for the Jar, and Graham declines both. The Jar stays on Q18; the coast stays at minus thirty.
- **(094):** Fifty in every hundred again, and this time it goes straight back into the ground: one square for the north, ten a square for one in the south-east and one in the south centre. Sixteen squares, and the chest empty again.
- **(097):** The crown cannot pay its reader, so the five goes on the slate beside the loan: thirty owed to the man with the only full set at the table. End of the chapter: sixteen squares, 320 owed, nothing built in two years, and the coast thirty in debt.
