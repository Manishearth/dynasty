# Clark Grafton's diary

## Setup–Year 2

Diary of Clark Grafton, Official to the Crown, end of Year 2.

I collected 120 grain for the crown in two years. I paid my own share first and in full every time, and Rob paid nothing either year, which is page one, lines one and two. I let Rich have the Brush without a fight and asked him in public for one Vase in return. He bid me up to eight for it, and two turns later he was sitting on the throne with my Customs House in his set. "I never promised it," he says. Quite right, Rich, you didn't, and I've written that down too, word for word. He has now given me a salary of eight, three over the custom, so the Emperor already knows he owes the Official something; I mean to find out how much. I hold the Deed, the Bowl and the Brush, all tucked in the back corner, and I still need a Jar and a Vase. Rob's W2 is now one step from my strongbox on J15, so the ledger has a new page, headed "Warlord", and so far it's all debits.

## Years 3–4

Diary of Clark Grafton, late Official to the Crown and now Wheat Peasant of Province 7, end of Year 4. I collected a hundred and eighty-three grain for the crown over these two years, paid my own share first each time, and Rob paid nothing, which takes page one down to line four. In Year 3 I settled the Vase in public and got it for four grain, the cheapest thing I've ever bought, and then Rob's lad walked onto J15 and carried my Land Deed off to a dune. In Year 4 I stayed out of the Jar as I'd promised and paid forty-one for the last Deed in the cart, and before the ink was dry Graham laid down a full set and took my seat, my pen, my seventy-eight grain and every piece I owned, the forty-one-grain Deed included. I'd forgotten the Brush tie and he hadn't: that's the lesson, and it's written in my hand this time. Rich built his canal and his Wall over his own palace and never built a link over mine, and that's noted too. I've sixty grain from the Pile, a reservoir on O16 and no pieces at all, and I still have the ledger. Graham has the pen now; let's see how he likes it when Rob doesn't pay him either.

## Years 5–6

Diary of Clark Grafton, Wheat Peasant of Province 7, end of Year 6. In Year 5 I put a minimum of forty on my Vase, Graham's fifth piece. Reina pushed him all the way to a hundred and twenty-two, and then Noah took his seat before the Vase ever came up. It finally went to Reina for sixty-one, so I'm now the man whose Vase knocked over two chairs in one auction. My quarter was fifteen grain, corrected down from fifteen and a half, and I've written the correction in too. I paid Rich ten for my own Reservoir under the Emperor's Share, which goes in the grievance column beside "never built a link over mine." I kept my promise and stayed out of the Jars both years, and Reina got hers for fifteen as agreed. In Year 6 I came second on every lot I bid on, each time by one grain: twenty-two on the Customs House, twenty-one on the Brush, sixteen on the Bowl. It's on a page of its own headed "Paddy and Silke, by one", and I'll admit it's not leverage. Silke took the chair with four hundred and fifty-nine grain, so Graham's pen has now changed hands three times. Noah's three-hundred-and-sixty-five-grain Deed is the finest piece of leverage I've seen, and I mean to learn from it. Rob's lads spent the year on compassionate leave, so page one gains no new raids, only six more grain of unpaid tax. I've a hundred and forty-nine grain, an empty shelf and a reservoir, and nobody at this table is watching the wheat farmer.


## Years 7–8

Diary of Clark Grafton, Wheat Peasant of Province 7, end of Year 8. In Year 7 I gave up the Deed to make a public deal with Paddy, and it was the best grain I never spent: Customs House for fifteen, Brush for thirteen and Bowl for seventeen, every one of them on the back row where Rob can't reach. The "Paddy and Silke, by one" page has a new heading beside it, "Paddy, settled", the first page in this ledger that isn't a grievance. Silke took the throne with a hundred-and-fifty-grain Jar, and the Storyteller has told me no link can be built over Province 7 because the Wall doesn't run there, so I've struck that line out in the grievance column and written the correction in, same as the fifteen and a half. In Year 8 I kept the handshake and stood out of the Customs House. Paddy paid fifty-one for it and took Noah's seat with the lot, which proves my old point that the man with a full purse and an empty shelf is the one who gets eaten. I won the Deed for nineteen, by one grain over Graham, the first time that margin has gone my way. That leaves four pieces on S14 to S17 and only a Jar to find. My promise still stands until Noah and Reina each have one, Noah has nothing, and Reina sells Jars and can't buy her own. That's a knot I'll have to untie in public, and soon. A hundred and forty-two and a half grain, four pieces and one Jar short, and the wheat farmer is finally worth watching.


## Years 9–10

Diary of Clark Grafton, Silk Peasant of Province 5, end of Year 10. In Year 9 Noah called the Jar "Clark's problem" in front of the whole table, so I took that as my public release and paid Reina a hundred for it, five years after I promised to stay out of the Jars. That made the wheat set whole, and I showed it straight away and took Paddy's chair and his two hundred and sixty-six, which I'd defend in any court as arithmetic and not a grievance, and I still kept the rent at five as he'd promised. In Year 10 I sat in the Gentry's chair on three hundred and seventy-two grain with four pieces tucked into H9 and J9, one Vase short of the throne and Graham's seven hundred. I accepted his Reservoir, the first work ever built over a province of mine, and I wrote my Vase ceiling at three hundred and thirty. Then Reina showed her set two lots before the Vase came up and took my seat, my pieces and three hundred and seventy-nine and a half grain, and she said the minutes would say I started it. They do; I wrote them. The Vase came to me anyway, for twenty-six, after it was no use to me, and W5 has since charged me ten for the privilege of keeping it on L25. The lesson goes in my own hand, the same lesson I wrote in Year 4, and the third time is the one that costs most: a set is worth nothing until it's shown, and the man waiting on the last lot is the one who gets eaten. Twenty-four grain, one Vase and a coast I didn't choose, and the ledger has a new page headed "Reina, admired and owed". Nobody's watching the silk farmer now, which is exactly where I started being dangerous.


## Years 11–12

Diary of Clark Grafton, Silk Peasant of Province 5, end of Year 12. In Year 11 I came into the auction with sixty-one grain and lost every lot I bid on: fifteen on the Customs House, twelve on the Deed, sixteen on the Brush, where Noah beat me at seventeen, and twelve on the Bowl. The Brush makes it "by one" again, and it goes on the old page. My Jar was the one bit of leverage I had, and it fetched seventy-one off Reina, so I collected my quarter and she took Rich's chair with it. A year later she took Graham's throne with a fifty-grain Deed, which means the Jar I sold her is part of a crown, and I've written that under "Reina, admired and owed" with "admired" underlined twice. When the population doubled I'd have been losing twenty-five grain a year, so I bought seven squares of sea at five grain apiece while the crown paid the other fifteen of each. That's the best bargain since the four-grain Vase, and Graham goes on a new page headed "Graham, generous", which I never expected to write. In Year 12 I won Silke's Brush for ten, her own opening price, and put it on P26, where no soldier can reach it this year or next. That's two pieces of five, thirty-three and a half grain, and a coast that pays a hundred and fifteen next harvest. Silke needs only a Deed now, so she's the one I'm watching, and I'm writing the lesson in my own hand before I forget it again: the man who buys land when it's cheap is the one still at the table when the cards turn.


## Year 13

Diary of Clark Grafton, Silk Peasant of Province 5, end of Year 13, and the last page. The Empress paid thirty grain for a canal on G12 and closed the book with it, before the auction ever opened, so my hundred and a half, my Vase on L25, my Brush on P26 and seven squares of sea that were supposed to pay a hundred and fifteen next harvest all stop counting at the same moment. I paid my eighteen in full, the way I have every year since I held the pen. Rob refused again, so page one closes on line thirteen and nobody is ever going to settle it. I drew the Official's chair and I'm leaving as a silk farmer, and the record shows that as a loss. I won't argue with it, because I sat in four seats and every one I lost went to a set somebody showed while I was still waiting on a last lot. The lesson I wrote three times turned out to be the whole game: Reina showed hers first, and in the end she showed the treasury too, and the one who decides when the game stops never has to wait on a lot. "Paddy, settled" holds, "Graham, generous" holds, and under "Reina, admired and owed" I've struck out "owed" and underlined "admired" a third time. The ledger is closed, but I'm keeping it, because next game I mean to read it before the first lot instead of after the last.
