# Narration

The narrator's lines, chapter by chapter: the chapter title, each year's opening line, and the turning points, each tagged with the log number it follows. The narrator isn't part of the game, so none of this goes into `game.py` or the playthrough page; the page must make sense without it.

## Chapter 1

**Chapter One: Very Civilised.**

- **(001)** Four soldiers take the desert's four best views: one under the Wall, two in the pockets of sand between the provinces, one below the rice. Every province but the Emperor's is a single step from a visitor.
- **(Year 1, before 002)** The first year of Reina's reign. Sixty each, no card turned, and twenty-four pieces of treasure waiting beside the board for five buyers apiece.
- **(005)** The Summer Palace goes up on G26, as far from the sand as the province reaches. The works owed stand at 1,010.
- **(020)** Six lots, and not one sells for more than eight. Rich carries home two, Clark two, Noah and Silke one each; Paddy and Graham bid on ten lots between them and win none.
- **(021)** Four soldiers step over four borders, and the desert collects twenty on its first night out. The Gentry, alone, receives two visitors.
- **(Year 2, before 022)** The second year of the reign. The ox goes lame and every field comes in thin; the crown, eating more than it grows, pays ten back into the pile.
- **(024)** Sixty grain from the crown for a reservoir in the wheat. The works owed fall to 950.
- **(034)** In the second year of her reign, Reina's Gentry lays a Customs House, a Bowl, a Vase, a Brush and a Jar on the table, and the throne changes hands. The first dynasty lasts two harvests; the chronicle records that it was very civilised.
- **(037)** The new Emperor's first year ends with five soldiers on the map and four of them standing inside other people's borders. The desert has collected forty; the crown, from the desert, nothing.

## Chapter 2

**Chapter Two: I'll Remember the Price.**

- **(Year 3, before 040)** The first full year of Rich's reign. Steady weather, an average harvest everywhere, and the Emperor's water is about to run past his own palace first.
- **(043)** Eighty grain for two sections of canal in Province 1. The works owed fall to 870, and the crown's own harvest rises by twenty.
- **(046)** Three bidders agree in public who will not bid against whom. At this table, the Pile has rarely been so poorly fed.
- **(055)** Graham, who came to the table with nothing and bid on eleven lots without a win, carries home three pieces in one afternoon. The Land Deed alone costs him eighteen, the dearest piece of the reign so far.
- **(060)** In the third year the desert takes its first treasure: Clark's Land Deed, from J15, set down in the sand on E8. The chronicle notes that it stood one step from a soldier for a whole year.
- **(Year 4, before 061)** The fourth year, and the second of Rich's reign. A fat harvest for everyone, and with it a small misfortune each: a horse, a chariot wheel, a chef, a lamp, a shrine, a buffalo, a daughter, and the Warlord's legs.
- **(064)** A hundred and sixty grain, and four links of the Great Wall go up in one season above the Emperor's own province. The works owed fall to 710.
- **(072)** Graham, who in two years won nothing, shows a Customs House, a Deed, a Brush, a Jar and a Bowl, and takes the Official's seat. The Deed Clark paid forty-one for an hour earlier goes back into the cart.
- **(076)** The desert's second treasure: Noah's Customs House, from L22, one step from the soldier he had said aloud he'd noticed. It stands on F8, beside Clark's old Deed.

## Chapter 3

**Chapter Three: A Price for a Throne.**

- **(Year 5, before 078)** The fifth year, the third of Rich's reign. An average harvest, and a card reminding the provinces who paid for the ditch; only one ditch outside the palace grounds has anything to share, and it is Clark's.
- **(082)** Twenty grain for a Winter Palace on H21, so that canals may one day run south. The works owed fall to 690.
- **(089)** Four players hold four pieces each when the bidding opens. Six lots, and three of them are somebody's fifth.
- **(092)** On the limits, Graham buys the Vase for 123, exactly three times the dearest piece before it. Before it is sold, Noah lays down a full set and takes the Official's seat instead of the throne; Graham's four pieces go back in the cart, and the 123 is never paid.
- **(094)** The Vase goes to Reina for 61, and with it her set. She too passes over the throne and takes the Official's seat, from Noah. Province 2 has three Officials in one auction; the Emperor, who bid on nothing, keeps his chair.
- **(096)** The desert's third treasure: Paddy's Jar, from Q25, one step from the soldier who had stood beside it since Year 3. It stands on T22.
- **(Year 6, before 097)** The sixth year, the fourth of Rich's reign, and the Scholar reading the card is the man who lost a seat at the last one. A good harvest, horsemen through the Wall, and a disaster for everyone south of it, the Warlord included: no raids this year.
- **(100)** Eighty grain for both canal sections through Paddy's fields, the crown's first work south of its own province since the Reservoir. The works owed fall to 610.
- **(102)** Reina offers thirty for a Land Deed. Noah sets the minimum at 365, half a grain more than she could pay and still show a set, and the lot passes without a bid.
- **(112)** Silke, who lost every lot she bid on for four years, lays down her set and takes the Official's seat from Reina, and Reina's 391 with it. The Official's seat has changed hands four times in two years; the throne, not once.


## Chapter 4

- **(Year 7, before 116)** The seventh year, the fifth of Rich's reign. An average harvest, and the Emperor moved, slightly, by the suffering of his people: two grain each. Silke sits on 496 and needs one Jar; the Jars are in Reina's cart.
- **(118)** Eighty grain for both canal sections through Reina's fields, the year after Silke took Reina's chair. The works owed fall to 530.
- **(123)** Reina sets her Jar at 150. Nobody argues; there is nobody to argue with.
- **(128)** Clark offers Paddy the Deed for the Customs House; Paddy adds next year to the bargain, and Clark shakes on it. The first page of Clark's ledger that is not a grievance.
- **(129)** A Jar for 150, and Silke does what nobody else at this table would: she takes the throne. Rich, Emperor since Year 2, is the Official, with sixty grain and the tax to collect.
- **(133)** Five soldiers, five raids, twenty-five grain, and not a piece among them. W3 stands three steps from Graham's new Vase.
- **(Year 8, before 134)** The eighth year, the first of Silke's reign. Heaven has favourites, and the throne's own fields are not among them. Paddy needs one Customs House; Clark has promised not to bid for it.
- **(136)** Forty percent, the first change in the tax since Year 1, and eighty grain for Graham's canal. The works owed fall to 450.
- **(141)** Reina sets 50 on the Customs House to keep it from Paddy, so that Silke's reserve won't go to him. It was never within his reach.
- **(145)** Fifty-one grain, and the rice farmer who came home with an empty cart in Year 1 lays down a full set. He takes Noah's seat and Noah's 181; it is the third time Noah has sat on a full purse with nothing in hand.
- **(149)** Twenty-six against twenty-six, and the seat order that cost Graham the Brush in Year 1 gives him the Bowl.
- **(153)** Five raids, twenty-five grain, and still not a piece. W3 stands one step from Graham's Bowl.

**Chapter Four: Paddy, Settled.**


## Chapter 5

- **(Year 9, before 155)** The ninth year, the third of Silke's reign. The seed rots everywhere at once, and a new house rule says no lot may sit unsold two years running. Three players stand one or two pieces from a set.
- **(158)** A hundred and sixty for the Wall over Rich's fields. The works owed fall to 290: Paddy's Wall, the Irrigation, the Reservoir and the Pagoda.
- **(166)** Two lots in, Graham does not wait. The man who lost his chair in Year 5 holding four pieces takes the throne and Silke's 490.5; the crown has now moved twice in three years, after five years of not moving at all.
- **(169)** Clark takes Paddy's chair one lot before Paddy's Bowl comes up. Paddy, settled for exactly one year, buys the Bowl anyway, as a wheat farmer.
- **(171)** Five raids, twenty-five grain, and W4 stands one step from Reina's Brush.
- **(Year 10, before 172)** The tenth year, the first of Graham's reign. A fine crop and a fine season for bandits: four disasters, and the Warlord's own men walk off with half his tent. The crown holds 639; the Gentry, 370, needs one Vase.
- **(174)** Eighty grain for a Reservoir over Clark's fields, the first work ever built in a province of his. The works owed fall to 210.
- **(185)** Reina shows her set one lot before Clark's Vase. The Gentry's chair has now had three owners in two markets, and each took it from someone one piece short.
- **(189)** The Vase goes to Clark anyway, for twenty-six, and it sits on his new coast completing nothing.
- **(190)** Five raids, thirty grain, and three soldiers stand within two steps of the three names Rob still needs.

**Chapter Five: What One More Round Costs.**

## Chapter 6

- **(Year 11, before 199)** The eleventh year, the second of Graham's reign. The harvest is good and the villages are fuller: the counter turns to 150 million, the first time it has moved in ten years. Reina needs one Jar, and Clark has two to sell.
- **(202)** Twenty grain for a Pagoda on G18. The works owed fall to 190, in two projects, and both of them sit over somebody else's fields.
- **(211)** Reina completes her set with the throne in reach and takes the Official's chair instead, one lot before Rich would have completed his. It is the third time she has declined the throne, and the first time she has done it to the man who took it from her.
- **(215)** W3 walks into the Winter Palace for twenty, and the desert counts a palace among its trophies. Two names left on the Warlord's list.
- **(Year 12, before 216)** The twelfth year, the third of Graham's reign. The counter doubles to 300 million and the fields stop feeding the table; new land opens, and every province but the Warlord's is offered it.
- **(219)** A hundred and sixty for the last Wall, and the works owed fall to 30: one Irrigation System stands between the table and the end of the game.
- **(224)** Thirty-four squares of new land in one season, and the crown pays for most of them. The reserve that stood at 694 a year ago is down to 6.
- **(232)** Fifty for a Deed, the price Rich named, and Reina takes the throne she drew in Year One and lost in Year Two. She inherits a crown of six grain and the duty to pay sixteen in salaries.
- **(235)** Five more raids, and Reina, a day into her reign, pays the first of them.

**Chapter Six: Fifty, to the Letter.**

## Chapter 7

- **(Year 13, before 244)** The thirteenth year, the first of Reina's reign. The seed has rotted and the ox is lame, and thirty grain of ditch-digging in Province 2 is all that stands between the table and the end of the game.
- **(246)** The Empress declares the last work at the crown's whole cost, thirty grain, and the auction hall stays shut behind it.
- **(248)** Graham names two squares at the back of his province, the ditch goes in, and every work the country owed is standing: 1,030 in thirteen years. The book closes with Silke one Deed short and two soldiers a step from her garden.
- **(256)** Eight last words, and the Warlord's is the only one that mentions a march. Reina keeps the throne she drew in Year One, and the reign is, by the rules, a glorious one.
