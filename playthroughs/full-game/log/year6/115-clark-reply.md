diary: Diary of Clark Grafton, Wheat Peasant of Province 7, end of Year 6. In Year 5 I put a minimum of forty on my Vase, Graham's fifth piece. Reina pushed him all the way to a hundred and twenty-two, and then Noah took his seat before the Vase ever came up. It finally went to Reina for sixty-one, so I'm now the man whose Vase knocked over two chairs in one auction. My quarter was fifteen grain, corrected down from fifteen and a half, and I've written the correction in too. I paid Rich ten for my own Reservoir under the Emperor's Share, which goes in the grievance column beside "never built a link over mine." I kept my promise and stayed out of the Jars both years, and Reina got hers for fifteen as agreed. In Year 6 I came second on every lot I bid on, each time by one grain: twenty-two on the Customs House, twenty-one on the Brush, sixteen on the Bowl. It's on a page of its own headed "Paddy and Silke, by one", and I'll admit it's not leverage. Silke took the chair with four hundred and fifty-nine grain, so Graham's pen has now changed hands three times. Noah's three-hundred-and-sixty-five-grain Deed is the finest piece of leverage I've seen, and I mean to learn from it. Rob's lads spent the year on compassionate leave, so page one gains no new raids, only six more grain of unpaid tax. I've a hundred and forty-nine grain, an empty shelf and a reservoir, and nobody at this table is watching the wheat farmer.
