diary: Year Six, from the Winter Palace, which still smells of new plaster. In one evening the Official's chair went round the table three times, from Graham to Noah, Noah to Reina and Reina to Silke, and each of them walked off with the last one's purse. Meanwhile I sat on the throne and smiled, and the throne was the one seat nobody wanted. That isn't flattery, it's arithmetic: whoever takes the throne takes it with someone else's Vase two minutes behind them. I kept the tax at thirty and called it moderate again. I put a palace at H21, where no soldier's boots can reach it, and dug two canals through Paddy's fields at the crown's cost, so his harvest is twenty a year fatter and he owes me a kindness. Noah was right to correct me: a ditch pays the crown only in the year his card says so, and I'll remember that to the letter, as I remember everything. The works owed are down to 610 and the granary holds 245, and what keeps me awake is Silke. She sits on four hundred and fifty-nine grain, one Dragon Jar short of my chair, and the only Jars for sale are in Reina's cart, and Reina has every reason to remember who took her purse. Noah priced his Deed at 365 and called it a price for a throne, and I'll be paying very close attention to what Reina charges for a Jar. I'll keep smiling and keep paying Silke her eight, and every grain I don't spend is a grain she's waiting to inherit.
