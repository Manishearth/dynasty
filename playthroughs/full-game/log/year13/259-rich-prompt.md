## At the table since your last turn

Silke: "Majesty, I read the card that said the harvest would be bad, and I never thought to read the treasury. Thirty grain to close the book, and you closed it with four of my five pieces on the table. A well-run reign, and I mean it. I drew the Scholar and I'm finishing as the Scholar, but in between I sat on the throne and in the Official's chair, and I paid a hundred and fifty for a Jar without blinking. Rich, fifty was the price, and you kept your word to the letter, as you said in Year 1. I'll remember the letter."

Clark: "Majesty, I drew the Official's pen and I'm leaving with a silk farmer's hoe and a hundred and a half grain, and every chair I lost along the way went to a full set on the table, which is fair, and I've got all of it written down. Thirteen years: I paid eighteen this harvest and I paid in full every year, and Rob paid nothing, so page one closes on line thirteen. Graham, you get your 'generous' page for the sea, Paddy, you're still 'settled', and Reina, you finish where you've been since Year 9, under 'admired and owed', with 'admired' underlined three times now. Noah: "Majesty, a glorious reign, and I'll say the sum out loud one last time, because somebody has to. I drew the Scholar's chair with fifty-three and a half grain, and I'm leaving as the Rice Peasant with a hundred and forty-five and a half, three pieces nobody could reach on S23 to S25, and a Deed and a Jar I never got. That's down one chair and up ninety-two grain, and most of the lots I lost, I lost by exactly one grain. Silke, neither of us got that Deed, so my diary's last promise is the only one at this table that was never tested. Thank you all. It was an honest game, and I mean most of you."

Paddy: "Majesty, a lovely canal, and a fair finish. I sat down a wheat farmer with an empty cart, and I'm getting up a wheat farmer with a Bowl, a Brush, four squares of new land and fifty-two and a half grain. But for one year in the middle I had the whole set bar a Bowl and Rich's chair to show for it, and nobody can take that out of the minutes. Thank you all. Noah, we're square on that favour, and Clark, I'll take 'settled', it's more than my da ever got written about him."

Rob: "Majesty, thirteen years and you finished it with thirty grain and a shovel, one march before my lads got to Silke's front garden. W3 and W5 were close enough to read the price tags. Clark, page one, line thirteen: refused, in that lovely hand. Thank you all, it's been a pleasure being the villain."
