diary: Year 13. Thirteen years, darling, and in the end it came down to thirty grain and a shovel on G12. I sat down at this table as the Emperor and lost the chair in Year Two to a set I never saw coming. After that I spent eleven years learning what the throne really costs, and it isn't grain, it's one auction. So when Graham read the rule out loud for everyone, that the last work ends the game before the market opens, I'd already counted it. I paid for his canal myself, kept the tax at twenty in a rotten year, paid the salaries I'd promised, and shut the auction hall with Silke four pieces into her set and Rob's lads two marches from her garden. It cost me thirty grain and a little of Graham's goodwill, and I finish on the throne I drew with four hundred and ninety-four, the most at the table. Clark has me under "admired" with three underlines, and I'll let him keep his "owed". As for what comes next, nothing does, and that was the plan. This time the Empress watched every cart and made sure none of them ever reached the hall.
