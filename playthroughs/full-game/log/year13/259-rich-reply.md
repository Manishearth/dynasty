diary: Year Thirteen, the last entry, from Province 3, by a candle I'm told I needn't bother saving now. The harvest was bad, the tax was twenty, and Reina spent thirty grain on a canal nobody will drink from and closed the book on all of us before the auction hall could open its doors. I'd have done exactly the same, and I'd have smiled while I did it, and I suppose that's the cruellest compliment I can pay her. I drew the Gentry and I finish as the Gentry, last in grain with forty-eight and a half, but I'm back in the seat I drew, which is more than Clark, Noah or Paddy can say. In thirteen years I broke my word to nobody, and Silke says she'll remember the letter of it, which I take as a compliment too. I held three Deeds that everyone wanted and sold none of them, because fifty was the price and fifty was the promise. That cost me a sale and kept me my name, and on the last night I find that's a trade I don't regret. I taught the table the auction, the narrow promise and the sentence about arithmetic, and Reina learned the one lesson I never gave anyone, which is when to stop. If there's a next game, I'll sit down with sixty grain and a smile and remember that every trick I teach comes back to me with interest.
