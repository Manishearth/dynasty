## At the table since your last turn

(Storyteller's note: The Emperor doesn't bid at auction, so Silke can't bid on any lot.)

Graham: "Rich, Paddy, thank you both for starting at two. I'll be honest too, since everyone else is: I'm bidding on the Customs House and the Deed and nothing else. Anyone who wants either of them should know I've written my figure down, and I won't be talked off it this time. My Brush goes up at two. Somebody take it home."

Reina: "Paddy darling, I won't pour grain into the Pile over a Bowl. Noah has two, so you take one and I'll take the other, and neither of us goes past thirty. Clark, my Jar opens at a hundred. That's what a full set and a seat are worth, and you can do those sums better than anyone."

Noah: "Here's my arithmetic, out loud as usual: eighty-five and a half grain, five empty shelves, so it's fourteen to sixteen on each lot and not a grain past it. Graham, if your written figure's over fourteen, the Customs House is yours and I'll clap. Reina, a hundred for a Jar is a seat's price and I haven't got a seat's purse, so that one's Clark's problem. My Bowl goes up at eight. Paddy, that's your honest price, and if Reina wants the other one it's eight for her too."

Clark: "For the minutes: Noah just called the Jar 'Clark's problem' in front of eight witnesses, and Reina put it up for sale to me by name. I'll take that as my public release, and it's noted and carried forward that I stood out of the Jars for five years. Reina, a hundred's a steep price for a pot, and I'll pay it to you rather than wait for the new house rule to hand it to me at two next spring. Rich, standing aside goes in the ledger under 'admired', right next to Noah's Deed. My Vase opens at twelve, which is cheaper than it's ever been, and it's the last one I'll sell."

Rob: "Third year running the desert sits on its hands, friends. Go on, bid each other up, fill the Pile, and buy yourselves something nice. Rich especially. A man with five empty shelves is a man I can't visit, and I do hate to leave anyone off my rounds. Graham, that Bowl on K19 is lovely. I can see it from here. Well, W3 can."

The first two Year 9 lots: Rich's Customs House to Graham for 51 (Noah stopped at 14, Reina at 50 to keep it from him); Paddy's Land Deed to Graham for 31 (Noah 14, Rich 16, Reina 30). Graham now holds a Vase, Jar, Bowl, Customs House and Land Deed: the Scholar's full set, with 60.5 grain. The other lots wait on what he does.

Graham: "Vase, Jar, Bowl, Customs House, Deed. That's five pieces in the hall and sixty and a half grain in hand, and I'm showing it before anyone else gets to the next lot. Your Majesty, you were kind to me about the canal and the two extra, and I mean it when I say this isn't a grievance. I've just learned what happens to a man who keeps his set in a drawer for one more round. I'll take the throne."

Graham places the Customs House on K23 and the Deed on L23, then GRAHAM TAKES THE THRONE. He shows the Scholar's complete set and 60.5 grain and replaces Silke as Emperor, taking her whole reserve of 490.5 (551 in all); all five pieces go back to stock. Silke, who holds nothing, draws 60 from the Harvest Pile and is now the Scholar, in Province 4; the next lot, Province 4's last Brush, is hers to sell. The later lots are settled from the same limits: lot 3, the Brush, to Rich for 17 (Noah stopped at 16); lot 4, Reina's Jar, to Clark for 100, its minimum. With the Jar Clark holds the Wheat Peasant's full set (Customs House, Brush, Bowl, Deed, Jar) with 61.5 grain.

Clark: "Customs House, Brush, Bowl, Deed, and Reina's Jar on S13: five pieces on the back row and sixty-one and a half grain, so I'm showing it now. Paddy, it's not a grievance, it's arithmetic: you were one Bowl away from that same chair, and I've just watched Graham teach the whole table what one more round costs. Your page in my ledger stays headed 'Paddy, settled', and I'll keep the rent at five, same as you promised."

Clark places the Jar on S13, then CLARK TAKES PADDY'S SEAT. He shows the Wheat Peasant's complete set and 61.5 grain and replaces Paddy as the Gentry, in Province 3, taking Paddy's 266 (327.5 in all). His Deed goes back to Province 3's stock, which he now sells; his Customs House, Brush, Bowl and Jar move with him to Province 3. Paddy returns his four pieces to stock, draws 60 from the Harvest Pile and is now the Wheat Peasant, in Province 7; Province 7's last Vase, the sixth lot, is his to sell. Clark now pays the harvest and asks the rent; as the Gentry he needs a Vase and 20 grain. The last two lots, settled from the same limits (Paddy's cut to the 60 he now holds): lot 5, Noah's Bowl, to Paddy for 31 (Rich stopped at 14, Reina at 30); lot 6, the Vase, to Rich for 17 (Noah stopped at 16).

Rich: "Two thrones and one Gentry's chair changed hands before the pots were cold, and here I am with a Brush and a Vase for seventeen apiece. Congratulations, Your Majesty Graham, and congratulations, Clark. Silke, Paddy, I know exactly how that floor feels, and you'll both get up again. Rob, both pieces sit tucked under my new Wall, so you'll have to admire them from a distance."
