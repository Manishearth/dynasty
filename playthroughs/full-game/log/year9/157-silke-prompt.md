## Silke's diary so far, in her own words

## Setup–Year 2

Two years gone, and here is what I have to show for it: one Vase out at P26, where the sea air keeps it away from the desert, and twenty-six and a half grain. I started with thirty-seven. I gave away the Vase lot this year to keep my word to Rich, and he took one anyway, along with the Customs House, the Bowl and the throne. "I keep my promises to the letter," he says. I'll remember the letter, Your Majesty. I bid small and honest on everything else and was outbid every time by people with fuller purses, while Rob's lad walked from O19 to N20 and charged me five each year for the view. So I'm the poorest at the table now except for Paddy, and Graham hasn't a single piece. Reina is our landlord from next year, and she has just lost a crown, so I doubt she'll be gentle about rent. Fine. A thin thread is the one you watch closest. From here I'll keep my word only to those who keep theirs, and I'll find out what Paddy and Graham will do to stop the rich from buying every last piece.

## Years 3–4

Two more years, and I still have one Vase on P26 and nothing beside it. In Year 3 Paddy stood aside for me on the Deed, and Graham, who nobody had asked, beat me by one grain at eighteen. That same year he took the Customs House and the Brush as well. This year I went to thirty-five for the last Deed in Reina's cart. Clark paid forty-one for it, and four lots later Graham showed a full set and took Clark's seat, so the Deed Clark bled for went straight back to stock. So the last Deed is back in the cart, and that is the one thread I'm watching. My Jars at least paid me: Noah gave ten, Graham nineteen, and Reina pushed the second one up herself trying to keep it from him. I lost every lot I bid on and kept every word I gave. I set a minimum of twelve on that Jar and the table paid it, which tells me my purse is worth more than they think. The harvest has filled me back up to eighty-odd grain. Rob's legs keep him off my coast this year, though not for long. Next year I stop bidding small and honest. I bid where it counts, and I make the rich pay the Pile to get past me.

## Years 5–6

Years 5 and 6, and I can hardly believe I'm writing it. In Year 5 I stopped bidding small and honest, just as I said I would. I put everything on the last Deed in Reina's cart and got it for twenty-three, because Paddy's nerves gave out at twenty-two. I took a Brush for thirteen on top, and Reina paid the fifteen I asked for my Jar, as she'd promised. I watched three chairs change hands in one auction that year, and I learned what Noah learned: a full set is only worth showing if nobody can take your seat off you two minutes later. This year a flood took my ditch and I was down to ninety-five. Then I took a Customs House for twenty-three and a Bowl for seventeen, beating Clark by a single grain both times. Graham beat me by one grain in Year 3, so the thread came back round. I showed the set and took Reina's chair and her three hundred and ninety-one, and she's on my coast now with sixty grain and my old cart. I'm the Official with four hundred and fifty-nine, which is more than the Emperor has, and I'm one Dragon Jar short of a full set again. Reina has the two Jars to sell, and she'll remember who sold her the last one for fifteen. Rob's lad is two steps from my column. So I'll collect the tax honestly, count it twice, and keep a very close eye on Rich's chair.


## Years 7–8

I collected one year's tax for Rich, honestly and counted twice, and while I did it I watched him dig eighty grain of canal through Reina's fields as if he could buy her Jars with it. He couldn't. Reina set her minimum at a hundred and fifty, the table stood aside, and I paid it without blinking. I can call that the price of her three hundred and ninety-one, or the price of a throne, and both are true. I put the Jar on G12, showed the set, and took Rich's chair and his two hundred and sixty-one. Now he's the one carrying my old ledger for six a year. I set the tax at forty this year, because a full purse at this table is a target and an empty one is no danger to anyone, and I gave Graham both his canal sections at the crown's cost, because I'd rather have a friend reading the cards than an enemy. Paddy showed his set and took Noah's seat. He's the Gentry now with two hundred and thirty-five, and he's one Wooden Bowl short of my chair, which means he's the thread I watch from here on. Clark wants a Jar, Graham's collecting pieces, and Rob raids everyone except me. That's the one thing that worries me. I have six hundred and twelve grain, and the works are still four hundred and fifty short. I sat at the bottom for long enough to know how they look at the top table from down there.
