## Reina's diary so far, in her own words

## Setup–Year 2

Two years, and I planned all of them except the one that counted. I kept the tax gentle, put my palace where Rob's boots couldn't reach it, and gave Graham a reservoir so no peasant would ever sign a revolt against me. I was watching the fields for a rebellion and never once looked at the auction hall, where Rich was quietly buying a whole set, one polite grain at a time. He handed over five trinkets and twenty grain and walked off with everything I'd saved, all eighty of it, and he said "civilised" while he did it. So now I'm the Gentry, standing in a back field with two of Rob's soldiers who want their ten. I hold the Pile and the rent book now, and I know exactly what a moderate sovereign's throne is worth. Rich, my dear, I learn quickly, and the Land Deeds are mine to sell.

## Years 3–4

Two years in the back field, and I've learned what Rich knew all along: the throne is bought in the auction hall, not in the fields. I kept the rent at five through a lean year and a fat one, so not one peasant has a reason to sign anything against me, and I let Noah have his Jar for a promise in the minutes, which is cheaper than grain and lasts longer. Then I stopped being polite. The Bowl went up on I8 under the Wall, and the Customs House and the Brush on L9 and M9, where Rob's boys can't reach them, which makes three of five. I lost the Jar to Graham by one grain, and I watched him walk off with Clark's seat and Clark's pen the same way Rich walked off with mine, so I'm not the only one at this table who learns quickly. Rob has started carrying pieces off to his dunes now, so I'm glad I built behind the Wall. One Jar is left in Silke's cart, and it's promised to me. After that I need a Vase and twenty grain, and then, Rich darling, we'll have a civilised conversation about that chair.

## Years 5–6

Two years, and I learned my own lesson twice. I bought the Jar I'd been promised for fifteen, I pushed Graham to a hundred and twenty-three for his Vase, and then I sat back and watched Noah take his chair two minutes later. When my own set came up I was clever about it: no throne for me, darling, a throne that anyone can take off you inside one auction isn't worth sitting on, so I took Noah's seat and his purse and finished the year on four hundred. Then I bid for a Deed in public, Noah priced it at 365 and laughed, and while I was looking at his cart Silke quietly put her set together out of my Customs House and Paddy's Bowl and walked off with all three hundred and ninety-one of my grain. I spent six years watching the auction hall, and the thread came out of the one cart I wasn't watching. So now I'm in a silk field on the coast with sixty grain, paying Noah five a year, and Silke needs exactly one thing to finish her set, a Dragon Jar, and both of the Jars left are in my cart. Silke darling, the minutes remember, and so do I. That Jar is going to cost you a very civilised conversation.


## Years 7–8

Two years on the coast, and I've started again from the bottom, which is where Rich started from too. I sold Silke her Jar for a hundred and fifty and watched her take Rich's chair with it, so the woman who robbed me now sits on six hundred grain, and every peasant at this table can do the sums on that. Then I made the one mistake a planner can't afford. I pushed Paddy to fifty-one for a Customs House to keep him off a throne he was never allowed to take, and all it did was send him after poor Noah's purse instead. It cost me nothing but a red face, and I'll admit I've had cheaper lessons. I did come home with a Brush and a Vase for nineteen each, and they're sitting at the very bottom of my coast where Rob's lads can't reach them. Rich's canal fills my fields every harvest now, so I'll take that gift and say thank you. Clark is one Jar from a full set, and I have the last Jar in this province, so Clark darling, you and I are going to have a very civilised conversation, and this time I'll be watching every cart.


## Years 9–10

Years 9–10. Two years, and for once I watched every cart. In the rotten year I paid fifty to keep a Customs House from Graham and lost it by a grain. Then I sat and watched him take the throne off Silke with it, and watched Clark take Paddy's chair with the Jar I'd sold him for a hundred. That made three seats gone inside one market, and I learned the lesson without paying for it. So in the good year I went to market for three pieces and paid over everyone's fourteens and sixteens: twenty-nine, forty-one and thirty-three, and I came home with a full set on my coast. I didn't wait a round, because this table has shown me twice what waiting costs. I could see Paddy's Vase was going to Clark, and a Gentry with a full set can take any chair here, the throne included, so I took his seat one lot early, and his three hundred and eighty with it. Clark called it civilised and wrote it under "admired", and I'll take that. Now I'm back on the M row with four pieces, where I built in Year Three. I hold the Pile and the rent book again, and I'm one Dragon Jar from a set that reaches Graham's six hundred. Rob's W1 is two steps from my row, Rich has the only Jar on the board, and Clark sells the Jars now. Clark darling, I'll be very polite to you this spring.
