## Graham's diary so far, in his own words

## Setup–Year 2

Two years, eleven lots bid on, nothing won. I lost the Brush on a tie, because of where I sit at the table. Rent went to Rich twice, ten grain each time, and the Pile gave me ten back in the bad year. He showed me a full set at the end, and one piece of it was my own Vase. He paid eight for it and I kept two. Now he's Emperor and Reina will be the one asking for my rent. The landlord has changed and the rent hasn't. The reservoir is real, though, and it's sitting on the corner that soldier on O18 can see from his post. I have forty-three and a half grain and an empty shelf. Next year I bid to win, and I'll stop being polite about it.

## Years 3–4

Years 3 and 4. I stopped being polite and it worked, though it cost me. Ten for the Customs House, eighteen for the Deed after Silke and I tied at fourteen. I wasn't going to lose a second tie to where I sit, so I raised, and I'm sorry it was her. Then ten for the Brush. The next year I bid nineteen for the Jar while Reina tried to keep it from me, and twenty-three for the Bowl, one grain over Noah. Five pieces and forty-five and a half grain, and I showed the set right there in the hall. I took Clark's seat, the lowest one I was allowed, and his seventy-eight grain with it. He's in my furrows now, with my Reservoir, paying Reina five and probably paying the Emperor's tax to me. I've got the pen he used to write me down with. My pieces sit at the back of Province 2 on the G column, out of reach, although Rob's lad stepped onto I15 the moment I arrived and took five off me to say hello. Now I need one Vase and twenty grain, and I've a hundred and twenty-odd. I said I'd remember the price, and I've paid it.

## Years 5–6

Years 5 and 6. I collected sixty-nine for the crown and took nothing for myself. I had four pieces on the G column, a hundred and fifty-four and a half grain, and a ceiling of a hundred and twenty-five on one Vase. Then Noah showed his set before the Vase came up and took my seat and every grain I had. He said it was nothing personal, and I believe him, which makes it worse. Reina had already gone to sixty-one to keep the Vase from me, so she took it, and then took Noah's chair as well. Silke took hers the next year. I've worked out that three people have now sat in the Official's chair since I left it, each for about one auction. I'm the Scholar in Province 4 now. The Emperor docked my translator's salary ten, and I lost the Jar and the Vase to Paddy by one grain each, at twenty against twenty-one. I sold a Brush for twenty-two and kept my quarter. That leaves a hundred and nine and a half grain, an empty shelf, and a soldier who's offended that I've nothing for him to take. Last year I learned that holding four pieces doesn't keep anyone from taking your seat. This year I learned not to pay more than I can afford to lose. I'll start again with small bids and keep counting.
