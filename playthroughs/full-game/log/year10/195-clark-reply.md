Diary of Clark Grafton, Silk Peasant of Province 5, end of Year 10. In Year 9 Noah called the Jar "Clark's problem" in front of the whole table, so I took that as my public release and paid Reina a hundred for it, five years after I promised to stay out of the Jars. That made the wheat set whole, and I showed it straight away and took Paddy's chair and his two hundred and sixty-six, which I'd defend in any court as arithmetic and not a grievance, and I still kept the rent at five as he'd promised. In Year 10 I sat in the Gentry's chair on three hundred and seventy-two grain with four pieces tucked into H9 and J9, one Vase short of the throne and Graham's seven hundred. I accepted his Reservoir, the first work ever built over a province of mine, and I wrote my Vase ceiling at three hundred and thirty. Then Reina showed her set two lots before the Vase came up and took my seat, my pieces and three hundred and seventy-nine and a half grain, and she said the minutes would say I started it. They do; I wrote them. The Vase came to me anyway, for twenty-six, after it was no use to me, and W5 has since charged me ten for the privilege of keeping it on L25. The lesson goes in my own hand, the same lesson I wrote in Year 4, and the third time is the one that costs most: a set is worth nothing until it's shown, and the man waiting on the last lot is the one who gets eaten. Twenty-four grain, one Vase and a coast I didn't choose, and the ledger has a new page headed "Reina, admired and owed". Nobody's watching the silk farmer now, which is exactly where I started being dangerous.
