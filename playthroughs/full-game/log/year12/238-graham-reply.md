diary: Years 11 and 12. I held the throne for two years and three auctions. I taxed thirty percent both years and never docked a salary, I built a Pagoda on the quietest square I had, and Rob's lad walked onto my Winter Palace anyway and charged me twenty for the view. Then the population doubled, nobody had any income to tax, and I paid fifteen of every twenty on thirty-four squares of new land, because a crown that won't pay for land gets a revolt. That took the reserve from six hundred and thirty-four to six. I gave Rich a hundred and forty of Wall for twenty of his, and in return he promised that no Deed would open under fifty. He kept that to the letter. Fifty was exactly what Reina paid, and two lots later she was on the throne with my six grain. I asked for a price, and that was the price I asked for. She'd been planning it since Year Two, she said, and I believe her, the same way I believed Noah. I'm the Official in Province 2 now, with fifty-eight grain, an empty shelf, and two of Rob's men standing in the front room. Silke needs one Deed, and Rich is selling them. This year I learned that when you spend the whole treasury to be generous, you get thanked in the minutes, and then the minutes go to whoever has the most grain left. Next year I go back to small bids and honest figures, I count from zero again, and I don't make a promise that only covers the floor.
