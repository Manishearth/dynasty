diary: Diary of Clark Grafton, Silk Peasant of Province 5, end of Year 12. In Year 11 I came into the auction with sixty-one grain and lost every lot I bid on: fifteen on the Customs House, twelve on the Deed, sixteen on the Brush, where Noah beat me at seventeen, and twelve on the Bowl. The Brush makes it "by one" again, and it goes on the old page. My Jar was the one bit of leverage I had, and it fetched seventy-one off Reina, so I collected my quarter and she took Rich's chair with it. A year later she took Graham's throne with a fifty-grain Deed, which means the Jar I sold her is part of a crown, and I've written that under "Reina, admired and owed" with "admired" underlined twice. When the population doubled I'd have been losing twenty-five grain a year, so I bought seven squares of sea at five grain apiece while the crown paid the other fifteen of each. That's the best bargain since the four-grain Vase, and Graham goes on a new page headed "Graham, generous", which I never expected to write. In Year 12 I won Silke's Brush for ten, her own opening price, and put it on P26, where no soldier can reach it this year or next. That's two pieces of five, thirty-three and a half grain, and a coast that pays a hundred and fifteen next harvest. Silke needs only a Deed now, so she's the one I'm watching, and I'm writing the lesson in my own hand before I forget it again: the man who buys land when it's cheap is the one still at the table when the cards turn.
