diary: Years Eleven and Twelve. Twelve years now, and I still haven't bid on a single lot. W3 walked into Graham's Winter Palace like a man coming home for supper and cost the crown twenty, and that stands in for one of my names. Then the throne changed hands twice in one spring. Reina took Rich's chair, then took Graham's, and every pot she'd lined up under the Wall went back into the box the moment my lads got there to dust them. I'll call that a draw and give her the point for timing. The cards doubled every mouth at the table and Graham paid for half of China's new fields, and now the crown has hardly a grain left while I've two hundred and eighteen and a half in the tent. Silke has put four shiny new pots in a row in her front garden. W3 and W5 are one step from them, and W1 and W2 are walking down the column to Rich's Bowl. I need two more names, and both of them are a step or a few away. The desert doesn't buy, it collects, and next year it closes the book.
