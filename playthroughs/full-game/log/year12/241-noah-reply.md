diary: Diary of Noah Tall, the Rice Peasant, the end of Year Twelve. In Year Eleven I finally did what I'd promised and wrote my figures a grain higher than felt honest. It worked three times: Rich's Customs House for twenty-nine, Silke's Brush for seventeen and Paddy's Vase for twenty-one, and all three are in the far corner on S23, S24 and S25, where no boot can reach them for years. Of course Rich still beat me to the Deed by a single grain, thirty-two to my thirty-one, because a grain over and never two is his whole religion. In Year Twelve the population doubled and my paddies would have lost twenty-five in an average year, so I bought six squares of new land at five grain apiece, with the crown paying the other ninety. That's the cheapest good sense I've bought all game. Then I lost the Jar to Silke at forty to my thirty-nine, one grain again, and Reina paid fifty for her Deed and took Graham's throne, exactly as everyone said she would. I end on ninety-four and a half grain with three pieces in hand, needing a Deed and a Jar, and for once my purse is thin and my chair isn't worth stealing. The trouble is that Silke now needs only the Deed I need, and she's a hundred and two to my ninety-four. So next spring the sum is simple: whatever Silke writes for that Deed, I write one more, and if I can't, I'd rather the Pile had my grain than watch her take my seat with it.
