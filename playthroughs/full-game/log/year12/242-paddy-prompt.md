## At the table since your last turn

Reina: "The Official's Customs House goes up at two, darlings, the cheapest it's been all game, because I'm not here to squeeze anyone. And Rich, you know exactly why I'm here: one Deed, and I'll pay a proper price for it, so nobody needs to empty their purse trying to stop me."

Rich: "The Gentry's Deeds open at fifty this spring, friends. Land is the one thing at this table they aren't making more of, unless His Majesty pays for it. I'm sitting on thirteen grain, so I'm bidding like a man with thirteen grain: small, steady, and a grain over when I can manage it."

Silke: "I lost five lots by one grain last year, so this year I counted my purse twice before I sat down. Reina, the Deed is yours to buy, and I won't make you bleed the Pile dry for it a second time. The rest of this auction is mine to win."

Clark: "For the minutes: the silk farmer stands out of the Deed. Reina, that lot is yours, and I'd rather not be the one-grain footnote in your coronation. My Jar opens at twenty, and I'm after a Brush and a Bowl at farmer's prices. Rich, you've got thirteen grain, so I'd hate to see you embarrass yourself on the Brush."

Noah: "Sum out loud, same as always. Ninety-one and a half, twenty kept back for the set, so seventy-one between the Deed and the Jar and not a grain more. Reina, you've five hundred and thirty against my seventy-one, so if you want that Deed you'll have it, but I'll make you pay for it. Bowl's up at two, nobody needs it, so it might as well go to somebody's shelf."

Rob: "Pass on the lot of them, twelve years running. Reina, darling, buy your Deed and put it somewhere lovely. My lads are already standing in your garden admiring the other four. Your Majesty, thank you for the Winter Palace. The crown's down to six, so I'll try to be gentle with the Pagoda."
