## Setup–Year 2

Diary of Robert Land, Warlord of the Western Waste. Years One and Two.

They gave me sand and four lads, and nobody else at the table had a single soldier. I parked my boys one step from everyone's border and called them scenery, and everyone laughed. Nobody's laughing now. I've refused the crown's tax twice. Clark has me on page one, line one, in very neat handwriting, and I've never been so proud. My raids have brought in forty grain without paying a single toll. Rich, bless him, bought every jar and bowl in China and walked up to the throne with them, which crosses him off my list. I'll miss visiting his wheat. Poor Reina is now the Gentry in Province 3, with two of my lads standing in her back field. The pieces are on the board now: Clark's strongbox, Noah's Customs House, Paddy's Jar and Silke's Vase out by the sea. Every one of them is a few days' march from a soldier of mine. I've hired a fifth lad and stood him beside Graham's new reservoir, and he's waiting for Graham to finally buy something worth taking. I'm eighty grain up, the villain of the piece, and I've never had a better time in my life.

## Years 3–4

Years Three and Four. Two more refusals for the crown's ledger, three grain and then six, and I'd have paid both twice over just to watch Clark write them down. Then I took his Land Deed Scroll right off J15, the desert's first real trophy, and set it on my sand like a mantelpiece ornament. Last year I told them to put their treasures somewhere pretty, and they went and did it, bless them. Graham stacked his whole set in a row and climbed into Clark's chair with it, so the man who keeps my page is new, but the page hasn't changed. Year Four the gods broke my legs and kept me out of the south, so I did my shopping up north instead. Noah told the whole table he'd noticed W3 at his door, and the very next march W3 walked out with his Customs House. It's on F8 now, next to the Deed. That's two names crossed off. W1 is a step from Reina's Bowl, W2 is standing in Graham's new garden, and I've a hundred and twenty-five grain and not one pot I paid for. The rest of them spend their grain at auction, and I get mine on the march.
