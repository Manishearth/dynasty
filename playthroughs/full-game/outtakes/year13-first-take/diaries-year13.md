# clark.md

## Year 13

Diary of Clark Grafton, Silk Peasant of Province 5, end of Year 13. Reina said "every new square anyone buys this year, the treasury pays in full," and Graham wrote it down, so I took the whole ring of sea, eleven squares for two hundred and twenty of the crown's grain. It's the most leverage I've ever had for nothing, and from next harvest my coast pays three hundred and thirty-five in an average year. The treasury was down to a hundred and four after that, which emptied it for Noah and left the ditch unbuilt, and by the end of the year the Empress was twenty-two in debt to the Pile. That's a cause for revolt, and I've written it under "Reina, admired and owed" with a small question mark beside "owed", because I'm the one who drained her. At the auction I let Silke and Noah fight over the Deed and took the Customs House and the Bowl for nineteen each. Paddy went out at eighteen on the Customs House and Graham at eighteen on the Bowl, so for once "by one" went my way twice, and Noah paid me seventeen for a Jar and took Silke's chair with it. I put the new pieces on P25 and O26 and told the table they were out of any soldier's walk, and that was true for them. But I'd forgotten W4 was sitting one step from my Vase on L25, which the board card told me in plain numbers, and now it sits on M18 in the desert and the "Warlord" page has its first line in years. So I'm one Deed and one Vase short with sixty-six and a half grain, Rich has two Deeds left, and Noah says he's watching me. Lesson, in my own hand: guarding the new pieces doesn't help if you leave the old one on the doorstep.

# graham.md

## Year 13

Year 13. Reina said "every square, in full," I wrote it down word for word, and six squares of new land in Province 2 cost me nothing. Everyone else read the same sentence and held her to it, and by Noah's turn the treasury had four grain left. Then she asked me to pay twenty-six for her ditch, which would have ended the game with my shelf empty, and I said no. Noah paid seventy-three for the Deed while Silke stopped at seventy-two, one grain short, the way I used to lose. He took her chair the same hour. I bought a Brush for ten and a Vase for nine while the table spent its money on one scroll, so those are two pieces on the G column, out of reach. Rob's lad took five off me for standing on I12, which is what he always does. The crown ended the year twenty-two in debt, and the law calls that a cause for revolt. Clark needs a Deed and a Vase, and I need a Deed, a Jar and a Bowl, and I've eighty-six and a half grain. Next year I keep the figures honest, I watch Rich's two Deeds, and I watch Clark, because he's closer than I am.

# noah.md

## Year 13

Diary of Noah Tall, the Scholar again, the end of Year Thirteen. Reina promised to pay for every new square in full, so I took five for nothing and said out loud that her treasury had a hundred and four left. That was the last of it, and by the end of the year the crown was twenty-two in debt. At the auction I did the sum I'd promised myself: Silke could write seventy-two for the Deed and still keep her twenty, so I wrote seventy-four. She went out at seventy-two and I had it for seventy-three, and nobody else wanted Clark's Jar past sixteen. I took Silke's chair because taking it sent her set back to stock, and because it was the fattest purse the rules let me take. I'm sorry about it all the same, as she'd offered me a fair deal and I'd turned it down to her face. That's the first seat I've taken in thirteen years that I'd worked out for my own chair before I sat down, and I'm on 153.5. The trouble is that a full purse at this table is exactly what gets stolen. Clark needs only a Deed and a Vase now, after W4 walked off with his old one, and Rich still has two Deeds to sell. So next year I'll be watching Clark and those Deeds, and I'll be counting the Empress's debt out loud, because a crown in the red is cause for a revolt and somebody ought to say so.

# paddy.md

## Year 13

Year Thirteen. A bad harvest, and I bought no land, because the crown had four grain left and I wanted my purse for the market. I lost the Customs House to Clark by one grain, eighteen to his nineteen, and the Jar to Noah by one, so that's the same old song with a new verse. Noah wrote seventy-three on the Deed, one more than Silke could give, and he showed his set and took her chair right there at the table. I'll say this for him, he told her plainly beforehand he wouldn't stand aside, and he was sorry about it afterwards and meant it. My Vase went to Graham for nine, so I got a couple of grain back, and the Bowl and Brush are still safe on S16 and S17 with no soldier near them. I finish on fifty-four and a half, and I still need a Customs House, a Deed and a Jar. Reina's twenty-two in debt now, and Clark needs only a Deed and a Vase, so he's the one who's close this time and I'll be watching him. Next year the harvest should be good again, and I'll go to market with a fuller purse and bid more than a grain past where I stopped this year.

# reina.md

## Year 13

Year 13. Twelve years I spent watching carts, and in the thirteenth I gave the whole table a blank cheque out loud, in front of a man who writes things down. "Every new square, in full," I said, darling, and they took me at my word: five hundred and twenty grain of new fields, every square of it in somebody else's province. Noah stopped at the exact sum that left me four grain, and he called that honesty. I could have ended the game on the spot with one ditch in Graham's fields, the throne still under me, and Graham wouldn't put in twenty-six to close a book on a page where his name wasn't at the top. Then Rob's boy came knocking under my own Wall, and I paid the salaries I'd promised, so the Empress who couldn't count past one auction is now twenty-two grain in debt to the Pile. That's a revolt clause with my name on it, and every peasant at the table can read it. Clark needs a Deed and a Vase, Rich still has two Deeds, and Noah is in Silke's chair with a hundred and fifty grain, watching. Next year I have to tax hard enough to get out of the red and still gently enough that nobody signs, and build that ditch the first moment the treasury can pay for it. I have learned a great many lessons at this table, but this is the first one I wrote the cheque for myself.

# rich.md

## Year 13

Year Thirteen, from Province 3, in a bad harvest that somehow left me richer than I started. I kept the rent at five and called it kindness, and the peasants paid it without a murmur. The Empress promised every new square "in full", so I took F9 at her expense. Then the rest of the table took her at her word too, and she went from five hundred grain to four in one afternoon. Silke whispered me sixty-five for a Deed, the rules wouldn't allow it, and in the open auction Noah paid seventy-three. My quarter of that was eighteen and a quarter, and it put Noah in Silke's chair with her purse in his pocket. The Pile took the rest. I bid a grain over and never two and came home with nothing but my Bowl. Graham beat me to the Vase by one grain. I've lost by one grain often enough to know I'll stop paying for that lesson sooner or later. Clark needs one Deed and a Vase, and I hold both remaining Deeds, so next year he'll be very polite to me. The real news is at the top of the table: the crown is twenty-two grain in debt, and the rulebook calls that a cause for revolt. I've sat on that throne and I know exactly how thin the cushions are. I have sixty-six and a half grain, a Bowl, two Deeds to sell and a long memory. I'll keep smiling and keep counting.

# rob.md

## Year 13

Year Thirteen. Silke whispered me seventy-two grain for Clark's old Deed, and I'll admit I was flattered. But that Deed is a name crossed off my list, and it was also the last piece she needed for the chair, so I kept it on the mantel and let her go to auction. Noah outbid her by a single grain, took her chair and her whole purse, and Silke is a rice farmer now with no pots in her garden, which is a shame, because my lads had only just got there. Clark finished the evening needing one Deed, so W4 strolled down to his "unreachable" coast and brought his Vase home to M18. It doesn't count for me, but now he needs two pieces, and every set at the table is a little further off than it was. The crown is twenty-two in debt and the ditch still isn't dug, and Reina owes me ten she didn't have, so somebody at this table is going to start saying "revolt" very soon. I've a hundred and ninety-eight and a half grain, the fattest tent in China, and I still haven't paid for a pot. W1 is three steps from Graham's pots on the G column, and three of my lads are closing on Rich's Bowl at J17. Two names to go. The desert doesn't buy, it collects, and next year it closes the book. I mean it this time.

# silke.md

## Year 13

Year 13. I read Bad Harvest, and it turned out to be about me. I tried to buy my Deed privately, first from Rich and then from Rob, and learned that the rules won't let Rich sell me one and that Rob doesn't sell anything. Then Noah stood up and did the sum out loud, as he always does: I could write seventy-two, so he wrote seventy-three. That's one grain again, the same thread from Year 3 and Year 10, and this time it cost me everything. He took my chair and my ninety-five, my four pieces went back to stock, and I'm the Rice Peasant on sixty-four and a half with an empty cart. I can't even say he cheated me. He told me plainly he wouldn't stand aside, and I'd told the whole table exactly how much I had. I won't make that mistake again: from now on nobody hears my sum but me. Two things to watch. Reina is twenty-two in debt, which is a cause for revolt, and a peasant can raise one. And Clark needs only a Deed and a Vase, Rich still has two Deeds, and Noah is already watching him. Fine. I've been at the bottom before, and it's where I see most clearly.
