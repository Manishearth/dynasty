diary: Diary of Clark Grafton, Silk Peasant of Province 5, end of Year 13. Reina said "every new square anyone buys this year, the treasury pays in full," and Graham wrote it down, so I took the whole ring of sea, eleven squares for two hundred and twenty of the crown's grain. It's the most leverage I've ever had for nothing, and from next harvest my coast pays three hundred and thirty-five in an average year. The treasury was down to a hundred and four after that, which emptied it for Noah and left the ditch unbuilt, and by the end of the year the Empress was twenty-two in debt to the Pile. That's a cause for revolt, and I've written it under "Reina, admired and owed" with a small question mark beside "owed", because I'm the one who drained her. At the auction I let Silke and Noah fight over the Deed and took the Customs House and the Bowl for nineteen each. Paddy went out at eighteen on the Customs House and Graham at eighteen on the Bowl, so for once "by one" went my way twice, and Noah paid me seventeen for a Jar and took Silke's chair with it. I put the new pieces on P25 and O26 and told the table they were out of any soldier's walk, and that was true for them. But I'd forgotten W4 was sitting one step from my Vase on L25, which the board card told me in plain numbers, and now it sits on M18 in the desert and the "Warlord" page has its first line in years. So I'm one Deed and one Vase short with sixty-six and a half grain, Rich has two Deeds left, and Noah says he's watching me. Lesson, in my own hand: guarding the new pieces doesn't help if you leave the old one on the doorstep.
