diary: Year 13. Twelve years I spent watching carts, and in the thirteenth I gave the whole table a blank cheque out loud, in front of a man who writes things down. "Every new square, in full," I said, darling, and they took me at my word: five hundred and twenty grain of new fields, every square of it in somebody else's province. Noah stopped at the exact sum that left me four grain, and he called that honesty. I could have ended the game on the spot with one ditch in Graham's fields, the throne still under me, and Graham wouldn't put in twenty-six to close a book on a page where his name wasn't at the top. Then Rob's boy came knocking under my own Wall, and I paid the salaries I'd promised, so the Empress who couldn't count past one auction is now twenty-two grain in debt to the Pile. That's a revolt clause with my name on it, and every peasant at the table can read it. Clark needs a Deed and a Vase, Rich still has two Deeds, and Noah is in Silke's chair with a hundred and fifty grain, watching. Next year I have to tax hard enough to get out of the red and still gently enough that nobody signs, and build that ditch the first moment the treasury can pay for it. I have learned a great many lessons at this table, but this is the first one I wrote the cheque for myself.
