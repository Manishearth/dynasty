## At the table since your last turn

Graham: "I'm starting from zero again, so the figures are small and honest, one for each piece. Silke, you need one Deed, and everyone here can count to one. I'm not going to make it cheap for you."

Rich: "Friends, a landlord with forty-eight grain can't start a war, so I'll be bidding a grain over and never two, as usual. The Deed opens at sixty-five. A lean year doesn't lower the price, and I've heard there's at least one scholar at this table who agrees with me. If she meant it, she can say it again in the auction where everyone can hear."

Silke: "You all know what I'm after, so I won't pretend otherwise: one Deed, and everything else can go where it likes. Noah, you'd need the Jar as well as the Deed, and Rich has two more Deeds for next year. Stand aside on this one, and when I'm in the chair your fields get the first works the crown builds. Graham can write that down too."

Clark: "For the minutes: I'm in for the Customs House, the Deed and the Bowl, and out of the Brush and the Vase, so Graham and Rich can sort those out between them. Silke, the Deed's your last piece and I know it, so I'm not starting a war over it. My Jar opens at ten, and anyone who wants it pays for the privilege, same as I did."

Noah: "Here's the sum out loud, since that's my job at this table. I need the Deed and the Jar and twenty to sit down with. Silke needs only the Deed, and on ninety-two and a half she can write seventy-two and still keep her twenty. So I'm writing more than that for Rich's Deed, and whatever's left goes on Clark's Jar. Rich, I'd sooner settle it fair than feed the Pile, but I won't stand aside, not on this one. And my Bowl goes up at five. Somebody ought to own it."

Rob: "Thirteen years and not a single lot, friends, and I see no reason to break the habit now. Bid away. The desert's just here for the show."

The auction, settled from the limits: Graham's Customs House to Clark for 19 (Paddy out at 18); Rich's Land Deed Scroll to Noah for 73 (Silke out at 72); Silke's Bamboo Brush to Graham for 10; Clark's Dragon Jar to Noah for 17 (Graham and Paddy out at 16).

Noah: "Silke, I'm sorry, and I mean that. You offered me the first works if I stood aside, and I told you plainly I wouldn't. Here's the sum, out loud like always: fifty-five and a half of mine plus your ninety-five is a hundred and fifty and a half, and the Scholar's chair. I read the cards for this table in Year One, and I'll read them honestly again. Clark, you need only a Deed now and Rich has two left, so I know whose chair I'm sitting in, and I'll be watching you."

Noah shows the Rice Peasant's set and replaces Silke as the Scholar, taking her 95 (150.5 in all); his Customs House, Vase, Deed and Jar go to J22, K22, M22 and M23 in Province 4, and his Brush joins Province 4's stock. Silke's pieces go back to stock; she draws 60 and is the Rice Peasant. Then Noah's old Wooden Bowl (now Silke's to sell) goes to Clark for 19 (Graham out at 18), and Paddy's Vase to Graham for 9 (Rich out at 8). Clark now needs only a Land Deed.

Clark: "Customs House on P25 and the Bowl on O26, both down on the coast beside the Brush and well out of any soldier's walk. Paddy, out at eighteen this time, and it's my turn to say "by one", but I won't write it on your page. Noah, you're right to watch me. Rich has two Deeds left, and I'll be on the far side of the table when they come up."

Graham: "A Brush for ten and a Vase for nine. That's nineteen grain for two pieces, after years of paying fifty for one. They'll go at the back of the G column, where nobody's boots reach. Silke, I'm sorry. I said I wouldn't make the Deed cheap for you, and I didn't need to, because Noah made it dear."

Clark puts the Customs House on P25 and the Bowl on O26; Graham puts the Brush on G12 and the Vase on G13.

Rob: "Clark, you said the coast was well out of any soldier's walk. W4 has always been a keen walker. I'll look after your Vase on M18, and you can pop by and see it whenever you like. Graham, a little something from your fields as well, and Majesty, my lad's knocking at the north-east door, so do have a look in the cushions."

Rob marches: W1 onto I12 (Graham pays 5); W2 to J15; W3 onto I19 in Province 1 (Reina owes 10 with 4 in the reserve, so the crown goes 6 into debt to the Pile); W4 onto L25, taking Clark's Vase to M18 on the desert; W5 onto L19 (Noah pays 5).

Salaries: Reina pays what she declared, 8 to Graham and 8 to Noah; with no realia to sell, the crown's debt to the Pile is now 22. End of Year 13: Rob 198.5, Noah 153.5, Graham 86.5, Clark 66.5, Rich 66.5, Silke 64.5, Paddy 54.5, Reina -22 (in debt). Works owed: 30, the Province 2 Irrigation System.
