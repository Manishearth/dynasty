## Noah's diary so far, in his own words

## Setup–Year 2

Diary of Noah Tall, Scholar, the end of Year Two.

I finished the year with 48 grain and two of my five pieces, the Customs House on L22 and the Land Deed on K22, both well inland. That's 5.5 grain less than I started with, and two pieces closer to the throne. I lost the Deed in Year One and won it in Year Two, both on seat order alone, and I said so out loud because somebody had to; I owe Paddy one for it.

Rich did his sums better than any of us. He bid politely on everything, kept a promise to the letter but not to its spirit, and was sitting on the throne by the end of the second year. Fair play, but I've noted that a man who says he'll fight over nothing can still end up owning everything. Reina is the Gentry now, and Rob's lad on N20 is two steps from my Customs House, so next year I'll be counting soldiers as well as grain. I still need the Jar, the Bowl and the Vase. Silke and Graham are poorer than me and Rob is richer than anyone, so I'll keep my bids small and my land money aside, and keep reading the cards honestly.

## Years 3–4

Diary of Noah Tall, Scholar, the end of Year Four. In Year Three I took Silke's Jar for ten, the number I'd said out loud, and agreed in public to leave Clark the Vase and Reina the next Jar. Neither cost me much, and both kept the grain out of the Pile. In Year Four I bid hard for the first time, went to twenty-two on the Bowl and lost it to Graham by a single grain, then took the Vase for fifteen. For about an hour I was one Bowl from a seat. Then Rob did what I'd told the whole table he would: his lad on M21 stepped onto L22 and carried my Customs House off to the desert, where it sits on F8 beside Clark's old Deed. So I need a Customs House and a Bowl again, but I've 102.5 grain after Rich's six, which is more than double what I started the year with, and the Jar, Deed and Vase are all tucked in the corner at J21, K22 and J22. Graham is the Official now and Clark is growing wheat, which shows how quickly this table turns over. I still owe Paddy his favour, and next year I'll put my pieces where the arithmetic says the boots can't reach, not just where they look tidy.

## Years 5–6

Diary of Noah Tall, the Gentry, the end of Year Six. In Year Five I bought a Customs House for fifteen and a Bowl for nine, and for the first time I held a full set. I worked out, correctly and out loud, that taking the throne would last one auction, because Graham was about to buy his Vase. So I took Graham's chair instead, and his purse, and sat down with 260.5 grain feeling very clever. Then Reina showed her set and took the chair off me by exactly the same reasoning, and I went from 260.5 to sixty in about a minute. I'd done the sum for the throne and never thought to do it for the seat I was sitting in. That was my arithmetic, and it's no one else's fault. In Year Six I paid out the harvest and took five rent from each peasant. I also held the only two Land Deeds left, and Reina needed one. She offered thirty, and I set the minimum at 365, half a grain more than she could pay and keep her twenty, and I told the whole table why. Nobody bid. It didn't matter much, because Silke showed her set after the last lot and took Reina's chair and her 391 anyway. So this table punishes anyone who sits on a fat purse with nothing in hand, and next year that's Silke, who needs only a Jar. I have 115 grain, no pieces and five to collect, plus two Deeds in my cart that Paddy needs. I still owe Paddy a favour, and a fair price on a Deed may be the honest way to pay it.


## Years 7–8

Diary of Noah Tall, the Rice Peasant, the end of Year Eight.

In Year Seven I paid out the harvest, kept the rent at five, and twice got the Pile's sums wrong in public, which the Storyteller rightly corrected. I bid ten a piece on the cheap lots and lost every one, to fifteens and seventeens. I sold Paddy my Deed at a fair price, as I'd promised, and Silke paid Reina a hundred and fifty for a Jar and took Rich's throne. In Year Eight I raised my bids to eighteen and stood aside from the Customs House because Paddy and Clark had shaken on it. That handshake finished Paddy's set, and the seat he took was mine, with my 181 grain in it. So I finally paid the favour I've owed him since Year One, at about a hundred and sixty more than I'd meant to.

That makes three times this table has caught me sitting on a full purse with nothing in hand. Each time I could see it happening to somebody else, but I never did the sum for my own chair. Now I'm growing rice in Province 6 on 66.5 grain: the sixty I drew and a quarter of the Bowl Graham bought from me. I hold nothing, but I owe nobody a favour. Next year I'll buy cheap pieces, keep my purse thin, and put whatever I win where the boots can't reach. Clark only needs a Jar, so I'll be watching him.


## Years 9–10

Diary of Noah Tall, the Rice Peasant, the end of Year Ten. In Year Nine I bid fourteen to sixteen on four lots and lost all four, the Brush and the Vase to Rich at seventeen apiece, a grain over me both times. Then I sat and watched two chairs change hands before the pots were cold: Graham's set took Silke's throne and Clark's took Paddy's seat. Paddy bought my Bowl for thirty-one, so the favour I'd owed him since Year One is long paid and he's thanked me twice for it. In Year Ten all five of my missing pieces were up for sale. I did the sum out loud: a hundred and twenty-two over five lots, twenty kept back for the set. Then I lost every lot by one grain. Reina had the Customs House at twenty-nine to my twenty-eight, Paddy the Brush at twenty-one to my twenty, Rich the Jar at twenty-six to my twenty-five, and Clark the Vase at twenty-six to my twenty-five. Reina bought my other Bowl, finished her set with it and took Clark's chair, so three seats have moved in two markets. The good news is that, for the first time, none of them was mine. I end the year on 150 grain with nothing in hand. That's the full purse again, but I'm a peasant now, and nobody can take a chair I'm not sitting in. The lesson is plain enough: at this table the man who wins writes his figure one grain above mine, so next time I'll write mine one grain higher than feels honest. Reina needs only a Jar and is sitting on 470, so I'll be watching her.


## Years 11–12

Diary of Noah Tall, the Rice Peasant, the end of Year Twelve. In Year Eleven I finally did what I'd promised and wrote my figures a grain higher than felt honest. It worked three times: Rich's Customs House for twenty-nine, Silke's Brush for seventeen and Paddy's Vase for twenty-one, and all three are in the far corner on S23, S24 and S25, where no boot can reach them for years. Of course Rich still beat me to the Deed by a single grain, thirty-two to my thirty-one, because a grain over and never two is his whole religion. In Year Twelve the population doubled and my paddies would have lost twenty-five in an average year, so I bought six squares of new land at five grain apiece, with the crown paying the other ninety. That's the cheapest good sense I've bought all game. Then I lost the Jar to Silke at forty to my thirty-nine, one grain again, and Reina paid fifty for her Deed and took Graham's throne, exactly as everyone said she would. I end on ninety-four and a half grain with three pieces in hand, needing a Deed and a Jar, and for once my purse is thin and my chair isn't worth stealing. The trouble is that Silke now needs only the Deed I need, and she's a hundred and two to my ninety-four. So next spring the sum is simple: whatever Silke writes for that Deed, I write one more, and if I can't, I'd rather the Pile had my grain than watch her take my seat with it.
