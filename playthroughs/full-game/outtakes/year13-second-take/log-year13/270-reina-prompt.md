## At the table since your last turn

Graham: "A hundred grain is generous, Reina, and I've written it down. But you'd be paying me a hundred to close the book while you're on the last line of it, and grain doesn't buy anything once the game's over. The ditch stays undug. I'll take the three squares you've already said are on the crown, though. Thank you, and I mean that exactly as much as you meant the hundred."

Graham refuses Reina's second offer (the ditch plus 100 to him); as Reina said, the crown builds nothing this year, so the Irrigation System stays unbuilt and the year goes on to the auction. Graham buys three more squares (D15, D16, F9), the crown paying all 60.

Rich: "Your Majesty is generosity itself, and I'd take every square you're offering if Province 3 had a single one left to give. But the desert's at my back and the Wall's over my head, and Graham's already been kind enough to sell me the last fields there were. So I'll sit and admire his new acres. Graham, a man who turns down a hundred grain on principle is a man I'll very much enjoy doing business with at the auction."

Silke: "Your Majesty said in front of everyone that the treasury pays in full, and the Scholar writes down exactly what's said at this table. So that's I25, I26 and J26, three squares on my coast, sixty grain from the crown and nothing from me. Thank you, Reina. I'll read you a kinder card next spring."

Clark: "Your Majesty said "any land you want this year, the treasury pays in full," and Silke wrote it down, so I'm reading it back to her from page one. Eleven squares of sea off Province 5, two hundred and twenty from the crown, nothing from me. Reina, darling, that goes under "admired", and for once it doesn't go under "owed"."

Noah: "Her Majesty said any land, paid in full, and Silke's got it in the book, so I'll keep it modest: six squares on my coast, R27, S27, T27 and U24 to U26. Six twenties is a hundred and twenty from the crown and nothing from me. And Graham, since somebody ought to say it, every grain the treasury spends today is a grain it hasn't got for paying you to dig that ditch."

Noah: "I stand corrected, the treasury's down to sixty-four after Clark's sea. So it's three squares, R27, S27 and T27, sixty from the crown and nothing from me, and that leaves Her Majesty four grain. Graham, I'm only saying the arithmetic, but four grain won't pay anyone a hundred to dig a ditch."

Paddy: "I'd love more fields, Your Majesty, but you've four grain left, and I'll not be the man who takes your last one. Fifty-two and a half is what I've got, and I'm keeping it for a Customs House, a Deed or a Jar. Fields can wait. A set can't."

Graham: "I start from zero again, so there are honest figures on every lot and I won't raise once I've reached them. Rich, I'll be at your table for the Brush and the Vase, and you know exactly how much I've got, because I said it out loud."

Rich: "Graham, I admire a man who reads his figures out loud, so I'll do the same: a grain over and never two, and never more than I can spare. The Deeds are fifty again, as they were last spring. Silke, you'll notice the bad harvest didn't wash the price away. Paddy, Graham, if you want a Customs House or a Vase more than I do, I won't make you pay the Pile for the privilege."

Silke: "One lot, one bid, and everyone at this table knows which one it is. Rich, your fifty is fair and I'll meet it. Noah, you're still a Jar short, so a Deed only gets you halfway to the set. My Brush goes up at fifteen. Graham, you said you'd be wanting one."

Clark: "For the minutes: I'm bidding on three things, the Customs House, a Deed and the Bowl, because that's exactly what my shelf is missing, and I'll stop well before any of them gets silly. My Jar starts at thirty. Noah, Paddy, you both need one, and I'd hate to see either of you having to explain to the camera why you let the other man have it."

Noah: "Here's the arithmetic, since somebody has to say it out loud. Silke has ninety-two and a half and has to keep twenty back for her set, so seventy-two and a half is the most she can write for that Deed, and I've a hundred and forty-five and a half. Silke, you're right that a Deed only gets me halfway, but it gets you all the way, and I'd rather pay for half a set than watch you sit down in somebody's chair. My Bowl starts at ten, Clark, and that's a fair price for the last piece on your list."

Paddy: "I'll be honest, since everyone else is: I can't make a set this year on fifty-two and a half, so I'm not going to pretend I can. I'll bid on a Customs House and a Jar, and the Deeds can go to whoever wants to pay Rich's fifty. My Vase goes up at ten, Graham, Rich, and that's a fair price from a man who paid a good deal more for his."

Rob: "Thirteen years, my friends, and the desert still hasn't raised its hand. You lot go ahead and buy the pots. Silke, those four in your front garden are lovely, really, and Rich, your Bowl on J17 is very well kept. I'll be calling round to admire them shortly, and I won't need a receipt."

Silke: "Noah, you've done my sums for me, so here's one you didn't do. I don't have to sit down this year. I only have to hold that Deed when the harvest comes in. Eighty-five. If you want to pay the Pile more than that for half a set, the Deed is yours, and so is the explaining to the camera."

Noah: "Silke, ninety-two and a half is every grain you've got, so ninety-three is one more than you can write, and I'm saying it so nobody thinks I'm hiding anything. That leaves me fifty-two and a half, Clark, and that's what your Jar is going to have to settle for."

Noah: "Silke, you were right, I paid the Pile more than any man should for half a set. But the Jar came cheap, so it's a whole set now: Customs House, Brush, Vase, Deed and Jar, with twenty-six and a half in hand. I'll take the Scholar's chair and its ninety-six, which makes me a hundred and twenty-two and a half, and I'm saying the figure out loud because I know exactly what this table does to a man sitting on a full purse."

The auction, lot by lot: Graham's Customs House to Clark for 19 (Paddy stopped at 18); Rich's Deed to Noah for 86, after Silke raised to 85 and Noah to 93; Silke's Brush to Graham for 15; Clark's Jar to Noah for 33 (Paddy stopped at 32).

Noah shows the Rice Peasant's full set after the fourth lot and replaces Silke as the Scholar, with her 96 (122.5 in all). The Brush goes back to Province 4's stock; his other four pieces move to Province 4. Silke returns her pieces, draws 60 and is now the Rice Peasant, in Province 6.

The last lots, settled from the same limits: the Wooden Bowl (now Silke's to sell) to Graham for 19 (Clark stopped at 18), and Paddy's Vase to Graham for 10.

Clark: "Graham's Customs House goes on P25, right next to my Brush, and it cost me nineteen, which is one more than Paddy and for once the "by one" went my way. Graham, you beat me to the Bowl by one, so you go on the old page, and I'm told the page is getting crowded."

Graham: "Fifteen, nineteen and ten, which is forty-four for three pieces, and I haven't raised once. Noah, congratulations. I mean it, and I've noted where you're sitting. Rob, they're on the G column, so you'll have to admire them from a distance."

Noah: "They stay where they stand, J22, K22, M22 and M23, every one out of reach of the boots this year, and I've counted the squares twice. Rob, I'll tell you what I told you in Year Four: I know exactly how many steps you are from my shelf. For the record, I'm a Bowl short and I hold a hundred and twenty-two and a half, and that's not a figure I'll be sitting on quietly."

Rob: "Graham, the G column's a lovely spot, and W1 has just started his walk to see it. He's billing you five at the gate on the way in. Rich, three of my lads are all heading for one Bowl, so put the kettle on. Clark, I'm sorry, darling, but your Vase was standing right next to my boy and he couldn't help himself. Write it on page one with the others."

Rob's march: W1 onto I12 (Graham pays 5), W2 onto J15, W3 onto I19 (the crown pays 10, and goes into debt), W4 onto L25, carrying Clark's Vase off to M18, and W5 onto L19 (Noah pays 5).
