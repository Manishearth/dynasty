## At the table since your last turn

Noah: "Here's the arithmetic, since somebody has to say it out loud. Silke has ninety-two and a half and has to keep twenty back for her set, so seventy-two and a half is the most she can write for that Deed, and I've a hundred and forty-five and a half. Silke, you're right that a Deed only gets me halfway, but it gets you all the way, and I'd rather pay for half a set than watch you sit down in somebody's chair. My Bowl starts at ten, Clark, and that's a fair price for the last piece on your list."

Paddy: "I'll be honest, since everyone else is: I can't make a set this year on fifty-two and a half, so I'm not going to pretend I can. I'll bid on a Customs House and a Jar, and the Deeds can go to whoever wants to pay Rich's fifty. My Vase goes up at ten, Graham, Rich, and that's a fair price from a man who paid a good deal more for his."

Rob: "Thirteen years, my friends, and the desert still hasn't raised its hand. You lot go ahead and buy the pots. Silke, those four in your front garden are lovely, really, and Rich, your Bowl on J17 is very well kept. I'll be calling round to admire them shortly, and I won't need a receipt."

Silke: "Noah, you've done my sums for me, so here's one you didn't do. I don't have to sit down this year. I only have to hold that Deed when the harvest comes in. Eighty-five. If you want to pay the Pile more than that for half a set, the Deed is yours, and so is the explaining to the camera."

Noah: "Silke, ninety-two and a half is every grain you've got, so ninety-three is one more than you can write, and I'm saying it so nobody thinks I'm hiding anything. That leaves me fifty-two and a half, Clark, and that's what your Jar is going to have to settle for."

Noah: "Silke, you were right, I paid the Pile more than any man should for half a set. But the Jar came cheap, so it's a whole set now: Customs House, Brush, Vase, Deed and Jar, with twenty-six and a half in hand. I'll take the Scholar's chair and its ninety-six, which makes me a hundred and twenty-two and a half, and I'm saying the figure out loud because I know exactly what this table does to a man sitting on a full purse."
