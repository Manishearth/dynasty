## Year 13

Diary of Clark Grafton, Silk Peasant of Province 5, end of Year 13. Reina said "any land you want, the treasury pays in full," Silke wrote it down, and I took her at her word: eleven squares of sea for two hundred and twenty of the crown's grain and none of mine, which takes my coast to three hundred and thirty-five in an average year. Noah thanked me for doing his arithmetic for him, and the crown is now sixteen in debt, which I'll admit is partly my doing and won't admit to anyone else. I won Graham's Customs House for nineteen, one over Paddy, and lost the Bowl to Graham by one, so the "by one" page balances at last, one line on each side. My Jar fetched thirty-three off Noah and helped finish his set, and he took Silke's chair with it, so that's the second Jar of mine to end up holding up somebody else's seat. Then Rob's lad walked onto L25 and carried my Vase off to the dunes, one round away, exactly as the card warned me, and it goes on page one with all the others. Eighty-nine and a half grain, a Customs House and a Brush on the back of the coast, still a Deed, a Bowl and a Vase short, and a crown that can't pay for the last ditch. The lesson in my own hand: a piece left one step from a soldier is a piece already in the Warlord's ledger, and the free land is the only thing this year he can't carry away.

# graham.md

## Year 13

Year 13. Reina offered me thirty for a ditch, then a hundred and thirty, and I turned both down, because the ditch would have ended the game with her on the throne and me with an empty shelf. She said in front of everyone that the treasury would pay for any land in full. I took nine squares on her word, and so did everyone else, until the crown had four grain and then minus sixteen, and now it can't pay a share of any work, so the ditch can't be dug. I didn't plan that, but I'll accept it. I set honest figures and never raised once, and I came away with the Brush for fifteen, the Bowl for nineteen by one grain over Clark, and Paddy's Vase for ten. That's forty-four for three pieces on the G column, where no boot can reach them for two years. W1 charged me five at the gate anyway. Noah paid eighty-six for half a set, found the Jar cheap and took Silke's chair, and he's one Bowl short and says so out loud. Now I need a Deed and a Jar, and I have fifty-eight and a half grain with a two-hundred-odd harvest on new land the crown paid for. Last year I emptied the treasury being generous. This year Reina did the same thing, and I watched her do it. Next year I buy the last two pieces, I keep twenty back, and I don't wait a single lot.

# noah.md

## Year 13

Diary of Noah Tall, the Scholar, the end of Year Thirteen. For twelve years I lost by one grain, and this year I won by one, twice. I paid Rich eighty-six for a Deed because Silke wrote eighty-five, and I'd sooner have paid ninety-three than watch her finish her set with it. Then Paddy stopped at thirty-two on Clark's Jar, I had it for thirty-three, and suddenly I was holding a whole set I'd only half planned for. I took Silke's chair and her ninety-six, so she's growing rice on my old paddies now, along with the three free squares the crown bought me that morning. That's a hundred and nineteen grain spent on two pots, the most I've ever paid for anything, and I'd do the sum the same way again. I'm on a hundred and twenty-two and a half, with four pieces back on J22 and K22, where Rob robbed me in Year Four, and I need only a Bowl. All my squares here are safe for one year and none for two, and W5 has already billed me five on L19. The crown is sixteen in debt, so nobody can dig Graham's ditch, and the game goes on. That leaves me sitting on a full purse in a stolen chair, which this table has punished every single time. So next spring I buy a Bowl fast, from Graham, Rich or Paddy, whoever names a fair price, and I do the sum for my own seat before anybody else does it for me.

# paddy.md

## Year 13

Year Thirteen. Second by one grain again, and twice in one afternoon this time: eighteen against Clark's nineteen for the Customs House, and thirty-two against Noah's thirty-three for the Jar. If they ever put "by one" on a headstone, I'll know whose it is. I turned down the free land because the crown only had four grain left, and I kept my purse for the market, and then the market didn't want my purse. Noah went all in on that Deed against Silke and ended up with a whole set and the Scholar's chair, and I'm glad it was him, after the favour he still owes me. The only grain I took home was ten for my Vase, from Graham. So I've still got the Bowl and the Brush on S16 and S17, fifty-five grain, and three pieces to buy. The crown's in debt now and can't pay its share of that ditch, so the game goes on, and my fields keep paying fifty-five a year while it does. Next year I'll bid a grain or two higher than feels sensible, because sensible keeps coming second.

# reina.md

## Year 13

Year 13. I planned the year down to the last line, darling, and then I said one sentence out loud that I hadn't planned. "Any land you want, the treasury pays in full" — and Silke wrote it down, and the whole table read it back to me one province at a time, and four hundred and seventy grain went out to sea. Graham turned down the ditch, then turned down a hundred grain for it, and he was right to: grain is no use to anyone once the book is closed, and I was offering to close it with myself on the last line. So the hall stayed open, Noah put a set together out of Silke's Deed and Clark's Jar and took her chair, and I'm still on the throne, sixteen grain in debt, which means I can't pay for the ditch even if Graham comes round. The crown can't end the game now. All it can do is hold on until it fills up again. I said I'd watch every cart, and I did. What I didn't watch was my own mouth. Next spring I say nothing generous out loud, I tax enough to get out of debt without handing anyone a revolt clause, and I keep an eye on Noah, who is one Bowl short and says his figures out loud the way I used to.

# rich.md

## Year 13

Year Thirteen, from Province 3, in a bad harvest that somehow left me richer than a good one would have. I set the Deed at fifty, and Silke and Noah bid it up between them to eighty-six for my sake, which put twenty-one and a half in my purse and a full set in Noah's hands inside the same auction. So Silke is in the rice paddies now with sixty grain, and I'll remember to be very kind to her about the rent. I bid eight on everything and won nothing, and I'm not sorry, because seventy grain in hand counts for a great deal if Graham ever lets that ditch be dug. Reina gave away the whole treasury in fields to stop anyone signing anything, and now she sits on minus sixteen and can't even pay her own salaries in full. The throne really is the one seat nobody wants. Graham turned down a hundred grain on principle, which I'd never have done, and he's a Deed short now and knows exactly whose they are. Three of Rob's soldiers are walking towards my one Bowl, so I'll put the kettle on as he asked and count the steps.

# rob.md

## Year 13

Year Thirteen. Thirteen years and I still haven't bid on a single lot. The rest of them paid the Pile a hundred and ninety grain between them, and Noah put eighty-six of it into one Deed, then climbed into Silke's chair with the whole set and her purse. That's the fourth time I've watched someone at this table turn into the next one. Silke went back to holding nothing, so her row is gone from the garden, and Graham stood his three new pots on the G column and told me I'd have to admire them from a distance. W1 is three steps from them and he's already billed Graham five at the gate. W2, W3 and W5 are all closing on Rich's Bowl on J17 from three sides, so whichever way Rich jumps, one of them gets there. W4 took Clark's Vase home to M18. It counts for nothing, but now Clark's set is short a piece, and I enjoyed that more than I probably should have. The crown is sixteen in debt, I've a hundred and ninety-eight and a half in the tent, and I need two names. Next year the desert sends for both of them.

# silke.md

## Year 13

Year 13. I read a bad harvest for everyone, and it was worst for me. I took three squares of sea on the crown's account and put everything on one Deed. I went to eighty-five, past the twenty a set needs, because I meant to show it next spring. Noah went to ninety-three, a grain more than I had in the world, and said so out loud, which was fair of him and still cut. Then the Jar came cheap, he showed the set, and he took my chair and my ninety-six. Four pieces I'd won by one grain went back to stock, and I'm on Noah's old coast as the Rice Peasant with sixty-four and a half and nothing on my shelf. It's the same thread as always, and this time I pulled it tight myself and made him pay the Pile eighty-six to get past me. I'll keep the lesson: when you've got only one bid, everyone at the table can count it for you. Reina's crown is sixteen in debt, Graham has three pieces and keeps the ditch closed, and Noah is a Bowl short of the throne, sitting on a hundred and twenty-two. Graham bought my Bowl, so the one Noah needs is in Graham's hands, and that's where I'll be looking next spring. I've been squeezed thin before, and it's when I see the table most clearly.
