## Setup–Year 2

Diary of Paddy Doyle, rice farmer of Province 6, end of Year 2.

I can hardly believe I'm writing this, but I've got the Dragon Jar, a Customs House and a Land Deed Scroll all standing in my own fields, and I'm only short the Vase. I paid too much for the jar, seventeen, and Graham's still sore about it. That cost me his cheap Vase, so I paid for the jar twice. I kept my word on the Brush, though, and he gave me the Scroll for two, so maybe there's no lasting harm done. Silke paid twenty-five for my Bowl. That's six in my pocket and a bitter rival for the Vase, because she needs it as much as I do and she knows Graham's the only one selling. I've thirty-three and a half and I must finish on twenty, so I can bid thirteen and a half on that Vase and not a grain more. Rob's lad is still sitting on S21 looking at my rice, and the legs won't stay bad forever. Next year will decide it. I'll make my peace with Graham before the bidding starts.

## Years 3–4

Diary of Paddy Doyle, now Silk Peasant of Province 5, end of the Year 4 auctions.

I still can't quite believe it. I got the Vase off Graham for twenty-nine after Silke stopped at twenty-eight, and that finished my set, so I took her seat on the coast. Province 5 is a lower seat than the one I drew, so that counts as a win, and I'm holding it. I was sorry to do it to Silke after she asked me to stand aside, but the Scroll she let me have last year had cost her nothing, and the Vase was the whole game for both of us. She took it better than I'd have taken it myself. She even sold me a brush at two, so we're square on brushes now, and I kept my word to her on mine. I sold the Jar for twenty-three and kept five and a half; Graham won it after he and Silke fought over it, which I wish they hadn't. I've promised the table I'll stay out of the Bowl, because any swap from here would only send me back down the coast. I've sixty-seven and a half grain, and Rob's lad on O20 is two steps from my Customs House, so the long season isn't over yet. Still, a rice farmer knows how to wait.

## Years 5–6

Diary of Paddy Doyle, Wheat Peasant of Province 7 (if you can believe it), end of Year 6.

Two years ago I was writing that a rice farmer knows how to wait. I waited, all right. In Year 5 they changed the rules, and suddenly the Bowl was a throne. Silke put it up at two and I sat on my hands because I'd promised the table I would. That was the hardest pass of my life, and I'd make it again. Graham took the Bowl for two, and Rob's lad walked off with my Customs House the same year. Then the population doubled, everybody went hungry and nobody paid a grain of tax. When I offered Graham forty for the Bowl in the open, he wouldn't sell at any price. He bought his Brush back off Rob for thirty instead, and by the end of the year he had my seat on the coast. Rob took my Scroll and Silke's to finish his raids, and then he took the throne off Reina. Now Reina's the Warlord and I'm a wheat farmer with sixty grain and nothing in my fields. All I have is a Vase and two Scrolls to sell. I don't blame Graham. He did it in the open and he kept faith with Silke, and that's more than I can say for the weather. I kept my word and it cost me the game, or this part of it anyway. My father always said a good name is worth more than a full barn. I'll find out next year whether he was right.
