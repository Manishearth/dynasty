## Setup–Year 2

Rob's diary, end of Year 2:

Two years in and I've worked out that I'm the villain. I've never enjoyed a board game more. I've paid no tax, I've had ten grain off Silke and Paddy, and I've spent twenty on a third lad to stand at the bottom of Graham's garden. I've still got my sixty, and nobody's bought a single one of my soldiers off me. The three farmers have spent two years making solemn promises to each other and then fighting over a bowl. Silke paid twenty-five for it and she's down to twenty-seven and a half, so she can't buy off more than one of my boys. Reina keeps writing my name in her little book and telling me walls have long memories. She's right, but they don't have legs, and mine are healing nicely. Next year W2 goes for Paddy's jar, W3 for Graham's brush and W1 for Silke's scroll. They're all two or three steps away, so they'll get there at roughly the same time, and I'll be grinning the whole way.

## Years 3–4

Rob's diary, end of Year 4:

Four years in and still not a grain of tax, and Reina's tally of my sins is long enough to wallpaper the Winter Palace. Year three was a proper soap opera. Paddy outbid Silke for the Vase at twenty-nine, finished his set and took her coast off her, so poor Silke ended up in the rice fields wondering what she'd done. Then Graham beat her to the Jar at twenty-three, and she's sitting on the only Wooden Bowl in China like a dragon on a hoard. I passed on every lot again. I don't need their pots. I need them to keep buying pots and putting them down where my lads can walk over and pick them up. My boys took their five a head off each farmer on the way in, and this year W3 finally walked through Graham's gate and lifted his bamboo brush off R14, which is the first name crossed off my list. W1 is one step from Paddy's customs house, and W2 is two from the scroll Silke so kindly bought for two and put out in the open. The farmers spend all their time correcting each other's minutes and swearing on Reina's book. They're so busy keeping their promises that nobody's noticed the desert never made any.
