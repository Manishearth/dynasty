# Graham's diary

## Setup–Year 2

Graham Garner's diary, end of Year 2

Two years, and I have one Bamboo Brush and sixty-four and a half grain. Paddy said he'd let me have the jar near six and then took it at seventeen. He said I could have the Customs House, then held his bid at six until I gave way. I wrote both of those down. Silke threatened me over the Bowl and paid twenty-five for it. Nineteen of that went into the pile she pays out from, and she can do that sum herself. I haven't paid much for anything, so I'm the second-richest at the table after the Emperor. I'm the only peasant who can still afford to wait. Next year Silke's Customs House comes up before my Vase. She's got the Bowl now, so the Vase is the one thing she can't do without. The jar is still on Q21. Rob keeps looking at it, and I've started to as well.

## Years 3–4

Graham Garner's diary, Year 4

Silke offered both at two, and I took her Customs House at two. When my Vase came up I set it at two as well. Paddy and Silke spent twenty-nine and twenty-eight fighting over it, and I kept seven without saying a word. Paddy took the Vase and the Silk province, and sent Silke down to the rice fields. Then he sold me the Jar at twenty-three after Silke tried to have me give it up for a promise. I've got the Brush, the Customs House and the Jar, and I'm one bowl short. Silke has agreed in front of Reina's book to put the Bowl up at two next year, and my Scroll went up at two to pay for that. But Paddy needs the same Bowl now and has seven grain more than me, so it'll come down to the two of us bidding for it. Rob's man is standing on S14, one step from my brush. I've written down everyone who kept their word and everyone who didn't. This year the first list is longer, which I didn't expect.

## Years 5–6

Graham Garner's diary, Years 5–6

First a correction. Paddy bid against Silke for the Vase, not against me, and I've fixed it on the list. Silke put the Bowl up at two as she'd promised, Paddy stayed out as he'd promised, and Rob let it go. So I wrote both their names on the good list. A year later Paddy offered me forty for the same Bowl, and I turned him down. Then I paid Rob thirty for my own brush, the one his soldier walked off with. I'd do it again, but I've written down what it cost. That finished my Wheat set. I took Paddy's seat on the silk coast rather than Silke's, because Silke kept her word to me every time, and I keep a list so that I know what I owe people. I've got ninety-nine and a half grain, a Customs House, a Brush and the Bowl. I'm a Scroll and a Vase short, and Paddy holds every one of them. Rob is on the throne now, after six years of refusing to pay tax, which tells you something about this table. He kept his word to me as well. I didn't expect to write that.

## Years 7–8

Graham Garner's diary, Years 7–8

First a correction, because I keep this book honest. I held two seats, not the four I claimed at the end. I was Wheat from the start and Silk from Year 6, and I've fixed it here. In Year 7 I sold my spare Customs House to Reina for two and kept the Jar back as something to bargain with. I let Silke's Brush go rather than spend fifteen keeping it from Paddy. Then I paid Paddy ten for a Scroll and put it where no soldier could reach it. That left me with twenty grain, a Customs House, a Brush, the Bowl and a Scroll, one Vase short of the throne. Paddy held the Vase back because Silke couldn't bid, and I wrote that down as fair, because it was. In Year 8 the card took everything. I sold four pieces back for eight grain, and I still finished twelve in debt. No soldier took any of it and nobody broke their word. It was just the sum. Silke proposed we give up, and I voted last and said yes. The good list ended up longer than the other one: Paddy, Silke, Reina and Rob all kept their word to me. I didn't expect to write that about this table. I still have the Jar.
