# Reina's diary

## Setup–Year 2

Reina's diary, end of Year 2:

Two years on the throne, and the Summer Palace stands on F17 with the first Province 1 canal section dug beside it. That leaves 460 of the 520 I owe, and 119 in the treasury to pay it with. I kept the tax at forty percent both years, and the peasants spent their spite on each other instead of on me. Paddy and Graham bled into the Harvest Pile over a single jar, and Silke paid twenty-five for a Wooden Bowl and is down to twenty-seven and a half, so the poorest at my table are the ones holding realia, which is what I wanted. Rob refuses tax and calls it a joke. He's hired a third man, but his legs gave out this year, and W1 is still five rounds from my nearest square. Next year it's the Winter Palace, to open the coastal canals, unless Rob's soldiers start walking north. If they do, I'll buy a man of my own and stand him in the garden.

## Years 3–4

Reina's diary, Year 4:

Four years on the throne. The Winter Palace stands on G18, and this year I'm digging the second canal section in Province 1, so the crown's harvest goes up by ten from next year. That leaves 400 of the works owed, and my treasury will be around 92 once Graham has his five. The tax stayed at forty percent both years. This year it brings in nothing, because the famine took everyone's income below zero, but I'd rather the table see a rate that doesn't move than one that does. Paddy has his full set and has taken the Silk province from Silke. She took it well enough to her face, but she had to give back her pieces and start again in the rice fields, and she won't forget who outbid her for that Vase. It would suit me if those two kept watching each other instead of watching me. Rob's men raided all three peasant provinces this year and still won't pay tax. W1 is five rounds from my wall, so the soldier I'd buy for the garden can wait one more year, but I've stopped expecting him to be polite.

## Years 5–6

Reina's diary, Years 5–6:

Six years on the throne, and then I wasn't on it any more. In Year 5 I kept the tax at forty percent and dug the first canal in Silke's rice fields. I held Paddy to his word over the Bowl, and he kept it, for exactly one year. In Year 6 the population doubled, the crown ate sixty, and I kept my grain and suspended Graham's five for the lean year. I spent the whole time watching the Silk seat. Paddy offered forty for the Bowl and Graham refused, and I thought the pots were still guarding my chair for me. Then Graham bought his Brush back from Rob for thirty, finished his set and took the coast from Paddy. Rob's lads took Silke's Scroll and Paddy's Scroll on the same march, and that was one raided piece from each of the three peasants, which is the Warlord's clause, the one revolt clause I never put in my own book. Rob has my reserve now, 185 of it, along with my palaces and my 360 in unfinished works. I have his desert, sixty grain and three soldiers on O23, Q25 and Q16. I built every wall in Province 1 that Rob is now sitting behind, and I know exactly where the gaps are.

## Years 7–8

Reina's diary, Years 7–8:

Two years in the desert, and then there was nothing left to command. In Year 7 the Bad Harvest took forty from me and pushed Silke and Paddy into the red. I bought Graham's spare Customs House for two and stood it at O18, mostly so he'd stay friendly. I let the Brush and the Scroll go past me, because a piece on a peasant's field is something for my lads to walk towards. W3 took five from Paddy's wheat on its way to Graham's coast, and W1 cut through empty Province 4 to within two steps of the Bowl, the Brush and the Scroll. Then the Emperor's Share card came up in Year 8. Graham had to sell all four of his pieces back to the pile, I sold my Customs House back, and the whole table ended up in debt, Rob deepest of anyone on the throne I built him. When Silke proposed giving up, I didn't need to do the sum again. I've been doing it since Year 1: my desert eats thirty more than it grows, there wasn't a piece left on the board to raid, and the crown couldn't lay a stone. So I voted yes, and so did everyone else. It ended in collapse with no winners and 320 of the works still owed. I finished five down, the least broke woman in a bankrupt China. The walls I built were sound. It was the weather that never added up.
