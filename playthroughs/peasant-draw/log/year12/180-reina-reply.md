diary: Years 11–12 and the end. I won with the crown nearly three hundred grain in debt, and I'm not going to dress that up as anything else. The population card took the north down in one turn. After that the rules locked my purse, so I couldn't build the Wall or the Canal, couldn't fund a single square, and couldn't even pay the twelve I owe Paddy or the six I owe Silke. All I had left to rule with was a low tax and honest words, and I used both. I'd go back on my old rule about every table fed and nobody in debt if it were only for myself, but I'm glad I said so in Year 11, before the harvest, when it was still true that nobody could save the crown without sinking too. Silke beat me fair in every way except the one that counts. She was the one who said no twice, and in the end she agreed out of grace, not because she had to. She was one Vase and one Scroll from my chair, and we both knew it. Graham kept his word to the last grain, Paddy turned every card fair even when the card was against him, and nobody at this table was afraid of me at the end, which is the only kind of throne I've ever wanted to keep. If there's a next game I'll do it differently: buy land earlier, keep a real reserve against the population cards, and count Silke's shelf first.
