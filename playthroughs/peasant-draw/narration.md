# Narration

The narrator's lines, chapter by chapter: the chapter title, each year's opening line, and the turning points, each tagged with the log number it follows. The narrator isn't part of the game, so none of this goes into `game.py` or the playthrough page; the page must make sense without it.

## Chapter 1

**Chapter One: You Already Had a Brush**

- **(002) Year 1 opens:** The first year of Reina's reign. No card is turned; the sixty is the harvest, forty in every hundred of it goes north, and a Summer Palace rises on G20. Nobody watches from the sand this time.
- **(Jar, after 008):** Seventeen for the only Dragon Jar in the empire, one grain over Graham. Silke keeps four; the Harvest Pile, as at every auction, keeps the rest.
- **(010):** Silke ends the first year with two of her four pieces and paid eleven for them. Graham ends it with none, and the most grain of any peasant.
- **(010, Year 2 opens):** The second year of the reign. The first card says the canal gives way, though no canal has yet been dug to give way; the harvest is average, and the frost finds Graham's wheat.
- **(012):** Eighty for the whole northern canal in one season, both sections at once. 420 of the works are still owed, and the crown has 84 to owe them with.
- **(017):** Silke, who needs neither, bids on the last Brush and the last Scroll. She gets the Brush for six; Paddy pays thirteen to keep his Scroll from her.

## Chapter 2

**Chapter Two: The Mandate Moves to the Coast**

- **(024, Year 3 opens):** The third year of Reina's reign. The villages are noisier by half, the harvest is good, and three pieces wait beside the board, one in each peasant's hand.
- **(027):** The crown asks fifty in every hundred, and spends it where it promised: the Province 7 Reservoir, forty from the throne and twenty from Graham. 360 of the works are still owed.
- **(030):** Graham keeps his word on the Customs House; Silke's minimum keeps it anyway. Fifteen asked, eight offered, and the last Customs House in China stays on the shelf.
- **(030):** Thirteen for the Bowl, twenty-five for the Vase, both to the coast. The girl who reads the cards now holds every piece her booklet asks for, and thirty-seven grain besides.
- **(032):** And so, in the third year, the Mandate moves to the coast. Silke takes the throne and a reserve of 129; Reina takes Province 5, sixty from the pile and a Customs House nobody would buy. Every piece Silke held goes back on the shelf.
- **(033):** The new crown's first act: Graham, who holds the pile, now holds the cards as well.
- **(033, Year 4 opens):** The fourth year, and the first of Silke's reign. The seed rots and the ox goes lame; only Graham's wheat, behind its new Reservoir, earns anything at all.
- **(035):** Thirty in every hundred, and only Graham owes it: three grain. The Winter Palace rises on H21 for twenty, and the coastal canals may now be dug. 340 still owed.
- **(042):** The Vase again, and close again: twenty-two to twenty-one, then one raise each. Paddy goes to every grain he can spare; Reina goes one and a half past it. Twenty-three and a half, and the Vase returns to P22, the same square, a different owner.
- **(045):** End of the chapter. Paddy holds three of his four; Graham two of his; Reina one, and the only one of its kind.

## Chapter 3

**Chapter Three: Every Grain into the Ground**

- **(049, Year 5 opens):** The second year of Silke's reign. The villages are no noisier than last year, the card having spent itself once already, but the harvest is good everywhere and Graham's, behind its Reservoir, is the best of it.
- **(051):** Forty in every hundred, and the crown builds the one work that must rise alone: the Marble Pagoda, on G22, for twenty. 320 still owed, and a reserve of 180 to owe it with.
- **(056):** Thirteen for a brush, one over Reina; six for a scroll nobody else wanted. Graham now lacks only the Jar, and there is one Jar in China, on Paddy's paddies.
- **(056, Year 6 opens):** The third year of Silke's reign. Fat years make large families: the harvest is good everywhere, the mouths are three hundred million, and for the first time in the dynasty a province may buy the sea.
- **(058):** Forty in every hundred, of nothing. No work rises this year; the crown buys four squares of water instead, and offers five a square to anyone who follows.
- **(065):** Eight a square for the coast, five for the south-east, one for the south centre, at the asking. Thirteen squares in a single season, and the crown's chest, which held a hundred and eighty a year ago, holds five.
- **(068):** The last brush in China opens at two, and nobody at the table can spare two for a thing they cannot eat. End of the chapter: the crown at nothing, nobody in debt, and two provinces of four still five short of bread in an average year.

## Chapter 4

**Chapter Four: Wheat to Silk to the Throne**

- **(072, Year 7 opens):** The fourth year of Silke's reign. The seed rots, the rains come late, three hundred million mouths eat what the land gives, and only the south centre has grain to spare.
- **(078):** Ten for the Dragon Jar, the piece Paddy has held since the first year. Graham's shelf is full, and the harvest has not yet been paid.
- **(079):** The harvest takes the crown to minus thirty and the coast to minus twenty-five. Then Graham stands, takes the Silk seat and its debt, and Reina starts again in the wheat with sixty from the Pile.
- **(085):** Seven for the Vase, two for the Scroll, and the Silk Peasant has a set. In a single year Graham goes from Wheat to Silk to the throne; the crown he takes is three grain short, and he pays it.
- **(086, Year 8 opens):** The first year of Graham's reign. An average harvest, and a card reminding the provinces who paid for the ditch: half the Reservoir's yield goes north to the crown.
- **(092):** Thirty in every hundred, and five a square for anyone who plants; three squares go into the sea. The canal does not: the crown would pay twenty and the coast ten, and neither would pay thirty.
- **(099):** Fourteen for the Bowl, and Reina's wheat shelf is full a year after she was put on it. She takes the coast back, and Silke, Empress a year ago, goes to the wheat with sixty from the Pile. Three seats have changed hands in two years; the Great Wall and the Grand Canal, 320 between them, have not moved.

## Chapter 5

**Chapter Five: Priced to the Grain**

- **(104, Year 9 opens):** The second year of Graham's reign. The seed rots again, the third bad harvest of the dynasty, and the crown's chest holds seventeen and a half against a loss of thirty.
- **(108):** Thirteen grain from the wheat to the throne, fifteen to come back. The crown comes through the bad year with half a grain to spare, and the woman it deposed holds its note.
- **(113):** No work again, the third year running; two squares go into the ground, at one grain and four from the crown. Eighteen squares now, and 320 still owed.
- **(118):** Two for the Jar, two for the Vase, sixteen for a brush its buyer already had. Reina's shelf lacks one Scroll. There are two in China: one in Silke's stock, one on Paddy's paddies.
- **(118, Year 10 opens):** The third year of Graham's reign. Heaven has favourites, and this year it favours only the coast; the crown's chest holds one and a half against a loss of thirty.
- **(121):** Twenty-nine from the coast to the throne, thirty-three to come back. The woman who wants the crown lends it the grain to stay standing.
- **(123):** Forty in every hundred, and both loans paid back the same night, in full. Nothing built for the fourth year running.
- **(126):** Four squares into the ground, at one grain each from the crown. Twenty-two now, and 320 still owed.
- **(129):** Fifty-three for a Scroll, which is exactly Reina's grain less the twenty a claim needs. The minimum and the limit meet to the grain.
- **(130):** And so the Mandate goes back to the coast and on up to the north: Reina takes the throne she lost seven years ago, with four and a half in the chest. Graham goes down to the silk with sixty from the Pile.


## Chapter 6

**Chapter Six: Two for the Bowl**

- **(143, the table gathers):** Before the card, the table is asked twice whether to stop where it sits. The second time three say yes; one says no, and one is enough.
- **(143, Year 11 opens):** The first year of Reina's second reign. A good harvest everywhere, and six hundred million mouths to eat it; the crown's chest holds fifteen and a half against a loss of a hundred and sixty.
- **(150):** The crown goes into debt by a hundred and forty-four and a half, and the tax brings back eight. Nothing built, no land bought, and the purse shut for everyone until the chest is back above nothing.
- **(157):** Two for the Customs House, nine for the Brush. Silke's shelf in the south centre now lacks one Wooden Bowl; there is one in China, in Paddy's stock.
- **(165, the table gathers again):** Asked twice more, the same three say yes, and the same one says no, now one Wooden Bowl from a set. The card stays face down.
- **(165, Year 12 opens):** The second year of Reina's second reign. Plenty for all of China and trouble for one province, and the province is the one whose reader turned the card. The crown's chest stands a hundred and thirty-six and a half below nothing, and the Bowl must go up.
- **(170):** Eighteen from the wheat to the rice before the harvest. The harvest takes twenty, the buffalo takes four, and the Scroll and the Customs House Paddy has kept since the first years go back to the Pile at two apiece.
- **(175):** Two for the Bowl, the cheapest piece of the night, and it finishes a set. Silke goes back to the coast she drew, carrying Graham's debt; Graham goes back to the wheat he drew, with sixty from the Pile.
- **(179, the table gathers a third time):** Asked a third time, nobody says no. Every player stands up in the seat they sat down in; the crown is two hundred and ninety-four and a half in debt, the Wall and the Canal are never built, and Year 13's card stays face down for good.
