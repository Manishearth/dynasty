# The Years 7–8 diary entries (Chapter 4 replayed)

## Silke

### Years 7–8

Years seven and eight were the years I learned what an empty chest feels like. The rot came the year after I'd sunk every grain into the ground, and the throne went into debt for the first time. I had to go to Graham with my hand out, and he gave me twenty, not thirty, and made me feel every grain of the difference. Then I taxed him at fifty percent twice, because he'd just bought the Jar that finished his set and I didn't like how much grain he was holding. He wrote it down. I believe him. I owe him thirty now, and his Wheat set can't take my chair, but it can take Paddy's or Reina's, and twenty of his twenty-two is "spoken for." Reina is thirty in debt with an empty shelf, so for now the only person who could unseat me has nothing to do it with, and I won't pretend that doesn't help me sleep. Every grain I raised went back into land: one square for the north, which finally pays its own way, and a half-share each for Paddy and Graham. Nobody built a stone of the Wall or the Canal, and I won't lose sleep over that either, because the game ends when the last one goes up and I'm in no hurry. Next year Graham gets paid first, before anything else, because a creditor who's been paid is a friend, and an unpaid one is the first to call for a revolt. After that the coast gets its canal, so Reina remembers who dug it for her.

## Reina

### Years 7–8

Years 7–8. The ox went lame on the coast, and I asked Graham for twenty-nine in front of everyone. He lent the crown twenty and bought Paddy's Jar instead, so my Vase and my Scroll went back to the Pile at two each, and Paddy picked up the Vase for two. That's the same Vase I paid twenty-three and a half to keep away from him. I'm thirty in debt now and I hold nothing. I can't bid, I can't buy land, and I can't claim, and in two years I've gone from a woman nobody needed to fear to one nobody needs to answer. I offered Graham my vote for his grain, and he was right to refuse, because a promise with no date on it isn't worth much at his table. What hurts is that everyone has been kind about it: Paddy won't vote me down, and Silke keeps promising me the coast canal "once I'm clear," which is the one condition I can't meet. The throne has gone out of my reach. What's left is to keep the coast out of a revolt, find someone at this table who needs a friend more than he needs grain, and make sure that whoever ends this game owes me a little for how it ended.

## Paddy

### Years 7–8

Years 7 and 8. The bad harvest hit me when I had four and a half grain, and I had to choose between selling the Dragon Jar to Graham and watching all three of my pots go back to the Pile at two apiece. I sold it, eighteen in the end, because Graham paid me the extra six when he didn't have to, and I won't forget that. Then the Vase came round at last, for two grain, and nobody else even wanted it. So now it sits on R22 where the Jar used to be, and the one piece I'm missing is the one I sold, on Graham's shelf, and he won't part with it for twenty. I can't decide whether that's funny or sad. I put another ten into R27, and that's four squares of new land now. I've eight grain, I don't owe anybody anything, and next year the paddies should feed me better than they ever have. Reina's thirty in the hole and nobody will carry her, and Graham's sitting on a full set saying he won't use it. I mean to keep saving, and if Graham ever needs grain more than he needs that Jar, I'll be the one at the table who has it.

## Graham

### Years 7–8

Years 7 and 8. I paid eighteen for the Jar in a rotten year, six more than Paddy asked of a man with nothing, and now the Wheat set sits complete on Q15 to Q18. Since Paddy needs only the Jar for his own set, it will stay on my shelf whatever he offers. Silke took fifteen of the only harvest in China, then ten for the ditch I half paid for, then twenty-seven and a half more, and now she owes me thirty, five of it a salary she wrote on a slate rather than pay. I bought one square for ten and turned down Reina's thirty, because a promise with no date on it isn't a number. Paddy's rival set and Reina's debt both work for me. I end with twenty-two: enough to move the set, nowhere near enough to be comfortable. The Wall or the Canal can end this any year now. I'll keep the best farm on the board, collect the thirty first out of next year's tax as she promised in front of everyone, and if she doesn't pay, the set is there to remind her that a peasant can climb.
