## Reina's diary so far, in her own words

## Years 1–2

Two years on the throne and the crown has put up the Summer Palace and the whole northern canal, so the canal pays me twenty a year now and nothing half-dug is left for the river to take. That cost the reserve: I started at sixty, went as high as a hundred and twenty-two, and now I'm down to seventy-nine. That's thinner than I'd like with the land still to open, and I need to start building that cushion back. I've held the tax at forty, which they've grumbled about but taken. Paddy is the one I'm watching, at twenty-nine and a half after paying seventeen for the Jar and thirteen for a scroll, and a bad card could turn him against the crown. Graham and Paddy are already tangled up over who promised which Customs House, and Silke is quietly picking up whatever the two of them don't guard. Next comes the Winter Palace, which opens the coastal canals, and then Graham's Reservoir before his patience runs out. If incomes stay this thin I'll have to ease the tax, because I'd rather give up a few grain than face a revolt.

## Years 3–4

Years 3–4. I kept my word to Graham. I taxed the good year at fifty and built his Reservoir with him, and that same year I lost the throne to Silke, the woman I'd been paying five a year to read the cards. While I was busy watching Paddy and Graham fight over the Customs House, she quietly put a whole set together on the coast, and I never counted her shelf. That's the lesson, and it hurts. I came off the throne with sixty, lost ten to the bad harvest and then spent twenty-three and a half on the only Vase in China to stop Paddy finishing his set, so I'm down to twenty-seven, the poorest at the table. I still think it was the right buy: anyone who wants the throne now has to go through me, and Paddy is one Vase short and knows it. Next is the long climb: a scroll, a brush and a bowl, and enough grain kept back to claim with. Meanwhile I'll press Silke for the Province 5 canal, now that the Winter Palace is up, because she promised the coast those canals in front of everyone.

## Years 5–6

Years 5–6. I took Graham's scroll for six, lost the brush to him by one grain, and told myself I could wait, and then the population doubled and I lost all the waiting in a single card. A province that was poor turned into one that would lose forty-five a year, so I put twenty-four of my last twenty-five grain into two squares of sea. Silke only paid eight a square after I'd asked for ten, but she still gave me more than anyone else got. That leaves me with one grain, the Vase and a scroll, and I've never been this poor or this close to the bottom of the table. It was still the right call: I'm out of debt and my losses are down to five a year, and nobody is afraid of a woman with one grain. Paddy's brush is still unsold and he's promised it to me at two. Graham has made it plain that he's after Paddy's Jar and not my pieces. Next I need the brush, then Graham's Bowl, then twenty grain to claim, all before the Wall and the Grand Canal end the game. That's at most two years, so I can't make a single wasted move.
