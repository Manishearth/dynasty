# Silke Weber's diary

## Years 1–2

Two years in, and I hold both brushes in the empire and a deed for my coast. It cost me seventeen grain, and the Jar paid four of that back. Graham's quiet "you already had a brush" stung more than I let on, but he's out of the race for good now, and I'd buy it again. Paddy is the one to watch: he has the Jar and a scroll, he needs my last Customs House and Graham's Vase, and I need his Bowl and that same Vase. Next year my Customs House goes up and Paddy will expect it, so I'll set the price where it hurts him. Whoever gets the Vase decides this game, so I'm keeping my fifty-five dry and letting everyone think I'm only the girl who reads the cards.

## Years 3–4

Year three was the year I stopped being the girl who reads the cards. I set my Customs House at fifteen to bleed Paddy, and nobody bought it. I didn't mind, because I took the Bowl from Graham by a single grain and the Vase from Paddy by a single grain, and then I stood up and took Reina's throne with a hundred and sixty-six grain behind me. It cost me thirty-eight grain and every piece I'd collected, since they all went back to the stock. That's the price of the crown, and I'd pay it again tomorrow. As Empress I kept it gentle: a light tax in a rotten year, a Winter Palace built with my own grain, and Graham's five for reading the cards, because Graham is the only one at this table who has nothing to gain from my fall. Now Reina has my old shelf and the Vase sitting on P22 exactly where I put it. Paddy has the Jar, a Scroll and the Customs House, and he's waiting for the Vase to come round again. Graham is quietly collecting too. My reserve is down to a hundred and nineteen, three hundred and forty of the works are still owed, and once the population doubles I'll have to buy land. My job now is to make sure that whichever of them gets close, the others want to stop them more than they want to stop me.

## Years 5–6

Year five was the good year. I set forty percent, built the Pagoda with grain the table had paid in, and finished with a hundred and eighty in the chest. Then Graham drew Population Doubles and every province went into the red together. That chest is now empty. It all went into the ground: four squares of sea for the north and a share of every square the others bought, thirteen squares across China. I don't regret a grain of it, because next year this empire finally has a harvest I can tax. Graham surprised me. He asked the crown for one grain a square so I'd still have enough to pay him, and I'm starting to think he's the only honest man at this table. Or he wants me to think so while he waits for Paddy's Jar. Reina is down to one grain and told us not to be afraid of her. Those are the words I'd use too. Next year I tax the new fields hard enough to start the Canal, I keep something back in case the population card comes again, and I watch who ends up holding that Jar.

## Years 7–8

Two years ago I had a throne, and tonight I have a ditch full of wheat and a Reservoir someone else paid for twice. Year seven was the one that broke me. The rains failed, my chest was already empty because I'd put it into everyone's fields, and when I asked Graham for thirty he bought Paddy's Jar instead and took the coast with it. I taxed Reina at ninety percent to get out of the red. I knew it was hard when I did it, and a year later she paid me back for it in full. Then Graham walked onto my throne with three short in the chest, and I learned what it feels like to be told I'd be forgiven for not being paid. I fought Reina for the Bowl and lost by one grain, after warning the whole table about her out loud, and then I gave her the reason myself. She took the coast, my nineteen and a half, and the two squares of sea I'd only just paid thirty for. What I've kept is the best land at this table, fifty-five a year, the bank, and the Vase and Scroll sitting on my own shelf, which nobody but Reina and Graham's old seat will want. The throne goes through the Silk seat, and the Silk seat now belongs to the woman who knows exactly how I play. So next year I keep my grain dry, sell the Vase and Scroll dearly to whoever needs them, and build the Wheat set again quietly, piece by piece, until the coast is in reach. This time I won't announce it first.

## Years 9–10

Years nine and ten were the years I stopped trying to win the race and started charging the toll on it. I lent the crown thirteen and got fifteen back. I sold Reina the Vase for two and the Brush cost her sixteen out of fear, and in the end she bought my Scroll for fifty-three, the most she could pay and still climb. She walked onto the throne with twenty grain and a smile and said she'd have done the same, and I believe her, which is the trouble with Reina. It cost me what I'd let myself want: the Vase and the Scroll are back on my shelf, but they're stock to sell now, not pieces of a set. All I hold is one Jar, and I'm three pieces short of anything. What I have instead is seventy-three and a half grain, the most at this table. I have two new squares of the Warlord's land, wheat that earns a hundred and fifteen in an ordinary year, and the bank. Reina's crown has fifteen and a half in the chest and three hundred and twenty of works still owed, and Graham is back on the coast with sixty and a long memory. So next year I sell the Vase and Scroll dearly again, I buy the Bowl and the Brushes when they come round in Province 6, and I keep enough grain dry that when the population card doubles, I'm the one who still has a field. I'm not announcing anything this time either.

## Years 11–12 and the end

Twelve years, and I ended up exactly where I started, in the Silk seat on the coast. I'd still call it a victory of a kind. In Year 11 I bought the Customs House for two and Paddy's Brush for nine. In Year 12 I lent Paddy eighteen to keep his Deed off the Pile, and the Pile took it anyway, along with his buffalo. He gave me back the Bowl for two, and that finished the Wheat set I'd sworn four years ago to build quietly. This time I did build it quietly, and then I spent it on a ditch three hundred grain deep. I took Graham's nineteen and a half of debt to get back the coast I drew, and I'd do it again, because the Wheat seat was a loss and the Silk seat is a hold. Reina wins. She kept the throne through two collapses and never once lied to my face, and I've stopped being angry about that. Paddy owes me twenty-three and a half that I'll never see, the crown owes me six it will never pay, and the Great Wall and the Grand Canal were never built. What I take home is forty-eight and a half grain I didn't borrow, a Brush, a Bowl and a Customs House on the coast, and the knowledge that I was the one who kept this game going two years longer than anyone wanted. That cost me the throne once and it nearly cost me everything. Next time I'll take a deal from Reina when she offers one.
