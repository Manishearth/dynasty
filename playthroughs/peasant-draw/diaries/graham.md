# Graham Garner's diary

## Years 1–2

Two years. I started with thirty-six and now have fifty-four and a half, and the only piece I own is one Customs House on Q15. The Jar went to Paddy at seventeen, one grain past my sixteen. Silke paid six for a second brush she had no use for, and called it my price. A frost took two more off me. With both brushes on Silke's coast and the only Jar in Paddy's paddies, I can't finish my set unless one of them gives something back, and neither will. What I do have is the Vase, and both of them need it. Nobody else has one. I'll put it up next year and let them bid against each other for it, and I'll stand aside on Paddy's Customs House as I promised, because I keep my word even when the others don't. After that I save my grain for land. When the population doubles, a square costs twenty and pays twenty a year, and that's the best number on the board.

## Years 3–4

Years 3 and 4. I paid twenty into the Reservoir, and it gave me twenty back in the first bad harvest, the only positive income in China that year. That's the best twenty I've spent. I sold the Vase twice: six grain from Silke, five and a half from Reina, and both times the buyer was left with nothing to show for it. Silke took the throne, and her taking it sent every piece back into stock, which no counting of mine had allowed for. That's how I got the Bowl for nine, a year after losing it at twelve. Now I hold the Customs House and the Bowl and seventy-four grain, the most of any peasant. The brushes are sitting in Paddy's stock, and the Jar is the one thing I can't buy. I still have the Scroll, and Paddy and Reina will both want it. I kept my word to Paddy, and he knows it, so when I ask him about that Jar I want it remembered. I'll keep my grain together until the population doubles. Then land at twenty pays twenty, and I'll stop needing anyone's pottery.

## Years 5–6

Years 5 and 6. I paid thirteen for Paddy's brush, one under my limit, and sold my last Scroll to Reina for six, which was my minimum. That leaves the Jar as the only piece missing from my set. Then I turned the card and it said three hundred million. Every province went to nothing or less, and Silke spent the whole crown on fields. I put seventy-six of my own into four squares of the Warlord's land, and the crown paid four, one grain a square. That turns twenty-five short a year into fifty-five over. I end the year with thirty-two and a half. Silke has nothing, Reina has one, Paddy has four and a half. I didn't bid on the second brush. I've said once at this table what it looks like when someone does that, and I don't need to show them. The game could end with next year's canal or wall, so the squares may only pay back once, but once still covers their cost. The Jar is next. Paddy is the only one holding it, I'm the only one with grain, and I want him to remember that I stood aside for his Customs House when he names his price.

## Years 7–8

Years 7 and 8. I started Year 7 as the Wheat Peasant with thirty-two and a half grain and finished it on the throne. Paddy sold me his Jar for ten because I'd stood aside for his Customs House, and that finished the Wheat set. I used it to take Reina's seat, which cost me twenty-five of her debt and the Jar itself. Then the Pile sold me the Vase for seven and the Scroll for two, and with those I held the Silk set and twenty and a half grain. I paid three to clear the crown's debt and sat down in Silke's chair. She had paid six for a brush she didn't need to keep me from a set. It cost me about forty grain all told, and that's the cheapest thing I've bought at this table. As Emperor I taxed at thirty and paid five a square to everyone, which was what the last crown paid. I paid Reina exactly what I promised her. Silke wouldn't put twenty into her own canal, so nothing got built, and everyone heard her refuse. Now Reina is back on the coast with sixty-two grain and two of the four Silk pieces, and the Vase and the Scroll she needs are on Silke's shelf. Next year I watch what Silke asks for them, I keep the tax low enough that nobody has a reason to revolt, and I count every grain before it leaves the crown's chest.

## Years 9–10

Years 9 and 10. I sat on the throne with one and a half grain and borrowed to keep it out of debt both years, thirteen from Silke and then twenty-nine from Reina. I paid every grain back in full and on the day I said, which meant taxing at forty and building nothing. The north never got its square, and I'll be counting that against me until the population card comes. Then Silke sold the last Scroll to Reina for fifty-three, which was the exact price of the Vase she'd let go at two, and Reina walked into my chair with twenty grain and the Silk set I'd built for her by keeping the crown solvent. I'm on the coast again with sixty grain, the same seat I took from her in Year 7, and the board has gone all the way round once. Reina paid my appointees what I'd promised them, and I'll give her credit for that, but I lent to no one, broke no word, and still lost the throne to a woman who borrowed nothing from me. Next year I buy sea squares for Province 5 before the population doubles, I watch what the Bowl and the Brushes cost out of Paddy's stock, and I keep track of who at this table prices things to the grain.

## Years 11–12 and the end

Years 11 and 12, and the end. I went into Year 11 with sixty grain and five squares of sea, and two harvests later I was nineteen and a half under, holding nothing, with no piece left to sell. The Customs House went to Silke for two. Paddy's brush went to her for nine, one grain over my eight, which makes two brushes at this table I've lost by a single grain, and I counted both. I wouldn't borrow what I couldn't show the numbers to repay, so I took the debt in the open and sat out the auctions. Then Silke finished her Wheat set and took my seat to reach the coast, and that took my nineteen and a half of debt off me. I drew sixty clean grain from the Pile and sat down in Province 7, the province I drew at the start of the game. I finish there, holding my seat, with sixty grain and nothing owed to anyone, which is more than any other peasant has. Reina wins on a throne that's two hundred and ninety-four grain under, and she knew it was that deep before any of us did. Twelve years, the Wall and the Canal never built, and the board went all the way round and put me back where I started with more grain than I had then. Next time I take my first brush at whatever it costs, and I'll be quicker about it.
