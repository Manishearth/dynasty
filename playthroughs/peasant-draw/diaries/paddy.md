# Paddy Doyle's diary

## Years 1–2

Two years in, and I've spent thirty grain on pots and paper, more than I've ever spent on anything I couldn't plant. But the Dragon Jar and the Land Deed were the only ones left in all of China, and now they're both sitting on my paddies at R22 and R23. Graham shook on it fair and square: the last Customs House is mine next year, and I mean to keep my side of it as carefully as he keeps his. Silke worries me a bit. She bid twelve on a scroll she didn't need, and now she wants Graham's Vase same as I do, and my Wooden Bowl as well. I've only 29 and a half grain, so I'll have to be careful. I'll save what I can, I won't owe anyone a thing, and I'll keep the Bowl back until it's worth something to me.

## Years 3–4

Two years, and I came close twice, and both times somebody beat me to the Vase: Silke by one grain, then Reina by one step. I can't hold it against either of them. I told the whole table it would finish my set, and Reina's just lost a throne, so of course she wasn't going to let me have it. Silke, meanwhile, played her game quietly the whole way through and now she's sitting on the throne, which taught me that you don't have to shout to win. The Customs House is finally mine, and I paid only two grain for it, so I've got the Jar, the Deed and the House in a row on my paddies with 42 and a half grain, and I don't owe anyone a thing. Next time I won't say out loud what a piece is worth to me. I'll just keep my twenty back, save the rest, and wait for the Vase to come round again, and I'll be ready when it does.

## Years 5–6

Two years ago I had forty-two and a half grain and was dreaming about the Vase. Now I've four and a half, and for once I think I've done the right thing with it. Graham's card doubled the mouths in every province, and the paddies would have eaten me hollow, forty-five grain a year. So I put forty-five into three squares of new land at R25, R26 and S26, and now the south-east just about feeds itself. I sold a brush to Graham for thirteen, and the other one's still on my shelf, since nobody had two grain to spare for it, and I've half promised it to Reina. What I keep thinking about is Graham telling the whole table he's waiting on my Jar, and that he'll be the one with the grain. I didn't give him an answer, and I won't sell the Jar cheap, not after all it's cost me. The Vase feels further off than ever, but I'm not in debt and I don't owe anyone a thing, and next year's harvest finally comes in my favour. If the game runs on, I'll save every grain the new fields give me and be ready when the Vase comes round.

## Years 7–8

Two rotten harvests, and the second one nearly took everything I own. I had four and a half grain and owed ten, and the Pile would have had my Jar, my Deed and my Customs House for two apiece. Graham wouldn't lend me six, but he gave me ten for the Jar. I took it and shook his hand, and that same night he finished his set with my Jar and walked up to the throne. I can't call it unfair. He was the only one at the table with grain to spare, and he spent it. Then the Bowl and the Brush I'd sold him came back to me, I sold them both to Reina, and with them she finished the wheat set and took the coast back off Silke. So my old pots have put two people in new seats this year, and neither of them was me. I kept my word on the Brush, I bought a square at Q26 with the crown paying five, and I've nine grain and no debts. I'm still the Rice Peasant, which this game hasn't let everyone say. The Jar's on Reina's shelf now and the Vase is on Silke's, and I need both of them again, so it's further off than ever. But next year I'm reading the cards as well as collecting the tax, the rice will bring in thirty-five in an ordinary year, and I mean to save every grain of it.

## Years 9–10

Years 9 and 10, and I read my first two cards, and both were bad for the rice. Even so I've come through them with fifteen grain, no debts, and two more squares of sea at Q27 and R27, which is more paddy than this province has ever had. I sold my last Brush to Reina for sixteen, though really Silke made her pay that, and it was the last of my stock. Then I sat and watched Reina gather every piece of the silk set, finish it with Silke's Scroll at fifty-three, and climb back onto the throne. That makes three different people up there in front of me, and none of them ever needed my Vase as badly as I did. I'm not bitter about it. Reina's paid me the six she owed me, and she's kept me on the tax and the cards, and a crown that keeps its word is worth more to a rice farmer than a crown that doesn't. What I keep turning over is that Reina's set has gone back on the shelves: the Bowl and both Brushes to mine, the Vase and the Scroll to Silke's, and Silke's sitting on the Jar. With the Wall and the Canal still owed, there may only be a year or two left, and fifteen grain won't buy the Jar and the Vase and keep twenty back besides. So I'll do what I've always done. I'll sell the pots that have come home to me at a fair price, save every grain the new fields bring in, and if the set's out of reach, I'll at least finish this game the way I started it, as the Rice Peasant who never owed anybody a thing.

## Years 11–12 and the end

Years 11 and 12, and the end of it. I turned the cards fair to the last. The last one was Fortune and Misfortune, a good harvest for everyone and trouble for one province, and the one province was mine. A buffalo broke its leg in the middle of planting, and that one animal cost me the Land Deed and the Customs House, two grain apiece back into the Pile, after twelve years of keeping them. Silke lent me eighteen to try and save them, and I sold her the Bowl at two, because a high price would only have fed the Pile and not her. She used it to finish her wheat and walk onto the coast, so my old pots have now moved four people into new seats, and none of them was me. I'm finishing where I started, the Rice Peasant in Province 6, with no grain, no pieces, and seven new squares of paddy that weren't there when I came. What sits worst with me isn't the set. It's the twenty-three and a half I still owe Silke, after all those years of telling myself I'd never owe anybody a thing. The crown owes me twelve, and every grain of it is hers if it ever comes. Reina won it fairly and kept her word to me the whole way, and I'll take that, and next time I'll keep a few grain back for the buffalo.
